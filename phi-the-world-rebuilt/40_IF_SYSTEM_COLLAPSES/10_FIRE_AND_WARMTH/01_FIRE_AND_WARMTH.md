# FIRE AND WARMTH

## How to Make Fire (5 Methods)

### 1. Matches or Lighter (Easiest)
If you still have matches or a lighter, use them. Store them in a dry bag. Keep extras. This is the obvious one — don't overthink it.

### 2. Flint and Steel
Strike a piece of high-carbon steel against flint or quartz. Catch the sparks on tinder — cotton, char cloth, or dried fungus. Keep your tinder bundle loose and fluffy so it catches air.

```
  STEEL ──►  FLINT
   \         /
    \       /
     \     /
      * * *  ← sparks land on tinder
       \_/
    (tinder bundle)
```

### 3. Bow Drill (Friction Fire)
This one takes practice but works with just sticks and a string.

**What you need:**
- **Bow** — a curved stick, ~arm's length, with a loose string
- **Spindle** — a straight stick, ~12 inches long, pointed on both ends
- **Fireboard** — a flat, dry piece of softwood (cedar, willow, cottonwood)
- **Socket** — a stone or hardwood piece to press down on the spindle

**How it works:**
```
         SOCKET (press down)
            │
     ┌──────┴──────┐
     │   SPINDLE   │  ← spins when bow is pulled back and forth
     └──────┬──────┘
     ═══════╧═══════  ← FIREBOARD (flat, with notch cut)
    ┌────────────────┐
    │   notch here   │  ← catch the ember in the notch
    └────────────────┘
            │
       BOW STRING wraps around spindle
```

**Steps:**
1. Cut a small notch in the fireboard's edge
2. Spin the spindle in the notch using the bow
3. Dust collects in the notch — when it smokes, you have an ember
4. Transfer the ember to a tinder bundle and blow gently

### 4. Hand Drill (Friction Fire — No Bow Needed)
Same concept as bow drill, but you roll the spindle between your palms instead of using a bow. Harder work, but fewer parts.

```
     PALMS (rolling)
     ┌─────┐
     │ ║║║ │  ← spindle between palms
     │ ║║║ │
     └──╚╝──┘
     ════════  ← fireboard
```

Roll your palms apart while pressing down. Speed and pressure create the ember.

### 5. Magnifying Glass
Any convex lens — a magnifying glass, eyeglasses, even the bottom of a clear glass bottle — can focus sunlight into a tiny, hot point. Aim it at dark-colored tinder on a calm day.

```
        ☀ SUN
         │
         │
       ┌─┴─┐
       │ ◯ │  ← lens (magnifying glass)
       └─┬─┘
         │  ← focused beam
         ▼
       * * *  ← point of light on tinder
       (dark cotton, char cloth)
```

## The Phi-Fire

Fire follows nature's ratio. Build it right and it burns hotter, cleaner, and uses less wood.

### Kindling-to-Fuel Ratio
Build your fire at the golden ratio:

```
    kindling : fuel = 1 : φ
                         = 1 : 1.618

    So if you use 1 handful of kindling,
    use about 1.6 handfuls of fuel.
```

This gives enough airflow to feed the flame while maintaining enough mass to hold heat.

### The Phi-Fire Layout
Lay your logs at **137.5°** to each other — the golden angle. This creates natural airflow gaps so the fire breathes without collapsing.

```
        LOG 1
        ╱
       ╱ 137.5°
      ╱
    LOG 2 ──────── LOG 3
          137.5°
```

Stack in a loose teepee or log cabin pattern with each layer rotated 137.5° from the one below.

### The Phi-Cooking Fire
Standard cooking temperature is about **350°F (175°C)**. A phi-ratio cooking fire runs at:

```
    350 × φ⁻¹ = 350 × 0.618 ≈ 216°F (102°C)
```

This is slow-cooking temperature — perfect for stew, drying meat, or keeping water warm without boiling it away. Low and slow.

## How to Stay Warm

Cold kills faster than hunger. Here's how to hold heat.

### 1. Insulate from the Ground
The ground steals heat faster than air. Never sleep on bare earth.

- Straw, leaves, cardboard, pine boughs — pile them under you
- The thicker the better. Aim for at least **4-6 inches** of insulation
- Even newspaper or plastic bags work in a pinch

```
     YOU (sleeping)
    ┌─────────────────┐
    │  ══════════════ │  ← insulation layer (6 inches)
    │  ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░ │  ← ground
    └─────────────────┘
```

### 2. Trap Heat
- Reflective surfaces (tin foil, mylar blankets, even a car sunshade) behind you bounce body heat back
- Build a small wind wall from branches, logs, or snow
- Seal gaps in shelters with moss, mud, or packed snow

```
     REFLECTOR WALL
     (logs/tin foil)
     │▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│
     │▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│  ← reflects your heat
     │▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│     back toward you
     └─────────┘
      ┌───────┐
      │  YOU  │  → heat flows this way
      └───────┘
```

### 3. Share Body Heat
Two people sharing a blanket stay much warmer than one. Sleep close together — bodies give off about **100 watts** of heat. That's a light bulb. Use it.

### 4. The Phi-Blanket
Layer your coverings at phi-ratio thicknesses for the best insulation-to-weight ratio:

```
    Layer 1 (base):    1 unit thick  — wool or fleece
    Layer 2 (middle):  1.6 units     — down or synthetic fill
    Layer 3 (outer):   2.6 units     — wind/water shell

    Each layer traps air at a different scale.
    Phi-spacing keeps them from compressing each other.
```

This mimics how fur works on animals — different hair lengths at golden-ratio intervals trap air at multiple scales.

## Fire Safety

- Clear a **10-foot diameter** circle around your fire
- Keep water or dirt nearby to extinguish
- Never leave a fire unattended — put it out before sleeping
- Burn fires down to ash, then scatter the cold ash
- Check local regulations before building open fires

