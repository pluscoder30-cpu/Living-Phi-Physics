# ENVIRONMENT ASSESSMENT GUIDE

## How to Read the Land

The land talks to you if you know how to listen. Here are the signs.

### Water Signs

Water is life. Find it first.

| Sign | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| Green vegetation in a dry area | Water is underground or nearby |
| Flocks of birds flying together | They are heading to or from water |
| Lots of insects (mosquitoes, flies) | Standing water is close |
| Dense bushes or trees in a line | They follow an underground water vein |
| Animals walking in a line | They made a path to water |

### Soil Signs

Soil tells you what you can grow and build.

| Soil Type | How to Spot It | Good For |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Dark, rich soil | Almost black, smells earthy | Growing food |
| Red soil | Reddish color, sticky when wet | Making bricks |
| Sandy soil | Gritty, falls apart, drains fast | Drainage, building paths |
| Clay soil | Smooth, holds water, gets hard when dry | Sealing water containers |
| Rocky soil | Lots of stones, thin layer on top | Stone walls, tools |

### Weather Signs

Know what is coming before it arrives.

| Sign | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| Red sky at night | Good weather tomorrow |
| Red sky in morning | Bad weather coming |
| Low, dark clouds | Rain within hours |
| High, wispy clouds | Rain in 1-2 days |
| Wind shifting to the north | Cold weather coming |
| Wind shifting to the south | Warm weather coming |
| Birds flying low | Low air pressure, rain coming |
| Crickets chirping more than usual | Warm weather ahead |

### Danger Signs

Some things mean you should stay away.

| Sign | Danger |
|------|--------|
| Dead trees in a group | Disease or poisoned soil |
| Strange chemical smell | Contamination |
| Discolored water (orange, green, milky) | Pollution or chemicals |
| No insects or animals around | Something is wrong |
| Bubbles rising in still water | Gas or contamination below |
| White crust on soil | Salt or chemical buildup |
| Mutant plant growth (twisted, extra leaves) | Radiation or chemical exposure |

## How to Test Your Environment

Never trust alone. Always test.

### Water Test

1. **Find water.** River, stream, pond, rain, or well.
2. **Look at it.** Clear is better than cloudy. No smells.
3. **pH test.** Use pH strips if you have them. Target: 6.5 to 7.5. Below 6.5 = too acidic. Above 7.5 = too alkaline.
4. **Taste test (last resort).** Take a tiny sip. Wait 10 minutes. If it tastes strange, sour, metallic, or makes your throat feel weird, do not drink it.
5. **Boil it.** Always boil water before drinking if you are not sure. Rolling boil for 1 full minute kills most germs.

### Soil Test

1. **Dig a hole.** About 1 foot deep.
2. **Smell the soil.** Good soil smells earthy and fresh. Sour or rotten smell = something is wrong.
3. **Squeeze test.** Grab a handful and squeeze. It should hold its shape for a moment, then crumble when you poke it.
4. **Worm test.** If you see earthworms, the soil is alive and healthy. No worms = soil may be dead or poisoned.
5. **Plant test.** Plant three seeds. If none sprout in a week, the soil needs help.

### Air Test

1. **Light a candle.** Hold it in open air.
2. **Watch the flame.** Steady flame = calm, clean air. Flickering wildly = strong wind or air currents. Goes out immediately = something may be wrong with the air (gases, fumes).
3. **Smell the air.** Clean air smells like nothing. Chemical, sulfur, or rotten egg smells = danger.
4. **Check your lungs.** If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or get a headache outdoors, leave the area.

### Radiation Test

- **You cannot see, smell, or taste radiation.**
- If you find something glowing, **stay away.**
- If you find a metal object that looks like it came from a military or industrial site, **do not touch it.**
- If many people or animals in an area are getting sick with no clear reason, **leave the area.**
- A simple Geiger counter (if available) can detect radiation. Normal background is 0.1 to 0.3 microsieverts per hour. Above 1 is concerning. Above 10 is dangerous.

## The Phi-Environment Map

Map your area using phi-principles. Phi (1.618) is the golden ratio found everywhere in nature. Use it to space and organize your resources.

### Step 1: Mark Your Sources

Draw a simple map of your area on paper. Use these symbols:

```
W = Water source
F = Food source
S = Shelter
D = Danger zone
```

### Step 2: Connect with Phi-Spaced Paths

Distance your resources using phi-ratios. If your closest water source is 100 steps away, your food source should be about 162 steps (100 x 1.618) away. This spreads your resources so one disaster cannot take out everything.

### Step 3: Build the Map

Here is a blank template:

```
                    N
                    |
                    |
         D         |         F
       (danger)    |       (food)
                    |
  W ────────────────+──────────────── S
 (water)            |              (shelter)
                    |
                    |
         F         |         W
       (food)      |       (water)
                    |
                    |
                    S
                (shelter)


  SCALE: Each dash = ~50 steps

  LEGEND:
  W = Water       F = Food
  S = Shelter     D = Danger
  + = Base camp (center of your phi-map)
```

### Step 4: Mark Phi-Distances

From your base camp (+), measure and mark:

| Resource | Phi-Space | Distance (example) |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|
| Primary water | 1x | 100 steps |
| Secondary water | 1.618x | 162 steps |
| Primary food | 1x | 100 steps (different direction) |
| Secondary food | 1.618x | 162 steps |
| Shelter 1 | 0.618x | 62 steps |
| Shelter 2 | 1x | 100 steps |

This pattern means no single threat (flood, fire, contamination) can destroy all your resources at once.

## Assessment Checklist

Use this checklist when you arrive in a new area:

- [ ] **Water found** — at least 2 sources identified
- [ ] **Water tested** — pH is safe (6.5-7.5), no bad taste or smell
- [ ] **Soil checked** — dark, earthy, has worms, holds shape when squeezed
- [ ] **Food sources mapped** — wild edibles, fruit trees, fish, game trails
- [ ] **Shelter identified** — at least 1 safe place to sleep
- [ ] **Danger zones marked** — flood areas, cliffs, contaminated sites
- [ ] **Weather pattern noted** — prevailing wind direction, cloud patterns
- [ ] **Air quality tested** — candle test passed, no strange smells
- [ ] **Animals present** — birds, insects, mammals (means ecosystem is alive)
- [ ] **Escape routes planned** — at least 2 ways out in different directions

## Danger Signs Quick Reference

Print this. Keep it with you.

### Environmental Dangers

- Fast-rising water = flash flood, move to high ground NOW
- Ground cracking or sinking = sinkhole, move away
- Dead fish floating = water contamination
- sudden silence (no birds, no insects) = predator or chemical danger
- Steam or mist from ground = volcanic activity or chemical leak
- Earth trembling = earthquake, get to open ground away from buildings

### Contamination Signs

- Oil sheen on water = petroleum contamination
- Bright green water = algae bloom (toxic, do not drink)
- White foam on streams = chemical runoff
- Rust-colored water = iron or heavy metals
- Unusual plant death in a zone = soil contamination
- Chemical smell near industrial sites = toxic air

### Human-Made Dangers

- Unmarked metal drums or containers = possible toxic waste
- Abandoned buildings with boarded windows = structural danger
- Downed power lines = electrocution risk (stay 30+ feet away)
- Radiation warning signs (yellow triangle with black fan) = stay away
- Military or industrial sites = unknown hazards

1. **Read the land** — water signs, soil signs, weather signs, danger signs
2. **Test everything** — water, soil, air, radiation
