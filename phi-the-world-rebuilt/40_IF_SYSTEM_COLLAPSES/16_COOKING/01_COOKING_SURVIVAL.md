# COOKING WITHOUT ELECTRICITY OR GAS

When the grid goes down, you still need to eat. Here's how to cook food with no electricity, no gas, and no modern appliances. Everything here works with things you can find outside or buy for almost nothing.

---

## Cooking Methods (6 Total)

### 1. Open Fire
The oldest cooking method. Build a fire with wood, let it burn down to coals, and cook over the flames or coals. Simple, reliable, works anywhere.

### 2. Rocket Stove
A super-efficient wood-burning stove you can build from tin cans. Uses way less fuel than an open fire because the chimney effect pulls air through and burns hot.

### 3. Solar Cooker
Use the sun. Black pot absorbs heat. Reflective panels focus sunlight onto the pot. Can boil water or cook food on a sunny day. No fuel needed.

### 4. Earth Oven
Dig a hole. Line it with hot rocks. Bury food inside. The earth holds the heat and slow-cooks the food. Great for meat and root vegetables.

### 5. Dutch Oven
A heavy cast iron pot with a lid. Set it over coals, put coals on the lid too. Acts like an oven. Can bake bread, roast meat, make stew.

### 6. Ash Cooking
Wrap food in leaves or foil. Bury it in the hot ash of a dying fire. The ash keeps a steady, low heat. Good for potatoes, corn, eggs.

## The Phi-Rocket Stove

Build a super-efficient stove from 2 tin cans in about 10 minutes.

**Materials:**
- 1 large tin can (like a #10 can or large coffee can)
- 1 small tin can (like a soup can)
- A knife or can opener

**Build Steps:**

1. Take the large can. Cut a rectangular hole near the bottom on one side. This is your air intake.

2. Take the small can. Cut the bottom out completely. This is your fuel chamber.

3. Drop the small can inside the large can, centered. The small can's open bottom faces up (where the pot sits). The air intake hole on the large can lines up with the bottom of the small can.

4. Cut phi-spaced air holes around the base of the small can inside the large can. Space them at golden ratio intervals for the best airflow.

**How it works:** Air enters the bottom hole, feeds the fire in the small can, and the chimney effect pulls flames upward. Super hot, super efficient.

## The Phi-Solar Cooker

Cook with no fuel. Just the sun.

**Materials:**
- 1 black pot with lid (black absorbs more heat)
- Cardboard or foil for reflective panels
- Tape or sticks to hold panels

**Build Steps:**

1. Get a black pot. Paint an old pot black if it isn't already. Black absorbs more sunlight.

2. Cut cardboard panels and cover them with aluminum foil (shiny side out). These reflect sunlight onto the pot.

3. Attach the panels around the pot at the golden angle — 137.5 degrees from the pot. This is the phi angle. It reflects the most sunlight onto the pot.

4. Set the whole thing in direct sunlight. Prop panels to follow the sun as it moves.

5. Cover the pot with a clear lid or plastic wrap to trap heat (greenhouse effect).

**How hot?** On a good sunny day, a solar cooker can reach 300°F (150°C). Hot enough to boil water, cook rice, bake bread, or slow-cook meat.

## The Earth Oven

Slow-cook food underground. Ancient method, works perfectly.

**Build Steps:**

1. Dig a hole about 2 feet deep and 2 feet wide.

2. Build a big fire in the hole. Let it burn for 1-2 hours until you have a bed of hot coals and the dirt walls are hot.

3. Remove excess coals (leave some). Line the bottom with flat rocks that were heated in the fire.

4. Wrap food in wet leaves, banana leaves, or damp cloth. Place on the hot rocks.

5. Cover everything with more hot rocks, then dirt. Seal it tight.

6. Wait 4-12 hours depending on what you're cooking.

## The Dutch Oven

If you can only own one cooking pot, make it a cast iron Dutch oven. It does everything.

**Cooking with a Dutch oven:**

- **Baking bread:** Put dough inside, set over 8 coals, put 16 coals on lid. 4:1 ratio top to bottom. Check in 30 min.
- **Roasting meat:** Sear meat first, then put lid on with coals on top. Low and slow.
- **Stew:** Brown meat, add water and vegetables, put lid on. Let it simmer.

**Coal ratio rule:** For baking, put 2x as many coals on the lid as underneath. This gives even heat like an oven.

## 10 Simple Recipes (No Electricity)

### 1. Rice and Beans
- 1 cup rice + 2 cups water + handful of dried beans
- Boil water, add rice and beans
- Cover, reduce heat (move pot to edge of fire)
- Cook 25-30 minutes until water absorbed
- Add salt at the end

### 2. Soup
- Pot of water + any vegetables you have (onions, carrots, potatoes, greens)
- Bring water to boil
- Chop vegetables, add to water
- Add salt, pepper, herbs if you have them
- Cook 20-30 minutes until vegetables are soft
- Add meat if available

### 3. Bread (Ash Bread)
- 2 cups flour + 1 cup water + 1 tsp salt + 1 tsp baking soda (if available)
- Mix into dough. Knead 5 minutes.
- Shape into a flat round loaf
- Place directly in hot ash (not flames)
- Cover with more ash
- Cook 30-45 minutes. Knock off ash. Done.

### 4. Roasted Vegetables
- Potatoes, carrots, onions, corn — whatever you have
- Wrap in foil or wet leaves
- Place directly on hot coals (not flames)
- Turn every 10 minutes
- Cook 20-40 minutes depending on size
- Done when soft when poked with a stick

### 5. Smoked Meat
- Cut meat into thin strips
- Build a small smoky fire (green wood or wet leaves make smoke)
- Hang meat strips above the smoke on sticks
- Smoke for 6-12 hours
- Store in a cool, dry place

### 6. Jerky
- Slice meat very thin (freeze meat first to make slicing easier)
- Rub with salt (lots of salt)
- Lay strips on sticks or hang from a line
- Leave in sun and wind for 2-3 days
- Should be dry and tough when done

### 7. Porridge
- 1 cup oats + 3 cups water + pinch of salt
- Bring water to boil
- Add oats, stir
- Cook 10-15 minutes, stirring occasionally
- Add honey, berries, or salt to taste

### 8. Fried Eggs
- Heat pan over fire
- Add a little oil or fat
- Crack eggs into pan
- Cook until whites are solid (3-5 minutes)
- Flip if you want hard eggs

### 9. Pasta
- Pot of water, bring to boil
- Add pasta, stir
- Cook 8-12 minutes (taste test)
- Drain water (or eat as soup)
- Add salt, oil, or any sauce

### 10. Stew
- Cut meat into chunks, brown in pot with oil
- Add water (about 4 cups per pound of meat)
- Add potatoes, carrots, onions, whatever you have
- Add salt, herbs
- Bring to boil, then simmer (low fire) for 2-3 hours
- The longer it cooks, the better it tastes

## Fire Tips for Cooking

**Types of wood for cooking:**
- **Hardwood** (oak, hickory, maple) — best for cooking. Burns slow, makes good coals.
- **Softwood** (pine, cedar) — burns fast, makes more flame. Good for starting fires, not ideal for cooking.
- **Never use:** Poisoned wood, treated wood, or driftwood (toxic fumes).

**How to control heat:**
- **High heat:** Cook over flames
- **Medium heat:** Cook over glowing coals
- **Low heat:** Move pot to edge of fire, or set on coals further from flames
- **Oven heat:** Put pot on coals, put coals on lid

**Saving fuel:**
- Soak dried beans overnight so they cook faster
- Put a lid on your pot (boils faster, uses less fuel)
- Cut food into smaller pieces (cooks faster)
- Pre-soak everything you can

## Water Safety

**Before you cook, make sure your water is safe:**

1. **Boiling:** Bring water to a rolling boil for 1 minute. This kills most bacteria and parasites. At high altitude (above 6,500 feet), boil for 3 minutes.

2. **Solar disinfection:** Fill a clear plastic bottle with water. Leave in direct sunlight for 6+ hours. UV rays kill most pathogens.

3. **When in doubt, boil it.** Boiling is the most reliable method with no chemicals or filters.

**Water sources (best to worst):**
1. Rain water (collect in clean containers)
2. Moving water (streams, rivers — still should boil)
3. Standing water (ponds, lakes — always boil)
4. Ground water (wells — often safest if deep)

## Food Preservation (Without Refrigeration)

**Smoke it:** Hang meat in smoke for 6-12 hours. The smoke preserves it.

**Salt it:** Pack meat or fish in salt. The salt draws out moisture and prevents bacteria.

**Dry it:** Cut food thin, leave in sun and wind. Makes jerky, dried fruit, dried herbs.

**Pickle it:** Soak vegetables in vinegar or salt water. Keeps them for weeks.

**Cool it:** Keep food in a hole in the ground (earth stays cool), or hang it in a stream, or wrap in wet cloth in a breezy spot.
