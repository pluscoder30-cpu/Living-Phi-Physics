## 2. BASIC LIFE SUPPORT

### CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)

If someone stops breathing and has no pulse, start CPR.

**Step 1: Check the scene**
Make sure it's safe. Don't become a victim yourself.

**Step 2: Check response**
Tap their shoulder and shout. If no response, call for help.

**Step 3: Open the airway**
Tilt head back, lift chin.

```
    HEAD TILTED BACK
    ┌──────────────┐
    │   NOSE       │
    │    ↓         │
    │   MOUTH      │
    └──────────────┘
         ↑
    Chin lifted
```

**Step 4: Check breathing**
Look, listen, feel for 10 seconds. If not breathing normally, start CPR.

**Step 5: Chest compressions**
- Kneel beside them
- Place heel of hand on center of chest (between nipples)
- Interlock fingers
- Push hard and fast: **2 inches deep, 100-120 compressions per minute**
- Think of the beat of "Stayin' Alive" by the Bee Gees

```
    HAND POSITION
    ┌─────────────┐
    │  ╔═══╗      │
    │  ║ + ║  ← heel of hand
    │  ╚═══╝      │
    │    │        │
    │    ▼        │
    │  CHEST      │
    └─────────────┘
```

**Step 6: Rescue breaths**
After 30 compressions, give 2 breaths.
- Pinch nose shut
- Cover their mouth with yours
- Blow for 1 second each
- Watch for chest rise

**Step 7: Continue**
30 compressions, 2 breaths. Don't stop until help arrives or they start breathing.

### Choking (Conscious)

**If they can cough, speak, or breathe:** Encourage coughing. Don't slap their back.

**If they can't breathe, speak, or cough:**

1. Stand behind them
2. Make a fist with one hand
3. Place thumb side against their stomach, just above the belly button
4. Grasp fist with your other hand
5. Thrust inward and upward

```

         ┌───┐
         │   │  ← person
         │   │
    ─────┤   ├─────
         │ ▲ │
         │ │ │  ← fist thrusts up
         │   │
         └───┘
```

**For infants (under 1 year):**
- Place face down on your forearm
- Support head
- Give 5 back blows between shoulder blades
- Turn over, give 5 chest thrusts (two fingers on breastbone)
- Repeat until object comes out

## 3. WOUND CARE

### Cleaning a Wound

1. Wash your hands (or use gloves)
2. Rinse wound with clean water for 5 minutes
3. Remove dirt or debris with tweezers (clean with alcohol first)
4. Apply antibiotic ointment
5. Cover with bandage

```
    WOUND CLEANING
    ┌─────────────┐
    │   WATER     │
    │     ↓       │
    │  ~~~~~~~~   │  ← rinse for 5 minutes
    │   WOUND     │
    └─────────────┘
```

```
    Phi-spacing prevents compression and promotes healing.
```

This mimics how skin layers work — different thicknesses at golden-ratio intervals.

### Bleeding Control

**Minor bleeding:**
- Apply direct pressure with clean cloth
- Elevate the injured area
- Apply antibiotic ointment and bandage

**Serious bleeding:**
1. Apply firm, direct pressure
2. Don't remove the cloth — add more on top
3. If bleeding doesn't stop, apply a tourniquet

### Tourniquet (Last Resort)

Use only for life-threatening bleeding from a limb.

1. Place a strip of cloth (2 inches wide) above the wound
2. Tie a half-knot
3. Place a stick on top of the knot
4. Tie a full knot over the stick
5. Twist the stick until bleeding stops
6. Note the time — write it on the person's forehead

```
    TOURNIQUET
    ┌─────────────┐
    │  STICK      │
    │   ║        │
    │   ║ ← twist
    │   ║        │
    │  CLOTH     │
    │  ═══════   │  ← above wound
    │  ARM       │
    └─────────────┘
```

## 4. BURNS

### Degrees of Burns

| Degree | What it looks like | What to do |
|--------|-------------------|------------|
| First | Red, painful | Cool water 10 min, aloe vera |
| Second | Red, blistered, very painful | Cool water, don't pop blisters, cover |
| Third | White or charred, no pain | Cover, get medical help |

### Cooling a Burn

1. Run cool (not cold) water over burn for **10 minutes**
2. Remove jewelry or clothing stuck to burn
3. Cover with non-stick bandage
4. Take ibuprofen for pain

**NEVER use:**
- Ice (causes frostbite)
- Butter or oil (traps heat)
- Toothpaste (not sterile)

### The Phi-Cooling Ratio

For burns, cool the area at phi-ratio temperature intervals:

```

    Cool water: 60°F (15°C)

    98.6 × φ⁻¹ = 98.6 × 0.618 ≈ 61°F (16°C)

    So cool water at about 60°F is phi-optimal for burn cooling.
```

## 5. FRACTURES AND SPRAINS

### How to Tell the Difference

| Sign | Fracture | Sprain |
|------|----------|--------|
| Swelling | Immediate | Gradual |
| Pain | Severe, worsens with movement | Moderate, improves with rest |
| Deformity | May look bent or twisted | Normal shape |
| Sound | May hear a snap or crack | No sound |
| Weight | Can't put any weight on it | Can put some weight |

### Splinting a Fracture

**Goal:** Keep the bone from moving until it heals.

1. Find something rigid (sticks, cardboard, rolled newspaper)
2. Pad the splint with cloth
3. Place splint on both sides of the injury
4. Tie with cloth strips (not too tight)
5. Check circulation below the splint (warm toes? can move them?)

```
    SPLINT POSITION
    ┌─────────────┐
    │  ═══════    │  ← splint (rigid material)
    │  ───────    │  ← injured limb
    │  ═══════    │  ← splint (other side)
    └─────────────┘
         ↑
    Tie at top and bottom
```

### The Phi-Splint

Space your ties at phi-ratio distances along the splint:

```
    Splint length: 12 inches
    Tie positions: 12 × φ⁻¹ ≈ 7.4 inches from bottom
                   12 × (1 - φ⁻¹) ≈ 4.6 inches from top

    This distributes pressure evenly along the fracture.
```

## 6. SHOCK

Shock is a life-threatening condition where the body doesn't get enough blood flow.

### Signs of Shock
- Pale, cool, clammy skin
- Rapid, weak pulse
- Rapid breathing
- Confusion or anxiety
- Nausea or vomiting

### Treating Shock

1. Lay the person flat on their back
2. Elevate their legs 12 inches (if no spinal injury)
3. Cover with a blanket to keep warm
4. Do NOT give food or drink
5. Treat the underlying cause (bleeding, burn, etc.)
6. Monitor breathing and pulse

```

    │   HEAD      │
    │   BODY     │
    │   LEGS     │
    └─────────────┘
         ↑
    Elevate legs 12 inches
```

## 8. ENVIRONMENTAL INJURIES

### Hypothermia (Too Cold)

**Signs:** Shivering, confusion, slurred speech, drowsiness

**Treatment:**
1. Move to warm shelter
2. Remove wet clothing
3. Warm the center of the body first (chest, neck, head)
4. Give warm drinks (no alcohol)
5. Use skin-to-skin contact under a blanket

### Heat Stroke (Too Hot)

**Signs:** Hot, red skin; no sweating; rapid pulse; confusion

**Treatment:**
1. Move to cool area
2. Cool body with water (wet cloths, misting)
3. Fan the person
4. Give small sips of water
5. Get medical help

### The Phi-Temperature Balance

Your body maintains balance at phi-ratio temperature differentials:

```

    95 × φ⁻¹ ≈ 59°F (15°C) — danger zone

    Stay within phi-balanced temperatures for optimal health.
```

## 9. POISONING

### Signs of Poisoning
- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
- Dizziness or confusion
- Difficulty breathing
- Burns around mouth

### What to Do

1. Call Poison Control: **1-800-222-1222**
2. Try to identify the substance
3. Don't induce vomiting unless told to
4. If conscious, rinse mouth with water
5. If unconscious, place in recovery position

### Recovery Position

1. Roll them onto their side
2. Tilt head back to keep airway open
3. Place top leg forward for stability
4. Monitor breathing

```
    RECOVERY POSITION
    ┌─────────────┐
    │   HEAD      │
    │   BODY     │
    │   LEGS     │
    └─────────────┘
         ↑
    Tilted on side, top leg forward
```

## 12. WHEN TO GET HELP

Go to a hospital or call for help if:

- Heavy bleeding that won't stop
- Difficulty breathing
- Chest pain or pressure
- Severe burns (larger than palm)
- Suspected spinal injury
- Head injury with vomiting
- Signs of infection (red streaks, fever)
- Bone visibly broken through skin

**Remember:** In a collapse, hospitals may be overwhelmed. Your first aid skills may be the only help available. Practice these techniques now.
