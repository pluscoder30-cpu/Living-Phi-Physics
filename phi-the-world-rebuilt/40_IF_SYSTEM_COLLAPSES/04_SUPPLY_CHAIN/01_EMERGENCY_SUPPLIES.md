# Emergency Supply Chain: Getting Food, Water, Medicine & Resources When the System Is Down

When stores close, trucks stop, and the power grid goes dark, you need a plan. This guide tells you exactly what to have ready, where to find things in the wild, and how to trade when money means nothing.

---

## Where to Find Things When Stores Are Closed

The world doesn't become empty when systems fail. Everything you need is already out there. You just need to know where to look.

### Water Sources (Ranked by Ease)

```
WATER SOURCES: WHAT TO USE FIRST
=================================

1. TAP WATER (if pressure remains)
   First 24-48 hours: water may still flow
   Boil it anyway

2. HOT WATER HEATER
   Holds 30-80 gallons of clean water
   Open the drain valve at the bottom
   Usually overlooked — your hidden reserve

3. TOILET TANKS (NOT bowls)
   Clean water stored in the back tank
   Use before anyone uses the toilet

4. RAINWATER
   Collect in buckets, tarps, plastic sheeting
   Boil or filter before drinking

5. RIVERS & STREAMS
   Flowing water > standing water
   Filter through cloth, then boil

6. PONDS & LAKES
   Standing water = more bacteria
   Always filter and boil

7. DEW COLLECTION
   Drag cloth through grass at dawn
   Wring into container — free water

8. PLANTS
   Some plants hold water in leaves/stems
   Cattails, bamboo, certain vines
```

### Food Sources (Ranked by Availability)

```
FOOD SOURCES: WHAT TO EAT FIRST
=================================

IMMEDIATE (no tools needed):
- Kitchen pantries (yours + neighbors)
- Vending machines (break the glass)
- Garbage bins (fresh restaurant waste)

SHORT-TERM (basic foraging):
- Fruit trees in neighborhoods
- Nuts (acorns, walnuts, pecans)
- Dandelions (leaves, roots, flowers)
- Wild berries (ONLY if you know them)

MEDIUM-TERM (tools help):
- Fish (hooks, lines, nets, traps)
- Insects (crickets, grubs, ants — protein!)
- Seaweed (if near coast)
- Cattail roots (starchy, nutritious)

LONG-TERM (skills needed):
- Hunting (small game first)
- Trapping (snares, deadfalls)
- Gardening (save seeds NOW)
- Composting (turn waste to soil)
```

### Medicine & Healing

```
HERBAL MEDICINE CHEAT SHEET
============================

WOUND HEALING:
- Plantain leaf (chew and apply to cuts)
- Yarrow (stops bleeding fast)

PAIN & FEVER:
- Willow bark (natural aspirin — chew it)
- Willow bark tea = painkiller
- Elderberry (fever reduction)

DIGESTION:
- Ginger root (nausea, stomach pain)

CALMING:
- Chamomile tea (anxiety)

CRITICAL: If someone has a broken bone,
dislocated joint, or deep wound that won't
stop bleeding — that's when you go to
the hospital, not when you play doctor.
```

## The Scavenging Guide

When you enter an abandoned building or search through debris, follow this checklist. Don't wander aimlessly — be systematic.

### Scavenging Checklist

```
ABANDONED BUILDING SEARCH PATTERN
===================================

START: Front door
1. Check for structural damage (don't enter unsafe buildings)
2. Sweep LEFT to RIGHT, room by room
3. Check every drawer, cabinet, closet
4. Look UP — shelves, lofts, rafters
5. Look DOWN — under furniture, in floor vents

PRIORITIES (in order):
[ ] Water containers (bottles, jugs, barrels)
[ ] Food (canned goods, dried food, spices)
[ ] Medical supplies (bandages, antibiotics, painkillers)
[ ] Tools (hammer, screwdriver, pliers, wrenches)
[ ] Batteries (any size — check expiration)
[ ] Flashlights and lanterns
[ ] Rope, string, cord (any kind)
[ ] Clothing (warm layers, rain gear)
[ ] Cookware (pots, pans, can openers)
[ ] Books (first aid, survival, repair, farming)
[ ] Electronics (for parts — not the whole thing)
[ ] Copper wire and electrical components
[ ] Glass containers (jars, bottles)
[ ] Sealed containers (any — could be useful)

SKIP:
- Perishable food (if power was out more than 4 hours)
- Chemicals (unless you know what they are)
- Anything moldy (mold spreads)
- Broken glass (careful with it)
```

## The Trade System

When money stops working, value doesn't disappear — it just changes form. Skills, food, water, and medicine become currency.

### What's Valuable

```
TRADE VALUE HIERARCHY
=====================

MOST VALUABLE (will always get what you need):
  ★★★★★  SKILLS (medical, farming, building, repair)
  ★★★★★  CLEAN WATER (endlessly in demand)
  ★★★★★  MEDICINE (especially antibiotics, painkillers)

VERY VALUABLE (easy to trade):
  ★★★★  FOOD (canned, dried, preserved)
  ★★★★  FIRE (lighters, matches, fire starters)
  ★★★★  TOOLS (hammers, saws, knives)

VALUABLE (useful but common):
  ★★★  CLOTHING (warm layers)
  ★★★  BATTERIES (any kind)
  ★★★  SHELTER (materials, space)

LESS VALUABLE (still tradeable):
  ★★  BOOKS (depends on the book)
  ★★  CONTAINERS (jars, buckets, bags)
  ★★  FUEL (wood, charcoal, solar power)

LEAST VALUABLE (but not worthless):
  ★  DECORATIONS (moral boost matters)
  ★  MUSICAL INSTRUMENTS (morale matters)
  ★  GAMES (morale matters)
```

### The Phi-Fair-Trade Rule

Fair trade isn't 1-for-1. It's about creating value for both sides. Use the golden ratio:

```
PHI-FAIR TRADE SYSTEM
======================

GIVE: 1 unit of value
GET:  1.618 units of value (or perceived value)

WHY 1.618?
- It's the golden ratio (phi)
- Both sides feel they got MORE than they gave
- This builds trust and repeat trades
- Trust is the REAL currency after collapse

EXAMPLE:
  You give: 1 can of soup (actual value: $1)
  You get:  First aid kit repair (perceived value: $1.618)
  Both sides win. Both sides want to trade again.

TRADING RULES:
1. Always trade fairly — reputation is everything
2. Don't trick people — word spreads fast
3. Give a little extra when you can — build allies
4. Trade skills when you can — they're renewable
5. Document good trades — help others find good partners
```

## The Three-Day Rule

In a collapse, your first 72 hours determine everything.

```
72-HOUR TIMELINE
================

HOUR 0-6: ASSESS
  - Check family/friends safety
  - Fill bathtubs with water
  - Gather emergency kit
  - Listen to radio for information

HOUR 6-24: STABILIZE
  - Secure shelter (stay or move)
  - Ration food and water
  - Find or create light source
  - Build small fire if safe

HOUR 24-48: ORGANIZE
  - Inventory what you have
  - Make contact with neighbors
  - Establish a water collection point
  - Set up a basic sanitation area

HOUR 48-72: EXPAND
  - Begin scavenging nearby areas
  - Start trades with neighbors
  - Plan for longer-term survival
  - Begin any food-growing projects

AFTER 72 HOURS:
  - You've survived the immediate crisis
  - Now build toward sustainability
  - Community is everything
```

## Final Thought

The supply chain doesn't break — it transforms. Instead of trucks delivering goods, people trade and forage. Instead of money, skills and trust become currency. Instead of stores, the whole world becomes your supply store.

The person who survives isn't the one with the most stuff. It's the one who knows where to find what they need and has the skills to trade for the rest.
