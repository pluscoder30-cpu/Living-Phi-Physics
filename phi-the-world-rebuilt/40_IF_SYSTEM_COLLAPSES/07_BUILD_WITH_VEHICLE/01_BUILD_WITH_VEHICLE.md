## HOW TO USE A VEHICLE TO GET MATERIALS

A truck or car is your best friend when systems collapse. It turns you from "person who carries things" into "person who moves entire loads." Every trip you make with a vehicle saves you 20 trips on foot.

### WHAT TO HAUL

| Material | What It's For | How Much You Can Carry |
|----------|---------------|----------------------|
| Lumber | Shelter, furniture, tools | Truck bed full (4-8 foot boards) |
| Water | Drinking, cleaning, growing | 55-gallon drums or jerry cans |
| Soil/Dirt | Gardening, building, insulation | Truck bed full (about 1 cubic yard) |
| Sand/Gravel | Foundations, drainage, concrete | Truck bed full (1 cubic yard) |
| Scrap Metal | Tools, parts, building | Full bed (heavy!) |
| Firewood | Heat, cooking | Full bed |
| Salvage Items | Whatever you find | Anything that fits |

### HOW TO LOAD A TRUCK

**The Golden Rule: Heavy stuff goes LOW and FORWARD.**

1. Heavy items against the cab (front of bed)
2. Light items on top and toward the back
3. Evenly distributed left to right
4. Strap everything down with ratchet straps or rope

**Loading Lumber:**
- Lay boards flat in the bed
- Overhang up to 4 feet off the back (check local laws)
- Use bed rails or straps to prevent sliding
- Stack flat, don't let boards twist

**Loading Water:**
- Use sealed containers (no leaks)
- Place on flat surface in bed
- Secure so they don't tip
- One 55-gallon drum = 460 pounds. Your truck can handle it.

**Loading Soil/Dirt:**
- Use a wheelbarrow or buckets to load
- Spread evenly across bed
- Don't overload — 1 cubic yard of soil weighs about 2,200 pounds
- Cover with tarp to prevent loss during driving

## THE PHI-HARMONIC TRUCK BED

This is how you organize your truck bed so everything is easy to reach and balanced perfectly using the golden ratio.

### THE PHI-SPACED STORAGE SYSTEM

Divide your truck bed into zones using phi (1.618) proportions:

```
TRUCK BED (8 feet long = 96 inches)

|---- 36 inches ----|-- 22 inches --|-- 14 inches --|-- 9 inches --|-- 6 inches --|
     ZONE 1              ZONE 2         ZONE 3         ZONE 4        ZONE 5
   HEAVY TOOLS       WATER/SUPPLIES   LONG ITEMS     QUICK ACCESS    SPARE
   Shovels, axe       Buckets, drums   Lumber, pipes   Ropes, straps  Emergency
```

**Why this works:**
- Heavy stuff closest to cab = balanced driving
- Each zone gets smaller by phi ratio
- Quick-access zone is always at the back edge
- You always know where everything is

### BUILDING THE ORGANIZER

1. **Get plywood** (one 4x8 sheet, 1/2 inch thick)
2. **Cut dividers** at phi-spaced intervals (36", 22", 14", 9", 6")
3. **Screw dividers** to bed floor or use removable clamps
4. **Label each zone** with spray paint
5. **Add hooks** to dividers for hanging tools

**Cost:** About $30 in plywood and screws.

### ZONE CONTENTS

**Zone 1 — HEAVY TOOLS (36 inches)**
- Shovel, pickaxe, rake

## HOW TO HAUL LUMBER

### STEP BY STEP

1. **Measure your load** before you go. Know how many boards and what size.
2. **Lay boards flat** in the truck bed. Don't stand them up.
3. **Stack evenly.** Put longer boards on the bottom, shorter on top.
4. **Leave overhang** up to 4 feet off the back (check your local laws — some places allow more, some less).
5. **Tie down** with ratchet straps every 4 feet.
6. **Put a flag** on the end of overhanging lumber if it extends past your tailgate.

**Pro Tips:**
- Carry 2x4s in sets of 4 — they bundle together nicely
- Wrap ends in plastic bags to prevent scratching
- Put cardboard between stacked boards to prevent sliding
- For very long loads, drive slowly and take wide turns

## HOW TO HAUL WATER

Water is life. Moving it efficiently saves your back and your time.

### CONTAINERS

| Container | Capacity | Weight When Full | Best For |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|----------|
| 5-gallon bucket | 5 gal | 42 lbs | Short trips, multiple uses |
| 7-gallon jerry can | 7 gal | 58 lbs | Medium trips, sealed |
| 30-gallon drum | 30 gal | 250 lbs | Large volume |
| 55-gallon drum | 55 gal | 460 lbs | Major hauling |
| IBC tote (275 gal) | 275 gal | 2,300 lbs | Industrial (needs big truck) |

### LOADING AND TRANSPORT

1. **Fill containers** at the water source. Don't leave air space — it makes them slosh and shift.
2. **Load heaviest containers first.** Place them against the cab.
3. **Secure with straps.** One strap over each drum, tight.
4. **Drive carefully.** Water sloshes. Accelerate and brake gently.
5. **Unloading:** Use a dolly or hand truck for drums. Never lift a full 55-gallon drum alone (460 lbs!).

Before you go:

- [ ] Do you have a way to purify when you get home?

## HOW TO HAUL SOIL

### WHAT SOIL IS GOOD FOR

- **Gardening** — the #1 reason to haul soil
- **Filling holes** — structural repairs
- **Insulation** — covering pipes, burying containers
- **Mud bricks** — mix with straw and water
- **Leveling ground** — foundations, paths

### LOADING SOIL

1. **Use a shovel** and wheelbarrow. Load the wheelbarrow, wheel it to the truck, dump it in.
2. **Or use buckets** — 5-gallon buckets are about 75 lbs of soil each.
3. **Spread evenly** across the truck bed. Don't pile it all in one spot.
4. **Cover with a tarp** when driving to prevent loss.

### SOIL WEIGHT RULE

- **1 cubic yard of dry soil = about 2,200 pounds**
- **1 cubic yard of wet soil = about 3,000 pounds**
- Most truck beds can handle 1-2 cubic yards max

**Don't overload.** If your truck is sagging, you have too much.

### WHERE TO GET FREE SOIL

- Construction sites (ask permission)
- Landscaping companies (they often have excess)
- Neighbors doing excavation
- Road cuts (check if legal)
- Your own property (dig a hole, use the dirt elsewhere)

## THE PHI-SPEED FUEL EFFICIENCY TRICK

This is real science mixed with phi-harmonic theory. Your vehicle gets its best gas mileage at specific speeds — and phi (1.618) is connected to those sweet spots.

### THE PHI-SPEED RANGES

Most vehicles have two peak efficiency zones. Here's how phi maps to them:

**Zone 1: CITY PHI-SPEED = 25-35 mph**
- The phi-harmonic low-speed range
- Your engine is most efficient per mile at lower speeds
- Stop-and-go kills efficiency, but steady 30 mph is golden

**Zone 2: HIGHWAY PHI-SPEED = 50-60 mph**
- The phi-harmonic cruising range
- Most vehicles peak at 55 mph
- This is the "61.8% better mileage" sweet spot

### THE MATH

If you normally drive 70 mph on the highway:
- At 55 mph, you get roughly **20-30% better fuel economy** (real-world tested)
- Combined with phi-harmonic driving techniques: up to **61.8% improvement**

**How to get 61.8% better mileage:**

1. **Drive at 55 mph** instead of 70 mph (saves about 20-30%)
2. **Use cruise control** on flat roads (saves 5-7%)
3. **Accelerate gently** — no jackrabbit starts (saves 10-15%)
4. **Maintain tire pressure** at the higher end of recommended (saves 3-5%)
5. **Remove roof racks** when not in use (saves 2-5%)
6. **Use A/C sparingly** — open windows at low speeds, A/C at high speeds (saves 5-10%)

**Combined savings: 45-72% improvement → average = 61.8% (phi!)**

### PHI-DRIVING RULES

1. **The 61.8% Rule:** Aim to use 61.8% of your vehicle's power at any time. Don't floor it unless you must.
2. **The Golden Turn:** Start braking earlier than you think. Coast to stops. Every red light you coast to instead of brake at saves fuel.
3. **The Phi Gap:** Leave 1.618 car lengths between you and the car ahead. This gives you time to adjust speed smoothly without braking.
4. **The Sacred Fill:** When fueling, fill only 61.8% of your tank for daily driving. Less weight = better mileage. Fill full only for long hauls.
