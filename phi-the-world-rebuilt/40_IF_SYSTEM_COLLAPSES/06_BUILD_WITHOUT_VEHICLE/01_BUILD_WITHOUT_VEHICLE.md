# BUILD WITHOUT VEHICLE

You don't need a truck. You don't need a car. You don't need gas money. You need your legs, a little creativity, and maybe a friend with a bicycle. This guide shows you how to get everything you need and build a safe shelter using nothing but your own two hands.

---

## HOW TO GET MATERIALS

### Walking (Your Primary Vehicle)

Your body is your vehicle. Here's what it can carry:

| Method | Distance | Weight | Best For |
|--------|----------|--------|----------|
| Walking, nothing in hands | Unlimited | 0 lbs | Scouting, finding materials |
| Backpack (school bag) | 1-2 miles | 20-30 lbs | Daily supplies, food runs |
| Heavy-duty backpack | 2-3 miles | 40-50 lbs | Multiple supplies at once |
| Two plastic bags | 0.5-1 mile | 15-20 lbs each | Grocery runs, light loads |

**Tips for walking long distances:**
- Go early morning or evening (avoid heat)
- Carry water first, everything else second
- Make multiple trips instead of one huge load
- Know your route before you leave
- Wear shoes that fit (blisters stop you cold)

### Bicycle with Trailer (Your Heavy Hauler)

A bicycle changes everything. With a small trailer or even a milk crate zip-tied to the back, you can haul 50-100 lbs.

```

                     or bungee cords
```

**What you can haul:**
- 2x4 lumber (strapped lengthwise)
- Bags of soil or cement
- Buckets of water

- Multiple bags of rice/beans

### Wheelbarrow (If You Can Find One)

A wheelbarrow is gold. Check:
- Construction sites (ask, don't take)
- Garage sales ($5-10)
- Neighbors (borrow, return clean)
- Dumpsters behind hardware stores

Capacity: 100+ lbs. Pull it behind you by hand.

### Sled (For Flat Ground and Winter)

Pull heavy items across flat ground. Use cardboard on grass, sleds on snow.

```
SLED METHOD
==========

  ____ROPE____
 /            \
|  [HEAVY     |-----> YOU (pulling)
|   STUFF]    |
 \____________/
  ____[____]___
    skids on ground
```

### River Floating (If You're Near Water)

Seal materials in plastic bags or wrap in tarp. Let the current carry them downstream. Walk to meet them.

**Only float:** sealed containers, wrapped tools, plastic bins
**Never float:** food (animals will get it), paper, electronics

### Neighbor Network (Share Rides)

You don't need to own a vehicle. You need ONE person with a vehicle who owes you a favor, or who needs something you have.

**Trade skills for rides:**
- Fix their fence → they drive you to the hardware store
- Watch their kids → they haul your lumber
- Cook a meal → they let you use their truck Saturday morning

## HOW TO BUILD WITHOUT POWER TOOLS

### Hand Saw Cuts Wood

A $10 hand saw cuts 2x4s, tree branches, and plywood. You don't need electricity. You need patience.

**Technique:**
1. Mark your cut with a pencil or knife scratch
2. Place wood on a stable surface (brick, log, bucket)
3. Start the cut at an angle (30 degrees)
4. Once the groove is established, saw flat
5. Let the saw do the work — don't push hard
6. Backstroke clears sawdust

**Cuts you'll make:**
- Rip cuts (along the grain) — for posts
- Cross cuts (across the grain) — for beams
- Notch cuts — for joints

### Axe Splits Logs

If you find fallen trees, an axe (or even a hatchet) splits them into usable lumber.

**Split along the grain:**
```
LOG SPLITTING
=============

     axe
      \
   ____\_______
  /  |  |  |   \       Place axe on end grain
 |   |  |  |    |      Hit axe with mallet
 |   |  |  |    |      Wood splits along grain
 |   |  |  |    |
 |___|__|__|____|
  split into planks
```

### Hammer and Nails Join Wood

**Basic joints:**

```
BUTT JOINT (simplest)
  ___________
 |           |
 |     +     |___________
 |     nail  |           |
 |___________|           |
             |___________|

LAP JOINT (stronger)
  ___________
 |     ______|______
 |     |    |       |
 |_____|____|       |
       |    |_______|
       |____________|

CORNER JOINT (for frames)
  __________
 |          |
 |    +     |____
 |    nail  |   |
 |__________|   |
            |   |
            |___|
```

**Nail tips:**
- Pre-drill with a nail punch if wood is splitting
- Use 2 nails minimum per joint
- Angle nails slightly for grip (toe-nailing)
- Remove bent nails with the claw end

### Rope and Knots Tie Things

Four knots cover 90% of building needs:

```
1. SQUARE KNOT (ties two ropes together)
   ____
  /    \
 |  ()  |---+
  \____/    |
    \_______/
    "right over left, left over right"

2. CLOVE HITCH (ties rope to pole)
   ====[====]====
       |    |
    wrap twice,
    tuck under

3. TIGHT LINE KNOT (tightens under load)
   ____
  /    \----->
 | ()   |     rope pulls tight
  \____/
  adjustable loop

4. BOWLINE (makes a fixed loop)
   ____
  /    \
 |  ()  \----  (loop that won't slip)
  \____/
  "the rabbit comes out of the hole"
```

### Adobe Bricks (No Tools Needed)

Mud + straw + sun = brick. People have built with this for 10,000 years.

**Recipe:**
1. Dig clay-heavy soil (not sandy)
2. Mix with water until it's like thick cookie dough
3. Add straw or dried grass (30% straw by volume)
4. Stomp on it to mix (use your feet)
5. Pack into a mold (wooden frame, milk carton, shoe box)
6. Let dry in sun for 3-5 days
7. Turn once halfway through

**Mold dimensions:**
```
ADOBE BRICK MOLD
================

  +------------------+
  |                  |  12 inches long
  |                  |  6 inches wide
  |                  |  3 inches thick
  +------------------+

  Bottom removed — pack mud in from top,
  flip out onto flat ground to dry
```

**Wall building:**
- Stagger bricks like regular bricks
- Use mud as mortar between bricks
- Let each course dry before adding the next
- 12-inch thick walls keep you cool in summer, warm in winter

### Lashing (Tying Sticks Together)

No nails? No problem. Tie sticks together with rope or cordage.

```
SQUARE LASHING (joins two poles at right angles)
================

    |||||||
    ||  +--+--- horizontal pole
    ||  |XX|
    ||  +--+--- vertical pole
    |||||||

  1. Clove hitch on vertical pole
  2. Wrap rope around both poles (3 times each direction)
  3. Frap (wrap between poles to tighten)
  4. Clove hitch to finish

TRIPOD LASHING (makes a standing frame)
================

       /\
      /  \
     / () \     three poles tied together
    /  ||  \    at the top, spread legs
   /   ||   \   to stand up
  /    ||    \
 /_____||_____\
```

## THE WALKING ROUTE MAP

Plan your supply runs before you leave home.

```
WALKING SUPPLY ROUTE
====================

  [HOME BASE]
       |
       | 0.2 miles
       |
  [WATER SOURCE] ---------> fill containers first
       |
       | 0.5 miles
       |
  [GROCERY STORE] --------> rice, beans, herbs, seeds
       |
       | 0.3 miles
       |
  [HARDWARE STORE] -------> saw, hammer, nails, rope
       |
       | 0.4 miles
       |
  [DOLLAR STORE] ----------> duct tape, containers, bags
       |
       | 0.6 miles
       |
  [HOME BASE]

  TOTAL LOOP: ~2 miles
  TIME: 45-60 minutes walking
  LOAD: 30-50 lbs in backpack
```

**Route tips:**
- Hit stores closest to home last (your arms are tired)
- Bring a bag for each store (don't carry everything at once)
- Know store hours — some open early, some close early
- Bring exact cash (no card = no problem)
- Go on weekdays (less crowded, shorter lines)

## FINAL NOTES

1. **Start walking now.** Don't wait for the crisis. Build your endurance today.
2. **Learn one knot.** The clove hitch. Practice it until you can tie it blind.
3. **Find one friend.** Two people with backpacks carry twice as much.
4. **Scout your route.** Walk the path to the store. Know every turn.
5. **Carry water always.** Everything else can wait. Water cannot.

You don't need a vehicle. You need a plan, a backpack, and the will to walk.
