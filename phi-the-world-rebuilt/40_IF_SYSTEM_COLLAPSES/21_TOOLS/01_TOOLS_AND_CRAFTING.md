## HOW TO MAKE A KNIFE

A knife is a sharp edge held in your hand. That's it.

### Materials
- A sharp rock (flint, obsidian, quartz, chert)
- A stick (hardwood — oak, hickory, maple)
- Rope or sinew (animal tendon or plant fiber)

### Steps

```
  STEP 1: FIND YOUR EDGE
  ───────────────────────

       /\
      /  \         Look for rocks that break into
     / FL \        sharp, thin pieces. Flint and
    / INT  \       obsidian are the best. They
   /________\      fracture like broken glass.

  STEP 2: SHAPE IT (Flint Knapping)
  ───────────────────────────────────

     Strike here
          ↓
     _____/\_____
    /            \   Hold your rock. Hit the edge
   /   FLINT      \  with another hard rock at an
  /    KNAPPED     \  angle. Thin flakes will pop
 /________________\  off, leaving a sharp edge.

  STEP 3: HAFT IT
  ─────────────────

      ┌─── Rope wrapped tight
      │
      ▼
    ╔═══╗
    ║BLD║──── Sharp edge sticks out
    ╠═══╣
    ║   ║
    ║   ║ ← Stick (handle)
    ║   ║
    ╚═══╝

  Tie the blade to the stick. Wrap rope
  over the blade's base and around the
  stick. Pull tight. You have a knife.
```

### Tips
- Work slowly — rushing breaks the blade
- Wear gloves or wrap your hand with cloth
- A dull knife is more dangerous than a sharp one
- Practice on cheap rocks before using good flint

## HOW TO MAKE ROPE

Rope holds everything together. Without rope, you have a pile of stuff. With rope, you have a shelter, a trap, a tool.

### Materials
- Long plant fibers: inner bark (basswood, elm), long grass, cattail leaves, yucca, hemp
- Patience

### Steps

```
  STEP 1: GATHER FIBERS
  ──────────────────────

    ╭───────╮
    │ PLANT │  Peel the inner bark off branches.
    │ FIBER │  Or cut long grass. You want strands
    ╰───────╯  at least 3 feet long.

  STEP 2: PREPARE STRANDS
  ────────────────────────

    ═══════════════  Strand A (twist clockwise)
    ═══════════════  Strand B (twist clockwise)
    ═══════════════  Strand C (twist clockwise)

    Dry fibers slightly. Too wet = rot.
    Too dry = snap. Slightly damp = perfect.

  STEP 3: BRAID THREE STRANDS
  ────────────────────────────

         A     B     C
          \   / \   /
           \ /   \ /
            X     X      Cross A over B, then
           / \   / \     B over C. Keep going.
          /   \ /   \    Each twist = φ-width
         A     B     C   (about 62% of strand diameter)

  STEP 4: TWIST THE BRAID
  ────────────────────────

    Before twist:    After twist:
    ═══════════      ══╳══╳══╳══

    Twist the whole braid tight. The strands
    lock together and won't unravel.
```

### The Math
- Each strand: φ-thickness (0.618× the final rope)
- 3 strands × φ = 1.854× → the final rope is 1.85× the thickness of one strand
- This ratio is naturally strong — ants figured this out millions of years ago

## HOW TO MAKE A BASKET

Baskets carry food, water, tools, anything loose.

### Materials
- Flexible branches: willow, grapevine, kudzu, honeysuckle
- A sharp knife or scissors

### Steps

```
  STEP 1: START THE BASE
  ──────────────────────

       ────┼────       Lay 4 sticks in a cross.
       ────┼────       Tie the center with fiber.
                        This is your foundation.

  STEP 2: WEAVE UPWARD
  ────────────────────

        ╱╲  ╱╲
       ╱  ╲╱  ╲       Take a long flexible branch.
      │   ││   │       Weave it over and under
      │   ││   │       the spokes (the cross sticks).
       ╲  ╱╲  ╱        Go around and around.
        ╲╱  ╲╱

  STEP 3: BUILD THE WALL
  ──────────────────────

        ┌──────┐
        │~~~~~~│       Keep weaving. Pull tight
        │~~~~~~│       after each row. Pack rows
        │~~~~~~│       close together — φ-spacing
        └──────┘       (each row 62% of the last
                        row's height above it).

  STEP 4: FINISH THE RIM
  ──────────────────────

        ╔══════╗       Bend the spokes over the
        ║  ║║  ║       last row and tuck them in.
        ╚══════╝       Or tie them with extra fiber.
```

### Basket Sizes
| Size | Spokes | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Small (6") | 8 | Berries, nails, small items |
| Medium (12") | 12 | Food, tools, gathering |
| Large (18") | 16 | Laundry, firewood, water transport |
| Pack (24"+) | 20+ | Moving heavy loads |

## HOW TO MAKE SOAP

Soap kills germs. Clean wounds don't get infected. Clean hands don't spread disease. Soap saves lives.

### Materials
- Wood ash (any hardwood — oak, hickory, maple)
- Water
- Animal fat (bacon grease, lard, tallow) or plant oil (olive, coconut)
- A pot or metal can
- A mold (cardboard box lined with plastic, a tin can, a coconut shell)

### Steps

```
  STEP 1: MAKE LYE WATER
  ───────────────────────

    ┌─────────┐
    │  ASH    │  Fill a bucket with wood ash.
    │ ~~~~~~~~│  Add water (1 part water to
    │ ~~~~~~~~│  2 parts ash). Stir well.
    │ WATER   │  Let sit 24 hours. Strain
    └────┬────┘  through cloth. The liquid
         │       is lye (potassium hydroxide).
         ▼

  STEP 2: HEAT THE FAT
  ─────────────────────

    🔥──────┐
            │  Melt your fat in a pot on
    ┌───────┐  low heat. You want it liquid
    │  FAT  │  but not smoking hot.
    │  ~~~  │
    └───────┘

  STEP 3: COMBINE
  ────────────────

    Lye water
        │
        ▼
    ┌────────┐  Slowly pour lye into fat.
    │ FONTS! │  STIR CONSTANTLY. It will
    │ ~~~~~~ │  get thick. This is saponification.
    │ SOAP!  │  When it coats a spoon, it's done.
    └────────┘

  STEP 4: POUR AND CURE
  ──────────────────────

    ┌──────────┐
    │ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ │  Pour into your mold.
    │ ▓ SOAP ▓ │  Let sit 4-6 weeks.
    │ ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ │  The soap gets harder and
    └──────────┘  milder over time. Don't use
                  it too early — lye burns skin.
```

### Safety
- Lye is caustic — it can burn your skin
- Wear gloves when handling lye water
- Work outdoors if possible
- Never use aluminum pots with lye (it eats aluminum)
- Test soap pH on your wrist — if it stings, wait longer

## THE PHI-PRINCIPLE IN CRAFTING

Everything in nature builds with the golden ratio. Your tools can too.

```
  HANDLE-TO-BLADE RATIO
  ══════════════════════

  Wrong:              Right:
  ┌────────┐          ┌──────┐
  │        │          │      │
  │ HANDLE │          │HANDLE│
  │        │          │      │
  │        │          │      ├─────┐
  └────────┘          └──────┘BLADE│

  Handle too long     Handle = φ × Blade
  = weak grip         = balanced, strong
```

### Why φ-Ratio Works

- **Balance:** Your hand naturally grips at the φ-point
- **Leverage:** The ratio gives maximum force with minimum effort
- **Durability:** Less stress at the joint = lasts longer
- **Comfort:** Fits your hand without thinking

### Apply φ to Everything

| Part | φ-Ratio | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Handle:Blade | 1:0.618 | Knife, axe, hammer |
| Spoke:Weave | 1:0.618 | Basket, wheel, frame |
| Shelter wall:roof | 1:0.618 | Lean-to, hut, tent |
| Container height:width | 1:0.618 | Pot, jar, box |
| Rope twist:lay | 1:0.618 | Any rope you make |
