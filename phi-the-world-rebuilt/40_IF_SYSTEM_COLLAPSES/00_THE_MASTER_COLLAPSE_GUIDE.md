# PART 4: WATER

## Finding Water

**Best sources (in order):**

1. **Springs** — Water comes out of the ground already filtered. Best option.
2. **Streams and rivers** — Moving water is safer than still water.
3. **Rainwater** — Clean if you catch it before it hits the ground.
4. **Ponds and lakes** — Still water. Needs more treatment.
5. **Wells** — Dig until you hit water. Deeper = cleaner.

**If you find no water source:**

- Dig a hole in low ground, near trees. Water seeps in.
- Look for animal tracks. They lead to water.
- Follow a valley downhill. Water flows down.
- In the desert, look for green plants in dry areas. Roots go deep to water.

## Cleaning Water

**Method 1: Boiling (best)**
- Bring water to a rolling boil for 1 minute
- At high altitude (above 6,500 feet), boil for 3 minutes
- Let it cool before drinking
- This kills almost everything bad in water

**Method 2: Solar disinfection**
- Fill a clear plastic bottle with water
- Leave it in direct sunlight for 6 hours
- The UV light kills bacteria
- Works best in sunny, hot climates

**Method 3: Filtration**
- Layer materials in a bottle (cut the bottom off, flip it upside down):
  - Bottom layer: small pebbles
  - Middle layer: coarse sand
  - Top layer: fine sand + charcoal (from a campfire)
- Pour water through slowly
- This removes dirt and some bacteria
- Still boil or solar-disinfect after filtering

**Method 4: Water purification tablets**
- Drop one tablet per liter
- Wait 30 minutes
- Good for emergencies when you cannot boil

## Storing Water

- Store in clean, sealed containers
- Glass or food-grade plastic only (no milk jugs — they grow bacteria)
- Keep in a cool, dark place
- Replace every 6 months
- Label containers with the date you filled them

**How much to store:**
- 1 gallon per person per day (drinking + cooking)
- 2 gallons per person per day (include cleaning)
- Store at least 7 days worth

## Testing Water

**Taste test:** If it smells bad or tastes bad, do not drink it without treatment.

**Clarity test:** Hold water up to light. If you can see through it, it is better than murky water. But clear water can still be dangerous — always treat it.

**The safe rule:** If you are not sure, treat it. Boiling is always safe.

# PART 6: SHELTER

## Immediate Shelter

**Tarp + rope = shelter.**

This is the fastest way to protect yourself from rain, wind, and sun.

**The A-frame tarp shelter:**
1. Tie a rope between two trees at chest height
2. Drape a tarp over the rope
3. Stake the edges of the tarp to the ground
4. You now have a roof and two walls

**Time to build: 15 minutes.**
**Cost: $10 for a tarp, $5 for rope.**

## Better Shelter: Adobe Bricks

Adobe is sun-dried brick. Humans have built with it for 10,000 years. It is free, strong, and insulates well.

**Recipe:**
- 1 part clay soil
- 1 part sand
- 0.5 parts straw or dried grass
- Water (enough to make thick mud)

**How to make bricks:**
1. Mix clay soil and sand on a flat surface
2. Add straw and mix it in
3. Add water slowly. Mix until it holds its shape when squeezed
4. Pack into a mold (wooden frame, any size — 12"×8"×4" is standard)
5. Smooth the top with a flat board
6. Remove the mold. The brick holds its shape
7. Let dry in sun for 2-4 weeks, flipping once halfway

**How to build a wall:**
1. Lay bricks in rows, like stacking
2. Fill gaps with more mud mixture
3. Let each row dry before adding the next
4. Build walls 8-12 inches thick for insulation

## Homemade WiFi

From the communication guide: You can build a simple communication system with basic parts.

**What you need:**
- Two old WiFi routers or wireless access points
- Antenna wire (any copper wire, about 12 inches long)
- Power source (solar jar or battery)

**How it works:**
Two routers can talk to each other without the internet. They create a private network between them. If you are within range (about 100-300 feet with homemade antennas), you can send messages.

**Setup:**
1. Set both routers to the same network name (SSID)
2. Set both to the same password
3. Connect a device to each one
4. Use a messaging app that works on local network (like SimpleSS)

**Range:** 100-300 feet with homemade antennas, up to 1 mile with directional antenna.

## Mesh Network

A mesh network connects many devices together. Each device can talk to any other device nearby. Messages hop from device to device until they reach their destination.

**Think of it like a bucket brigade:** Person A passes a bucket to Person B, who passes to Person C. The water gets where it needs to go.

**To build a mesh network:**
1. Get 3-5 devices (old phones, laptops, or Raspberry Pi)
2. Load mesh networking software on each
3. Place them in different locations
4. They automatically connect and pass messages

**This works even without the internet.** If one device fails, the others keep working.
