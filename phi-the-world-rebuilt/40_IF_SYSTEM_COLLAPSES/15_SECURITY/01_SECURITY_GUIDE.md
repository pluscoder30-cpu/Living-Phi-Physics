## PERSONAL SAFETY

The most important rule in any collapse: **stay calm, stay aware.** Panic kills faster than any threat. When everything goes wrong, your mind is your best tool.

### Rules for Staying Alive

1. **Stay calm, stay aware** - Breathe. Look around. Think before you act.
2. **Travel in groups when possible** - Two or three people are safer than one. You watch each other's backs.
3. **Avoid confrontation** - If you can walk away, walk away. Fighting is always a last resort.
4. **Share resources** - Cooperation is safer than competition. A neighbor who owes you a favor is better than a neighbor who hates you.

### The Phi-Principle of Safety

Here's the math behind why cooperation works:

```
cooperation_threshold = φ⁻² = 0.382
```

This means: when the cooperation level in a group drops below 38.2%, everyone becomes unsafe. When it rises above that threshold, the group becomes stronger than any individual threat.

**Translation:** If even 3 out of 8 people in your group are cooperating, the whole group is safer than any one person alone.

### The Wooden Bar System

The simplest, most effective lock:

1. Cut a strong wooden pole (hardwood if possible)
2. Place it across the door inside
3. Props into brackets on each side
4. Takes 2 seconds to remove from inside (escape route)
5. Takes 30+ seconds to break from outside (deters entry)

### Watch System

### Perimeter Awareness

- Keep the area around your home clear (no hiding spots for threats)
- Know what "normal" looks like (so you notice when something's wrong)
- Make noise when you walk (alerts others, signals you're alert)

### Light at Night

Light is the cheapest security system:
- Deters most opportunistic threats
- Shows you're awake and aware
- Helps you see trouble coming
- Cost: one candle or small lamp

## FOOD SECURITY

When supply chains break, food becomes currency. Protect yours.

### Storage Rules

1. **Store food out of sight** - Don't put it in your front window
2. **Use multiple locations** - Don't keep everything in one place
3. **Rotate stock** - Eat oldest first, replace with new
4. **Keep it dry** - Moisture ruins everything

### Growing Food

Community gardens are safer than individual ones:
- More people watching = less theft
- Shared knowledge = better crops
- Shared labor = less burden on any one person

### Wild Food Knowledge

Learn now what you can eat later:
- Which plants are safe (dandelions, cattails, many berries)
- Which plants are poisonous (learn the difference!)
- Where to find them (fields, edges of woods, near water)
- When to harvest (seasons matter)

### Preservation

Food that lasts = food that saves lives:
- **Drying** - Sun-dry fruits, herbs, meat (jerky)
- **Smoking** - Meat and fish last months
- **Salt curing** - If you can find salt
- **Root cellars** - Cool, dark, dry = potatoes, carrots last months
- **Fermentation** - Sauerkraut, pickles, kimchi (healthy and preserved)

## WATER SECURITY

You can survive weeks without food. You can't survive days without water. Water security is life security.

### Rules for Water

1. **Protect water sources** - Don't let anyone contaminate your supply
2. **Don't contaminate your own** - Keep waste away from water
3. **Know multiple sources** - If one dries up or gets contaminated, you need another
4. **Store backup water** - At least 3 days worth, ideally more

### Water Sources (Ranked by Reliability)

| Source | Reliability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Rain collection | High | Works anywhere, just need clean containers |
| Springs | High | Natural filtration, usually clean |
| Rivers/streams | Medium | Risk of contamination upstream |
| Lakes/ponds | Low | Stagnant = bacteria, need filtration |
| Wells | Medium | Good if deep, risk of contamination if shallow |

### Simple Water Filtration

If your water looks dirty:

```
LAYER 1: Gravel (removes big particles)
    ↓
LAYER 2: Sand (removes medium particles)
    ↓
LAYER 3: Charcoal (removes chemicals and bad taste)
    ↓
LAYER 4: Clean cloth (final filter)
    ↓
WATER (still needs boiling to kill bacteria!)
```

**Boil water for at least 1 minute to kill bacteria.** This is not optional.

### Storage

- Clean containers (food-grade plastic or glass)
- Store in cool, dark place
- Rotate every 6 months
- Label with date

### Why Fibonacci Works

- **1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21** - These are the numbers nature uses (sunflower seeds, pinecones, galaxies)
- Each level can communicate directly with the next
- No one has to manage more than 8-13 people directly (cognitive limit)
- Groups can split or merge easily (3 becomes 2+1, 5 becomes 3+2)
- Decision-making is distributed but coordinated

### Communication System

When phones and internet die, you need alternatives:

1. **Mesh network** - People relay messages to each other
2. **Signal system** - Flags, lights, smoke signals
3. **Runner system** - Designated messengers
4. **Watch schedule** - Same phi-interval system as home security

### The Cooperation Equation

Remember the phi-principle:

```
cooperation_level > 0.382 → group is SAFE
cooperation_level < 0.382 → group is DANGER
```

**Translation:** If more than about 38% of your community is cooperating (sharing, helping, communicating), the whole community is safer. If less than 38% are cooperating, the community is at risk.

**How to raise cooperation:**
- Share something (even small)
- Help someone (even once)
- Include someone (even if they're different)
- Communicate openly (even when it's hard)

- Three blasts = universal distress signal
- Louder than shouting, uses less energy
- Can be heard over 1/4 mile away
- Scars away animals and threats

- Reflect sunlight to signal planes/helicopters
- Can be seen from 10+ miles away

- Tie shelter together
- Climb up or down safely

- Make a simple clothesline to dry food
- Create a trip wire for alerts

- Pliers for gripping

### Security Zones

**Zone 1: Perimeter (outermost)**
- Clear all hiding spots (bushes, debris, tall grass)
- Look for signs of trouble (footprints, broken branches)
- Post watch at highest point

**Zone 2: Homes and Storage**
- All doors barred from inside
- Food and water stored out of sight
- Escape routes planned (back doors, windows)

**Zone 3: Community Area (center)**
- Meeting spot for coordination
- Shared resources (tools, first aid, fire)
- Communication hub
