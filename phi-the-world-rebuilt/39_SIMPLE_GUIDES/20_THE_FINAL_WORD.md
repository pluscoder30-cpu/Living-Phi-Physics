## What We Built

We built 18 simple guides.

Not textbooks. Not academic papers. Not theory.

**Guides.** Written at a reading level a 12-year-old can understand. Designed so that a person with nothing — no money, no degree, no connections — can pick one up and start building something real.

We started with food. How to grow it. How to cook it. How to eat well for almost nothing. We moved to medicine — not the kind you buy, but the kind you make with sound, plants, and understanding. We built shelter with golden-ratio angles and $500 budgets. We found water, made energy, learned to talk to each other, taught each other, traded with each other, cleaned up our messes, made fair rules, took care of our minds, survived emergencies, made music, moved our bodies, asked questions, fed picky kids, cooked real meals, and grew gardens in pots on balconies.

Eighteen guides. Covering everything from food to space.

Each one follows the same rules:
- No jargon
- No expensive stuff
- No permission needed

That's it. That's the whole design.

Right now, as you read this:

- **2 billion people** don't have access to safely managed drinking water.
- **828 million people** go to bed hungry every night.
- **1.6 billion people** live in inadequate housing.
- **258 million children** are out of school worldwide.

These aren't abstract numbers. These are people. People with names. People with kids. People who laugh and cry and hope and worry just like you.

The thing is — the solutions to these problems aren't complicated. They've never been complicated. A person can grow food in a pot. A person can filter water through sand and gravel. A person can build a wall with earth and straw. A person can teach a child to read using nothing but their voice and time.

What's been missing isn't knowledge. It's **access**. It's having the right information, in the right language, at the right reading level, in the hands of the person who needs it.

That's what these guides are.

**They're the missing manual for being alive.**

**To keep going:** Less than you think. The average family spends about $4,000 a year on food. With these methods — growing your own, cooking from scratch, preserving what you grow — that drops to about **$40 per person per year**. That's **$1.08 per person per day**.

One dollar and eight cents.

That's what it costs to eat well. Not rice and beans survival. Real food. Vegetables. Herbs. Meals that taste good and make you feel good.

The Shelter Guide? If you build with earth bags or straw bales, a 500-square-foot house costs about **$500 in materials**. Not $50,000. Not $5,000. Five hundred dollars.

The Energy Guide? A basic solar setup costs about **$200** and gives you enough power for lights, phone charging, and a small fan. Permanently.

The Medicine Guide? A frequency generator costs **$35**. Herbs grow for free once you plant them. The $35 Home Kit covers 80% of what most families need.

This isn't charity. This isn't a fantasy. This is math. Simple, beautiful, golden-ratio math.
