# 💧 THE SIMPLE WATER GUIDE 💧

## Why Water Matters

You die in 3 days without water. That's it. No fancy tricks. This guide keeps your family alive and healthy with the simplest possible methods. If you can follow a recipe, you can follow this.

---

## 1. 🔍 How to Find Clean Water

### Rivers & Streams
- Moving water is better than still water
- Look for clear, odorless water
- Avoid water near industrial areas or dead animals
- Collect from the middle of the stream, not the muddy banks

### Rainwater
- The cleanest natural source
- Collect it before it hits the ground
- Free! Just need a funnel and barrel

### Wells
- Dig downhill from where you live
- Look for spots where water naturally pools
- Morning dew can also be collected on clean cloth wiped across grass

### Pro Tip
🌿 The ΦPhi-1 principle: water follows the golden ratio path downhill. Look for streams that curve gently — they've been naturally filtered by the earth over those curves.

## 2. 🧹 How to Clean Dirty Water

### Method 1: Boiling (Simplest)
1. Get water in any clean pot
2. Bring to a **rolling boil** (big bubbles everywhere)
3. Boil for **1 full minute**
4. Let it cool naturally — don't add ice
5. Pour into clean container

✅ Kills 99.9% of bacteria and viruses

### Method 2: Sun Treatment (SODIS)
1. Fill clear plastic bottles (1-2 liter size)
2. Place on a tin roof or metal surface in direct sun
3. Leave for **6+ hours** on sunny days
4. UV rays kill the bad stuff

### Method 3: ΦWater-1 Compound
1. Take 1 level teaspoon of regular calcium hypochlorite (pool shock)
2. Mix into 1 gallon of water — this is your **stock solution**
3. Add 1 part stock solution to 100 parts clean-ish water
4. Stir and wait **30 minutes**
5. Should smell slightly of chlorine — if not, add a tiny bit more

⚠️ Use pool shock labeled "65-70% calcium hypochlorite" — NOT "stabilized" chlorine

## 3. 🏺 How to Store Water

### Container Rules
- Use **food-grade** plastic or glass
- Clean containers with soap and rinse 3 times before first use
- Fill completely — air = germs
- Label with date: "Water — [DATE]"

### Storage Location
- **Cool** — room temperature is fine, no freezer needed
- **Dark** — sunlight grows algae
- **Off the ground** — use a shelf or pallet

### Shelf Life
| Method | Lasts |
|--------|-------|
| Boiled water | 6 months |
| Chlorinated water | 12 months |
| Sealed commercial bottles | Indefinite |

🔄 Rotate every 6 months. Put a reminder on your calendar.

## 4. 🪣 How to Build a Simple Water Filter

This is the **bucket filter** — it removes dirt, sediment, and some bacteria.

### What You Need
- 1 clean 5-gallon bucket with lid
- 1 drill or nail + hammer
- Materials (in order):
  - Coffee filters or clean cloth
  - Activated charcoal (aquarium charcoal works)
  - Fine sand
  - Coarse sand
  - Small gravel
  - Large gravel

### Assembly (Bottom to Top)
```
┌─────────────────────┐
│   COFFEE FILTER     │ ← Catches fine particles
├─────────────────────┤
│ ACTIVATED CHARCOAL  │ ← Removes chemicals, taste, odor (2 inches)
├─────────────────────┤
│    FINE SAND        │ ← Catches tiny particles (2 inches)
├─────────────────────┤
│    COARSE SAND      │ ← Catches medium particles (2 inches)
├─────────────────────┤
│    SMALL GRAVEL     │ ← Catches big particles (2 inches)
├─────────────────────┤
│    LARGE GRAVEL     │ ← Base layer (2 inches)
├─────────────────────┤
│    BOTTOM           │
└──────────┬──────────┘
           │
    Drain hole (1/4 inch)
```

### Steps
1. Drill 1 small hole in the bucket bottom (¼ inch)
2. Layer large gravel on bottom (2 inches)
3. Add small gravel (2 inches)
4. Add coarse sand (2 inches)
5. Add fine sand (2 inches)
6. Add activated charcoal (2 inches)
7. Top with folded coffee filter or clean cloth
8. Pour dirty water SLOWLY into the top
9. Collect clean water from the bottom

⚡ **Important:** This filter removes dirt and improves taste. **ALWAYS boil or chlorinate filtered water before drinking.**

### Cleaning the Filter
- When flow slows down, rinse each layer separately
- Rebuild in same order
- Replace charcoal every 3 months

### Simple System
```

  └──────┘                     │
                              ▼

                    │  (keeps      │
                    │   leaves out)│
                    └──────┬───────┘
                           │
                           ▼
                    ┌──────────────┐
                    │              │
                    │   BARREL     │ ← 55-gallon drum or any big container
                    │              │
                    └──────┬───────┘
                           │
                           ▼
                      Spigot ───→ Clean water!
```

### Build Steps
1. Place barrel under gutter downspout
2. Cover top with screen or pantyhose (keeps bugs and leaves out)
3. Cut downspout to aim into the barrel
4. Drill hole near bottom, install a cheap spigot
5. Done!

### First Flush Diverter (Optional but Smart)
The first rain washes dirt off your roof. Divert the first 10 gallons away:
1. Install a T-connector in your downspout
2. One path goes to barrel, other goes to ground
3. First water fills the ground path and overflows to barrel

📊 **How Much Do You Get?**
- 1 inch of rain on 1,000 sq ft roof = **600 gallons**
- A family of 4 needs ~12 gallons/day
- One decent rainstorm = weeks of water

## 6. 🧪 How to Test Your Water

### pH Testing (Super Easy)
1. Buy pH test strips (cheap, available at pool stores or online)
2. Dip strip in water
3. Compare color to the chart
4. **Target: pH 7.2** (slightly alkaline is perfect)

| pH Level | What It Means |
|----------|---------------|
| 6.5 or below | Too acidic — can leach metals from pipes |
| 6.5 - 7.5 | ✅ SAFE — drink this! |
| 8.0+ | Too alkaline — may taste bitter |

### Other Simple Tests
- **Turbidity test:** Fill a clear glass — can you read text through it? If yes → clear enough to treat
- **Smell test:** No sulfur (rotten egg), no chemical smell → good sign
- **Taste test (after treating):** If it tastes off, re-treat or re-filter

### When to Be Concerned
- Water near factories, mines, or farms → test for heavy metals and nitrates
- Water after flooding → boil everything
- Water from old wells → test for lead and bacteria

## 7. 🚽 How to Handle Sewage

**This is the part nobody wants to talk about, but it's life or death.** Bad sewage = cholera, dysentery, death.

### The Composting Toilet (Simple, Odor-Free!)

#### What You Need
- 5-gallon bucket with tight-fitting lid
- Seat cutout (buy a toilet seat that fits a bucket, or make one from plywood)
- Carbon material: sawdust, wood shavings, peat moss, or dry leaves
- Small container for the sawdust

#### How It Works
1. **Before each use:** Add 2 handfuls of sawdust to the bucket
2. **Do your business** — business number 1 AND number 2
3. **After each use:** Cover with another 2 handfuls of sawdust
4. **Close the lid** — the lid is what keeps it odor-free!
5. When bucket is ¾ full, carry to compost pile

#### The Compost Pile
- Dig a hole or use a dedicated bin
- Empty bucket contents into the pile
- Cover with dirt or more sawdust
- Wait **12 months minimum** before using on gardens
- Layer: waste → sawdust → dirt → waste → sawdust → dirt

#### Odor Control Secrets
- Always use the lid! (Seriously, always)
- Sawdust absorbs moisture and smell
- A little baking soda in the bucket helps
- Keep the bucket in shade, not sun
- Empty before it's totally full (¾ is the mark)

#### Hand Hygiene
- **Always wash hands** after using the toilet
- Soap and water, scrub for 20 seconds
- This is NOT optional. This is what prevents disease.
