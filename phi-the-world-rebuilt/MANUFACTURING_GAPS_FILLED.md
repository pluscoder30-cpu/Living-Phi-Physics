## PHI-Agriculture: Farm Equipment & Food Processing

### Product 1: PHI-SPIRAL GARDEN BED

**What it is:** A raised garden bed planted in phi-spiral pattern (137.5° spacing) for maximum yield per area.

**Raw Materials:**
- Reclaimed lumber or bamboo (any local wood, 2×4 or equivalent)
- Soil: 38.2% local clay, 38.2% sand, 23.6% compost (phi-ratio mix)
- Seeds (open-pollinated, locally adapted)
- Nails or twine (for joining)

**Equipment Needed:**
- Hand saw or machete
- Hammer or mallet
- Measuring tape
- String and stake (for marking spiral)

**Step-by-Step Assembly:**
1. Mark a circle on the ground, diameter = 2 meters (phi-adjusted: 1.618m for small, 2.618m for large)
2. Place stake at center. Tie string length = radius. Walk circle marking ground
3. Starting from center, walk outward at 137.5° from previous plant position
4. At each position, dig hole = φ × seed depth (e.g., bean at 3cm → 4.85cm)
5. Space plants at d(n) = d(0) × φ^(n/13) where n = plant index, d(0) = 5cm
6. Build raised bed border: stack wood in phi-proportioned rectangle (L:W = φ:1)
7. Fill with phi-soil mix: 38.2% clay + 38.2% sand + 23.6% compost by volume
8. Plant in phi-sequence: tallest plants at center (φ⁰), shortest at edge (φ⁻ⁿ)

**Quality Control:**
- Measure plant spacing: should follow 137.5° ± 5° between adjacent plants
- Soil pH test: target 7.2361 (phi-neutral), not 7.0
- Water test: pour 1L on bed, should absorb in < 5 minutes (phi-permeability)
- Yield check: phi-spiral bed should produce φ × (1.618×) more per m² than row planting

**Cost:** $5-20 (scavenged materials + saved seeds)

### Product 3: PHI-FERMENTATION VESSEL

**What it is:** A ceramic or food-grade container for phi-optimized fermentation of vegetables, kimchi, sauerkraut.

**Raw Materials:**
- Food-grade ceramic crock (5L) or glass jar (2L) — thrift store or local potter

- Cutting board
- Scale (any kitchen scale)

**Step-by-Step Assembly:**

8. Taste every φ days (every 2 days): sourness increases at phi-rate
9. When pH drops to 3.82 (φ⁻¹ × target), transfer to cold storage (4°C)

**Quality Control:**
- pH test: finished product pH 3.5-4.0 (phi-acidic range)
- Taste: pleasant sourness, no off-flavors
- Texture: crunchy, not mushy (phi-structure maintained)
- Smell: clean fermentation aroma, no mold or rot

**Cost:** $5-15 (scavenged vessel + salt + garden vegetables)

## PHI-Textiles: Fabric & Garment Production

### Product 5: PHI-HAND SPINDLE

**What it is:** A drop spindle for spinning fiber into yarn at phi-helix angle (31.7°).

**Raw Materials:**
- Wooden dowel (30cm long, 1cm diameter) — or straight stick from tree
- Wooden disc or CD (8cm diameter) — or carved wooden whorl
- Hook: bent wire or carved bone
- Whorl weight: 30-50g (phi-optimal for cotton/wool)

**Equipment Needed:**
- Knife or sandpaper
- Drill or heated wire (for hole in whorl)
- Glue or epoxy (optional)

**Step-by-Step Assembly:**
1. Cut dowel to 30cm
2. Cut or find disc 8cm diameter. Carve or sand to smooth
3. Drill hole in center of disc (slightly smaller than dowel)
4. Insert dowel through disc, position disc 5cm from bottom
5. Secure with glue or wedge
6. Bend wire into hook at top of dowel
7. Test spin: flick spindle, it should rotate for > 10 seconds (phi-balance)

**Fiber Preparation:**
1. Card fiber (wool/cotton) using two boards with nails at phi-spacing (1.618cm apart)
2. Draft fiber to phi-thickness: pull until thumb-and-forefinger span = 2cm × φ = 3.24cm
3. Attach fiber to hook, begin spinning
4. Maintain twist angle at 31.7° from vertical (phi-helix angle)
5. Wind on when spindle reaches bottom: wrap yarn at phi-angle on shaft

**Quality Control:**
- Yarn diameter: consistent within ±20% along length
- Twist angle: 31.7° ± 3° (use protractor)
- Strength: wrap yarn around two fingers, pull; should not break under moderate force
- Consistency: 1 meter of yarn should weigh within ±15% of average

**Cost:** $0-5 (scavenged stick + disc + wire)

### Product 6: PHI-LOOM (Frame Loom)

**What it is:** A simple frame loom for weaving phi-weave fabric (1:φ thread ratio).

**Raw Materials:**
- 4 wooden boards (60cm × 4cm × 2cm) — frame
- Nails or pegs (at phi-spacing: 1.618cm apart)
- String (for warp)
- Yarn (for weft — from Phi-Hand Spindle above)

**Equipment Needed:**
- Hammer
- Ruler or tape measure
- Saw

**Step-by-Step Assembly:**
1. Build rectangular frame: 60cm × 40cm (ratio ≈ φ:1 is 60:37 — close enough)
2. Mark nail positions on top and bottom rails at phi-spacing: start at 1cm, then every 1.618cm
3. Hammer nails halfway in (leave head protruding for warp)
4. Thread warp: wrap string around top nails, down to bottom nails, back up
5. Thread spacing: 1 nail = 1 warp thread (every 1.618cm)
6. Weft pattern (phi-twill):
   - Row 1: over 1, under 2, over 1, under 3 (repeat)
   - Row 2: shift by 1 (over 2, under 1, over 3, under 1)
   - This creates the 1:φ diagonal herringbone pattern

**Quality Control:**
- Thread count: measure 5cm of fabric, count threads. Should be ~3 threads/cm (1/1.618cm)
- Pattern check: diagonal line should be visible at 31.7° from horizontal
- Strength test: pull fabric from selvedge to selvedge; should resist tearing
- Evenness: hold up to light; gaps should be uniform

**Cost:** $5-15 (scavenged wood + nails + homemade yarn)

## PHI-Energy: Solar, Wind & Battery

### Product 8: PHI-SOLAR PANEL (Reclaimed Cell Assembly)

**What it is:** A small solar panel made from reclaimed cells, mounted at phi-tilt angle.

**Raw Materials:**
- Solar cells (10-20, reclaimed from old panels, ~0.5W each, $0.50-1 each on eBay/salvage)
- Tabbing wire (copper, thin, $5 for 10m)
- Flux pen (for soldering, $3)
- Plexiglass or clear plastic sheet (for cover)
- Plywood or cardboard (for backing)
- Silicone caulk (for sealing)
- Bus wire (for connecting strings of cells)

**Equipment Needed:**
- Soldering iron (or fire-heated metal tip)
- Solder (lead-free, $5)
- Ruler
- Pencil
- Drill (for mounting holes)

**Step-by-Step Assembly:**
1. Layout cells in phi-arrangement:
   - 4 strings of 4 cells each
   - String spacing: φ × cell_width (e.g., cell = 5cm → spacing = 8.09cm)
   - Within string: cells 1.618cm apart
2. Solder tabbing wire to front of each cell (negative)
3. Connect strings in series: front of string 1 to back of string 2
4. Connect bus wire to first and last cell (positive and negative leads)
5. Mount cells on plywood backing with silicone
6. Cover with plexiglass, seal edges with silicone
7. Drill mounting holes at phi-angles: 137.5° from horizontal for latitude-adjusted tilt
8. Mount on frame at tilt_angle = latitude × φ⁻¹ (e.g., 45° latitude → 27.8° tilt)

**Quality Control:**
- Voltage test: open circuit should read N_cells × 0.5V = 8V (for 16 cells)
- Current test: in full sun, should produce ≥ 0.5A (for 16 × 0.5W cells = 8W total)
- Tilt check: angle from horizontal = latitude × 0.618
- Seal test: spray with water, no leaks inside

**Cost:** $20-50 (reclaimed cells + solder + materials)

### Product 11: PHI-WATER FILTER (Multi-Stage)

**What it is:** A gravity water filter using phi-ratio layer depths for maximum purification.

**Raw Materials:**

**Equipment Needed:**
- Drill with 1" bit (for spigot hole)
- Bucket lid or plate (to separate stages)
- Measuring cup
- Funnel

**Step-by-Step Assembly:**
1. Drill hole in bottom bucket near base, insert grommet + spigot
2. Nest buckets: bottom bucket catches filtered water, top bucket holds layers
3. Drill small holes in bottom of top bucket (or use colander as insert)
4. Layer materials at phi-ratios (by depth):
   - Layer 1 (bottom, through holes): cotton cloth, φ⁻⁴ = 14.6% of total depth
   - Layer 2: activated charcoal, φ⁻³ = 23.6% of total depth (11.8cm in 50cm bucket)
   - Layer 3: fine sand, φ⁻³ = 23.6% of total depth (11.8cm)
   - Layer 4: coarse sand, φ⁻² = 38.2% of total depth (19.1cm)
   - Layer 5 (top): gravel, φ⁻¹ = 61.8% — overflow guard (just cover top)
5. Pre-rinse: pour 10L of clean water through to settle layers
6. Filter: pour raw water slowly into top. First 2L may be cloudy — discard
7. After that: clear water flows from spigot at ~1L/hour

**Quality Control:**
- Visual test: filtered water should be clear, no turbidity
- Taste test: no earthy, chemical, or chlorine taste
- Flow rate: 0.5-2L/hour (phi-optimized, not too fast)
- Bacteria test (if available): pour through petri dish, incubate 24h, count colonies

## PHI-Construction: Building Materials

### Product 17: PHI-ADOBEBLOCK

**What it is:** Sun-dried mud brick at phi-ratio mix for maximum strength.

**Raw Materials:**
- Soil (local, high clay content): 38.2% by volume
- Sand: 38.2% by volume
- Straw or grass: 23.6% by volume
- Water: enough to make moldable paste

**Equipment Needed:**
- Wooden mold: 30cm × 15cm × 10cm (ratio 2:1 ≈ φ:1)
- Mixing pit or tarp
- Shovel

**Step-by-Step Assembly:**
1. Dig clay-rich soil, screen through mesh to remove rocks
2. Measure by volume: 4 parts clay + 4 parts sand + 2.5 parts straw (≈ 38:38:24)
3. Add water gradually, mix until consistency of thick peanut butter
4. Pack into mold, pressing firmly to remove air
5. Strike off top with straight edge
6. Flip mold onto flat ground, lift off
7. Dry in sun for 3-5 days, turning once daily
8. Final drying: stack in phi-pattern (alternate direction) for 1 week

**Quality Control:**
- Dimension check: 30cm × 15cm ± 1cm
