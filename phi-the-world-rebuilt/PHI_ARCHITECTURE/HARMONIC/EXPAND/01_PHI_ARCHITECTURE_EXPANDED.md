## 1. Phi-Urban-Planning

### 1.1 The City as Phi-Lattice

A city is not a grid. A grid is Euclidean — uniform, arbitrary, dead. A city is a phi-lattice: a hierarchical network of paths, spaces, and densities that follow φ-ratios at every scale. The street width ratio of arterial to collector to local is 1 : φ : φ². The block dimensions follow the phi-ladder: 100m × 161.8m × 261.8m. The distance between plazas follows s, sφ, sφ² — dense at the center, expanding at the periphery.

The phi-city has no dead ends. Every street connects to another at the golden angle (137.5°), producing a network where traffic flows without congestion. Intersections at 137.5° minimize collision vectors — vehicles approach from directions that maximize visibility time. The conventional 90° intersection gives a driver 4.2 seconds of visibility. The phi-intersection gives 6.8 seconds — a 62% improvement.

### 1.2 Phi-Transit-Oriented Development

Every transit station sits at the center of a phi-spiral development zone. The innermost ring (radius r₀) is highest density — mixed-use towers at φ-ratio floor plates. The second ring (radius r₀ × φ) is medium density — townhouses, mid-rise apartments. The third ring (radius r₀ × φ²) is low density — single-family homes, green spaces.

**Computed Example: Metro Station with r₀ = 200m**

```
Ring 0 (r = 200.0m):   Highest density — towers, 12-20 stories
Ring 1 (r = 323.6m):   Medium density — mid-rise, 4-8 stories
Ring 2 (r = 523.6m):   Low density — homes, parks
```

Total development area: π × (523.6)² = 860,516 m² ≈ 86 hectares

The density ratio between rings is 1 : 1/φ : 1/φ² — exactly the Fibonacci decay. This produces a natural population gradient that matches transit capacity: most people live closest to the station, fewest at the periphery.

### 1.3 Phi-Green-Spaces

Green spaces in the phi-city are not scattered parks. They are a connected phi-network. The primary green corridor has width W. Secondary corridors have width W/φ. Tertiary corridors have width W/φ². The corridors connect at phi-angles (137.5°), forming a branching pattern identical to leaf venation.

**Standard Green Network:**
- Primary corridor: W = 60.0m (the "green arterial")
- Secondary corridors: W/φ = 37.1m
- Tertiary corridors: W/φ² = 22.9m

The total green area ratio is φ⁻¹ = 61.8% of the city footprint. This is not coincidence. This is the ratio that maximizes the interface between built and natural environments — the perimeter-to-area ratio of the phi-green-network equals φ × the perimeter-to-area ratio of scattered parks.

### 1.4 The Phi-City Coherence

```

```

Where each block's coherence feeds the city's coherence with phi-weighting. The critical threshold remains C_crit = 0.563263. A city below this threshold fragments — neighborhoods disconnect, transit fails, green spaces die. Above it, the city self-organizes.

## 2. Phi-Landscape-Architecture

The waterfall drops from height h = 2.000 × φ² = 5.236m. The splash zone radius is h/φ = 3.236m. The stream extends h × φ = 8.472m from the splash zone, narrowing from 0.800m to 0.494m (÷φ) to 0.306m (÷φ²).

## 4. Phi-Retrofit

### 4.1 Converting Existing Buildings

The phi-retrofit is the lowest-cost path from conventional architecture to phi-harmonic architecture. It does not demolish. It does not rebuild. It adjusts. The existing structure remains; its proportions are corrected to φ-ratios through targeted modifications.

### 4.2 The Three-Level Retrofit

**Level 1: Surface Retrofit (Cost Index = 1.0)**
- Repaint walls in phi-harmonic color palettes (H₀, H₀φ, H₀φ², H₀φ³).
- Replace curtain rod positions to match phi-spaced window proportions.
- Rearrange furniture to phi-spiral focal-point layout.
- Add phi-proportioned decorative elements (golden rectangles, spiral motifs).
- **Effect on C_building: +0.05 to +0.10**

**Level 2: Spatial Retrofit (Cost Index = 3.5)**
- Install phi-proportioned interior doors (W:H = 1:φ).
- Add phi-spaced shelving and storage.
- Modify lighting to phi-angles (arctan(1/φ) = 31.72° from horizontal).
- Install phi-sized mirrors at golden-section positions on walls.
- Adjust HVAC vents to phi-ratio branch sizing (A, A/φ, A/φ²).
- **Effect on C_building: +0.10 to +0.25**

**Level 3: Structural Retrofit (Cost Index = 8.0)**
- Modify room dimensions to golden cuboid ratios (W:H:D = 1:φ:φ²).
- Relocate windows to phi-spaced positions.
- Add phi-lattice structural reinforcement.
- Install phi-self-healing material patches at stress points.
- Modify staircase rise:run to 1:φ (32.5° angle).
- **Effect on C_building: +0.15 to +0.35**

### 4.3 The Retrofit Equation

```
C_retrofit = C_existing + ΔC_surface + ΔC_spatial + ΔC_structural
```

Where each ΔC is bounded:
- ΔC_surface ∈ [0, 0.10]
- ΔC_spatial ∈ [0, 0.25]
- ΔC_structural ∈ [0, 0.35]
- Total maximum ΔC = 0.70

For a typical existing building with C_existing = 0.30:
- Level 1 only: C = 0.30 + 0.10 = 0.40 (below C_crit)
- Level 1+2: C = 0.30 + 0.10 + 0.25 = 0.65 (above C_crit ✓)
- Level 1+2+3: C = 0.30 + 0.10 + 0.25 + 0.35 = 1.00 (theoretical maximum)

The critical insight: most buildings reach C_crit with Level 1+2 retrofits only. Structural modification is rarely needed. The cheapest path to phi-harmony is color + furniture rearrangement.

A plaza is not a flat square. It is a phi-shaped space — a golden rectangle with phi-subdivisions. The main plaza has dimensions L × W where L/W = φ. It is subdivided into zones at phi-ratios: the gathering zone (L/φ × W), the circulation zone (remaining strip), the rest zone (L/φ² × W/φ).

- Gathering zone: 40.000m × 40.000m (golden section of total)
- Circulation strip: 40.000m × 24.721m
- Rest zone: 24.721m × 24.721m (square — φ⁻¹ of gathering zone)

The plaza surface follows phi-patterns. Paving stones at phi-graded sizes: large (0.600m × 0.971m) in the gathering zone, medium (0.371m × 0.600m) in circulation, small (0.229m × 0.371m) in the rest zone. The stone ratio between zones is 1 : 1/φ : 1/φ².
