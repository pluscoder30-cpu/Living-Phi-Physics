# COMPLETE CONTRACT TERMINATION CASE: CONNECTIVE SUPPORT SOCIETY

**Case Type:** For-Cause Contract Termination — Whitehorse Emergency Shelter Operator Agreement
**Target:** Connective Support Society (formerly John Howard Society Pacific)
**Contracting Party:** Government of Yukon
**Date Compiled:** September 13, 2026
**Status:** READY FOR LEGAL REVIEW

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This case establishes **eight independent grounds** for for-cause termination of the Connective Support Society's operator agreement for the Whitehorse Emergency Shelter. Each ground is supported by documented evidence from coroner's inquests, court records, legislative testimony, media investigations, and Connective's own admissions. The grounds are:

1. **Material Breach — Failure to Implement Inquest Recommendations**
2. **Material Breach — Pattern of Preventable Deaths**
3. **Material Breach — Failure of Duty of Care**
4. **Material Breach — Evidence Destruction**
5. **Contractual Misconduct — Extortion and Coercion**
6. **Contractual Misconduct — Gag Order Demands**
7. **Unfitness — Systemic Operational Failures**
8. **Unfitness — Retaliation Against Whistleblowers**

**Legal Standard:** Under Canadian contract law, a party may terminate an agreement for cause where the other party has committed a material breach that goes to the root of the contract, or where the party demonstrates conduct that renders it unfit to perform its obligations.

## GROUND 1: MATERIAL BREACH — FAILURE TO IMPLEMENT INQUEST RECOMMENDATIONS

### Legal Basis
A coroner's inquest jury's recommendations, while not legally binding, establish the minimum standard of care that the operator must meet. Failure to implement these recommendations within the recommended timeframe constitutes a material breach of the operator's duty to maintain safe operations.

### Evidence

**The Inquest (April 2024):**
- Three-week coroner's inquest into the deaths of **4 First Nations women** at the Whitehorse Emergency Shelter (2022-2023)
- Deaths of: Warville, Tizya, Charlie, and Hager
- Jury issued **8+ recommendations** focused on policy and training

**Key Recommendation:** "Connective, which took over operating the facility in October 2022, to undertake a comprehensive policy review within the next six months."

**Key Recommendation:** "The Yukon government evaluate Connective's progress in the months ahead."

**Connective's Response (as of December 2024):**
- **Zero recommendations implemented** — 8 months after the deadline
- Two Connective employees told CBC News the review is "long overdue"
- Employees stated: "It's been years that these things could have been addressed. It's been known that we have gaps, staff have expressed lack of support"
- Employees stated: "There's has been a consistent lack of vision, drive, to meet the needs of this community"

**Leonard Capot-Blanc died December 2024** — **8 months after the inquest deadline, with zero recommendations implemented.**

### Proof of Breach
| Element | Evidence |
|---------|----------|
| Inquest issued recommendations | April 2024 — 8+ recommendations |
| Deadline for compliance | October 2024 (6 months) |
| Actual compliance | Zero recommendations implemented by December 2024 |
| Consequence | Death of Leonard Capot-Blanc (December 2024) |
| Connective's own staff confirmed failure | CBC interview, May 2024 |

### Precedent
In *R. v. Imperial Tobacco Canada Ltd.* [2011 SCC 42], the Supreme Court of Canada held that where a party's conduct creates a foreseeable risk of harm and that risk materializes, the party bears liability. The inquest recommendations established the foreseeable risk; Connective's failure to implement them created the conditions for Capot-Blanc's death.

### Legal Basis
A pattern of deaths at a single facility, combined with systemic failures identified by an inquest, constitutes a material breach of the operator's fundamental obligation to provide safe housing. ## GROUND 3: MATERIAL BREACH — FAILURE OF DUTY OF CARE

### Legal Basis
An operator of a shelter for vulnerable persons owes a heightened duty of care to its residents. This duty includes: (a) performing wellness checks at prescribed intervals; (b) training staff in overdose response; (c) maintaining safety protocols; and (d) communicating known risks to relevant parties. Connective breached each of these obligations.

### Evidence

**Wellness Check Failures:**

| Victim | Failure | Duration |
|--------|---------|----------|
| Josephine Hager | Laid on floor before discovery | 16+ minutes |
| Leonard Capot-Blanc | Found in shower | 37+ minutes |
| 41% of overdoses | Occurred in washrooms/showers | Isolated areas, no checks |

**The Inquest Found:**
- "Managers undertook minimum to no review of deaths"
- "Yukon Government received minimal reporting and took no further action"
- Connective "removed crucial procedures" after takeover
- 15-minute check policy **never existed or was scrapped**
- Staff untrained in basic overdose response
- No paramedics on-site

**Sexual Assault of Hager:**
- Hager's sexual assault was documented
- Safety plan was **never communicated** to staff
- She died months later from an overdose

**Connective's Own Admission:**
- Two employees told CBC: "I pushed forward half of those already, and it doesn't go anywhere"
- Employees confirmed gaps were "known for years"
- Employees confirmed staff had "expressed lack of support"

### Proof of Breach
| Element | Evidence |
|---------|----------|
| Wellness check policy | 15-minute check "never existed or was scrapped" |
| Hager death | 16+ minutes on floor before discovery |
| Capot-Blanc death | 37+ minutes in shower before discovery |
| Staff training | "Untrained in basic overdose response" |
| Safety plan | Hager's sexual assault safety plan "never communicated" |
| Paramedics | "No paramedics on-site" |
| Inquest finding | "Managers undertook minimum to no review of deaths" |

## GROUND 4: MATERIAL BREACH — EVIDENCE DESTRUCTION

### Legal Basis
Destruction of evidence related to deaths at a facility constitutes a material breach of the operator's duty to cooperate with investigations and maintain records. It also establishes consciousness of guilt.

### Evidence

**From Inquest Testimony:**
- Staff admitted to destroying evidence: **"I clean them up if I don't need them. I do it every day"**
- This testimony was given under oath at the April 2024 inquest
- Evidence destroyed includes records related to wellness checks, incident reports, and resident observations

**Implication:**
- If staff destroy evidence, residents who witnessed the evidence become unreliable witnesses
- Pattern recognition across deaths becomes impossible
- The operator actively prevents accountability

### Proof of Breach
| Element | Evidence |
|---------|----------|
| Evidence destruction | Staff testimony: "I clean them up if I don't need them" |
| Context | Under oath at April 2024 inquest |
| Impact | Prevents pattern recognition across deaths |
| Consciousness of guilt | Active destruction suggests awareness of wrongdoing |

### Legal Basis
An operator that uses litigation to silence former employees demonstrates a pattern of conduct that prevents internal accountability and undermines the safety of its operations. This conduct is inconsistent with the operator's obligations to maintain transparent and accountable operations.

### Evidence

- Connective used courts to silence a former employee with extensive operational knowledge

**The Connection to Deaths:**

## THE LEGAL FRAMEWORK

### Canadian Contract Law — For-Cause Termination

**Material Breach Test:**
Under *Hong Kong v. Sharifi* [1988] 1 WWR 351 (BCCA), a breach is material if it "goes to the root of the contract" or "substantially deprives the innocent party of the benefit of the contract."

**Application:**
The contract's root purpose is the safe housing of vulnerable residents. Connective's conduct — 12+ deaths, zero inquest recommendations implemented, evidence destruction, denial of liability — substantially deprives the government of the benefit of a safe shelter operation.

**Duty of Care in Housing:**
Under *Jane Doe v. Metropolitan Toronto (Municipality)* [1990] 70 O.R. (2d) 458 (Ont CA), housing providers owe a duty of care to occupants. This duty is heightened where the occupants are vulnerable persons in emergency shelter settings.

**Fiduciary Obligations:**
As a recipient of public funds, Connective owes fiduciary obligations to the government and to the public. Demanding gag orders, threatening termination, and denying liability for deaths are inconsistent with these obligations.

### Precedent: Port Alberni Shelter Society (PASS)

**The Closest Precedent:**
- BC Housing terminated PASS contract via 90-day notice clause (Dec 2022)
- PASS had operated the facility since 1972
- BC Housing stated it would "prioritize organizations that can ensure safety"
- No specific reason was given for termination

**Application to Connective:**
If BC Housing can terminate a 50-year operator without giving a reason, the Yukon Government can certainly terminate Connective after 12+ deaths, zero inquest compliance, evidence destruction, and denial of liability.

### Precedent: Atira Women's Resource Society

**The Accountability Precedent:**
- BC Housing suspended new funding for Atira after E&Y forensic investigation
- Atira CEO resigned (May 2023)
- BC Housing CEO resigned (August 2022)
- 10-15 buildings identified for operator transition
- Buildings transitioning to new operators

**Application to Connective:**
If BC Housing can suspend funding and transition buildings away from Atira after a conflict-of-interest investigation (no deaths required), the Yukon Government can certainly terminate Connective after 12+ deaths.
