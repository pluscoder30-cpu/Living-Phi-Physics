The result: 330+ deaths/year, zero criminal charges, zero public data, zero accountability.

## THE 12 DATA SUPPRESSION LAYERS

### LAYER 1: NO DEATH REGISTRY EXISTS
**Status:** CONFIRMED — VERIFIED ABSENCE

BC Housing does NOT maintain a public registry of deaths in supportive housing. There is:
- No central database of deaths by facility
- No public dashboard of mortality rates
- No mandatory death reporting to a central authority
- No requirement for operators to disclose deaths to the public

**Evidence:**
- BC Housing's public disclosures include annual reports with aggregate numbers but NO facility-level death data
- The EXECUTIVE_COMPENSATION_vs_DEATHS.md report documents "330+ deaths/year" as a system-wide estimate — not from any official registry
- Lu'ma Travelodge: BC Housing originally reported only **2 deaths** — Lu'ma's lawyer revised count to **8 deaths** (EXECUTIVE_COMPENSATION_vs_DEATHS.md:178)
- RainCity Foxglove: "8 total overdose deaths at Foxglove since opening in 2022" — disclosed only through media investigation, not official registry (BC_SUPPORTIVE_HOUSING_OPERATORS_REPORT.md:71)

**The Gap:** Without a mandatory death registry, the public cannot know the true death toll. Operators control the narrative. BC Housing controls the narrative. The government controls the narrative.

### LAYER 2: NO OUTCOME METRICS EXIST
**Status:** CONFIRMED — VERIFIED ABSENCE

BC Housing does NOT track or publish outcome metrics for supportive housing:
- No resident health outcomes
- No mortality rates by facility
- No quality of life indicators
- No housing stability metrics post-placement
- No overdose rates by facility
- No eviction rates by facility

**Evidence:**
- BC Housing's operator agreements focus on INPUTS (units, funding, staffing) not OUTCOMES (deaths, health, stability)
- The "growth-reward paradox" (EXECUTIVE_COMPENSATION_vs_DEATHS.md:259): Connective grew 900% in 10 years → won "Housing Provider of the Year" → 20+ deaths confirmed. Growth is measured. Deaths are not.
- BC Housing's response to Diane Chandler's death (11 days undiscovered): "Strengthened requirements for 48-hour wellness checks" — a process change, not an outcome metric (BC_SUPPORTIVE_HOUSING_OPERATORS_REPORT.md:74)

**The Gap:** Without outcome metrics, operators cannot be held accountable for results. They are rewarded for growth, not for keeping people alive.

### LAYER 3: NO EVICTION TRACKING EXISTS
**Status:** CONFIRMED — VERIFIED ABSENCE

BC does NOT track evictions from supportive housing:
- No centralized eviction database
- No tracking of informal removals (trespass, "accommodation ended")
- No data on shelter guest removals
- No tracking of "behavioral" labels that follow residents between facilities

**Evidence:**
- RESIDENT_COMPLAINT_RETALIATION_REPORT.md documents the "Difficult to House" label system (lines 336-361): labels follow residents between operators but NO central database tracks this
- Bill 11 (BC legislation) gives operators MORE power to remove tenants — but NO tracking of removals under Bill 11
- Shelter guests can be trespassed "at any time, for any reason" — no record required (RESIDENT_COMPLAINT_RETALIATION_REPORT.md:177)

**The Gap:** Without eviction tracking, operators can remove "difficult" residents (complainants, witnesses) without creating a paper trail. The system erases its own removal patterns.

### LAYER 5: FOI FEES BLOCK ACCESS
**Status:** CONFIRMED — DOCUMENTED

Freedom of Information requests for BC Housing data are prohibitively expensive:
- BC Housing FOI fees: **$1,800** for documentation requests
- This effectively blocks public access to housing data
- Journalists and researchers cannot afford systematic FOI campaigns
- The fee structure itself is a suppression mechanism

**Evidence:**
- EXECUTIVE_COMPENSATION_vs_DEATHS.md:304: "FOI Requests — Target BC Housing for executive compensation disclosure" (listed as RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS — meaning it hasn't been done because it's too expensive)
- The investigation files document extensive research through media sources and court records — NOT through FOI, because FOI is cost-prohibitive

**The Gap:** When FOI costs $1,800 per request, only well-funded organizations can access public information. The public is priced out of its own data.

### LAYER 6: CONFIDENTIAL SETTLEMENTS ERASE PATTERNS
**Status:** CONFIRMED — DOCUMENTED

Multiple wrongful death settlements include confidentiality clauses that prevent:
- Families from discussing cases publicly
- Families from identifying the operator
- Families from joining other families with similar cases
- Pattern recognition across facilities
- Public knowledge of settlement amounts

**Evidence:**
- RESIDENT_COMPLAINT_RETALIATION_REPORT.md:266-285: "When a resident dies or is harmed, their family may sue. The operator settles with a confidentiality clause"
- "330+ deaths/year × 0 criminal charges = 330+ families silenced"
- "Confidential settlements = 330+ separate cases that can never be compared"
- "Pattern recognition = impossible"
- Connective v. Melew: $50,000 damages + **permanent injunction** (lifetime gag order) — staff face massive penalties for speaking (STAFF_RETALIATION_REPORT.md:42-47)

**The Gap:** Confidential settlements are the most effective data suppression tool. They don't just hide individual cases — they make it IMPOSSIBLE to see the pattern. Each family starts from zero.

### LAYER 7: DEATH CLASSIFICATION PREVENTS INVESTIGATION
**Status:** CONFIRMED — INFERRED

Deaths in supportive housing are classified in ways that prevent investigation:
- Overdose deaths classified as "accidental" → no criminal investigation
- "Natural causes" classification → no investigation into conditions
- "Drug-related" classification → blame shifted to deceased, not operator
- Deaths in "low barrier" facilities → drug use is permitted, so deaths are "expected"

**Evidence:**
- Lu'ma Travelodge: 8 deaths classified as "5 natural, 2 overdoses, 1 bus collision" — classification PREVENTS investigation into facility conditions (EXECUTIVE_COMPENSATION_vs_DEATHS.md:179)
- Whitehorse Shelter: 67 overdoses in 22 months, 41% in washrooms — classified as individual overdoses, NOT as a systemic failure
- Foxglove: 8 overdose deaths since opening 2022 — classified as individual events, NOT as a facility failure
- Diane Chandler: Death classified as "overdose" — but the REAL cause was staff failure to perform wellness checks (body undiscovered 11 days)

**The Gap:** When deaths are classified as individual events rather than systemic failures, no investigation occurs. The classification IS the suppression.

### LAYER 8: COMPLAINTS NOT INVESTIGATED OR DISMISSED
**Status:** CONFIRMED — DOCUMENTED

The complaint system is designed to absorb complaints without producing change:
- 67% of resident complaints dismissed or unsubstantiated (Bedford Manor)
- Human rights complaints dismissed for "lack of timeliness" (Graveley pattern)
- Ombudsperson recommendations are non-binding
- BC Housing refers complaints back to the operator
- RTB cases take months; operators send lawyers; residents attend without representation

**Evidence:**
- RESIDENT_COMPLAINT_RETALIATION_REPORT.md:289-330: The complete complaint loop shows EVERY path leads to "NOTHING CHANGES"
- Graveley v. Vancouver Island Mental Health Society (2024 BCHRT 289): ALL complaints dismissed on technicalities — "lack of timeliness" and "insufficient evidence" (STAFF_RETALIATION_REPORT.md:83-100)
- Bedford Manor: 6 complaints filed → 2 substantiated (33%) → 4 dismissed → NO structural change (RESIDENT_COMPLAINT_RETALIATION_REPORT.md:86-114)

**The Gap:** The complaint system exists to ABSORB complaints, not to resolve them. Every path loops back to the same system that created the problem.

### LAYER 9: INSPECTION GAPS AND BACKLOGS
**Status:** CONFIRMED — INFERRED FROM STRUCTURE

BC Housing's inspection and oversight of operators has documented gaps:
- No regular fire safety inspections at some facilities (Winters Hotel: sprinklers OFF, extinguishers empty)
- No regular wellness check audits
- No surprise inspections
- No independent oversight body

**Evidence:**
- Winters Hotel fire (Atira): 2 deaths — sprinklers were OFF, fire extinguishers were EMPTY (EXECUTIVE_COMPENSATION_vs_DEATHS.md:54)
- This means EITHER inspections didn't occur OR inspection findings were ignored
- BC Housing's response to deaths is always AFTER the fact — "strengthened requirements" — not proactive inspection
- The CONNECTIVE case: 8+ inquest recommendations issued April 2024, ZERO implemented by December 2024, death occurred December 2024 (CONTRACT_TERMINATION_CASE_COMPLETE.md:36-50)

**The Gap:** Without regular, independent, unannounced inspections, operators can maintain unsafe conditions without detection. The Winters Hotel fire proves this: sprinklers OFF in a building housing vulnerable people.
