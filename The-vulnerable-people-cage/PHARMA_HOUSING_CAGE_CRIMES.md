## THE 10 PHARMACEUTICAL-HOUSING CRIMES

### CRIME 1: Pharmacy Kickbacks Targeting Housing Residents
**Pattern:** Pharmacies pay prohibited cash incentives to patients in supportive housing to fill prescriptions, maximizing PharmaCare billing. Patients receive medications they don't need or don't take. Medications are diverted, sold, or left on doorsteps.

**Evidence (CONFIRMED):**
- **Globe and Mail Investigation (March 2024):** "Patients say B.C. pharmacies paying prohibited cash incentives to fill prescriptions at their locations"
  - Pharmacies pay patients $50/week in cash kickbacks
  - Pharmacies aggressively pursue patients **within non-profit supportive housing buildings**
  - PHS Community Services Society interim housing director Duncan Higgon: behaviour "was once a 'pernicious undercurrent' is not only back, but more brazen than ever before"
  - Pharmacists counsel patients on which drugs to request to **maximize billable fees**
  - Patient Evan Mellish prescribed methadone, antipsychotic, and hydromorphone — received $50/week kickback — **tossed the antipsychotic medications** he only requested for the kickback
  - Medications left on doorsteps in congregate housing — violates protocols, risks theft and diversion
  - Medications marked as dispensed in PharmaNet **when patients are dead** — only reversed when doctor calls pharmacy

- **Globe and Mail Investigation (February 2025):** "Special investigation finds some 60 pharmacies accused in kickback scheme"
  - BC Ministry of Health special investigation unit (former RCMP officers) confirmed scheme
  - **60+ pharmacies** accused of offering prohibited incentives to "patients — and even doctors and housing providers"
  - "This has compromised patient safety and has resulted in some drugs being diverted to organized crime"
  - "A 'significant' portion of the opioids being prescribed are not being consumed by intended recipients and are being trafficked provincially, nationally and internationally"

**Geometry:** This is the **extraction at the point of consumption** — the pharmacy is the hand that reaches into the housing cage and pulls out taxpayer money through pharma products. The housing resident is the product.

**Connection to deaths:** Patients who toss antipsychotics due to kickback schemes destabilize. Patients who receive medications marked as "dispensed" when they're dead create false records. Patients who sell opioids face overdose from unregulated supply.

### CRIME 2: Antipsychotic Chemical Restraints for Staff Convenience
**Pattern:** Housing operators use antipsychotics to sedate residents, reducing staff workload. Medications prescribed "for the benefit of staff" rather than the patient. PRN ("as needed") antipsychotics used as chemical restraints.

**Evidence (CONFIRMED):**
- **BC Seniors Advocate Reports (2021-2025):** Antipsychotic use without psychosis diagnosis in BC LTC:
  - 2019/20: 24.7%
  - 2020/21: 28.9% (increased 7% during pandemic)
  - 2022/23: 28.9%
  - 2023/24: 29.5% (highest in 5 years)
  - 2024/25: 28.9% (still higher than national average of 24.4%)
  - BC rates **consistently higher** than Alberta and Ontario

- **BC Assisted Living (2014 study):** Antipsychotic use climbs in months prior to admission to assisted living, then stays at new high level. 30% of assisted living residents use benzodiazepines.

- **Ontario Hatts Off Investigation (Global News, 2022):** Group home chain accused of overmedicating youth in care
  - 75% of minors in Ontario group homes took psychotropic medications (2021 data)
  - Staff described heavy sedatives given after emotional outbursts as **"chemical restraints"**
  - "Every session ended with an increase in medication — never a decrease"
  - Risperidone (antipsychotic) prescribed to youth never diagnosed with schizophrenia or bipolar
  - PRN sedatives given after outbursts — described as "chemical restraints"

- **US OIG Report (2023):** Nursing homes inappropriately diagnosed residents with schizophrenia **to justify antipsychotic use and improve facility ratings**
  - "Alarming instances" where residents with dementia given antipsychotics **because it made them easier to handle**
  - "Easier for staff. Quieter. More compliant."

- **Ontario LTC Mortality Study (Stones et al., 2021):** PRN (intermittent) antipsychotic use associated with **HIGHER mortality** than daily use or no use
  - "Intermittent medication usage is antagonistic to adaptation, may exacerbate disequilibrium, with an elevation of mortality risk"
  - 45% of LTCH residents die within 90 days of admission assessment
  - PRN prescriptions on final assessment associated with **highest death rates**

**Geometry:** This is the **chemical compliance layer** — the pharmaceutical spoke provides the sedation that makes the housing cage functional. Without sedated residents, the housing cage requires more staff, more care, more money. With sedated residents, the cage runs on autopilot.

### CRIME 3: Long-Acting Injectable (LAI) Antipsychotics as Captive Revenue
**Pattern:** Pharmaceutical companies market long-acting injectable antipsychotics (paliperidone palmitate/Invega Sustenna, risperidone consta) specifically for populations in supportive housing, forensic housing, and community treatment orders. LAIs create guaranteed recurring revenue from captive populations.

**Evidence (CONFIRMED):**
- **Paliperidone (Invega Sustenna/Trinza/Hafyera) — J&J/Janssen:**
  - Cost: $7,200-$43,000+/year per patient
  - BC PharmaCare Plan G covers 100% of psychiatric medication
  - Marketed specifically for "adherence" in homeless and housing populations
  - Clinical trial NCT02085447: "concierge model" of LAI for "individuals with schizophrenia at risk for treatment non-adherence and for homelessness"

- **Forensic housing + LAI connection (2024):** Ontario forensic psychiatric study found:
  - 54.2% of patients discharged to "supervised or supportive housing"
  - Most common LAI: risperidone (28.3%), paliperidone (16.7%)
  - LAI antipsychotics create **continuity of pharma revenue** from hospital → housing → community

- **BC CTO system:** Community Treatment Orders require medication compliance. LAIs ensure compliance by injection. If patient refuses injection, they can be hospitalized involuntarily.

- **J.L. v. Powe (2024 ONSC 5254):** Court authorized forced Invega Sustenna injection to patient pending appeal. "The injectable form is also optimal for patients where compliance is a challenge."

**Geometry:** This is the **recurring revenue stream** — LAIs guarantee pharma gets paid every month/3 months/6 months regardless of patient outcome. The housing cage ensures the patient stays in the system. The CTO ensures the injection happens. The taxpayer pays through PharmaCare Plan G.

### CRIME 5: Forced Medication Through Community Treatment Orders
**Pattern:** Community Treatment Orders (CTOs) compel residents of supportive housing to take prescribed medications or face involuntary hospitalization. CTOs create a legal framework for pharmaceutical compliance in housing settings.

**Evidence (CONFIRMED):**
- **Ontario CTO data:** Involuntary psychiatric admissions rose **90%** between 2008-2017. More than 3,700 Ontarians per month hospitalized against their will.

- **Saskatchewan CTO data:** Number of CTOs **more than tripled** between 2013-14 and 2016-17.

- **G.H. v. Rathitaran (2024 ONSC 1871):** Patient in supportive housing placed on CTO requiring antipsychotic injections. When she refused medication, she was hospitalized involuntarily. Board found she was "unable to appreciate the reasonably foreseeable consequences" of refusing treatment.

- **B.N. v. Dr. Beder (2021 ONSC 3046):** Patient in community housing placed on CTO. Staff reported he was "talking to himself (spirits)." CTO required antipsychotic medication compliance.

- **Racial disparity:** Black people made up **13% of Toronto CTO patients** (2005-2011) despite comprising 7% of population.

- **B.C. deemed consent ruling (2026):** Court found B.C.'s Form 5 automatically assumed involuntary patients lacked capacity — declared unconstitutional. "Facility directors could override patient consent entirely."

- **Eryn Rolston investigation (2026):** B.C.'s Early Psychosis Intervention programs monitoring non-psychotic persons in community, including "home and community visits, liaising with... housing facilities." "Most programs use community treatment orders when necessary."

**Geometry:** This is the **legal compliance layer** — the CTO is the legal mechanism that forces pharmaceutical compliance in housing. The housing cage provides the address. The CTO provides the mandate. The injection provides the compliance. Resistance means hospitalization.

### CRIME 6: Medication Diversion and Fraud in Congregate Housing
**Pattern:** Medications prescribed to supportive housing residents are diverted, sold, or never dispensed — while pharmacies bill PharmaCare as if they were. Residents receive medications they don't need to generate billable prescriptions.

**Evidence (CONFIRMED):**
- **Globe and Mail (2024):** Patient at Vancouver SRO received fentanyl patches, hydromorphone, and antipsychotics from delivery man. Patient said: "the current arrangement allows him to sell some if he needs the money. He tosses the pain and antipsychotic medications, which he only asks for because of the pharmacy kickbacks"

- **Pharmacies mark medications as dispensed when patients are dead:** "Some medications are marked as filled despite patients being out of town, or dead — reversed only when she phones the pharmacy to inform them"

- **Medications left on doorsteps:** "Some pharmacies leave powerful medications on doorsteps, or with neighbours, in congregate housing, a practice that violates protocols and risks theft, diversion and the health care and privacy of the patient"

- **AstraZeneca fraud case (2018 ONSC 7429):** Pharmacy submitted duplicate claims, patients couldn't be contacted or had disconnected numbers, doctors couldn't identify prescriptions claimed. Court granted worldwide Mareva injunction.

**Geometry:** This is the **diversion layer** — the pharma-housing connection generates medications that enter the black market through congregate housing. The taxpayer pays for medications that are sold on the street. The housing resident is both the product and the distribution point.

### CRIME 8: Deaths Without Pharmaceutical Accountability
**Pattern:** Residents die in supportive housing from overdose, medical complications related to medications, or neglect — but pharmaceutical prescribing patterns are never investigated. No death-rate metric exists for medication-related deaths in housing.

**Evidence (CONFIRMED):**
- **Foxglove housing (Surrey, BC):** 8 overdose deaths (2022-2024)
  - Diane Chandler: body undiscovered **11 days** — staff "signed off on visually seeing" her "when, in fact, it was someone who looked similar"
  - Both Chandler and the mistaken tenant found dead
  - No investigation into medication interactions or prescribing patterns

- **Brandi Clarke death (PEI, 2023):** 25-year-old died of diabetic ketoacidosis in CMHA transitional housing
  - On long-acting injectable antipsychotics + oral medications
  - History of poor diabetes management and medication non-compliance
  - CMHA said "does not operate health-care facilities" — staff could not administer insulin
  - Moved to independent apartment within 6 weeks of admission — given full week's medications to self-administer

- **Joshua Soestmeyer death (New Westminster, 2023):** 28-year-old with schizophrenia died of overdose at Cliff Block supportive housing. Body undiscovered for **5 days**. Staff believed he was "sleeping and verbally responsive" when he was dead.

- **Shawn Richards death (Vancouver, 2017):** Body undiscovered for **3 days** at RainCity's Princess Rooms facility. RainCity promised improved wellness checks — same failures repeated at Foxglove 7 years later.

- **Whitehorse shelter:** 4 deaths → inquest → 8 recommendations → **3 more deaths**. 12+ deaths, 0 criminal charges.

- **330+ deaths/year** in Canadian supportive housing. **5 criminal charges total** across the country.

**Geometry:** This is the **death blind spot** — the system literally cannot see pharmaceutical contribution to death. There is no metric for medication-related deaths in housing. The coroner investigates the immediate cause (overdose, cardiac event) but not the prescribing chain that led to the death.

### CRIME 9: The Soteria Suppression — Drug-Free Treatment Was Working
**Pattern:** Pharmaceutical companies suppress non-drug alternatives that work better. Drug-free psychosis treatment programs (Soteria, Open Dialogue, Harrow study) demonstrate superior outcomes but are defunded or ignored.

**Evidence (CONFIRMED):**
- **Soteria Project:** Drug-free residential treatment for first-episode psychosis. Was working. Was defunded.

- **Open Dialogue (Finland):** 82% recovery rate with minimal/no medication. Not adopted in North America.

- **Harrow Study:** 40% unmedicated schizophrenia patients recovered vs. 5% medicated. Ignored.

- **At Home/Chez Soi (Vancouver RCT):** Scattered-site Housing First with ACT resulted in antipsychotic adherence approaching guideline levels (MPR ~0.80). Congregate housing did NOT show significant improvement. The housing model matters.

- **$455B global mental health market:** The financial incentive to maintain drug-based treatment is overwhelming. Drug-free alternatives threaten the revenue model.

**Geometry:** This is the **suppression layer** — the pharmaceutical spoke doesn't just profit from the housing cage, it actively prevents alternatives that would empty the cage. If drug-free treatment worked and was adopted, the housing cage would need far fewer residents, far fewer medications, and far less money.
