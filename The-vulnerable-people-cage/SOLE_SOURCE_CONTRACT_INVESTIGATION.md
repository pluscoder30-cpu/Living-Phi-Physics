## THE PATTERN

Operators use three mechanisms to secure sole-source contracts:

1. **Threat of Termination** — "Give us the contract or residents lose services"
2. **Emergency Justification** — "We need housing NOW, no time for competition"
3. **Rebrand Continuity** — "We're the same people, just a new name, so extend without competition"

**Result:** Taxpayers pay inflated prices. Operators grow. Residents die. Competition never happens.

## THE UNIVERSAL PATTERN: HOW OPERATORS GET CONTRACTS WITHOUT COMPETITION

### Mechanism 1: Emergency Justification
- **Who uses it:** Everyone (Toronto, NWT, Hamilton, Nova Scotia, Alberta)
- **How it works:** Government declares "emergency" → bypasses competitive process → contract awarded
- **Reality:** "Emergency" often manufactured or prolonged (Toronto hotel shelters continued for years)
- **Toronto:** $297M in sole-source contracts justified as "emergency needs"
- **Hamilton:** Mayor's directive created artificial deadline → sole-source to untested company
- **NWT:** COVID-19 "urgency" used for $473K in shelter catering contracts
- **Nova Scotia:** "Emergency" order for $4M Dexter Construction contract

### Mechanism 2: Threat of Termination / Abandonment
- **Who uses it:** Connective (Yukon), Atira (BC)
- **How it works:** Operator threatens to leave → government panics → sole-source contract awarded
- **Reality:** Operators know government can't quickly find replacement for vulnerable residents
- **Connective:** Demanded $22M sole-source 3-year contract; threatened early termination when resisted
- **Atira:** Bypassed channels, went "straight to senior BC Housing members for funding"
- **Key Insight:** Residents become hostages — operator uses vulnerability of residents as leverage

### Mechanism 3: Rebrand Continuity
- **Who uses it:** Connective (John Howard → Connective), ACSS (new entity from Quest Society)
- **How it works:** Organization rebrands or creates sister entity → claims continuity → contracts transfer
- **Reality:** Same people, new name, no competition
- **Connective:** Rebranded from John Howard Society Pacific in 2021; "existing contracts will not change"
- **ACSS:** Incorporated Jul 2024, same board as Quest Society, $12.9M in contracts by mid-2025
- **Key Insight:** Rebranding erases search history and prevents pattern recognition

### Mechanism 4: Political Connections
- **Who uses it:** Nordic Media (Alberta), Quest/ACSS (Nova Scotia)
- **How it works:** Former government official or political ally → sole-source contract
- **Reality:** Political loyalty rewarded with public contracts
- **Nordic Media:** Premier's former campaign manager received $72,500 sole-source contract
- **ACSS:** Executive director was FORMER government official from same department awarding contracts
- **Quest/ACSS Board:** Police employees, former Police Complaints Commissioner, political donors

### Mechanism 5: Single Supplier Claim
- **Who uses it:** Various
- **How it works:** Government claims "only one supplier available" → sole-source
- **Reality:** Competition always exists; government doesn't look
- **Toronto:** "No available suppliers" for security, catering, PR consulting — but other companies exist
- **Hamilton:** "Indigenous-certified company" claimed unique qualification — but other Indigenous companies existed
- **NWT:** "Only party available and capable" — but other parties existed
