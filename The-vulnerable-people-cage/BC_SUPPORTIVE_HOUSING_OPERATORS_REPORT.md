**Report Date:** September 12, 2026  
**Status:** Compiled from BC Housing directories, operator websites, news sources, and public records

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

British Columbia's supportive housing system is operated by **non-profit organizations** under contract with **BC Housing** (a provincial Crown corporation). The system serves approximately **10,000+ individuals** across **100+ facilities** in 22+ municipalities. This report maps **15 major operators** and their connections, incidents, and legal issues.

## 18. KEY FINDINGS

1. **Systemic undercounting of deaths:** No centralized tracking of deaths in supportive housing. BC Housing receives critical-incident reports but doesn't compile statistics. BC Coroners Service also doesn't track.

2. **Wellness check failures:** Multiple documented cases of bodies going undiscovered for days to weeks (3-11 days).

3. **Conflict of interest:** BC Housing CEO directed $90M+ to organization run by his wife over 6 years.

4. **Fire safety failures:** Winters Hotel inquest found no fire drills, no fire safety training, disabled sprinklers, empty extinguishers.

5. **Capacity concerns:** BCNPHA warns "capacity is thin" — few organizations can replace large operators like Atira.

6. **SRO concerns:** Jury recommended ending public funding of SROs in private buildings; BC Housing purchased some SROs but they remain problematic.

7. **Open drug use policy:** Low-barrier housing permits drug use, leading to overdose deaths. Families and opposition call for sober housing options.

8. **Regulatory gaps:** Residential Tenancy Act limits operators' ability to enter units for wellness checks, remove dangerous tenants, or control guest access.
