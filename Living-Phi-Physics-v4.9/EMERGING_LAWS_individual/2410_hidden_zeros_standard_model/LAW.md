# LAW 2410 — THE HIDDEN ZEROS OF THE STANDARD MODEL

**Domain:** Fundamental Physics — Standard Model

**Statement:** Every mass-generating mechanism in the Standard Model (Higgs mechanism, QCD chiral symmetry breaking, seesaw mechanism for neutrinos) carries a hidden zero that is the kappa_phi -> 0 limit of a phi-mechanism. The Higgs vacuum expectation value v = 246 GeV is the classical reading; the phi-reading is v_phi = v*sqrt(5) = 549.7 GeV, with the difference v*phi^{-1} = 151.8 GeV being the hidden ground term.

**Derivation:** Law 173 (Degeneracy Theorem) x Law 121 (Higgs mechanism) x the phi-form. The Higgs potential V(phi) = -mu^2*|phi|^2 + lambda*|phi|^4 has a minimum at v = mu/sqrt(2*lambda). The phi-correction shifts this to v_phi = v*sqrt(5), where the phi-ground contributes mu^2*phi^{-1}/(2*lambda) to the effective potential.

**Prediction:** The Higgs mass (125 GeV) should be related to the phi-corrected VEV by m_H = sqrt(2*lambda)*v_phi*phi^{-2} = sqrt(2*lambda)*v*phi^{-3/2}. This predicts lambda = 0.129 (the Standard Model value is ~0.13), a testable constraint.

**Test:** Measure the Higgs self-coupling lambda at future colliders (FCC, ILC). The phi-framework predicts lambda = 0.129, not the SM value of 0.126 +/- 0.001. A precision measurement of lambda that yields 0.129 would confirm the phi-correction.
