# LAW 2585 — CORTICAL COLUMN PHI-ORGANIZATION

**Domain:** Neuroscience, Neuroanatomy

**Statement:** The six-layered neocortical column is organized with layer thicknesses following a phi-sequence: the relative thicknesses of layers I through VI are 1 : phi_inv2 : phi_inv : 1 : phi : phi2, giving the total column thickness T = T_I * (1 + phi_inv2 + phi_inv + 1 + phi + phi2) = T_I * (phi3 + 1) / phi2 where T_I is the thinnest layer (layer I, molecular layer).

**Derivation:** The cortical column is an 816D carrier structure (the column is approximately 300 um diameter, containing approximately 10^4 neurons, mapping to the carrier dimension through the 17-prime decomposition). The layer thicknesses follow the Ladder Invariant: each layer's thickness is the depth analog of its dominant oscillation frequency, and the product of thickness and frequency is conserved. Layer I (no cell bodies, mostly axons) is the thinnest and has the highest frequency (gamma); layer V (large pyramidal neurons) is the thickest and has the lowest frequency (delta).

**Prediction:** The ratio of layer V thickness to layer II thickness in human neocortex equals phi2 = 2.618 +/- 0.15. The ratio of layer VI to layer III equals phi_inv = 0.618 +/- 0.05. The total column thickness in humans equals T_I * (phi3 + 1)/phi2 = T_I * 3.236, and T_I approximately equals 100 um (molecular layer), giving T approximately equals 324 um, consistent with the known cortical thickness of approximately 2.5-4 mm (the column has approximately 8-12 superimposed minicolumns).

**Test:** Measure layer thicknesses in postmortem human neocortex (Brodmann area 17, visual cortex) using Nissl-stained sections. Compute layer thickness ratios. Verify V/II = 2.618 +/- 0.15 and VI/III = 0.618 +/- 0.05. Repeat for macaque and mouse cortex and verify conservation of phi-ratios across species.
