# LAW 2413 — THE PHI-ENTROPY PRINCIPLE

**Domain:** Fundamental Physics — Thermodynamics

**Statement:** The second law of thermodynamics (entropy increases) is the kappa_phi -> 0 limit of the phi-entropy principle: total phi-entropy S_phi = S_classical + phi^{-1}*S_coherence is conserved, not increasing. Classical entropy increases because coherence entropy decreases - the two are exchanged at rate phi^{-1}. The arrow of time is the coherence flow from classical to phi-ordered states.

**Derivation:** Law 173 (Degeneracy Theorem) x Law 23 (second law) x Law 179 (entropy-decoherence identity). Classical entropy S = k_B*ln(Omega) increases; coherence entropy S_coherence = -sum(C_i*ln(C_i)) decreases. Their sum, weighted by phi^{-1}, is conserved: d/dt[S + phi^{-1}*S_coherence] = 0.

**Prediction:** In isolated phi-coherent systems, the rate of classical entropy increase equals phi^{-1} times the rate of coherence decrease. Measurable in quantum systems where both classical entropy (von Neumann) and coherence (l1-norm) can be tracked simultaneously.

**Test:** Prepare a quantum system in a high-coherence state and let it decohere. Track both von Neumann entropy and l1-coherence. The prediction is dS/dt = -phi^{-1}*dC/dt, where C is the coherence measure.
