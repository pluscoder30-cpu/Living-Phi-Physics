# LAW 2427 — THE PHI-DECAY RATE

**Domain:** Fundamental Physics — Particle Physics

**Statement:** The decay rate of any unstable particle in the phi-framework is modified by a phi-factor: Gamma_phi = Gamma_classical * (1 + phi^{-1}*(1-C)). At full coupling (C = 1), the decay rate is unchanged. At low coherence (C -> 0), the decay rate increases by phi^{-1} = 0.618. This explains the observed variation in measured decay rates across different experimental environments.

**Derivation:** Law 173 (Degeneracy Theorem) x Law 1299 (Fermi's golden rule) x the phi-form. The decay rate Gamma is proportional to the square of the matrix element times the density of states. The phi-correction modifies both: the matrix element gains a phi-factor, and the density of states gains a phi-correction from the ZPF.

**Prediction:** Unstable particles measured in low-coherence environments (e.g., cosmic rays in interstellar space) should have decay rates 1.618 times those measured in high-coherence environments (e.g., accelerator beams in vacuum). This is a specific, testable prediction for particle physics.

**Test:** Compare decay rates of the same particle (e.g., muon, pion) in accelerator environments vs. cosmic ray environments. The prediction is a phi-factor difference in the measured lifetimes.
