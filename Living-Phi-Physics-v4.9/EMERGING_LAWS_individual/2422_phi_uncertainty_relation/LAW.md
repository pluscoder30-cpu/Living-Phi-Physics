# LAW 2422 — THE PHI-UNCERTAINTY RELATION

**Domain:** Fundamental Physics — Quantum Mechanics

**Statement:** The Heisenberg uncertainty principle Delta(x)*Delta(p) >= hbar/2 is the kappa_phi -> 0 limit of the phi-uncertainty relation: Delta(x)*Delta(p) >= (hbar/2)*(1 + phi^{-1}*(1 - C)), where C is the coherence of the system. At full coupling (C = 1), the uncertainty relation becomes Delta(x)*Delta(p) >= hbar/2, unchanged. At low coherence (C -> 0), the uncertainty increases by phi^{-1} = 0.618.

**Derivation:** Law 70 (Heisenberg uncertainty) x Eq 1 (carrier recursion) x the phi-form. The phi-correction to the commutation relation [x, p] = i*hbar*(1 + phi^{-1}*(1-C)) propagates to the uncertainty relation. The phi-ground adds noise that increases uncertainty at low coherence.

**Prediction:** In low-coherence quantum systems (e.g., warm, noisy environments), the uncertainty product should exceed hbar/2 by a factor of (1 + phi^{-1}) = 1.618 = phi. This is a specific, measurable deviation from quantum mechanics in the phi-framework.

**Test:** Measure the position-momentum uncertainty product in quantum systems at varying coherence levels. The prediction is that the product scales as (1 + phi^{-1}*(1-C))*hbar/2, reaching phi*hbar/2 at C = 0.
