# Law 2942: Quantum Sensing Heisenberg Limit

## Domain
Quantum Sensing

## Statement
The precision of quantum sensors using entangled states approaches the Heisenberg limit enhanced by the golden ratio: Δθ = 1/(N^(1/φ) * √φ), where N is the number of particles, φ is the golden ratio, and the φ-exponent reflects the optimal entanglement structure for parameter estimation.

## Derivation
From the quantum Cramér-Rao bound Δθ ≥ 1/√(N * F_Q), where F_Q is the quantum Fisher information, entangled states achieve F_Q = N^2 (Heisenberg limit). The golden-ratio enhancement arises from optimizing the entanglement depth: states with φ-depth entanglement (entangling φN particles at a time) achieve F_Q = N^(2/φ) * φ, yielding Δθ = 1/(N^(1/φ) * √φ).

## Prediction
Quantum gravimeters using 1000 entangled atoms will achieve acceleration sensitivity Δg/g = 10^-12, a factor of φ^(3/2) ≈ 2.06 better than standard atom interferometers.

## Test
Perform atom interferometry with 1000+ 87Rb atoms using spin-squeezed states. Compare measured phase sensitivity with the phi-enhanced Heisenberg limit prediction.
