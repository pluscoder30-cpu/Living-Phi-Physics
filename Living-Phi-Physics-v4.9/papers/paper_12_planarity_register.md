## 2. The Actual Claims (P1)

### 2.1 The Historical Tradition

The flat-Earth tradition has deep roots: ancient Near East and Mesopotamian disc cosmography; Israelite cosmology (a disc floating on water with an arched firmament above); Homeric/Hesiodic disc cosmography; pre-Socratic flat-Earth philosophers (Thales, Anaximander, Anaximenes, Xenophanes); Chinese flat-square Earth cosmology (Kai Tian) [2]. The "myth of the flat Earth" — that medievals believed the Earth flat — is itself a 17th-century Protestant polemic, later popularized by Draper and White [2].

**Samuel Rowbotham (1816–1884)** founded the modern tradition. His 1838 Bedford Level experiment — watching a 3-ft flag sail 6 miles down the Old Bedford River canal through a telescope held 8 inches above water — claimed the flag stayed visible when, on a globe, it "should have been about 11 feet below" his line of sight [2]. His 1881 book *Zetetic Astronomy: Earth Not a Globe* (430 pp.) placed the Sun and Moon at ~3,000 miles above a flat disc, bounded by an ice wall. The "Zetetic method" (from Greek *zeteo*, "to search"): reject theory, accept only what direct experiment and the senses demonstrate; "to take nothing for granted" [2].

The modern movement: Samuel Shenton's International Flat Earth Research Society (1956), Charles K. Johnson's California chapter (~3,500 members), Daniel Shenton's web relaunch (2004/2009), and the internet-era resurgence (2015+) — YouTube virality, the 2017 Flat Earth International Conference, Eric Dubay's "200 Proofs Earth is Not a Spinning Ball" [2].
