### 4.1 Kaluza–Klein theory (1921–26) [VERIFIED as a theory; unconfirmed as reality]

Theodor Kaluza (1921) extended general relativity to five dimensions; Oskar Klein (1926) gave the 5th dimension a quantum interpretation as a tiny circle. The 5D vacuum Einstein equations produce — in four dimensions — the Einstein equations **plus** Maxwell's equations for electromagnetism, with electric charge identified with momentum along the 5th dimension (the "Kaluza miracle"). ### 4.2 Superstring theory — critical dimension D = 10 [VERIFIED as a framework; no experimental confirmation]

Superstring theory requires spacetime dimension **D = 10** for consistency (conformal-anomaly cancellation; the critical dimension discovered by J. H. Schwarz, 1972). The five consistent theories (Type I, IIA, IIB, heterotic E8$\times$E8 and SO(32)) are dual limits of the conjectural 11-dimensional M-theory. The extra six dimensions are compactified on a small scale (typically Calabi–Yau manifolds) or the world lives on a brane. The corpus's law 2312 documents the classical statement and its own phi-law over it. **[VERIFIED]** — the D = 10 requirement is established theory mathematics. The experimental register: *"No supersymmetric particles have been discovered"; "no report on suggesting large extra dimensions has been delivered from the LHC"* (Wikipedia, Superstring theory §Absence of physical evidence; LHC 2011, Tevatron 2006 exclusions). **[VERIFIED] no experimental confirmation to date.**

### 4.3 Supergravity — maximal D = 11 [VERIFIED as a framework; unconfirmed]

Supergravity is the gauge theory of local supersymmetry: the graviton (spin-2) acquires a spin-3/2 superpartner, the gravitino. The minimal 4D N=1 theory was built by Freedman, van Nieuwenhuizen & Ferrara (1976) and independently Deser & Zumino (1976); the maximal 11-dimensional theory — the largest dimension consistent with a single graviton, by Nahm's theorem — was built by Cremmer, Julia & Scherk (1978). The history records the "end of the SUGRA era": the compact manifolds containing the Standard Model were incompatible with supersymmetry, chiral fermions were hard to obtain, and the theories generically produced an unrealistically large cosmological constant. **[VERIFIED]** — real theory, documented history of its difficulty; no gravitino or superpartner observed.

### 4.5 Large extra dimensions / ADD model (1998) [VERIFIED as a theory; VERIFIED null]
