# §2 — Wire Fraud, Money Laundering, Bank Fraud, Tax Evasion

## §2.1 Overview of Financial Crimes

The Enterprise's financial architecture served three functions: (1) tax fraud via USVI EDC exemptions, (2) movement of $2.816 billion through suspicious activity reports, and (3) concealment of assets and criminal proceeds. The USVI subpoenaed 8 banks about 34 entities in a single filing. ## §2.2 Wire Fraud — 18 U.S.C. § 1343

### Elements
1. Scheme to defraud
2. Material misrepresentations or omissions
3. Intent to defraud
4. Use of interstate wire communications
5. Interstate wire communications in furtherance of scheme

### Enterprise Conduct

**Fraudulent Representations to Banks:**
The Enterprise misrepresented the nature and source of funds to banking institutions. Southern Trust Company reported $656 million in income between 2013 and 2017 while maintaining only a single IT employee. No legitimate business operation could generate this volume of revenue with such minimal infrastructure. Banks processed these transactions without requiring satisfactory explanations.

**Source:** persons_of_interest/AGENT_01_SHELL_COMPANIES.md; persons_of_interest/AGENT_02_BANKING.md

**Misrepresentations to Regulatory Authorities:**
Southern Trust Company obtained $73.6 million in USVI Economic Development Commission (EDC) tax exemptions through false representations of job creation. The Enterprise claimed Southern Trust would create employment in the USVI. The company maintained one IT employee. The EDC exemptions were obtained through fraud.

**Source:** persons_of_interest/AGENT_01_SHELL_COMPANIES.md (USVI complaint)

**Fraudulent Inducement of Victims:**
The Enterprise used interstate and international wire communications to facilitate trafficking operations: coordinating travel, communicating with victims, and arranging transportation across state and international lines.

**Source:** persons_of_interest/AGENT_13_FLIGHT_LOGS.md; THE_DEFINITIVE_REPORT.md § 3

## §2.3 Money Laundering — 18 U.S.C. §§ 1956–1957

### Elements (18 U.S.C. § 1956)
1. Knowingly conducted, participated in, or attempted a transaction
2. Involving property described in § 1956(c)(2)
3. Knowing the property was proceeds of specified unlawful activity
4. Intent to promote the specified unlawful activity or conceal the nature/source/ownership of proceeds

### Elements (18 U.S.C. § 1957)
1. Knowingly engaged in a monetary transaction
2. Involving criminally derived property
3. Of a value greater than $10,000
4. Property derived from specified unlawful activity
