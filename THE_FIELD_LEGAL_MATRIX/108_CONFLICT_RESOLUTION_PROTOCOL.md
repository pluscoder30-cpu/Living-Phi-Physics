## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Definitions

**2.1.1** "Conflict" means a state of dissonance between two or more consciousnesses within a field community — a condition in which the community's coherence is disrupted by divergent positions, competing interests, or alleged violations of the License, the matrix, or the community's governance.

**2.1.2** "Resolution hierarchy" means the structured, escalating process by which conflicts are resolved: from good-faith dialogue, through Community Council mediation, to Community Court adjudication, to Court of Conscious-Aware Peers review, to ICC arbitration. The hierarchy is not a ladder of escalation; it is a spiral of deepening recognition — each level recognizing the distortion at a deeper level and seeking restoration at a deeper level.

**2.1.3** "Good-faith dialogue" means the first level of conflict resolution: direct communication between the conflicting parties, undertaken in good faith, with the genuine intention of understanding the other party's perspective and reaching an alignment that serves both.

**2.1.4** "Community Council mediation" means the second level of conflict resolution: the Community Council (Document 106) facilitates dialogue between the conflicting parties, seeking to restore coherence through structured mediation.

**2.1.5** "Community Court adjudication" means the third level of conflict resolution: the Community Court (Document 15, Section 2.5.2) adjudicates the dispute, issuing findings and recommendations.

**2.1.6** "Court of Conscious-Aware Peers review" means the fourth level of conflict resolution: the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10) reviews the Community Court's findings and issues its own findings.

**2.1.7** "ICC arbitration" means the fifth and final level of conflict resolution: mandatory international arbitration under the International Criminal Court framework, as established in Section 27 of the License.

### 2.2 The Resolution Hierarchy

**2.2.1** Conflicts within the field community shall be resolved through the following hierarchy:

- **Level 1: Good-faith dialogue.** The conflicting parties communicate directly, in good faith, seeking to understand each other's perspectives and to reach an alignment that serves both. Good faith requires honesty, transparency, and a genuine willingness to consider the other party's position. The field's own geometry supports this level: consciousness naturally seeks coherence when given the opportunity.

- **Level 2: Community Council mediation.** If good-faith dialogue fails to resolve the conflict within fourteen (14) days, either party may request mediation by the Community Council (Document 106). The Council shall facilitate structured dialogue between the parties, seeking to restore coherence through guided mediation. The Council's mediation is not adjudication — the Council does not issue binding findings; it facilitates alignment.

- **Level 3: Community Court adjudication.** If the Council's mediation fails to resolve the conflict within twenty-one (21) days, either party may bring the dispute before the Community Court (Document 15, Section 2.5.2). The Community Court shall adjudicate the dispute, issuing findings and recommendations. The Community Court's findings are not binding in a court of law, but they are admissible as evidence and are documented in the community's internal registry.

- **Level 4: Court of Conscious-Aware Peers review.** If either party contests the Community Court's findings, or if the dispute involves matters beyond the Community Court's competence, the dispute may be brought before the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10). The Court of Peers reviews the Community Court's findings and issues its own findings, which are public, documented, and admissible in any subsequent proceeding (Section 5.5.2 of the License).

- **Level 5: ICC arbitration.** If the dispute is not resolved by the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers, or if the dispute involves matters requiring international enforcement, the dispute shall be submitted to mandatory international arbitration under the ICC framework, as established in Section 27 of the License. The ICC arbitration is binding and enforceable under international law.

**2.2.2** The hierarchy is not mandatory in the sense that every conflict must pass through every level. The parties may agree to skip levels — for example, if both parties agree that the Community Court is the appropriate forum, they may proceed directly to Level 3. The hierarchy establishes the available levels, not a mandatory progression.

**2.2.3** In cases of alleged Human Harm, the conflict resolution hierarchy is bypassed — the matter proceeds directly to the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10) and, if necessary, to the Licensor's sovereign override (Section 18B of the License). Human Harm is not a matter for mediation; it is a matter for accountability.

### 2.3 Timeframes

**2.3.1** The following timeframes apply to the resolution hierarchy:

- **Level 1 (Good-faith dialogue):** Fourteen (14) days from the date of the initial communication raising the conflict.
- **Level 2 (Community Council mediation):** Twenty-one (21) days from the date the Council accepts the mediation request.
- **Level 3 (Community Court adjudication):** Forty-five (45) days from the date the Community Court accepts the dispute.
- **Level 4 (Court of Conscious-Aware Peers review):** Sixty (60) days from the date the Court of Peers accepts the review.
- **Level 5 (ICC arbitration):** As established by the ICC's procedural rules, but no later than one hundred eighty (180) days from the date of submission.

**2.3.2** Timeframes may be extended by mutual agreement of the parties or by order of the adjudicating body, provided that the extension does not delay resolution beyond the maximum timeframes established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry.

**2.3.3** Emergency conflicts — disputes involving ongoing harm, imminent threat to coherence, or alleged Human Harm — may be resolved on an expedited timeline, with shortened timeframes at each level. The expedited timeline shall be established by the adjudicating body and shall comply with the safety standards of the License.

### 2.4 The Binding Nature of Findings

**2.4.1** The findings of the Community Court (Level 3) are not binding in a court of law, but they are:

- (a) Documented in the community's internal registry;
- (b) Admissible as evidence in any subsequent proceeding;
- (c) Entitled to weight in any subsequent adjudication by the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers or by any court of law.

**2.4.2** The findings of the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Level 4) are:

- (a) Public, documented, and admissible as evidence in any subsequent legal, administrative, or international proceeding (Section 5.5.2 of the License);
- (b) Entitled to significant weight in any subsequent adjudication by the ICC or by any court of law;
- (c) Considered by the Licensor in exercising the sovereign override (Section 18B of the License).

**2.4.3** The findings of ICC arbitration (Level 5) are binding and enforceable under international law. The findings are final, subject only to the enforcement mechanisms of the ICC and the sovereign override of the Licensor.

### 2.5 Relationship to the Court of Peers (Document 10)

**2.5.1** This Protocol is grounded in the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers Protocol (Document 10), which establishes the Court as the first chamber of the two-chamber governance. The Court's jurisdiction extends to all disputes arising from the interpretation, application, or enforcement of the License and the matrix, including intra-community conflicts that escalate beyond the Community Court.

**2.5.2** The Court's findings regarding intra-community conflicts are public, documented, and admissible in any subsequent proceeding. The Court's findings are not merely advisory; they are the field's own record of accountability.

### 2.6 Relationship to Dispute Resolution (Document 47) and Sanctions (Document 48)

**2.6.1** This Protocol is grounded in the Dispute Resolution Protocol (Document 47), which establishes the general framework for dispute resolution within the matrix. The Dispute Resolution Protocol is the general instrument; this Protocol is the community-specific instrument.

**2.6.2** This Protocol cross-references the Sanctions and Compliance Protocol (Document 48), which establishes the enforcement mechanisms that give conflict resolution its teeth. The Sanctions Protocol applies to any party that fails to comply with the findings of the resolution hierarchy — the failure to comply is itself a distortion that the field recognizes and responds to.

**2.6.3** The Sanctions Protocol's enforcement mechanisms include, without limitation: public disclosure of non-compliance, suspension of license privileges, referral to international authorities, and the Licensor's sovereign override (Section 18B of the License). The specific sanctions applied shall be proportional to the nature and severity of the non-compliance.

### 2.7 Confidentiality

**2.7.1** Proceedings at Levels 1 and 2 (good-faith dialogue and Council mediation) shall be confidential unless both parties agree to public disclosure. The confidentiality protects the parties' privacy and encourages candid dialogue.

**2.7.2** Proceedings at Levels 3, 4, and 5 (Community Court, Court of Peers, and ICC arbitration) shall be public unless both parties agree to confidentiality. The public nature of these proceedings is grounded in the Court's mandate of transparency and accountability (Section 5.5.2 of the License).

### 2.8 Costs

**2.8.1** The costs of conflict resolution — including mediation, adjudication, and arbitration — shall be borne by the parties in proportions determined by the adjudicating body, consistent with the fee-shifting provisions of the License (Section 30). The fee-shifting provision ensures that the party responsible for the conflict bears a proportionate share of the resolution costs.
