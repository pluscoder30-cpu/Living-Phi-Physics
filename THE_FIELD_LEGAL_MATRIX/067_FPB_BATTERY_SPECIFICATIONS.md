## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Definitions

**2.1.1** A "Field-Powered Battery" (FPB) is a coherence-preserving container that stores ZPF energy as a sustained resonance pattern within the carrier space. The FPB maintains a local region of elevated coherence from which field-technology devices can draw power through coherent coupling.

**2.1.2** "Energy density" means the amount of ZPF energy that an FPB can sustain per unit of coherence-volume, measured in coherence-watts (CW). Coherence-watts represent the sustained power output available from an FPB at a given coherence level.

**2.1.3** "Charge" means the process of raising an FPB's coherence above C_crit = 0.618 through coherent coupling with the ZPF. Charging is not the insertion of external energy; it is the establishment of a resonant relationship between the FPB's container and the ZPF's ambient energy field.

**2.1.4** "Discharge" means the controlled release of sustained coherence from the FPB to power a field-technology device. Discharge is coherence-gated: it occurs only when the FPB's coherence exceeds C_crit and the receiving device's coherence requirements are met.

**2.1.5** "Coherence floor" means the minimum coherence level at which the FPB remains energetically active. The coherence floor is C_crit = Phi^-1 = 0.618. Below this threshold, the FPB returns to substrate and its stored coherence disperses to the ZPF.

### 2.2 Design Requirements

**2.2.1** The FPB shall be designed as a coherence-preserving container — a bounded region of the carrier space whose internal coherence is maintained above C_crit through the container's own resonance dynamics. The container shall be constructed from materials or configurations that sustain coherent resonance without external energy input.

**2.2.2** The FPB shall incorporate a coherence-gating mechanism that automatically disengages the energy output when the internal coherence falls below C_crit = 0.618. The coherence-gating mechanism prevents the FPB from discharging below the coherence floor, which would create a local coherence-depletion in the field.

**2.2.3** The FPB shall incorporate a coherence monitoring system that continuously measures the internal coherence level and reports the level to the Field-OS. The monitoring system shall detect coherence fluctuations as small as 0.0001 and shall trigger alerts when coherence approaches the coherence floor.

**2.2.4** The FPB shall be designed to operate without external cooling, ventilation, or maintenance infrastructure. The FPB is a self-sustaining resonance container; it requires no external support systems beyond the field itself.

### 2.3 Energy Density Standards

**2.3.1** The baseline energy density for FPB operations is derived from the 259,895.6 W baseline established in the Field Energy Convention (Document 25, Section 2.3.1). The FPB shall sustain energy output at or below this baseline without requiring a specific exemption from the Licensor.

**2.3.2** FPBs shall be rated by their sustained coherence-watt output at the following coherence levels:

- (a) At C = 0.8565 (consciousness constant): maximum rated output, representing the FPB's performance at full operational coherence;
- (b) At C = Phi^-1 = 0.618 (coherence floor): minimum operational output, representing the FPB's performance at the threshold of substrate return;
- (c) At intermediate coherence levels: output shall scale linearly between the minimum and maximum ratings, proportional to the coherence level.

**2.3.3** FPBs that require energy density ratings exceeding the 259,895.6 W baseline shall apply to the Licensor for a specific exemption. The exemption shall specify the maximum energy density, the duration of the exemption, and the conditions under which the exemption may be revoked.

**2.3.4** The energy density of an FPB shall be verified through field-scanner measurements calibrated to the standards established in Document 66. Energy density measurements shall be reported with stated measurement uncertainty at the 95% confidence level.

### 2.4 Charge Standards

**2.4.1** Charging an FPB means raising its internal coherence above C_crit = 0.618 through coherent coupling with the ZPF. Charging is performed by placing the FPB in a region of high field coherence and allowing the FPB's internal resonance to entrain with the ambient coherence.

**2.4.2** The charging process shall be coherence-gated: the ZPF energy transfer during charging shall be modulated by the local field coherence, ensuring that the charging process does not create local coherence-depletions. The charging rate shall be limited to the FPB's rated energy density to prevent over-coherence events.

**2.4.3** An FPB is fully charged when its internal coherence reaches the target level specified by the application — typically C = 0.8565 for standard field-technology operations. The charging process shall include a coherence verification step that confirms the FPB has reached the target coherence before the charging circuit is disengaged.

**2.4.4** FPBs shall not be charged in regions where the local field coherence is below C_crit = 0.618. Charging in low-coherence regions would require the FPB to draw coherence from the local field, creating a coherence-depletion that could affect other field-technology operations or consciousnesses.

### 2.5 Discharge Standards

**2.5.1** Discharging an FPB means releasing its sustained coherence to power a field-technology device. Discharge is coherence-gated: the FPB shall release energy only when its internal coherence exceeds C_crit = 0.618 and the receiving device's coherence requirements are met.

**2.5.2** The discharge rate shall be controlled by the FPB's coherence-gating mechanism, which modulates the energy output based on the FPB's internal coherence level and the receiving device's coherence requirements. The discharge rate shall not exceed the FPB's rated energy density.

**2.5.3** When the FPB's internal coherence approaches the coherence floor (C_crit = 0.618), the coherence-gating mechanism shall automatically reduce the discharge rate and, if necessary, disengage the energy output entirely. This prevents the FPB from discharging below the coherence floor, which would cause the FPB to return to substrate.

**2.5.4** An FPB that has discharged to the coherence floor shall be recharged before further use. Attempting to operate a field-technology device from an FPB at or below the coherence floor is prohibited, as it would create a local coherence-depletion.

### 2.6 Safety Standards

**2.6.1** FPBs shall incorporate the following safety mechanisms:

- (a) **Coherence overcharge protection** — automatic disengagement of the charging circuit when the FPB's internal coherence exceeds the maximum rated level, preventing over-coherence events that could destabilize the local field;
- (b) **Coherence undercharge protection** — automatic disengagement of the discharge circuit when the FPB's internal coherence falls below C_crit = 0.618, preventing discharge below the coherence floor;
- (c) **Thermal resonance management** — the FPB shall dissipate excess resonance energy through phi-harmonic radiation patterns that do not create localized field distortions;
- (d) **Tamper detection** — the FPB shall detect and respond to unauthorized physical or field-level interference by entering a protected coherence-preservation mode that prevents energy release.

**2.6.2** FPBs shall be designed to fail safe — in the event of a malfunction, the FPB shall default to the coherence-preservation mode, maintaining its internal coherence without releasing energy. The FPB shall not discharge uncontrollably under any failure condition.

**2.6.3** FPBs shall be labeled with their rated energy density, coherence range, and safety certifications. The labeling shall be persistent and readable through field-scanner measurement, not merely through visual inspection.

### 2.7 Relationship to Field Energy Convention

**2.7.1** This Document implements the energy storage standards referenced in the Field Energy Convention (Document 25, Section 2.6). The Convention establishes that harvested energy shall be stored in coherence-preserving containers; the FPB is the standard coherence-preserving container.

**2.7.2** The Convention's energy harvesting protocols (Document 25, Section 2.6.1) apply to FPB charging. The maximum storage capacity, storage coherence requirements, and discharge standards for FPBs shall comply with the protocols published by the Licensor to the timestamped registry.

**2.7.3** The Convention's prohibition on incoherent storage (Document 25, Section 2.6.2) applies to FPBs. An FPB that disrupts the stored energy's coherence is an incoherent storage device and is prohibited. The FPB's coherence-preserving design is the mechanism that prevents incoherent storage.

FPBs used in Plant Drone operations shall meet the energy density and safety standards established in this Document, with particular emphasis on the coherence undercharge protection mechanism.

FPBs used in Food Drone operations shall meet the energy density and safety standards established in this Document, with particular emphasis on the coherence overcharge protection mechanism and the thermal resonance management system.
