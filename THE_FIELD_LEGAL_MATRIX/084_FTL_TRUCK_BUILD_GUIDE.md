**2.1.1** An "FTL truck" is a field-native cargo vehicle capable of faster-than-light transport through coherence tunneling. The FTL truck traverses the carrier space (816D) by tunneling through coherence connections between points, achieving effective velocity exceeding the speed of light while maintaining cargo coherence integrity throughout the transit.

**2.1.2** "Coherence tunneling" means the mechanism by which the FTL truck traverses the carrier space without traversing the intervening physical space. Coherence tunneling is governed by Law 083 (non-locality) and the FTL navigation mechanics (Eq 56): the truck establishes a coherence connection between its origin and destination, verifies that the connection's coherence exceeds C_crit = 0.618, and tunnels through the connection by aligning its carrier-stream with the destination's resonance profile.

The cargo chamber maintains coherence isolation between the cargo and the tunneling event's resonance dynamics, ensuring that the cargo's molecular structure and coherence state are preserved throughout the transit. **2.1.4** "Cargo integrity verification" means the process of confirming that the cargo's coherence state and molecular structure have been preserved after a tunneling event. Cargo integrity verification shall be performed before the cargo chamber is opened and the cargo is delivered.

**2.1.5** "Reinforced phi-harmonic composite" means a construction material whose internal structure is configured in golden-ratio proportions and whose composition is reinforced for the heavier loads and structural demands of cargo transport.

### 2.2 Component Specifications

**2.2.1 Chassis.** The FTL truck chassis shall be constructed from reinforced phi-harmonic composite — a material whose internal structure is configured in ratios consistent with Φ = 1.618033988749895 and whose composition is reinforced for heavy cargo loads. The chassis shall include:

- **(b) Cargo bay.** A phi-harmonic enclosure within the frame designed to carry cargo. The cargo bay shall incorporate one or more coherence-preserved cargo chambers, each maintaining coherence isolation between its contents and the tunneling event's resonance dynamics. The cargo bay shall be accessible through field-sealed doors that do not disrupt the vehicle's coherence during loading and unloading.

- **(c) Entanglement-mesh housing.** An enclosed, phi-harmonic compartment within the frame that houses the entanglement-mesh transceiver and the FTL navigation processor. The housing shall shield the entanglement-mesh system from external interference and maintain coherence isolation during tunneling events.

**2.2.2 Power System.** The FTL truck shall be powered by a high-output Field-Powered Battery (FPB, Document 67) rated for both standard hover operation and FTL tunneling energy requirements. The FTL truck's FPB shall be rated at a higher capacity than the FTL car's FPB, reflecting the additional energy required to maintain cargo-chamber coherence during tunneling with heavy loads. The FPB shall be mounted within the frame in a phi-harmonic mounting bracket that isolates its resonance from the cargo chamber and the entanglement-mesh system.

- **(a) Carrier-resonance mapper.** A field-scanner instrument that maps the resonance profiles of destination points in the carrier space, enabling the truck to identify destinations and calculate tunneling trajectories. - **(b) Tunneling planner.** A Field-OS process (Document 64) that calculates optimal tunneling trajectories through the carrier space, identifying the coherence connections with the highest coherence levels and the lowest energy costs. The planner shall account for real-time coherence conditions, destination occupancy, cargo weight and coherence requirements, and the FTL navigation protocol (Document 56).

- **(c) Entanglement-mesh transceiver.** A field-technology device that establishes, verifies, and maintains coherence connections with destination points. The transceiver shall comply with the non-locality standards established in Law 083 and shall maintain entanglement links with the field internet for destination coordination.

**2.2.4 Safety Systems.** The FTL truck shall incorporate the following safety systems:

- **(a) Coherence gate.** The coherence gate shall monitor the vehicle's internal coherence continuously. The gate shall disengage FTL coupling and initiate emergency return whenever coherence falls below C_crit = 0.618. The coherence gate shall comply with the standards established in the Vehicle Convention (Document 22, Section 2.2.1).

- **(b) Coherence verification.** The coherence verification system shall confirm that the coherence connection between origin and destination exceeds C_crit = 0.618 before initiating a tunneling event. The verification shall be continuous during the tunneling event; if the connection coherence drops below C_crit during transit, the system shall initiate emergency return to the origin point.

- **(c) Emergency return.** The emergency return system shall restore the truck to its origin point within two (2) seconds of activation. The emergency return shall be triggered automatically when: (i) the truck's coherence falls below C_crit; (ii) the coherence connection coherence drops below C_crit during transit; (iii) the operator activates the emergency return control; or (iv) the fleet coordinator or Licensor issues a return command. The emergency return shall comply with the Emergency Shutdown Protocol (Document 63).

- **(d) Portal Safety.** The Portal Safety system shall verify portal integrity before, during, and after each tunneling event, in compliance with the Portal Safety Protocol (Document 55). The system shall verify that the coherence corridor is stable, that the destination point is unoccupied, and that the tunneling event will not create coherence-wake effects that harm other consciousnesses or cargo.

- **(e) Cargo integrity verification.** The cargo integrity verification system shall measure the cargo's coherence state and molecular structure after each tunneling event, confirming that the cargo has been preserved. The system shall compare post-transit measurements against pre-transit baseline measurements and shall flag any cargo that fails verification for inspection before delivery.

### 2.3 Construction Steps

**2.3.1** The FTL truck shall be constructed in the following sequence:

- **(Step 1) Chassis fabrication.** Fabricate the reinforced phi-harmonic lattice chassis from reinforced phi-harmonic composite. Verify that the lattice node positions conform to golden-ratio spacing using field-scanner measurement calibrated to the standards in Document 66.

- **(Step 2) Cargo chamber installation.** Install the coherence-preserved cargo chamber(s) within the cargo bay. Verify that each chamber maintains coherence isolation above C_crit = 0.618 at all surface points. Test the chamber's coherence isolation under simulated tunneling resonance conditions.

- **(Step 3) Entanglement-mesh housing installation.** Install the entanglement-mesh housing at the chassis center, isolated from the cargo bay and the rider compartment. Verify that the housing maintains coherence isolation from the surrounding frame.

- **(Step 4) Power system integration.** Install the high-output FPB in the phi-harmonic mounting bracket. Connect the FPB to the FTL drive system, navigation system, safety systems, and cargo-chamber coherence-maintenance systems through coherence-gated power couplings. Verify that the FPB can deliver the tunneling burst without dropping below C_crit, including under maximum cargo load.

- **(Step 5) Navigation system installation.** Install the carrier-resonance mapper, tunneling planner, and entanglement-mesh transceiver. Calibrate all field-scanner instruments to the standards in Document 66. Verify that the navigation system can map destination resonance profiles and calculate tunneling trajectories under varying cargo loads.

- **(Step 6) Safety system installation.** Install the coherence gate, coherence verification system, emergency return system, Portal Safety system, and cargo integrity verification system. Verify that each safety system activates at the correct thresholds and within the specified response times.

- **(Step 7) System integration and testing.** Integrate all systems — chassis, cargo chamber, FTL drive, FPB, navigation, safety — and conduct a full-system coherence test. The test shall include a controlled tunneling event with a test cargo load, followed by cargo integrity verification. Upon certification, register the vehicle in the timestamped registry with its specifications, safety certifications, cargo capacity, and operator authorization. ### 2.5 Maintenance and Inspection

**2.5.1** The FTL truck shall be inspected at intervals not exceeding thirty (30) days. The inspection shall include:

- (a) Verification of chassis structural integrity and phi-harmonic alignment under cargo load;
- (b) Verification of cargo chamber coherence isolation integrity;
- (c) Verification of entanglement-mesh transceiver coherence and calibration;
- (d) Verification of FPB charge level, coherence range, and burst capacity under maximum cargo load;
- (e) Verification of navigation system calibration and carrier-resonance mapping accuracy;
- (f) Verification of all safety system thresholds and response times, including Portal Safety verification, emergency return timing, and cargo integrity verification accuracy;
- (g) A full-system coherence test, including a controlled tunneling event with a test cargo load and verified cargo integrity.

**2.5.2** Inspection results shall be logged and published to the timestamped registry. The truck shall not be operated if any inspection item fails to meet the standards established in this Document or the Vehicle Convention (Document 22).

**2.6.1** FTL trucks produce no exhaust emissions, no thermal pollution, and no acoustic pollution beyond the level inherent in the vehicle's coherent resonance with the field. The environmental impact is limited to coherence-wake effects during tunneling events and energy draw from the ZPF.
