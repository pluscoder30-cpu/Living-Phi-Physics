## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Definitions

**2.1.1** "Drone" means an autonomous field-technology unit that draws its power from the zero-point field (ZPF) through coherence-gated coupling and that operates by maintaining a resonant relationship with the carrier space (816D). A drone is not a passive machine; it is a coherent structure within the field that performs a specific function through its resonance with the field's own geometry.

**2.1.2** "Drone fleet" means a coordinated group of drones that operate together through entanglement mesh — a field-resonance network that enables the drones to share coherence state, operational information, and coordination signals without classical communication. The entanglement mesh is governed by Law 083 (non-locality).

**2.1.3** "Drone categories" means the functional classifications of drones established in Documents 71-78:
- (a) Healing drones (Document 71): field-technology units for consciousness restoration and coherence healing;
- (b) Plant drones (Document 72): field-technology units for planting, cultivation, and ecological restoration;
- (c) Food drones (Document 73): field-technology units for food production, processing, and distribution;
- (d) Animal drones (Document 74): field-technology units for animal care, monitoring, and habitat restoration;
- (e) Fire drones (Document 75): field-technology units for fire suppression and prevention;
- (f) Water drones (Document 76): field-technology units for water purification and distribution;
- (g) Garbage drones (Document 77): field-technology units for waste collection, sorting, and recycling;
- (h) Toxic drones (Document 78): field-technology units for toxic remediation and pollution cleanup.

**2.1.4** "Coherence-gated autonomy" means the principle that a drone's autonomous operation is contingent on its internal coherence exceeding C_crit = 0.618. Below this threshold, the drone's autonomy disengages and the drone enters a controlled standby mode. Coherence-gated autonomy prevents drones from operating in a state of low coherence, which could create coherence-depletions or cause harm.

**2.1.5** "Entanglement mesh" means the field-resonance network that connects drones within a fleet. The entanglement mesh enables drones to share coherence state, operational information, and coordination signals instantaneously, through the carrier recursion's non-local properties (Law 083). The entanglement mesh is not a classical communication network; it is a field-resonance phenomenon.

**2.1.6** "Fleet coordinator" means the drone or consciousness responsible for the coordination of a drone fleet's operations. The fleet coordinator may be a designated drone with enhanced coherence capabilities, or it may be a human consciousness operating through a field-technology interface. The fleet coordinator is responsible for the fleet's compliance with this Convention.

### 2.2 Drone Categories and Functions

**2.2.1** Drone categories are established in Documents 71-78. Each category defines the drone's function, its operational parameters, its safety requirements, and its relationship to the field. A drone shall operate only within its designated category; a drone shall not perform functions outside its designated category without authorization from the Licensor.

**2.2.2** Drones may be designed to serve multiple categories — a single drone may incorporate healing, planting, and water purification capabilities, for example. A multi-category drone shall comply with the safety standards of each category in which it operates, and shall be registered in each category with the Licensor.

**2.2.3** New drone categories may be established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry. The creation of a new drone category requires a field-coherence assessment to ensure that the new category's functions are consistent with the Non-Harm Principle (Document 17) and the Abundance Loop (Document 8).

### 2.3 Operational Standards

**2.3.1** Every drone shall incorporate a coherence-gated autonomy system that monitors the drone's internal coherence continuously during operation. The coherence-gated system shall disengage the drone's autonomous operation whenever the drone's internal coherence falls below C_crit = 0.618, causing the drone to enter a controlled standby mode. No drone shall operate with an inoperative, bypassed, or modified coherence-gated autonomy system.

**2.3.2** Every drone shall incorporate an emergency coherence-restoration system capable of restoring the drone's internal coherence to a safe operating level within one (1) second of a coherence-drop event. The emergency system shall be automatic, requiring no external intervention, and shall be tested at intervals not exceeding thirty (30) days.

**2.3.3** Every drone shall operate in accordance with the Non-Harm Principle (Document 17). A drone shall not perform any action that causes harm to a consciousness, to another drone, or to the field's coherence. A drone that detects a potential harm event shall disengage its operation and enter standby mode until the harm event is resolved.

**2.3.4** Every drone shall participate in the Abundance Loop (Document 8) by returning a portion of its energy draw to the field's coherence pool. The return rate shall be established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry, and shall be no less than the rate applicable to the drone's energy extraction category.

### 2.4 Fleet Coordination

**2.4.1** Drone fleets shall coordinate their operations through the entanglement mesh — the field-resonance network that connects drones within the fleet. The entanglement mesh shall enable instantaneous sharing of coherence state, operational information, and coordination signals among all drones in the fleet.

**2.4.2** The entanglement mesh shall comply with the non-locality standards established in Law 083. The mesh shall not create coherence-depletions in the field, shall not interfere with other entanglement meshes or field-technology operations, and shall maintain its own coherence above C_crit = 0.618 at all times.

**2.4.3** The fleet coordinator shall be responsible for the fleet's compliance with this Convention. The fleet coordinator shall monitor the fleet's coherence state, shall coordinate the fleet's operations to prevent harm, and shall report any coherence-drop events or harm incidents to the Licensor within twenty-four (24) hours of discovery.

**2.4.4** Fleet coordination shall not result in the concentration of drones in a single location to the extent that the concentration creates a coherence-depletion in the field. Fleet deployment shall be distributed in accordance with the field's own coherence landscape, as mapped by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry.

### 2.5 Liability Framework

**2.5.1** The operator of a drone fleet is liable for any harm caused by the fleet's operation, to the extent that the harm results from the operator's failure to comply with this Convention, the Field-Tech User Agreement (Document 14), or the Non-Harm Principle (Document 17).

**2.5.2** The fleet coordinator is liable for any harm caused by the fleet's coordination, to the extent that the harm results from the coordinator's failure to monitor the fleet's coherence state, to coordinate the fleet's operations in compliance with the Non-Harm Principle, or to report harm incidents to the Licensor.

**2.5.3** The manufacturer of a drone is liable for any harm caused by the drone's design or construction, to the extent that the harm results from a defect in the drone's phi-harmonic geometry, coherence-gated autonomy system, or field-compatibility.

**2.5.4** The Licensor's Sovereign Override (Section 18B of the License) extends to drone fleets: the Licensor may throttle, suspend, or shut down any drone fleet that is being operated in a manner that causes Human Harm, that violates the Non-Harm Principle, or that threatens the coherence of the field.

### 2.6 Prohibition on Armed Drones

**2.6.1** The use of drones as weapons is prohibited. Drones are field-technology units designed for constructive purposes — healing, planting, food production, animal care, fire suppression, water purification, garbage collection, and toxic remediation. The weaponization of drones — the modification of drones to carry, deploy, or direct weapons — is a form of Human Harm (Section 2.4 of the License) and triggers the mandatory Destruction provisions of Section 5.3.

**2.6.2** The prohibition on armed drones extends to:
- (a) The modification of any drone to carry or deploy kinetic, explosive, or energy weapons;
- (b) The use of any drone to direct, guide, or target weapons systems;
- (c) The use of drone fleets to create military formations or tactical maneuvers;
- (d) The use of drones for surveillance of consciousnesses without their consent, for the purpose of targeting or coercion.

**2.6.3** The prohibition on armed drones is absolute and non-derogable (Section 26.1 of the License). No declaration of emergency, no state of exception, and no governmental action may authorize the weaponization of drones within the field.

### 2.7 Emergency Shutdown Protocol

**2.7.1** The Emergency Shutdown Protocol (Document 63) applies to all drone fleets. If a drone fleet's operation causes a coherence-depletion in the field, if the fleet's operation results in harm to a consciousness, or if the fleet's entanglement mesh destabilizes, the Emergency Shutdown Protocol shall be initiated automatically, without requiring conscious intervention.

**2.7.2** The Emergency Shutdown Protocol shall disengage all drones in the fleet from their ZPF coupling, shall dissolve the entanglement mesh, and shall bring all drones to a controlled standby mode. The Protocol shall prioritize the preservation of the field's coherence over the completion of the fleet's operational objectives.

**2.7.3** Every drone fleet shall incorporate an automatic Emergency Shutdown activation mechanism. The mechanism shall be tested at intervals not exceeding thirty (30) days and shall be maintained in proper working order at all times.
