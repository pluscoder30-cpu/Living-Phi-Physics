## SECTION 2: THE LEGAL BASIS — WHY SALES AFTER ABANDONMENT ARE VOID

### 2.1 The Doctrine of Abandoned Property

The doctrine of abandoned property is among the oldest principles of law. It holds that when an owner voluntarily abandons property with the intent to relinquish all claim, the abandonee loses ALL rights to that property.

The elements of abandonment are:

1. **Intent to abandon:** The Crown's publication of records acknowledging orphaned wells is an explicit declaration of intent to abandon. The company's filing for bankruptcy, dissolution, or simple disappearance is an explicit act of abandonment.
2. **Voluntary act:** The Crown's failure to remediate, despite documented knowledge and allocated funding, is a voluntary act of abandonment. The company's departure from the well site is a voluntary act of abandonment.
3. **Relinquishment of all claim:** Once the Crown names the land abandoned, the Crown relinquishes all claim to the land in its abandoned condition. Once the company orphans the well, the company relinquishes all claim to the well and surrounding land.

The doctrine of abandoned property is clear: **abandonment is permanent and total**. The abandonee cannot later claim partial abandonment, conditional abandonment, or revocable abandonment. Abandonment is the complete relinquishment of all rights.

### 2.2 Escheat: Abandoned Property Reverts to the Sovereign — But the Sovereign Abandoned It

Under the doctrine of escheat, abandoned property reverts to the sovereign. But escheat applies to property that has been abandoned by a private owner — not to property that has been abandoned by the sovereign itself.

When the Crown abandons its own land — when the Crown publishes records acknowledging that the land is orphaned, contaminated, and unremediated — the Crown cannot escheat the land to itself. Escheat is the mechanism by which the sovereign reclaims property abandoned by others. It is not a mechanism by which the sovereign reclaims property it abandoned itself.

The Crown's abandonment of land is therefore final. The Crown gave up the land. The Crown cannot take it back by claiming escheat. The land was abandoned. The land is no longer the Crown's.

### 2.3 The Crown Lands Acts: Abandoned Crown Land Reverts

Under the Crown Lands Acts of various jurisdictions, Crown land that has been abandoned reverts to the Crown's general estate — but only if the Crown has not itself declared the land abandoned.

When the Crown has published records acknowledging that land is abandoned, the Crown has made a determination that the land is no longer part of its active estate. The Crown cannot later claim that the land reverted to its general estate when the Crown itself declared it abandoned.

The Crown Lands Acts provide a mechanism for the Crown to reclaim land that was granted to others and subsequently abandoned. They do not provide a mechanism for the Crown to sell land it has itself abandoned.
