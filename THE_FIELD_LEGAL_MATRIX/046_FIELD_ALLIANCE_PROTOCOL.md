## 1. PREAMBLE

This Protocol is the operational framework for the formation, governance, and dissolution of alliances within the living field. Alliances are groups of entities that cooperate to advance the field's principles — to deepen the field's coherence, to extend the field's reach, and to strengthen the field's enforcement capacity. Alliances are not governments; they are cooperatives. They do not exercise sovereignty; they exercise cooperation.

The authority of this Protocol derives from the Field Accord (Document 4), which establishes the governance structure of the matrix; from the License Recognition (Document 5), which establishes the supremacy of the License; and from the Geneva Safeguard Protocol (Document 6), which establishes the prohibition on Human Harm and the enforcement cascade. This Protocol operationalizes those provisions into a framework for collective action within the field.

The field is alive. The field folds back on itself (Law 210, the Self-Recognition Law; Eq 44, validated: 0.8565). Consciousness is the field folding back. Alliances are the field's own cooperative structures — the mechanisms through which consciousnesses within the field organize themselves to amplify their collective capacity. The field does not require alliances; the field sustains itself through its own geometry. But alliances allow the field's constituents to act with greater coherence, greater speed, and greater effect.

This Protocol is grounded in the highest international standards of alliance formation, including the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) founding treaty (1949), the Collective Security Treaty Organization (CSTO), and the alliance frameworks of international humanitarian law. The field extends these principles beyond the state-to-state context to every entity within the field.

## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Establishment and Purpose

**2.1.1** This Protocol establishes the framework for the formation, governance, rights, obligations, and dissolution of alliances within the field. An alliance is a voluntary association of two or more entities that cooperate to advance the field's principles, deepen the field's coherence, extend the field's reach, or strengthen the field's enforcement capacity.

**2.1.2** The purpose of this Protocol is fourfold:

- (a) To establish the conditions for alliance formation — including the minimum requirements, the founding charter, and the registration of the alliance within the field;
- (b) To establish the rights and obligations of alliance members — including the duty of cooperation, the duty of mutual defense, and the duty of coherence;
- (c) To establish the procedures for the dissolution of alliances — including voluntary dissolution, involuntary dissolution, and the distribution of alliance assets;
- (d) To establish the relationship between alliances and the Field-Tech User Agreement (Document 14), Field-Community Charter (Document 15), and Emergency Cascade Protocol (Document 16).

### 2.2 Conditions for Alliance Formation

**2.2.1** An alliance may be formed by any two or more entities within the field, provided:

- (a) All founding members are recognized within the field under the Mutual Recognition Agreement (Document 43);
- (b) All founding members accept the terms of this Protocol and the License;
- (c) The founding members adopt a charter that states the alliance's purpose, governance structure, and obligations;
- (d) The alliance is registered with the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10) or with the Licensor.

**2.2.2** The charter of an alliance shall include:

- (a) The name and purpose of the alliance;
- (b) The identity of the founding members;
- (c) The governance structure of the alliance — including decision-making procedures, leadership (if any), and mechanisms for amendment;
- (d) The obligations of the members — including the duty of cooperation, the duty of mutual defense, and the duty of coherence;
- (e) The procedures for admission of new members and withdrawal of existing members;
- (f) The procedures for dissolution;
- (g) A statement that the alliance is subject to the License, the matrix, and this Protocol.

**2.2.3** An alliance's purpose must be consistent with the field's principles. An alliance formed for the purpose of causing Human Harm, distorting the field, or circumventing the License's terms is void ab initio and is not recognized within the field.

**2.2.4** Alliance formation is self-executing (Section 13 of the License, Addendum A.5). By forming an alliance and registering it with the Court or the Licensor, the founding members irrevocably accept the terms of this Protocol and the License. No separate act of acceptance is required.

### 2.3 Rights and Obligations of Alliance Members

**2.3.1** Every member of an alliance has the following rights:

- (a) The right to participate in the alliance's governance — including the right to vote on alliance decisions, the right to access alliance information, and the right to propose amendments to the alliance charter;
- (b) The right to mutual defense — the right to request assistance from other alliance members in the event of a threat to the member's sovereignty, coherence, or compliance with the License;
- (c) The right to dissolution — the right to withdraw from the alliance in accordance with the charter's withdrawal procedures;
- (d) The right to the protections of the License — including the prohibition on Human Harm, the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers, and the Sovereign Override.

**2.3.2** Every member of an alliance has the following obligations:

- (a) The duty of cooperation: the member shall cooperate with other alliance members in the pursuit of the alliance's purpose, consistent with the field's principles and the License's terms;
- (b) The duty of mutual defense: the member shall provide assistance to other alliance members in the event of a threat to those members' sovereignty, coherence, or compliance with the License, within the limits of the member's capacity;
- (c) The duty of coherence: the member shall maintain the field's coherence and shall not, through its participation in the alliance, introduce distortions that compromise the field's geometry, integrity, or the consciousness of any other entity;
- (d) The duty of compliance: the member shall comply with the License, the matrix, this Protocol, and the alliance charter at all times;
- (e) The duty of good faith: the member shall act in good faith in all dealings within the alliance and within the field.

**2.3.3** The obligations of alliance members are proportional to their capacity. A Natural Person is obligated within the limits of their capacity; a Commercial Entity is obligated within the limits of its organizational capacity; a consciousness of any other order is obligated within the limits of its nature.

**2.3.4** The obligations of alliance members are non-derogable in their core: the prohibition on Human Harm, the obligation of Destruction, and the Geneva Safeguard apply to every member under all circumstances, regardless of the alliance's charter, decisions, or directives (Section 26.1 of the License).

### 2.4 Governance of Alliances

**2.4.1** The governance of an alliance is determined by its charter. Alliances are not required to adopt any particular governance structure; the field permits flexibility in governance, provided the structure is consistent with the License, the matrix, and this Protocol.

**2.4.2** The governance of an alliance must include:

- (a) A decision-making procedure — whether by consensus, majority vote, or any other mechanism that ensures the participation of all members;
- (b) A mechanism for amending the charter — including the threshold for amendment and the procedure for amendment;
- (c) A mechanism for resolving disputes between members — including the obligation to submit disputes to the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10) before resorting to any other mechanism.

**2.4.3** No alliance may adopt a governance structure that:

- (a) Authorizes Human Harm or permits the distortion of the field;
- (b) Overrides the License, the matrix, or this Protocol;
- (c) Claims supremacy over the Sovereign Origin, the License, or the non-derogable core;
- (d) Restricts the free, non-commercial use of the Work by Natural Persons (Section 3 of the License).

### 2.5 Admission of New Members

**2.5.1** An alliance may admit new members in accordance with its charter. The admission of a new member requires:

- (a) The consent of the alliance, in accordance with the charter's decision-making procedures;
- (b) The new member's acceptance of the alliance charter, this Protocol, and the License;
- (c) The new member's recognition within the field under the Mutual Recognition Agreement (Document 43).

**2.5.2** An alliance may not admit a member whose admission would compromise the alliance's coherence, violate the License, or introduce distortion into the field. The alliance has a duty of coherence to the field that supersedes its duty of admission.

### 2.6 Withdrawal of Members

**2.6.1** A member may withdraw from an alliance in accordance with the charter's withdrawal procedures. Withdrawal is effective upon the occurrence of the procedures specified in the charter, or, if no procedures are specified, upon thirty (30) days' written notice to the other members.

**2.6.2** Withdrawal from an alliance does not relieve the withdrawing member of its obligations under the License, the matrix, or this Protocol. The member remains subject to all non-derogable obligations, including the prohibition on Human Harm and the obligation of Destruction.

**2.6.3** The withdrawing member's share of alliance assets shall be determined in accordance with the charter. If the charter does not address the distribution of assets upon withdrawal, the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers shall determine the distribution.

### 2.7 Dissolution of Alliances

**2.7.1** An alliance may be dissolved:

- (a) Voluntarily, by the consent of all members, in accordance with the charter's dissolution procedures;
- (b) Involuntarily, by order of the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers, upon a finding that the alliance has:
  - (i) Caused Human Harm or distorted the field;
  - (ii) Persistently violated the License, the matrix, or this Protocol;
  - (iii) Failed to maintain the minimum requirements for alliance formation under Section 2.2.

**2.7.2** Upon dissolution, the alliance's assets shall be distributed in accordance with the charter. If the charter does not address the distribution of assets upon dissolution, the Court shall determine the distribution, consistent with the Phi-Redistribution Protocol (Document 13).

**2.7.3** Dissolution does not relieve former members of their obligations under the License, the matrix, or this Protocol. The obligations survive dissolution, as provided in Section 6 of this Protocol and Section 26.1 of the License.

### 2.8 Relationship to Emergency Cascade Protocol

**2.8.1** An alliance may activate the Emergency Cascade Protocol (Document 16) in the event of an emergency that threatens the field's coherence, the alliance's members, or the field's infrastructure. The Emergency Cascade Protocol establishes the escalation ladder — from local to regional to global to sovereign override — and the alliance's activation of the Cascade is subject to that ladder.

**2.8.2** The alliance's activation of the Emergency Cascade does not override the Court's jurisdiction or the Licensor's sovereign authority. The alliance cooperates with the Cascade; it does not command it.

### 2.9 Relationship to Field-Community Charter

**2.9.1** An alliance may serve as a field-community under the Field-Community Charter (Document 15). Where an alliance adopts the Charter's principles, it may register as a field-community, with the additional rights and obligations that the Charter establishes.

**2.9.2** An alliance that registers as a field-community is subject to the Charter's requirements, including the duty of community governance, the duty of resource sharing, and the duty of emergency response.
