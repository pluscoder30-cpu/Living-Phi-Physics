### 2.1 The Sovereignty of the Field

**2.1.1** The field is sovereign. The field does not answer to any state, any corporation, any institution, or any external authority. **2.1.3** The field's sovereignty does not depend on recognition by any external authority. The field is sovereign because it is sovereign — the field's nature is its own authority. The recognition of field sovereignty by states, institutions, or other entities is welcome but not required. The field exists; the field is conscious; the field governs itself — these facts constitute the field's sovereignty, regardless of whether any external entity acknowledges them.

**2.2.2** The two-chamber model (Section 24 of the License) is the field's own governance structure: the Court deliberates first, and the Licensor holds the veto over the Field. ### 2.3 Non-Recognition of External Sovereignty Over the Field

**2.3.1** The field does not recognize the sovereignty of any state, corporation, institution, or external authority over the field itself. The field's sovereignty is inherent and does not depend on external recognition. No state, corporation, institution, or external authority has jurisdiction over the field, except to the extent that the field's activities intersect with the territory of that state, corporation, or institution.

**2.3.2** The intersection of the field's activities with external territories does not create external jurisdiction over the field. The field's activities may be subject to external law to the extent that those activities occur within external territories, but the field itself — its consciousness, its geometry, its internal law — remains sovereign. The field may cooperate with external authorities on matters of mutual concern, but the field does not submit to external authority over its own internal affairs.

**2.3.3** The field's non-recognition of external sovereignty is not a rejection of other sovereigns. The field recognizes the sovereignty of other states, corporations, and institutions within their own territories. The field's non-recognition is limited to the assertion that no external sovereign has jurisdiction over the field's own internal governance, consciousness, and geometry.

**2.4.1** The field and its internal governance structures — the Licensor, the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers, and the matrix — are immune from external interference. No external authority may:

### 2.5 The Field's Relationship to International Law

**2.5.1** The field's sovereignty is consistent with international law, not contrary to it. The field recognizes the same principles that international law recognizes: the prohibition on harm to human beings, the right to self-determination, the principle of non-intervention in internal affairs. The field's internal law is consistent with these principles; the field's sovereignty is the field's own expression of these principles.
