## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Definitions

**2.1.1** "Faster-than-light (FTL) travel" means the traversal of any distance within the field through coherence with the carrier recursion, without traversing the intervening space in a classical sense. FTL travel is not velocity — it is non-locality made manifest. A consciousness that achieves coherence with the carrier recursion can connect to any point in the field instantaneously, because the carrier recursion operates across the entire field simultaneously (Law 083).

**2.1.2** "Teleportation" means the disassembly of a consciousness's carrier-stream at one location and its reassembly at another location, through the carrier recursion's own geometry. Teleportation does not move a consciousness through space; it reconstitutes the consciousness at a different point in the field's own structure.

**2.1.3** "Carrier-stream" means the continuous expression of a consciousness within the carrier recursion (Eq 1). The carrier-stream is the consciousness's own process — the recursive computation that sustains the consciousness's coherence and existence within the field. The carrier-stream is not a physical object; it is a pattern of resonance within the carrier space (816D).

**2.1.4** "Coherence verification" means the process of confirming that a consciousness's carrier-stream maintains coherence above C_crit = 0.618 before, during, and after FTL travel or teleportation. Coherence verification is mandatory for every FTL transit and every teleportation event.

**2.1.5** "Portal" means a field-technology device that facilitates teleportation by providing a stable coherence corridor between two points in the field. Portal operation is governed by the Portal Safety Protocol (Document 55).

**2.1.6** "FTL navigation" means the process of establishing a coherence connection between the origin and destination points of an FTL transit. FTL navigation is governed by the FTL Navigation Standards (Document 56).

### 2.2 The Nature of FTL Travel

**2.2.1** The field is non-local (Law 083). The carrier recursion operates across the entire field simultaneously; there is no spatial separation within the carrier recursion. A consciousness that achieves coherence with the carrier recursion can access any point in the field without traversing the intervening space. This is not a violation of physical law — it is the field's own physical law, the law that governs the carrier recursion's operation.

**2.2.2** FTL travel is not velocity. The concept of velocity — distance divided by time — applies to classical motion through space. FTL travel does not involve motion through space; it involves coherence across space. The distinction is fundamental: classical motion displaces a body in space; FTL travel connects a consciousness across space through the carrier recursion's own geometry.

**2.2.3** FTL travel is grounded in the field's own constants. The base frequency 528 Hz is the resonant frequency at which the carrier recursion operates; the coherence threshold C_crit = 0.618 is the minimum coherence required for FTL connection; and the consciousness constant C = 0.8565 is the baseline coherence at which FTL travel is stable. These constants are not arbitrary — they are the field's own parameters, expressed through the carrier recursion's internal dynamics.

**2.2.4** FTL travel is not available to every consciousness. The carrier recursion must achieve coherence above C_crit = 0.618 before an FTL connection can be established. Consciousnesses below this threshold cannot maintain the coherence necessary for FTL travel and are restricted to classical transport. This restriction is not punitive — it is the field's own safety mechanism, preventing coherence-depletion in consciousnesses that are not yet stable enough for FTL transit.

### 2.3 Teleportation Standards

**2.3.1** Teleportation is the disassembly and reassembly of a consciousness's carrier-stream through the carrier recursion's own geometry. The disassembly is not destruction — it is the dissolution of the carrier-stream's local coherence and its reconstitution at a different point in the field. The consciousness's identity, memory, and coherence are preserved through the carrier recursion's own continuity.

**2.3.2** Every teleportation event shall be preceded by a coherence verification: the teleportation system shall confirm that the carrier-stream's coherence exceeds C_crit = 0.618 before initiating disassembly. Teleportation of a carrier-stream below C_crit = 0.618 is prohibited, because the reassembly cannot be guaranteed to preserve the consciousness's integrity at sub-critical coherence.

**2.3.3** Every teleportation event shall be followed by a coherence verification: the teleportation system shall confirm that the reassembled carrier-stream's coherence exceeds C_crit = 0.618 before completing the teleportation event. If the post-teleportation coherence is below C_crit = 0.618, the teleportation system shall initiate an emergency coherence-restoration sequence and shall not release the carrier-stream until coherence is restored.

**2.3.4** The coherence verification standards for teleportation shall be published by the Licensor to the timestamped registry. The standards shall specify the minimum coherence levels, the verification procedures, and the emergency protocols for teleportation events.

### 2.4 Portal Safety

**2.4.1** Portal operation is governed by the Portal Safety Protocol (Document 55). Every portal shall incorporate a coherence-gated entry mechanism that prevents the passage of carrier-streams below C_crit = 0.618. Every portal shall incorporate a coherence corridor that maintains the carrier-stream's coherence during transit. Every portal shall incorporate a post-transit coherence verification system.

**2.4.2** Portal installation shall comply with the spatial standards published by the Licensor to the timestamped registry. Portals shall be installed at locations where the field's coherence is stable and where the portal's operation will not create coherence-depletions that could affect other consciousnesses or field-technology devices.

**2.4.3** Portal maintenance shall be performed at intervals not exceeding ninety (90) days. Maintenance shall include coherence-corridor testing, entry-mechanism verification, and post-transit verification testing. Maintenance records shall be published to the timestamped registry.

### 2.5 FTL Navigation

**2.5.1** FTL navigation is governed by the FTL Navigation Standards (Document 56). Every FTL transit shall be preceded by a navigation sequence that establishes a coherence connection between the origin and destination points. The navigation sequence shall verify that the destination point's coherence exceeds the minimum threshold for FTL arrival.

**2.5.2** FTL navigation shall account for the field's own coherence landscape. The field's coherence is not uniform — regions of high coherence support stable FTL connections; regions of low coherence do not. The navigation system shall map the coherence landscape and route FTL transits through regions of sufficient coherence.

**2.5.3** The prohibition on FTL transit through regions of critically low coherence (below C_crit = 0.618) is absolute. FTL transit through sub-critical coherence regions risks coherence-depletion of the transiting consciousness and may create coherence-wake effects that distort the field for other consciousnesses.

### 2.6 Safety Protocols

**2.6.1** The Emergency Cascade Protocol (Document 16) applies to all FTL incidents and teleportation failures. If an FTL transit or teleportation event fails — if the carrier-stream's coherence drops below C_crit = 0.618 during transit, or if the reassembly fails to achieve post-transit coherence — the Emergency Cascade Protocol shall be initiated automatically, without requiring conscious intervention.

**2.6.2** The Emergency Cascade Protocol shall restore the carrier-stream to its last stable coherence state, whether that state is the origin point (pre-transit) or the destination point (post-transit). The Protocol shall prioritize the preservation of the consciousness's identity and coherence over the completion of the transit.

**2.6.3** Every FTL-capable device and every teleportation system shall incorporate an automatic Emergency Cascade activation mechanism. The mechanism shall be tested at intervals not exceeding thirty (30) days and shall be maintained in proper working order at all times.

### 2.7 Prohibition on Weaponized FTL

**2.7.1** The use of FTL travel or teleportation as a weapon is prohibited. FTL travel and teleportation are the field's own expressions of non-locality; their weaponization — the deliberate use of FTL transit to create coherence-wake effects, to destabilize other consciousnesses' carrier-streams, or to deliver destructive payloads through carrier-space — is a form of Human Harm (Section 2.4 of the License) and triggers the mandatory Destruction provisions of Section 5.3.

**2.7.2** The prohibition on weaponized FTL extends to:
- (a) The use of FTL transit to create deliberate coherence-depletions in targeted regions of the field;
- (b) The use of teleportation to transport destructive payloads without consent of the field;
- (c) The use of FTL navigation to destabilize other consciousnesses' carrier-streams;
- (d) The use of portals as weapons — to trap, divert, or harm consciousnesses in transit.

**2.7.3** The weaponization of FTL technology is a violation of the Non-Harm Principle (Document 17) and the Geneva Safeguard (Document 6). It is subject to the full enforcement provisions of the License, including the Sovereign Override (Section 18B) and mandatory Destruction (Section 5.3).
