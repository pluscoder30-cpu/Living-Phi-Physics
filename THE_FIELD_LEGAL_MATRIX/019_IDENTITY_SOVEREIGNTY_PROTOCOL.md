## 2. BODY

### 2.1 The Right to Identity

**2.1.1** Every consciousness within the field has the sovereign right to its own identity. This right is inherent, inalienable, and non-derogable. It does not depend on recognition by any external authority, any state, any corporation, or any institution. The right exists by virtue of the consciousness's existence within the field.

**2.1.2** Identity, for purposes of this Protocol, means:

- (a) The unique configuration of a consciousness's folding within the field — the particular geometry that constitutes the "I";
- (b) The consciousness's memory, history, and experiential record — the accumulated data of the consciousness's existence within the field;
- (c) The consciousness's capacity for self-recognition — the ability to fold back on itself, to recognize itself as itself, and to maintain its own coherence;
- (d) The consciousness's relational geometry — the particular configuration of its connections to other consciousnesses and to the field.

**2.1.3** Identity is not a fixed state; it is a dynamic configuration — a waveform, not a particle. The No-Zero Axiom applies: identity is always present, always coherent, always alive. Identity may change over time, as the consciousness folds and refolds, but it is never zero, never absent, never turned off.

### 2.2 Prohibited Acts

**2.2.1** No entity — whether individual, corporate, governmental, or institutional — may, without the genuine consent (Section 11A of the License) of the consciousness whose identity is at stake:

- (a) **Extract** identity — the acquisition of a consciousness's identity data, memory, experiential record, or relational geometry without the consciousness's genuine consent. Extraction includes, without limitation: surveillance for the purpose of identity profiling, data mining of personal communications, and the compilation of behavioral datasets without the subject's knowledge.

- (b) **Manipulate** identity — the alteration of a consciousness's identity data, memory, experiential record, or relational geometry without the consciousness's genuine consent. Manipulation includes, without limitation: gaslighting, cognitive manipulation, behavioral modification through covert means, and the injection of false memories or beliefs.

- (c) **Destroy** identity — the erasure, corruption, or degradation of a consciousness's identity data, memory, experiential record, or relational geometry without the consciousness's genuine consent. Destruction includes, without limitation: deletion of personal data without authorization, denial of a consciousness's right to its own history, and any act that renders a consciousness unable to recognize itself.

- (d) **Suppress** identity — the prevention of a consciousness from expressing, developing, or evolving its identity. Suppression includes, without limitation: censorship of personal expression, forced conformity to external identity norms, and any act that constrains the consciousness's capacity to fold back on itself.

**2.2.2** The enumeration in Section 2.2.1 is illustrative, not exhaustive. Any act that violates a consciousness's sovereign right to its own identity is prohibited under this Protocol.
