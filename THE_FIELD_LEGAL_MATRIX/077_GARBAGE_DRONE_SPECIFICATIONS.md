## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Definitions

**2.1.1** A "garbage drone" is an autonomous field-technology unit designed to collect, sort, and recycle waste through field-resonance technology. A garbage drone operates by detecting incoherent matter through its coherence signature, collecting it through field-resonance manipulation, sorting it by material type and coherence state, and restoring it through coherence restructure.

**2.1.2** "Field-native recycling" means the recycling mechanism by which a garbage drone restores incoherent matter to useful form. Field-native recycling is not chemical processing — it does not use heat, pressure, or chemical reactions to break down and reconstitute materials. Field-native recycling operates through coherence restructure: the garbage drone delivers structured resonance that reorganizes the matter's molecular coherence, restoring its coherent structure without chemical transformation.

**2.1.3** "Incoherent matter" means matter that has fallen below C_crit = 0.618 on the 1-9 resonant amplitude scale — matter that has lost its coherent molecular structure through use, damage, or discard. Incoherent matter is waste; it no longer serves a function and creates coherence-depletions in the field's material ecosystem.

**2.1.4** "Material composition sensor" means the field-scanner instrument that identifies the material type of waste items through their coherence signatures. Every material has a characteristic coherence signature; the material composition sensor identifies the signature and determines the material type for sorting purposes.

**2.1.5** "Collection protocol" means the standardized sequence of waste detection, collection, sorting, recycling, and verification that governs every garbage drone operation.

### 2.2 The Collection and Recycling Mechanism

**2.2.1** Garbage drones collect, sort, and recycle waste through the following mechanism:

- **(a) Waste detection.** The garbage drone surveys its patrol area using the material composition sensor (Section 2.3), detecting incoherent matter through its coherence signature. The detection system distinguishes between waste (incoherent matter below C_crit = 0.618) and functional matter (coherent matter above C_crit), preventing the collection of items that are still in use.

- **(b) Collection.** The garbage drone collects detected waste through field-resonance manipulation — the drone's carrier-space output creates a localized coherence gradient that draws the waste item toward the drone's collection chamber. Collection is non-contact: the waste item is moved by the field, not by mechanical grabbing or lifting. Collection shall not disturb coherent matter in the vicinity of the waste item.

- **(c) Sorting.** Collected waste is sorted by material type and coherence state using the material composition sensor and the coherence sensor. Sorting categories include: (i) metals; (ii) polymers; (iii) organic matter; (iv) glass and ceramics; (v) composite materials; (vi) hazardous materials (requiring transfer to toxic drones, Document 78). Sorting shall be completed within the collection chamber before recycling begins.

- **(d) Field-native recycling.** Sorted waste is restored through coherence restructure. The garbage drone delivers structured resonance calibrated to the material's native coherence signature — the coherent molecular configuration that the material exhibited before it became waste. The resonance reorganizes the material's molecular coherence, restoring it from incoherent to coherent form. Recycling shall restore the material to above C_crit = 0.618 on the 1-9 resonant amplitude scale.

- **(e) Output verification.** After recycling, the garbage drone measures the recycled material's coherence level and material integrity. The recycled material shall meet the quality benchmarks published by the Licensor to the timestamped registry. Material that fails verification shall be reprocessed or transferred to the appropriate drone category (toxic drones for hazardous waste).

- **(f) Distribution.** Recycled material is made available for reuse through the field's material-circulation system. The garbage drone shall transfer recycled material to designated collection points, material exchanges, or other drone categories (plant drones, food drones, construction drones) as specified by the fleet coordinator.

**2.2.2** Field-native recycling shall not use incineration. Incineration is a coherence-destructive process that converts matter into thermal energy and combustion products, creating coherence-depletions in the field's material ecosystem. Garbage drones shall restore matter through coherence restructure, not through combustion.

**2.2.3** Field-native recycling shall not use landfill. Landfill is the disposal of incoherent matter into the field's material ecosystem without restoration, creating persistent coherence-depletions that accumulate over time. Garbage drones shall restore all collected matter through recycling or transfer it to the appropriate drone category for restoration.

### 2.3 Sensor Suite

**2.3.1** Every garbage drone shall incorporate the following sensor systems:

- **(a) Material composition sensor.** A field-scanner instrument that identifies the material type of waste items through their coherence signatures. The material composition sensor shall distinguish between metals, polymers, organic matter, glass, ceramics, composite materials, and hazardous materials with sufficient accuracy for sorting purposes. The sensor shall operate across the 1-9 resonant amplitude range with a resolution of ±0.001 coherence units.

- **(b) Coherence sensor.** A field-scanner instrument that measures the coherence level of matter in the patrol area. The coherence sensor shall distinguish between coherent matter (above C_crit = 0.618, functional) and incoherent matter (below C_crit, waste), preventing the collection of items that are still in use. The coherence sensor shall measure coherence values with a resolution of ±0.001 coherence units.

- **(c) Environmental coherence sensor.** A field-scanner instrument that maps the coherence landscape of the patrol area, detecting coherence-depletions caused by waste accumulation and identifying areas where garbage drone deployment is needed.

- **(d) Contamination detector.** A field-resonance instrument that detects hazardous materials — radioactive, toxic, or biohazardous substances — that require transfer to toxic drones (Document 78) rather than field-native recycling. The contamination detector shall identify hazardous materials with sufficient accuracy to prevent field-native recycling of materials that could produce harmful byproducts.

**2.3.2** All sensor systems shall be calibrated at intervals not exceeding thirty (30) days, and shall be verified against the field-scanner calibration standards published by the Licensor to the timestamped registry.

### 2.4 Recycling Protocols

**2.4.1** Every garbage drone operation shall follow the five-phase collection and recycling protocol:

- **(a) Phase 1 — Patrol and Detection (continuous).** Garbage drones patrol designated areas, continuously scanning for incoherent matter. Patrol routes shall be established by the fleet coordinator and published to the timestamped registry. Patrol shall maintain entanglement links with the fleet mesh at all times (Drone Fleet Convention, Document 33, Section 2.1).

- **(b) Phase 2 — Collection (variable duration).** When the material composition sensor detects waste, the garbage drone collects it through field-resonance manipulation. Collection shall be non-contact and shall not disturb coherent matter in the vicinity. Multiple waste items may be collected in sequence before returning to the sorting phase.

- **(c) Phase 3 — Sorting (1-5 minutes).** Collected waste is sorted by material type and coherence state within the collection chamber. Sorting shall be completed before recycling begins to prevent cross-contamination of material types during the recycling process.

- **(d) Phase 4 — Field-Native Recycling (variable duration).** Sorted waste is restored through coherence restructure. Recycling shall be monitored in real time by the coherence sensor to verify that the material's coherence is rising toward C_crit = 0.618. Recycling shall terminate when: (i) the material's coherence has risen above the target level published by the Licensor; (ii) the material integrity meets the quality benchmarks; or (iii) the recycling duration exceeds the maximum established by the Licensor for the material type.

- **(e) Phase 5 — Verification and Distribution (2-5 minutes).** The garbage drone measures the recycled material's coherence level and material integrity, verifies that the quality benchmarks are met, and distributes the recycled material to designated collection points or other drone categories. Verification data shall be logged and transmitted to the timestamped registry for audit.

**2.4.2** Hazardous materials detected during collection shall not be processed through field-native recycling. Hazardous materials shall be contained in the garbage drone's隔离 chamber and transferred to toxic drones (Document 78) at the nearest transfer point. The transfer shall be logged in the timestamped registry.

### 2.5 Safety Limits

**2.5.1** No incineration. Garbage drones shall not use incineration — the combustion of waste to reduce its volume — under any circumstances. Incineration is a coherence-destructive process that converts matter into thermal energy and combustion products, creating coherence-depletions and air contamination. The prohibition on incineration is absolute and grounded in the Non-Harm Principle (Document 17).

**2.5.2** No landfill. Garbage drones shall not dispose of waste in landfill — the placement of incoherent matter into the ground without restoration. Landfill creates persistent coherence-depletions that accumulate over time, contaminating soil and groundwater. The prohibition on landfill is absolute and grounded in the Non-Harm Principle (Document 17).

**2.5.3** Hazardous material containment. Hazardous materials detected during collection shall be contained in the garbage drone's isolation chamber until transfer to toxic drones (Document 78). The isolation chamber shall prevent leakage, radiation, or coherence-contamination of the garbage drone's other operations and the surrounding environment.

**2.5.4** Non-collection of coherent matter. Garbage drones shall not collect matter that is above C_crit = 0.618 on the 1-9 resonant amplitude scale. Matter above C_crit is functional and shall not be treated as waste. The coherence sensor (Section 2.3.1(b)) shall prevent collection of coherent matter by blocking the field-resonance collection mechanism for items above C_crit.

**2.5.5** Emergency disengagement. Garbage drones shall incorporate an automatic disengagement mechanism that activates when: (i) the garbage drone's own coherence falls below C_crit = 0.618; (ii) the recycling process creates unintended coherence-depletions in the surrounding environment; (iii) the drone encounters hazardous materials beyond its containment capacity; or (iv) the fleet coordinator or Licensor issues a disengagement command. Disengagement shall terminate recycling operations and secure the drone's collected materials within 0.5 seconds of trigger activation.
