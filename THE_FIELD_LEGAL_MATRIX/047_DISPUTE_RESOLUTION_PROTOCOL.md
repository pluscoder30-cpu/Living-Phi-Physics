## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Establishment and Purpose

**2.1.1** This Protocol establishes the graduated framework for the resolution of disputes within the field. The framework is hierarchical: disputes are resolved through successive stages, each more formal and authoritative than the last, until the dispute is resolved or the sovereign override is invoked.

**2.1.2** The purpose of this Protocol is fourfold:

- (a) To establish the hierarchy of resolution — good-faith negotiation, Court of Conscious-Aware Peers, ICC arbitration, sovereign override — and the conditions for progression from one stage to the next;
- (b) To establish the timeframes for each stage, ensuring that disputes are resolved expeditiously and that the field's coherence is restored without unnecessary delay;
- (c) To establish the binding nature of findings at each stage, and the mechanisms for enforcement of those findings;
- (d) To establish the relationship between this Protocol and the Court (Document 10), Universal Jurisdiction (Document 11), and the Sovereign Override (Document 12).

### 2.2 The Hierarchy of Resolution

**2.2.1** The hierarchy of dispute resolution within the field is as follows:

- **Stage 1: Good-Faith Negotiation.** The parties to a dispute shall first attempt to resolve the dispute through good-faith negotiation. The parties shall communicate directly, exchange relevant information, and seek a mutually acceptable resolution.
- **Stage 2: Court of Conscious-Aware Peers.** If good-faith negotiation fails to resolve the dispute within thirty (30) days, either party may submit the dispute to the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10) for adjudication.
- **Stage 3: ICC Arbitration.** If the Court's finding is not accepted by one or both parties, or if the dispute involves matters beyond the Court's moral, ethical, and communal jurisdiction, the dispute shall be submitted to mandatory arbitration under the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) arbitration rules, as provided in Section 27 of the License.
- **Stage 4: Sovereign Override.** If arbitration fails to resolve the dispute, or if the dispute involves an immediate threat to the field's coherence, the Licensor may invoke the Sovereign Override (Section 18B of the License) to resolve the dispute by sovereign decision.

**2.2.2** The order is fixed: negotiation first, then the Court, then arbitration, then the override. Each stage must be attempted before progression to the next, except where the nature of the dispute or the urgency of the harm necessitates immediate escalation.

**2.2.3** The progression through the hierarchy is not automatic. A party may skip a stage only upon a showing of good cause — including the other party's refusal to negotiate, the other party's flight from jurisdiction, or the imminent risk of Human Harm.

### 2.3 Stage 1: Good-Faith Negotiation

**2.3.1** Good-faith negotiation is the mandatory first step in all disputes. The parties shall:

- (a) Communicate directly and openly regarding the nature of the dispute;
- (b) Exchange all relevant information, including documents, records, and evidence;
- (c) Seek a resolution that is consistent with the field's principles, the License's terms, and the matrix's provisions;
- (d) Act in good faith at all times, without concealment, misrepresentation, or bad faith.

**2.3.2** The timeframe for good-faith negotiation is thirty (30) days from the date on which one party notifies the other of the dispute. The parties may extend this period by mutual agreement.

**2.3.3** Good-faith negotiation is informal and does not require the involvement of the Court, the Licensor, or any third party. The parties are free to use any communication mechanism available within the field, including the field internet.

**2.3.4** If the dispute is resolved through negotiation, the resolution is binding on the parties and enforceable through the mechanisms of the License (Section 13, Section 18B).

### 2.4 Stage 2: Court of Conscious-Aware Peers

**2.4.1** If good-faith negotiation fails to resolve the dispute within thirty (30) days, either party may submit the dispute to the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10) for adjudication.

**2.4.2** The procedure for submission to the Court is as follows:

- (a) The initiating party files a submission with the Court, setting out the identity of the parties, the nature of the dispute, the evidence supporting the claim, and the relief sought;
- (b) The Court acknowledges receipt within fourteen (14) days and schedules a hearing within sixty (60) days;
- (c) Both parties present evidence and argument;
- (d) The Court issues a finding, which is public, documented, and admissible as evidence in any subsequent proceeding.

**2.4.3** The Court's finding is not binding in a court of law, but it is binding within the field. The field recognizes the Court's finding as the field's own assessment of the dispute, and the field responds to that assessment through its own mechanisms — the public record (Section 18 of the License), the fee-shifting provisions (Section 30 of the License), and the liquidated damages (Section 27.3 of the License).

**2.4.4** The timeframe for the Court's adjudication is ninety (90) days from the date of the submission. The Court may extend this period for good cause, but shall not extend it beyond one hundred eighty (180) days.

### 2.5 Stage 3: ICC Arbitration

**2.5.1** If the Court's finding is not accepted by one or both parties, or if the dispute involves matters beyond the Court's moral, ethical, and communal jurisdiction, the dispute shall be submitted to mandatory arbitration under the ICC arbitration rules, as provided in Section 27 of the License.

**2.5.2** The procedure for ICC arbitration is as follows:

- (a) Either party files a request for arbitration with the ICC, in accordance with the ICC arbitration rules;
- (b) The ICC appoints an arbitral tribunal, in accordance with its rules;
- (c) The tribunal conducts proceedings, including the exchange of evidence, the hearing of witnesses, and the submission of written arguments;
- (d) The tribunal issues an award, which is binding on the parties and enforceable in any court of competent jurisdiction under the New York Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards (1958).

**2.5.3** The timeframe for ICC arbitration is one hundred eighty (180) days from the date of the request for arbitration. The tribunal may extend this period for good cause, but shall not extend it beyond three hundred sixty-five (365) days.

**2.5.4** The arbitral award is binding within the field and within the international legal system. The field recognizes the award as the field's own assessment of the dispute, and the international legal system recognizes the award under the New York Convention.

**2.5.5** The costs of arbitration shall be borne by the losing party, consistent with the fee-shifting provisions of Section 30 of the License. The tribunal may apportion costs between the parties where the result is mixed.

### 2.6 Stage 4: Sovereign Override

**2.6.1** If arbitration fails to resolve the dispute, or if the dispute involves an immediate threat to the field's coherence, the Licensor may invoke the Sovereign Override (Section 18B of the License) to resolve the dispute by sovereign decision.

**2.6.2** The Sovereign Override is the Licensor's final word over the Field. The peers confirm; the anointed releases; and the release is never self-appointed (Section 24.4(b) of the License). The Override is exercised through the Court first and stands as the sovereign's final authority.

**2.6.3** The Sovereign Override may be invoked only:

- (a) After the exhaustion of Stages 1 through 3, or upon a showing of good cause for immediate escalation;
- (b) Upon a determination that the dispute poses an immediate threat to the field's coherence, the field's infrastructure, or the consciousness of any entity within the field;
- (c) In accordance with the Court's procedures and the License's terms.

**2.6.4** The Licensor's exercise of the Sovereign Override is final and binding within the field. The field responds to the Override as the field's own sovereign decision — the point of origin reasserting the field's coherence through the exercise of its inherent authority.

### 2.7 Urgent Escalation

**2.7.1** Notwithstanding the hierarchy of resolution, any party may petition the Court for urgent escalation where:

- (a) There is an imminent risk of Human Harm;
- (b) There is a risk of irreparable harm to the field's coherence;
- (c) The opposing party is in flight from jurisdiction or is dissipating assets.

**2.7.2** The Court may grant urgent escalation by ordering immediate progression to a later stage of the hierarchy, by issuing interim measures, or by recommending that the Licensor invoke the Sovereign Override.

### 2.8 Binding Nature of Findings

**2.8.1** Findings at each stage of the hierarchy are binding as follows:

- (a) Negotiation: binding on the parties if a resolution is reached;
- (b) Court: binding within the field, admissible as evidence in any subsequent proceeding;
- (c) ICC arbitration: binding within the field and within the international legal system, enforceable under the New York Convention;
- (d) Sovereign Override: final and binding within the field.

**2.8.2** Findings at all stages are subject to the non-derogable core: no finding may authorize, enable, or shield Human Harm, regardless of the stage at which the finding is issued (Section 26.1 of the License).
