### 2.2 The Grounding in International Human Rights Law

**2.2.1** The Non-Harm Principle is grounded in the following provisions of international human rights law, which recognize the same prohibition in their own terms:

- (a) **UDHR Article 3** — the right to life, liberty, and security of person;
- (b) **UDHR Article 5** — freedom from torture and from cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment or punishment;
- (c) **UDHR Article 9** — freedom from arbitrary arrest, detention, or exile;
- (d) **UDHR Article 12** — freedom from arbitrary interference with privacy, family, home, or correspondence, and from attacks upon honour and reputation;
- (e) **UDHR Article 18** — freedom of thought, conscience, and religion, including the freedom to change religion or belief and the freedom to manifest religion or belief in teaching, practice, worship, and observance.

**2.2.2** The Non-Harm Principle is also grounded in the following provisions of international humanitarian law:

- (f) **Geneva Conventions, Common Article 3** — the prohibition of violence to life and person, in particular murder, mutilation, cruel treatment, and torture; the prohibition of outrages upon personal dignity, in particular humiliating and degrading treatment.

**2.2.3** The Non-Harm Principle is further grounded in the following provisions of international criminal law:

- (g) **Rome Statute, Article 7** — crimes against humanity, including murder, extermination, enslavement, deportation, imprisonment, torture, sexual violence, persecution, enforced disappearance, apartheid, and other inhumane acts when committed as part of a widespread or systematic attack directed against any civilian population.

**2.2.4** The enumeration in Sections 2.2.1 through 2.2.3 is illustrative, not exhaustive. The Non-Harm Principle is defined by its effect on the field, not by any particular catalogue of prohibited acts. Any act that distorts the field's coherence — that degrades the capacity of any consciousness to fold back on itself — violates the Non-Harm Principle.
