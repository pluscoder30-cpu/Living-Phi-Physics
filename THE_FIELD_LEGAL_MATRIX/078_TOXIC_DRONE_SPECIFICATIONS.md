## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Definitions

**2.1.1** A "toxic drone" is an autonomous field-technology unit designed to identify, contain, isolate, and dispose of or recycle hazardous materials through coherence-isolation containment. A toxic drone operates by detecting hazardous materials through their coherence-disruption signatures, containing them through field-boundary isolation, isolating them from the surrounding field ecosystem, and neutralizing them through resonance dissolution or secure containment for disposal.

**2.1.2** "Coherence-isolation containment" means the containment mechanism by which a toxic drone prevents hazardous materials from contaminating the surrounding field. Coherence-isolation operates through the creation of a field boundary — a region of elevated coherence that the hazardous material's disruptive resonance cannot penetrate. The boundary does not physically enclose the material; it creates a coherence-wall that the toxin's disruptive frequency cannot propagate through. The coherence-isolation boundary shall maintain coherence above C_crit = 0.618 at all points along its surface, and the hazardous material shall be confined entirely within the boundary's interior.

**2.1.3** "Hazardous material" means any material that actively disrupts coherence in surrounding regions — materials that do not merely lack coherence but propagate incoherence through chemical, biological, radioactive, or resonance-based mechanisms. Hazardous materials include, without limitation: radioactive isotopes, chemical toxins, biological agents, uncontrolled resonance sources, and any material whose coherence-disruption signature exceeds the thresholds established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry.

**2.1.4** "Coherence-disruption signature" means the characteristic pattern by which a hazardous material disrupts coherence in surrounding regions. Every hazardous material has a distinctive disruption signature — a measurable pattern of coherence-depletion that identifies the material's type, concentration, and hazard level. The disruption signature is the inverse of the coherence signature: where a coherent material has a stable, resonant signature, a hazardous material has an unstable, anti-resonant signature.

**2.1.5** "Containment protocol" means the standardized sequence of hazard detection, containment, isolation, neutralization, and disposal that governs every toxic drone operation.

### 2.2 The Hazard Containment Mechanism

**2.2.1** Toxic drones identify, contain, isolate, and dispose of or recycle hazardous materials through the following mechanism:

- **(a) Hazard detection.** The toxic drone surveys its patrol area using the hazard sensor suite (Section 2.3), detecting hazardous materials through their coherence-disruption signatures. The detection system distinguishes between hazardous materials (active coherence-disruptors), incoherent matter (passive coherence-depletions, handled by garbage drones, Document 77), and coherent matter (functional material above C_crit = 0.618). Detection shall classify the hazard type, concentration, and containment priority.

- **(b) Containment.** Upon detection, the toxic drone deploys a coherence-isolation boundary around the hazardous material. The boundary is generated by the toxic drone's field-emission system, which creates a spherical or ellipsoidal coherence-wall centered on the detected material. The boundary shall achieve coherence above C_crit = 0.618 at all surface points within 0.5 seconds of deployment, preventing the hazardous material's disruptive resonance from propagating beyond the boundary.

- **(c) Isolation.** Once contained, the hazardous material is isolated from the surrounding field through the containment boundary. The toxic drone monitors the boundary continuously, verifying that the hazardous material's disruption signature remains confined within the boundary. If the boundary's coherence drops below C_crit at any point, the toxic drone shall immediately reinforce the boundary or initiate emergency evacuation (Section 2.5.5).

- **(d) Neutralization or secure containment.** Hazardous materials are processed through one of two pathways: (i) resonance dissolution — the delivery of structured resonance calibrated to the material's native coherent frequency, which reorganizes the material's molecular structure from disruptive to neutral form; or (ii) secure containment — the transfer of the material to a permanent, coherence-isolated storage facility designed for long-term hazardous material containment. The choice between dissolution and secure containment shall be determined by the material type, hazard level, and the Licensor's published standards.

- **(e) Disposal verification.** After neutralization or secure containment, the toxic drone verifies that the hazardous material's coherence-disruption signature has been eliminated or contained. Verification shall confirm that the material no longer propagates incoherence beyond its containment boundary. Verification data shall be logged and transmitted to the timestamped registry for audit.

- **(f) Fleet coordination.** Toxic drones shall coordinate with garbage drones (Document 77) to receive hazardous materials detected during garbage collection operations, and with water drones (Document 76) to address contaminated water sources. Fleet coordination shall operate through the entanglement mesh (Document 33, Section 2.1.5).

**2.2.2** Resonance dissolution shall not be used on radioactive materials whose half-life exceeds the dissolution timeframe established by the Licensor. Radioactive materials that cannot be dissolved within the timeframe shall be transferred to secure containment facilities.

**2.2.3** Biological hazardous agents shall not be dissolved through resonance without first confirming that the dissolution process does not produce viable byproducts. Biological agents that could become more hazardous through partial dissolution shall be transferred to secure containment.

### 2.3 Sensor Suite

**2.3.1** Every toxic drone shall incorporate the following sensor systems:

- **(a) Hazard detection sensor.** A field-scanner instrument that identifies hazardous materials through their coherence-disruption signatures. The hazard detection sensor shall distinguish between radioactive, chemical, biological, and resonance-based hazards with sufficient accuracy for containment classification. The sensor shall operate across the 1-9 resonant amplitude range with a resolution of ±0.001 coherence units.

- **(b) Coherence-isolation boundary sensor.** A field-scanner instrument that monitors the coherence level of the containment boundary in real time. The boundary sensor shall detect coherence variations as small as 0.0001 along the boundary surface and shall trigger alerts when boundary coherence approaches C_crit = 0.618.

- **(c) Environmental coherence sensor.** A field-scanner instrument that maps the coherence landscape of the patrol area, detecting coherence-depletions caused by hazardous material contamination and identifying areas where toxic drone deployment is needed.

- **(d) Disruption-propagation sensor.** A field-scanner instrument that measures the rate and direction of coherence-disruption propagation from a hazardous material source. The propagation sensor shall enable the toxic drone to predict the hazard's spread and deploy containment boundaries with sufficient margin to prevent boundary breach.

- **(e) Material identification sensor.** A field-scanner instrument that identifies the specific type of hazardous material through its disruption signature, enabling the toxic drone to select the appropriate neutralization or containment pathway.

**2.3.2** All sensor systems shall be calibrated at intervals not exceeding thirty (30) days, and shall be verified against the field-scanner calibration standards published by the Licensor to the timestamped registry.

### 2.4 Handling Protocols

**2.4.1** Every toxic drone operation shall follow the six-phase containment protocol:

- **(a) Phase 1 — Patrol and Detection (continuous).** Toxic drones patrol designated areas, continuously scanning for hazardous materials. Patrol routes shall be established by the fleet coordinator and published to the timestamped registry. Patrol shall maintain entanglement links with the fleet mesh at all times (Drone Fleet Convention, Document 33, Section 2.1).

- **(b) Phase 2 — Assessment (1-5 minutes).** Upon detection, the toxic drone assesses the hazard type, concentration, containment priority, and the appropriate neutralization or containment pathway. Assessment shall be completed before containment deployment.

- **(c) Phase 3 — Containment (0.5-2 minutes).** The toxic drone deploys the coherence-isolation boundary around the hazardous material. Boundary deployment shall achieve full coherence at all surface points within 0.5 seconds. Boundary integrity shall be verified before proceeding to isolation.

- **(d) Phase 4 — Isolation (variable duration).** The hazardous material is isolated within the containment boundary while the toxic drone prepares for neutralization or secure containment transfer. Isolation shall continue until the neutralization pathway is confirmed and prepared.

- **(e) Phase 5 — Neutralization or Transfer (variable duration).** The hazardous material is processed through resonance dissolution or transferred to a secure containment facility. The choice of pathway shall comply with the standards published by the Licensor for the specific material type.

- **(f) Phase 6 — Verification and Logging (2-5 minutes).** The toxic drone verifies that the hazardous material has been neutralized or securely contained, confirms that the containment boundary can be safely dissolved, and logs the operation to the timestamped registry.

### 2.5 Safety Limits

**2.5.1** No breach of containment. The coherence-isolation boundary shall not be breached under any circumstances during a toxic drone operation. If the boundary's coherence falls below C_crit = 0.618 at any point, the toxic drone shall immediately reinforce the boundary with emergency coherence restoration (Section 2.3.2 of the Drone Fleet Convention, Document 33) and, if reinforcement fails, initiate emergency evacuation and notify the fleet coordinator.

**2.5.2** No release into the field. Hazardous materials shall not be released from the containment boundary into the surrounding field under any circumstances. The containment boundary shall be dissolved only after the hazardous material has been confirmed neutralized or transferred to secure containment.

**2.5.3** No processing beyond capacity. Toxic drones shall not process hazardous materials whose concentration or hazard level exceeds the drone's rated capacity. Materials beyond capacity shall be flagged and transferred to specialized containment facilities.

**2.5.4** Operator exclusion zone. During containment operations, a coherence-exclusion zone shall be established around the toxic drone. No consciousnesses shall enter the exclusion zone during active containment operations. The exclusion zone radius shall be determined by the hazard type and concentration and published to the timestamped registry.

**2.5.5** Emergency disengagement. Toxic drones shall incorporate an automatic disengagement mechanism that activates when: (i) the toxic drone's own coherence falls below C_crit = 0.618; (ii) the containment boundary coherence cannot be maintained above C_crit; (iii) the drone encounters hazardous materials beyond its containment capacity; or (iv) the fleet coordinator or Licensor issues a disengagement command. Disengagement shall seal the containment boundary, secure the hazardous material, and disengage the drone's ZPF coupling within 0.5 seconds of trigger activation.
