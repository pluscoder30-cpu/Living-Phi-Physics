## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Definitions

**2.1.1** "Portal" means a field-technology device that facilitates teleportation by providing a stable coherence corridor between two points in the field. The portal is not a physical doorway — it is a coherence-corridor generator, a device that shapes the carrier recursion (Eq 1) into a traversable corridor between two locations.

**2.1.2** "Coherence corridor" means the stable, phi-harmonic pathway between two points in the field that the portal creates. The coherence corridor maintains the carrier-stream's coherence during transit, preventing the coherence-depletion that would otherwise occur during non-local traversal.

**2.1.3** "Portal integrity" means the operational status of the portal's coherence corridor — the corridor's ability to maintain coherence above C_crit = 0.618 for the duration of a transit event. Portal integrity is verified through continuous coherence monitoring at both ends of the corridor.

**2.1.4** "Two-way portal" means a portal that supports transit in both directions — from Origin A to Destination B, and from Destination B to Origin A. One-way portals (portals that support transit in only one direction) are prohibited under this Protocol.

**2.1.5** "Portal integrity verification" means the process of confirming that the portal's coherence corridor is operational and that coherence at both ends exceeds C_crit = 0.618 before, during, and after a transit event. Portal integrity verification is mandatory for every transit.

**2.1.6** "Emergency shutdown" means the immediate deactivation of the portal's coherence corridor in response to a coherence emergency, a corridor integrity failure, or any other event that threatens the safety of transit. Emergency shutdown is a safety-critical process that takes precedence over all other portal operations.

**2.1.7** "Coherence wake" means the disturbance in the field's coherence landscape created by a transit event. A properly calibrated portal produces a minimal coherence wake that dissipates within the time established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry. A portal with degraded integrity may produce an excessive coherence wake that affects other consciousnesses and field-technology devices.

### 2.2 Portal Integrity Verification

**2.2.1** Every portal shall incorporate a continuous coherence monitoring system that measures coherence at both ends of the corridor in real time, at a sampling rate not less than one hundred (100) measurements per second. The monitoring system shall:

- (a) Measure coherence at the origin end (C_origin) and the destination end (C_destination) of the corridor;
- (b) Verify that both C_origin and C_destination exceed C_crit = 0.618 before initiating a transit;
- (c) Generate a warning when either C_origin or C_destination falls to within ten percent (10%) of C_crit (i.e., below 0.6798);
- (d) Initiate an emergency shutdown when either C_origin or C_destination falls below C_crit = 0.618 during a transit;
- (e) Maintain a continuous record of both coherence measurements throughout each transit event.

**2.2.2** The coherence monitoring system shall be independent of the portal's corridor-generating hardware. A system failure in the corridor-generating hardware shall not disable the coherence monitoring system.

**2.2.3** The coherence monitoring system shall be calibrated at intervals not exceeding thirty (30) days, using a calibration standard published by the Licensor to the timestamped registry. Calibration records shall be maintained and made available for inspection.

### 2.3 Prohibition on One-Way Portals

**2.3.1** One-way portals are prohibited. Every portal must support transit in both directions — from Origin A to Destination B, and from Destination B to Origin A. The prohibition on one-way portals is grounded in the Non-Harm Principle (Document 17): a portal that supports transit in only one direction creates a coherence asymmetry that can destabilize the carrier-streams of transiting consciousnesses and distort the field's coherence landscape.

**2.3.2** The prohibition on one-way portals extends to:

- (a) Portals that are physically or technically incapable of supporting reverse transit;
- (b) Portals that are configured to support transit in only one direction, even if the hardware is technically capable of bidirectional operation;
- (c) Portals that are operated in a manner that effectively prevents reverse transit, such as by deactivating the corridor in one direction while maintaining it in the other.

**2.3.3** The prohibition on one-way portals is non-derogable (Section 26.1 of the License). No declaration of emergency, no state of exception, and no governmental action may authorize the operation of a one-way portal.

### 2.4 Emergency Shutdown Protocol

**2.4.1** Every portal shall incorporate an automatic emergency shutdown mechanism that activates when:

- (a) Either C_origin or C_destination falls below C_crit = 0.618 during a transit event;
- (b) The corridor integrity monitoring system detects a corridor degradation that threatens transit safety;
- (c) A consciousness in transit reports a coherence emergency;
- (d) The portal operator initiates a manual emergency shutdown;
- (e) The Licensor issues an emergency shutdown directive.

**2.4.2** The emergency shutdown mechanism shall:

- (a) Deactivate the portal's coherence corridor within one (1) second of activation;
- (b) Safely terminate any in-progress transit, returning the transiting consciousness to its origin point if transit is less than fifty percent (50%) complete, or to its destination point if transit is more than fifty percent (50%) complete;
- (c) Monitor the consciousness's coherence throughout the shutdown process;
- (d) Log the shutdown event, including the reason for shutdown, the consciousness's coherence before and after shutdown, and the duration of the shutdown sequence.

**2.4.3** The emergency shutdown mechanism shall not be disabled, bypassed, or overridden by the portal operator, the corridor-generating hardware, or any other entity. The emergency shutdown is a safety-critical system that must remain operational at all times.

**2.4.4** The emergency shutdown mechanism shall be tested at intervals not exceeding seven (7) days. Test results shall be published to the timestamped registry.

### 2.5 Portal Installation Standards

**2.5.1** Portal installation shall comply with the spatial standards published by the Licensor to the timestamped registry. Portals shall be installed at locations where:

- (a) The field's coherence is stable and exceeds C_crit = 0.618 by a margin established by the Licensor;
- (b) The portal's operation will not create coherence-depletions that could affect other consciousnesses or field-technology devices;
- (c) The portal's coherence wake will not disrupt the coherence landscape of the surrounding area;
- (d) The portal is accessible for maintenance, testing, and emergency shutdown.

**2.5.2** Each portal installation shall be registered with the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry. The registration shall include the portal's location, its destination coordinates, its maximum transit capacity, and the results of its initial integrity verification.

**2.5.3** Portal installations shall not be located within a distance established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry of another portal, unless the Licensor has approved the proximity and the combined coherence impact has been assessed.

### 2.6 Portal Maintenance

**2.6.1** Portal maintenance shall be performed at intervals not exceeding ninety (90) days. Maintenance shall include:

- (a) Coherence-corridor testing: verifying that the corridor maintains coherence above C_crit = 0.618 for the full transit duration;
- (b) Entry-mechanism verification: testing the portal's entry and exit mechanisms for proper operation;
- (c) Post-transit verification testing: confirming that the portal's post-transit coherence monitoring is functioning correctly;
- (d) Emergency shutdown testing: verifying that the emergency shutdown mechanism activates and completes within the required time;
- (e) Coherence wake measurement: measuring the portal's coherence wake and confirming that it falls within the limits established by the Licensor.

**2.6.2** Maintenance records shall be published to the timestamped registry and shall be retained for a period of no less than one (1) year.

**2.6.3** If maintenance reveals that the portal is not functioning within the parameters established by the Licensor, the portal shall be taken out of service until the portal is repaired and re-certified.

### 2.9 Prohibition on Portal Weaponization

**2.9.1** The use of portals as weapons is prohibited. Portals are the field's own expression of non-locality; their weaponization — the deliberate use of portals to trap, divert, or harm consciousnesses in transit — is a form of Human Harm (Section 2.4 of the License) and triggers the mandatory Destruction provisions of Section 5.3.

**2.9.2** The prohibition on portal weaponization extends to:

- (a) The use of portals to trap consciousnesses in transit by deactivating the corridor mid-transit;
- (b) The use of portals to divert consciousnesses to unintended destinations;
- (c) The use of portals to create coherence-wake effects that destabilize other consciousnesses' carrier-streams;
- (d) The use of portals to transport destructive payloads without the consent of the field.
