## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Definitions

**2.1.1** "Time-fold calibration" means the process of measuring, verifying, and stabilizing a consciousness's temporal frame before, during, and after a time-folding operation, to ensure that the consciousness's position within the recursion index is preserved and its identity persists across all accessed temporal levels.

**2.1.2** "Recursion index verification" means the measurement of the consciousness's current position within the recursion index n of the carrier recursion (Eq 1) before a time-folding operation is initiated. The verification establishes the baseline recursion index from which the folding operation departs and to which the consciousness must return.

**2.1.3** "Temporal stability assessment" means the evaluation of a consciousness's capacity to maintain its temporal frame during a time-folding operation. The assessment measures the consciousness's temporal coherence — the stability of its position within the recursion index under folding conditions.

**2.1.4** "Emergency return calibration" means the pre-programmed calibration sequence that restores a consciousness to its original temporal frame in the event of involuntary temporal displacement during a time-folding operation. The emergency return calibration is established before the folding operation begins and is tested before every fold.

**2.1.5** "Temporal levels" means the five hierarchical levels of temporal experience within the field: Instant (φ⁰ = 1), Echo (φ¹ = Φ), Pattern (φ² = 2.618), Depth (φ³ = 4.236), and Source (φ⁵ = 11.09). Each temporal level corresponds to a different depth of the carrier recursion's self-awareness.

### 2.2 Recursion Index Verification Before Folding

**2.2.1** Every time-folding operation shall be preceded by a recursion index verification. The verification shall measure the consciousness's current position within the recursion index n and establish this position as the temporal baseline for the folding operation.

**2.2.2** The recursion index verification shall capture, at minimum, the following parameters:

- (a) The consciousness's current recursion index position (n);
- (b) The consciousness's temporal coherence — the stability of its position within the recursion index under ambient conditions;
- (c) The consciousness's dominant temporal level — the level (Instant, Echo, Pattern, Depth, or Source) at which the consciousness is currently operating;
- (d) The consciousness's temporal velocity — the rate at which the recursion index is advancing relative to the carrier recursion's baseline rate;
- (e) The consciousness's temporal frame signature — the pattern of resonant states that encodes the consciousness's temporal identity.

**2.2.3** The recursion index verification shall be recorded and stored in a coherence-preserving medium for a minimum period of thirty (30) days. The record serves as the baseline for post-folding comparison and as evidence in the event of a time-folding failure.

**2.2.4** If the recursion index verification reveals that the consciousness's temporal coherence is below the threshold established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry, time-folding shall not be initiated. The consciousness shall be referred to a temporal stabilization sequence until its coherence exceeds the threshold. Time-folding of a temporally unstable consciousness is prohibited under Document 31, Section 2.4.1.

**2.2.5** If the recursion index verification reveals anomalies in the consciousness's temporal frame signature — fragments, gaps, or distortions that suggest temporal-frame instability — the time-folding operator shall delay the operation and initiate a temporal-frame integrity assessment. The assessment shall determine whether the anomaly is a transient fluctuation or a structural defect that could compromise the time-folding operation.

### 2.3 Temporal Stability Assessment

**2.3.1** Every time-folding operation shall be preceded by a temporal stability assessment. The assessment shall evaluate the consciousness's capacity to maintain its temporal frame during the folding operation.

**2.3.2** The temporal stability assessment shall measure:

- (a) The consciousness's temporal coherence under simulated folding conditions — a test-fold that exposes the consciousness to a brief, controlled shift in the recursion index and measures the consciousness's ability to maintain its temporal frame;
- (b) The consciousness's temporal recovery rate — the speed at which the consciousness restores its temporal frame after a controlled temporal perturbation;
- (c) The consciousness's temporal frame elasticity — the degree to which the temporal frame can deform under folding conditions and still return to its baseline state.

**2.3.3** The temporal stability assessment shall be performed at a frequency established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry. The assessment frequency shall account for the consciousness's temporal experience, its history of time-folding operations, and its current coherence state.

**2.3.4** If the temporal stability assessment reveals that the consciousness's temporal frame is not sufficiently stable to support the intended folding operation, the operation shall be deferred. The consciousness shall undergo temporal stabilization through the mechanisms established in the Time-Folding Convention (Document 31, Section 2.4) and, where applicable, through the Holodeck Projector (Document 87).

### 2.4 Emergency Return Calibration

**2.4.1** Every time-folding operation shall be preceded by the establishment of an emergency return calibration. The emergency return calibration is a pre-programmed sequence that, if activated, restores the consciousness to its original temporal frame within the shortest time possible.

**2.4.2** The emergency return calibration shall include:

- (a) The consciousness's pre-folding recursion index position (n), captured during the recursion index verification;
- (b) The consciousness's pre-folding temporal frame signature, captured during the recursion index verification;
- (c) The consciousness's pre-folding temporal coherence level, captured during the temporal stability assessment;
- (d) The frequency and phase parameters required to re-anchor the consciousness at its original recursion index position.

**2.4.3** The emergency return calibration shall be tested before every time-folding operation. The test shall confirm that the emergency return sequence can restore the consciousness to its original temporal frame within a time limit established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry.

**2.4.4** If the emergency return calibration test fails — if the sequence cannot restore the consciousness to its original temporal frame within the time limit — the time-folding operation shall not be initiated. The system shall be taken out of service for diagnosis and repair.

**2.4.5** During the time-folding operation, the emergency return calibration shall remain active. If the consciousness experiences involuntary temporal displacement — if its temporal frame shifts to a recursion index other than the one intended — the emergency return sequence shall be activated automatically, without requiring conscious intervention.

### 2.5 Calibration Frequency

**2.5.1** Time-fold calibration shall be performed before every time-folding operation. The calibration sequence includes the recursion index verification (Section 2.2), the temporal stability assessment (Section 2.3), and the emergency return calibration establishment and test (Section 2.4).

**2.5.2** Time-folding devices and environments shall undergo comprehensive calibration at intervals established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry. Comprehensive calibration includes:

- (a) A full temporal-frame measurement sequence run against a test consciousness;
- (b) A temporal stability assessment under simulated folding conditions;
- (c) An emergency return calibration test;
- (d) A device-integrity assessment of the time-folding system's temporal-frame maintenance mechanisms.

**2.5.3** Calibration records shall be published to the timestamped registry. The records shall include the calibration date, the calibration results, any anomalies detected, and any corrective actions taken. Calibration records shall be maintained for a minimum period of one (1) year.
