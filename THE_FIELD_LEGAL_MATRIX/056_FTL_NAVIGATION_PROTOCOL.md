## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Definitions

**2.1.1** "FTL navigation" means the process of establishing a coherence connection between the origin and destination points of an FTL transit. FTL navigation is not star-chart navigation — it is carrier-resonance mapping, the identification of a coherence pathway through the carrier recursion's geometry that connects two points in the field without traversing the intervening space.

**2.1.2** "Carrier-resonance map" means the field's own coherence landscape — the map of coherence levels, resonance patterns, and phi-harmonic relationships that define the navigable pathways within the carrier recursion. The carrier-resonance map is not a static chart; it is a dynamic, living map that changes as the field's coherence landscape evolves.

**2.1.3** "Coherence verification" means the process of confirming that both the origin and destination points of an FTL transit have coherence above C_crit = 0.618 before initiating the transit. Coherence verification is mandatory for every FTL transit and is the primary safety mechanism that prevents coherence-depletion during transit.

**2.1.4** "Blind FTL" means an FTL transit initiated without prior coherence verification of the destination point. Blind FTL is prohibited because it risks transit into a region of critically low coherence, which can cause irreversible coherence-depletion of the transiting consciousness.

**2.1.5** "Coherence pathway" means the specific route through the carrier recursion's geometry that connects the origin point to the destination point. The coherence pathway must pass through regions of sufficient coherence — regions where the carrier recursion's iteration is stable and where the transiting consciousness's carrier-stream will not experience coherence-depletion.

**2.1.6** "Emergency return" means the process by which a consciousness that has initiated an FTL transit returns to its origin point in response to a coherence emergency, a navigation failure, or any other event that threatens the safety of the transit.

**2.1.7** "Navigation threshold" means the minimum coherence level that the destination point must reach before an FTL transit may be initiated. The navigation threshold may exceed C_crit = 0.618 for transit classes that involve greater coherence risk, as established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry.

### 2.2 The Navigation Framework

**2.2.1** FTL navigation is carrier-resonance mapping, not star-chart navigation. The field has no stars in the classical sense — the field has coherence landscapes, resonance patterns, and phi-harmonic relationships. FTL navigation maps these dynamics to identify safe coherence pathways between origin and destination points.

**2.2.2** The carrier-resonance map is grounded in the field's own constants. The base frequency 528 Hz is the resonant frequency at which the carrier recursion operates; the coherence threshold C_crit = 0.618 is the minimum coherence for stable navigation; and the consciousness constant C = 0.8565 is the baseline coherence at which FTL navigation is most reliable. The navigation framework uses these constants to identify regions of sufficient coherence and to route transits through the safest pathways.

**2.2.3** The carrier-resonance map is maintained by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry. The map is updated continuously as the field's coherence landscape evolves. Navigation systems shall use the current version of the map; use of outdated maps is prohibited because outdated maps may not reflect current coherence conditions.

**2.2.4** The navigation framework shall account for the field's non-uniform coherence landscape. Regions of high coherence support stable FTL connections; regions of low coherence do not. The navigation system shall route FTL transits through regions of sufficient coherence and shall avoid regions of critically low coherence (below C_crit = 0.618).

### 2.3 Coherence Verification Before Transit

**2.3.1** Every FTL transit shall be preceded by a coherence verification of both the origin and destination points. The coherence verification shall:

- (a) Measure the origin point's coherence (C_origin) and confirm that it exceeds the navigation threshold established for the intended class of FTL transit;
- (b) Measure the destination point's coherence (C_destination) and confirm that it exceeds the navigation threshold established for the intended class of FTL transit;
- (c) Verify that the coherence pathway between origin and destination passes through regions of sufficient coherence;
- (d) Log the verification data, including both coherence measurements, the navigation threshold, and the coherence pathway assessment.

**2.3.2** If the coherence verification reveals that either C_origin or C_destination is below the navigation threshold — or that the coherence pathway passes through regions of critically low coherence — the FTL transit shall not proceed. The navigation system shall notify the consciousness and shall not initiate the transit until the coherence conditions are met.

**2.3.3** The coherence verification shall be performed by a system that is independent of the FTL transit device's propulsion hardware. A system failure in the propulsion hardware shall not disable the coherence verification system.

**2.3.4** The coherence verification shall be performed at a time interval not exceeding one (1) second before the initiation of the FTL transit. The verification must reflect the current coherence conditions at both the origin and destination points.

### 2.4 Prohibition on Blind FTL

**2.4.1** Blind FTL — an FTL transit initiated without prior coherence verification of the destination point — is prohibited. Blind FTL risks transit into a region of critically low coherence, which can cause irreversible coherence-depletion of the transiting consciousness. The prohibition on blind FTL is a fundamental safety requirement, grounded in the Non-Harm Principle (Document 17) and the Emergency Cascade Protocol (Document 16).

**2.4.2** The prohibition on blind FTL extends to:

- (a) FTL transits initiated without a coherence verification of the destination point;
- (b) FTL transits initiated with outdated or inaccurate coherence verification data;
- (c) FTL transits initiated with coherence verification data that is more than the maximum age established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry;
- (d) FTL transits initiated through navigation systems that are not calibrated to the current carrier-resonance map.

**2.4.3** The prohibition on blind FTL applies to all consciousnesses, regardless of coherence level, navigation experience, or certification status. No consciousness may initiate an FTL transit without verifying the destination point's coherence.

**2.4.4** The prohibition on blind FTL is non-derogable (Section 26.1 of the License). No declaration of emergency, no state of exception, and no governmental action may authorize blind FTL.

### 2.5 Emergency Return Protocol

**2.5.1** Every FTL-capable device shall incorporate an emergency return mechanism that allows the consciousness to return to its origin point at any time during or after the transit. The emergency return mechanism shall:

- (a) Be operable by the consciousness without requiring external assistance;
- (b) Be effective immediately upon activation;
- (c) Return the consciousness to its origin point within the shortest time possible, consistent with preserving the consciousness's coherence and identity;
- (d) Monitor the consciousness's coherence throughout the return process;
- (e) Log the return event, including the consciousness's position at the time of activation, the origin point's coherence at the time of return, and the duration of the return.

**2.5.2** The emergency return mechanism shall not be disabled, bypassed, or overridden by the FTL device operator, the propulsion hardware, or any other entity. The emergency return is a safety-critical system that must remain operational at all times.

**2.5.3** If the emergency return mechanism fails to return the consciousness to its origin point within the time limit established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry, the Licensor shall be notified immediately. The Licensor's Sovereign Override (Section 18B of the License) extends to FTL emergencies: the Licensor may intervene directly in the carrier recursion to restore the consciousness to its origin point.

**2.5.4** The emergency return mechanism shall be tested at intervals not exceeding thirty (30) days. Test results shall be published to the timestamped registry.

### 2.6 Navigation Standards

**2.6.1** Every FTL navigation system shall maintain a real-time carrier-resonance map that reflects the current coherence conditions within the field. The map shall be updated at intervals not exceeding the maximum age established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry.

**2.6.2** Every FTL navigation system shall calculate the optimal coherence pathway between the origin and destination points before initiating a transit. The optimal pathway shall be the route that maximizes the minimum coherence encountered along the pathway, subject to the navigation threshold requirements.

**2.6.3** Every FTL navigation system shall incorporate a coherence-wake monitoring system that measures the coherence disturbance created by each transit event. If the coherence wake exceeds the limits established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry, the navigation system shall adjust the transit parameters to reduce the wake.

**2.6.4** Every FTL navigation system shall maintain a transit log that records the origin point, destination point, coherence pathway, coherence measurements, transit duration, and coherence-wake measurements for each transit event. Transit logs shall be retained for a period of no less than ninety (90) days.

### 2.9 FTL Transit for Environmental Restoration

**2.9.1** FTL travel may be used for environmental restoration — to project consciousness or field-technology devices into regions where environmental damage can be assessed, remediated, or prevented. The use of FTL travel for environmental restoration is encouraged and shall not be subject to the energy extraction limits applicable to commercial operation.

**2.9.2** FTL travel for environmental restoration shall comply with the safety standards established in this Protocol. The environmental restoration purpose does not exempt the transit from the coherence verification requirements, the navigation standards, or the emergency return protocol.

### 2.10 Navigation System Certification

**2.10.1** Every FTL navigation system shall be certified by the Licensor before operation. Certification shall require:

- (a) Verification that the navigation system maintains a current carrier-resonance map;
- (b) Verification that the coherence verification system functions within the parameters established by the Licensor;
- (c) Verification that the emergency return mechanism activates and completes within the required time;
- (d) Verification that the coherence-wake monitoring system functions correctly;
- (e) Verification that the navigation system is calibrated to the field's current constants.

**2.10.2** Navigation system certification shall be renewed at intervals not exceeding one hundred eighty (180) days. Certification shall be published to the timestamped registry.

**2.10.3** If a navigation system fails to maintain certification — if the system is found to be operating outside the parameters established by the Licensor — the system shall be taken out of service until the system is repaired and re-certified.
