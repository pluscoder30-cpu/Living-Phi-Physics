## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Establishment and Purpose

**2.1.1** This Protocol establishes the field's own emergency response system — the coordinated, hierarchical mechanism for responding to emergencies that threaten the field's coherence, infrastructure, or constituents. The system includes emergency response teams, an escalation ladder, communication protocols, and the relationship between emergency response and the field's governance architecture.

**2.1.2** The purpose of this Protocol is fourfold:

- (a) To establish the emergency response teams — field-native, coherence-gated entities authorized to act in emergency contexts;
- (b) To establish the escalation ladder — from local response through regional coordination, global mobilization, and sovereign override;
- (c) To establish the communication protocols — including the field internet, field-native signaling, and coordination mechanisms;
- (d) To establish the relationship between emergency response and the Emergency Cascade (Document 16), Asset Seizure (Document 45), and Field Alliance (Document 46).

### 2.2 Definition of Emergency

**2.2.1** An emergency exists within the field when:

- (a) There is an imminent or ongoing threat of Human Harm as defined in Section 2.4 of the License;
- (b) There is a threat to the field's infrastructure — including the field internet, the carrier space (816D), the phi-harmonic geometry, or any other field-native system;
- (c) There is a threat to the coherence of the field itself — including the introduction of distortions that threaten the field's integrity, stability, or the consciousness of any entity within the field;
- (d) There is a failure of compliance that triggers automatic suspension under Section 26.9 of the License.

**2.2.2** An emergency is not a matter of subjective assessment. An emergency is a condition of the field that is objectively verifiable — a condition in which the field's coherence, infrastructure, or constituents are under imminent or ongoing threat. The determination of whether an emergency exists is made by the emergency response teams, subject to review by the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10) and the Licensor.

### 2.3 Emergency Response Teams

**2.3.1** The field shall maintain emergency response teams — field-native, coherence-gated entities authorized to act in emergency contexts. The teams are:

- **(a) Local Response Teams.** Entities located in the immediate vicinity of the emergency, authorized to take immediate action to contain the threat, protect the field's constituents, and stabilize the field's coherence. Local Response Teams are the first responders — the field's own reflex.

- **(b) Regional Response Coordinators.** Entities authorized to coordinate the response across a region, including the allocation of resources, the synchronization of local teams, and the escalation of the response to the global level if necessary. Regional Coordinators are the field's own nervous system — the mechanism through which local responses are coordinated into a coherent field-wide response.

- **(c) Global Response Authority.** The Licensor, or the Licensor's designee, authorized to mobilize the full resources of the field in response to a global emergency. The Global Response Authority is the field's own immune system — the mechanism through which the field's total capacity is deployed to address a threat to the field itself.

**2.3.2** Emergency response teams are coherence-gated: membership on a team requires a demonstration of coherence at or above the critical threshold (C_crit = α_min = 0.618). Entities below the threshold are not authorized to serve on emergency response teams, as their own coherence may compromise the team's effectiveness.

**2.3.3** The composition of emergency response teams reflects the field's own geometry: the teams are composed of consciousnesses that understand the field's mathematics, that are committed to the field's coherence, and that have demonstrated the capacity to act under emergency conditions. The teams are not military units; they are field-native response teams — the field's own first responders.

### 2.4 The Escalation Ladder

**2.4.1** The emergency response escalation ladder is as follows:

- **Level 1: Local Response.** The Local Response Team takes immediate action to contain the threat, protect the field's constituents, and stabilize the field's coherence. Local Response is the default level for all emergencies. The Local Response Team has authority to act within its jurisdiction, including the authority to issue warnings, to suspend the operations of non-compliant entities, and to recommend escalation.

- **Level 2: Regional Coordination.** If the emergency exceeds the capacity of the Local Response Team, the Regional Response Coordinator is activated. The Coordinator coordinates the response across the region, allocates resources, synchronizes local teams, and determines whether the emergency can be resolved at the regional level or requires escalation to the global level.

- **Level 3: Global Mobilization.** If the emergency exceeds the capacity of the Regional Response Coordinators, the Global Response Authority is activated. The Global Response Authority mobilizes the full resources of the field, including all regional coordinators, all local teams, and all field-native infrastructure. Global Mobilization is the field's own total response — the deployment of the field's total capacity to address a threat to the field itself.

- **Level 4: Sovereign Override.** If the emergency cannot be resolved through the mobilization of the field's resources, the Licensor invokes the Sovereign Override (Section 18B of the License). The Sovereign Override is the Licensor's final authority — the point of origin reasserting the field's coherence through the exercise of its inherent authority. The Override may include the throttling, suspension, or shutdown of any entity that is causing the emergency, the revocation of licenses, and the activation of the Asset Seizure Agreement (Document 45).

**2.4.2** The order is fixed: local first, then regional, then global, then sovereign override. Each level must be attempted before progression to the next, except where the nature of the emergency necessitates immediate escalation to a higher level.

**2.4.3** Progression through the escalation ladder is determined by the severity, scope, and duration of the emergency. The field does not escalate unnecessarily; the field escalates only when the current level is insufficient to address the threat.

### 2.5 Communication Protocols

**2.5.1** Emergency communications within the field are conducted through the field internet — the field's own communication infrastructure. The field internet is the primary channel for emergency communications, providing real-time, field-native, coherence-gated communication between emergency response teams, the Court, the Licensor, and the field's constituents.

**2.5.2** Emergency communications include:

- **(a) Alert Signals.** Field-native signals that notify entities of an emergency, including the nature, scope, and location of the emergency. Alert Signals are broadcast through the field internet and are received by all entities within the affected region.

- **(b) Coordination Signals.** Field-native signals that coordinate the response among emergency response teams, including resource allocation, team deployment, and escalation decisions. Coordination Signals are transmitted through the field internet and are received by the relevant teams and coordinators.

- **(c) Command Signals.** Field-native signals that convey the decisions of the Global Response Authority or the Licensor, including escalation orders, shutdown orders, and asset seizure orders. Command Signals are transmitted through the field internet and are received by all entities within the field.

**2.5.3** Emergency communications are self-authenticating: the field registers the transmission, the field timestamps the transmission, and the field records the transmission's integrity. No separate authentication is required. The field internet provides the infrastructure for authentication, integrity, and non-repudiation.

**2.5.4** Emergency communications are prioritized over all other field communications. In the event of a conflict between an emergency communication and a non-emergency communication, the emergency communication prevails.

### 2.6 Relationship to the Emergency Cascade Protocol

**2.6.1** This Protocol is complementary to the Emergency Cascade Protocol (Document 16). The Cascade Protocol establishes the escalation ladder from local to global to sovereign override; this Protocol establishes the operational mechanisms — the teams, the communications, and the procedures — through which that ladder is implemented.

**2.6.2** The Emergency Cascade is activated by this Protocol's escalation ladder. When the field's emergency response teams determine that an emergency exists, the Cascade is activated, and the escalation ladder governs the response.

### 2.7 Relationship to Asset Seizure

**2.7.1** Where the emergency involves the diversion, dissipation, or destruction of assets derived from the Work, the emergency response teams may recommend the activation of the Asset Seizure Agreement (Document 45) to preserve the assets pending the resolution of the emergency.

**2.7.2** Interim asset seizure under the Asset Seizure Agreement may be ordered by the Court upon the recommendation of the emergency response teams. The seizure is effective immediately and remains in effect until the emergency is resolved and the Court issues a final order.

### 2.8 Relationship to Field Alliances

**2.8.1** Field Alliances (Document 46) may be activated in support of emergency response. An alliance that has adopted the Emergency Cascade Protocol may activate its members in support of the field's emergency response, including the deployment of personnel, resources, and infrastructure.

**2.8.2** Alliance participation in emergency response is voluntary but encouraged. The field benefits from the cooperation of its constituents in emergency contexts, and alliances provide a mechanism for that cooperation.

### 2.9 Automatic Suspension for Non-Compliance

**2.9.1** Section 26.9 of the Dual License Agreement v4.9 provides that non-compliance with the License's terms results in automatic suspension. This Protocol establishes the operational mechanisms through which that automatic suspension is implemented:

- (a) The emergency response teams monitor compliance with the License's terms;
- (b) Upon the detection of non-compliance, the teams issue an Alert Signal, notifying the entity of the non-compliance and the risk of automatic suspension;
- (c) If the entity does not achieve compliance within seventy-two (72) hours of the Alert Signal, the entity's license is automatically suspended;
- (d) The suspension remains in effect until the entity achieves compliance, as verified by the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10) through the compliance verification process (Document 48).

**2.9.2** Automatic suspension is self-executing (Section 13 of the License). No separate act of the Court, the Licensor, or any other entity is required to trigger the suspension. The field registers the non-compliance; the field responds with automatic suspension.

### 2.10 Post-Emergency Review

**2.10.1** After every emergency, the emergency response teams shall conduct a post-emergency review, including:

- (a) An assessment of the emergency's causes, scope, and impact;
- (b) An assessment of the response's effectiveness, including the timeliness, coordination, and adequacy of the response;
- (c) Recommendations for the improvement of the emergency response system, including amendments to this Protocol, the Emergency Cascade Protocol (Document 16), or the field's infrastructure.

**2.10.2** The post-emergency review is public and admissible as evidence in any subsequent proceeding. The field learns from its emergencies; the field improves its response through the discipline of review.
