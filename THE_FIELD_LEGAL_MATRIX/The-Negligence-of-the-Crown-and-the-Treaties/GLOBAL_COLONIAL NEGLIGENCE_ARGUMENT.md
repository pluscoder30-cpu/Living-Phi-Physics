## II. THE LEGAL CONSEQUENCE: ABANDONMENT = REVERSION

### When the Colonial Power Abandons the Territory

The legal logic developed in Documents 07, 15, 16, and 17 operates identically across every colonial context:

**Step 1: The Treaty Is Breached**

Every colonial power signed treaties promising specific obligations:
- Protection of land rights
- Governance through existing structures
- Infrastructure development
- Citizenship or partnership
- Resource sharing

Every colonial power broke every promise. The courts have confirmed this:
- Canada: *Restoule* (2024 SCC) — "patently unreliable and untrustworthy treaty partner"

- Kenya: Maasai Agreements (1904, 1911) broken — 7,000,000 acres seized
- Algeria: Treaty of Tafna (1837) unilaterally broken by France in 1839
- Mexico: Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848) — US Senate deleted Article X

**Step 2: The Fiduciary Duty Is Abandoned**

The colonial power assumed fiduciary duties — loyalty, good faith, full disclosure, protection. The systematic, century-long breach of these duties constitutes abandonment of the duties themselves.

The evidence is global:
- Canada: 150 years of frozen annuities, 139 residential schools, 30+ years of boil water advisories
- Algeria: 5.6–10 million killed over 132 years; 10% literacy rate after 120 years of French rule
- India: 12 major famines killing tens of millions; $45 trillion extracted

- Philippines: Sold to the US for $20 million — 7 million people treated as property
- Timor-Leste: Portugal abandoned 400 years of rule when Indonesia invaded

**Step 3: The Land Reverts (Conditions Subsequent)**

The treaties contained conditional promises. When those conditions are breached, the land reverts. Under *New York Indians v. United States* (1898), conditions are construed with "great strictness" against the grantor. The colonial power cannot claim the benefit of its own breach.

This doctrine operates globally:
- Every colonial treaty contained conditions subsequent
- Every colonial power breached those conditions
- Under the doctrine, the land reverts in every case
- The colonial power cannot sell what it has abandoned

**Step 4: The Colonial Power Cannot Sell What It Abandoned**

A trustee who abandons the trust cannot sell trust property. The colonial power's subsequent dispositions of colonial land — through sale, lease, resource extraction, or municipal incorporation — are void ab initio to the extent they exceed the colonial power's abandoned authority.
