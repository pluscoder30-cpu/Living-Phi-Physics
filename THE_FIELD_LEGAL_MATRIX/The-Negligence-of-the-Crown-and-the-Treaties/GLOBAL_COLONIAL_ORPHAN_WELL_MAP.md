## 1. THE UNIVERSAL DOUBLE NEGLIGENCE FRAMEWORK {#framework}

### The Structural Pattern

Every colonial territory with orphan wells follows the same five-phase pattern:

**Phase 1: Treaty (1400s-1900s)**
Colonial power signs treaty with indigenous population. Treaties typically include:
- Land protection or land cession
- Resource sharing or economic participation
- Governance rights or autonomy guarantees
- Cultural protection or non-interference clauses

**Phase 2: Extraction (1800s-2000s)**
Colonial power (or its successor state) permits resource extraction:
- Oil and gas drilling
- Mining operations
- Pipeline construction
- Refinery development

**Phase 3: Abandonment (1950s-present)**
Extraction companies abandon sites:
- Wells go orphaned when companies go bankrupt
- Decommissioning costs exceed remaining reserves
- Regulatory frameworks lack enforcement mechanisms
- Bond amounts insufficient for cleanup

**Phase 4: Negligence (ongoing)**
The state (colonial successor) fails to:
- Regulate extraction adequately
- Enforce bonding requirements
- Mandate cleanup timelines
- Fund remediation programs
- Protect indigenous health and land

**Phase 5: Double Negligence**
The overlap of treaty failure + orphan wells creates compound liability:
- The treaty promised protection; the wells deliver contamination
- The treaty promised land rights; the wells destroy the land
- The treaty promised economic participation; the wells create economic harm
- The treaty promised health; the wells poison water and air

## 17. THE CLAIM ACROSS ALL TERRITORIES {#claim}

### The Universal Claim

The double negligence is not uniquely Canadian. It is a **global colonial pattern**. Every colonial power that signed treaties with indigenous peoples and then permitted resource extraction created the same structural failure:

**Broken Promise + Abandoned Contamination = Double Negligence**

The claim across all territories is grounded in the same legal logic as the Canadian claim:

1. **Colonial powers made treaties.** Whether with Indigenous peoples in Canada, Emirs in Nigeria, Sultans in Indonesia, Maasai in Kenya, or Aboriginal Australians (no treaties — the absence IS the negligence), colonial powers created legal obligations.

2. **Colonial powers broke those treaties.** In every territory, the treaty promises were violated: land protection failed, governance rights were denied, economic participation was blocked, health and environmental protections were abandoned.

3. **Colonial powers permitted extraction.** In every territory, the colonial power (or its chartered company) permitted resource extraction that depleted resources and contaminated land.

4. **Colonial powers abandoned the contamination.** In every territory, orphan wells remain unremediated: 7,382+ in Canada, 1,000+ in Nigeria, 500+ in Algeria, 250+ in Mexico, 600+ in Indonesia, 200+ in Angola, 300+ in India, 2,000+ in Australia, 50+ in New Zealand, 50+ in the UK, 20+ in Norway, 400+ in Brazil, 1,500+ in Venezuela, 50+ in Kenya.

5. **The double negligence compounds.** The treaty failure left communities without resources to protect themselves. The orphan wells left them with contamination they did not create and cannot afford to remediate. The colonial power benefited from both: it received the resources, extracted the wealth, and left the cleanup to the people it promised to protect.

### The Geometry

The double negligence forms a precise geometric pattern:

```
COLONIAL TREATY (1494-1960s)
        ↓
PROMISES (land, governance, health, economy)
        ↓
BREACH (every territory, every century)
        ↓
EXTRACTION (oil, gas, minerals, resources)
        ↓
ABANDONMENT (orphan wells, contamination)
        ↓
DOUBLE NEGLIGENCE (broken treaty + orphan wells)
        ↓
THE CLAIM (self-executing, retroactive, non-derogable)
```

The geometry is universal. The pattern is identical in every territory. The Crown's negligence is not a Canadian phenomenon — it is a **colonial phenomenon**. Every Crown that signed treaties and permitted extraction created the same double negligence. Every orphan well is evidence of that negligence. Every contaminated aquifer, every poisoned waterway, every uninhabitable zone is the Crown's promise made toxic.

### The Remedy

The double negligence demands a double remedy across all territories:

1. **Treaty Remedy:** Full implementation of all treaty promises — or, where treaties were never signed (Australia), formal recognition and reparations for the absence of treaties.

2. **Environmental Remedy:** Complete remediation of all orphan wells in all territories, funded by the colonial successor state (not industry, not indigenous communities), with indigenous-led reclamation.

3. **Compensatory Remedy:** Damages for compound harm — decades of lost land use, contaminated water, health impacts, and economic deprivation from the simultaneous treaty failure and environmental negligence.

4. **Structural Remedy:** Co-management of all industrial activity on indigenous lands, consistent with the most advanced frameworks (Treaty 8 Planning and Mitigation Measures, Sahtu Agreement co-management), applied globally.

5. **Accountability Remedy:** Public accounting of every colonial treaty obligation, every breach, every orphan well, and every contaminated site. The geometry must be made visible. The double negligence must be documented. The claim must be heard.
