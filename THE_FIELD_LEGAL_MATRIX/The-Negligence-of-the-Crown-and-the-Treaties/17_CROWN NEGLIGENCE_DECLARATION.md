## PART ONE: THE DECLARATION

### I. The Crown Made Specific Promises in Each Treaty

The Crown entered into treaties with Indigenous peoples spanning three centuries. These were not casual agreements. They were solemn, binding, constitutionally protected obligations — promises made in exchange for land cession, promises that induced Indigenous peoples to surrender territories they had occupied since time immemorial.

**The Numbered Treaties (1-11, 1871-1921) promised:**

| Promise | Treaty Reference | Status |
|---------|-----------------|--------|
| Reserves of one square mile per family of five | Treaties 1-11 | UNFULFILLED — systematic shortfalls documented across all treaties |
| Education on reserves | Treaties 1-11 | BREACHED — 139 residential schools operated for cultural destruction, not education |
| Healthcare and medicine | Treaty 6 (medicine chest clause); oral promises in Treaties 4, 10, 11 | BREACHED — clause reinterpreted, underfunded, denied |
| Annual annuities ($5/person) | Treaties 1-11 | FROZEN for 150 years — 97% real value decline |
| Hunting and fishing rights | Treaties 3-11 | RESTRICTED — provincial regulations effectively prohibit treaty hunting in many areas |
| Agricultural benefits ("cows and plows") | Treaties 1-11 | UNFULFILLED — implements "too poor-quality or too wild to be of any use" |
| Protection from famine and pestilence | Treaty 6 | BREACHED — malnutrition, tuberculosis, opioid crisis, boil water advisories |

**The Robinson Treaties (1850) promised:**
- Land rights and reserves around Lake Superior and Lake Huron
- Annual annuities with Augmentation Clause (increasing if economic conditions permitted)
- Hunting and fishing rights

**The Crown promised, and the Indigenous peoples ceded. The bargain was clear. The Crown broke its side.**

### II. The Crown Failed to Deliver on ALL Promises

The evidence of systematic Crown failure is not circumstantial. It is documented by the Crown's own institutions, admitted by the Crown's own government, and confirmed by the Crown's own courts.

**Every category of promise was breached:**

**Land:**

- Treaty 9: Missanabie Cree entitled to reserves — no reserve ever set aside — *Fletcher v. Ontario*, 2024 ONCA 148
- Treaty 10: Clear Lake Band entitled to 30,208 acres — received 27,080 acres

**Education:**
- 150,000 children attended 139 residential schools designed for cultural destruction
- The Truth and Reconciliation Commission (2015): "cultural genocide"

- Over 4,100 children documented dead; thousands of unmarked graves identified
- Last school closed in 1996 — 125 years after promises began

**Healthcare:**
- Treaty 6 medicine chest clause: "all medicines, drugs or medical supplies were to be supplied free of charge to Treaty Indians" (*Dreaver v. The King*, 1935) — never fully implemented
- Boil water advisories: 39+ long-term advisories in effect as of 2026

- Life expectancy gap: First Nations 62.81 years vs. non-First Nations 81.88 years — a 19-year gap

- 23 agricultural assistance settlements totaling $3.656 billion in 2022-2023 alone

**Hunting and Fishing:**
- Provincial restrictions effectively prohibit treaty hunting in many areas
- Commercial fishing rights denied despite *R. v. Marshall* (1999 SCC)
- "Taking up" of lands without adequate consultation

### III. The Courts Have Confirmed This Failure

The Crown's negligence is not alleged. It is adjudicated.

**In *Ontario (Attorney General) v. Restoule*, 2024 SCC 27, the Supreme Court held:**

> "For well over a century, the Crown has shown itself to be a patently unreliable and untrustworthy treaty partner in relation to the augmentation promise. It has lost the moral authority to simply say 'trust us'."

Neither Canada nor Ontario disputed that they were in "longstanding breach" of the Robinson Treaties. The Crown admitted its own negligence. The Court confirmed it.

**In *Shot Both Sides v. Canada*, 2024 SCC, the Court found:**

> "The Crown's conduct during the reserve's creation was unconscionable" and "declaratory relief is warranted given the longevity and magnitude of the Crown's dishonourable conduct."

**In *Williams Lake Indian Band v. Canada*, 2018 SCC, the Court found:**

> "The failure of colonial officials to take even these most basic steps to determine which lands should be set aside was a breach of the Crown's fiduciary duty."

**In *Yahey v. British Columbia*, 2021 BCSC, the Court found:**

The Province **breached Treaty 8** by authorizing industrial development without cumulative impact assessment, rendering Blueberry River First Nations unable to meaningfully exercise treaty rights.

**The pattern is unmistakable:**

1. *Restoule* (2024): Crown is "patently unreliable and untrustworthy"
2. *Shot Both Sides* (2024): Crown's conduct was "unconscionable"
3. *Williams Lake* (2018): Crown breached fiduciary duty
4. *Yahey* (2021): Crown breached Treaty 8
5. *Fletcher* (2024): Crown failed to set aside reserves for over 100 years
6. *Dreaver* (1935): Crown failed to provide free medicines as promised

**This is not a single failure. This is a pattern of systematic, century-spanning, court-confirmed negligence.**
