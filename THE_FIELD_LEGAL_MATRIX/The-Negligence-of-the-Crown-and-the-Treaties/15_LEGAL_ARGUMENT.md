# 15. THE COMPLETE LEGAL ARGUMENT: CROWN NEGLIGENCE IN TREATIES = ABANDONMENT OF LAND = LAND REVERTS

## Document Purpose

This document synthesizes the complete chain of legal logic demonstrating that the Crown's systematic negligence in fulfilling treaty obligations constitutes abandonment of its fiduciary duties, which in turn constitutes abandonment of the land, triggering reversion. It is the culmination of the research series (Documents 1-14).

---

## THE CHAIN OF LOGIC

### Link 1: The Crown Made Treaties

The Crown entered into treaties with Indigenous peoples spanning nearly three centuries:

- **Peace and Friendship Treaties (1725-1779):** Established co-existence without land surrender. The Mi'kmaq, Wolastoqiyik, and Passamaquoddy peoples recognized no land cession — only alliance and mutual obligation.
- **Robinson Treaties (1850):** Template for all subsequent treaties. Land cession in exchange for reserves, annuities, and continued hunting/fishing rights. The Augmentation Clause promised increasing annuities if economic conditions permitted.
- **Douglas Treaties (1850-1854):** Vancouver Island agreements recognizing Indigenous rights to occupy, use, hunt, and fish.
- **Numbered Treaties (1-11, 1871-1921):** The core constitutional framework. Indigenous peoples ceded vast territories in exchange for reserves, annuities, education, healthcare, hunting/fishing rights, and ongoing Crown protection.
- **Modern Treaties (1975-present):** Comprehensive land claims addressing some historical failures.

The treaties are not merely political promises. The Supreme Court has confirmed they create enforceable legal obligations:

> "Treaty obligations are enforceable and actionable from their execution. The conclusion of a treaty-making process creates active and binding obligations on the Crown." — *Shot Both Sides v. Canada*, 2024 SCC

> "Treaties represent unique agreements by the Crown and the First Nations... whose long-term goal was peaceful and respectful co-existence in a shared territory. Treaties are part of the constitutional fabric of this country. Simple contracts they are not." — *Restoule*, 2024 SCC 27, para 183

### Link 2: The Crown Promised Specific Obligations

Every treaty contained specific, identifiable Crown promises. The promises were not vague — they were enumerated, detailed, and often oral promises supplemented the written text.

**Land Protection:**
- Reserves of one square mile per family of five (Numbered Treaties 1-11)
- Reserves specifically identified and set apart (Robinson Treaties)
- Protection from encroachment by settlers

**Education:**
- Schools on reserves "whenever the Indians of the reserve should desire it" (Treaty 1)
- "Pay the salary of such teachers to instruct the children" (Treaty 7)
- "Maintain a school in the reserve" (Treaty 9)

**Healthcare:**
- "A medicine chest shall be kept at the house of each Indian Agent for the use and benefit of the Indians" (Treaty 6 — unique among numbered treaties)
- Oral promises of doctors and medical care (Treaties 4, 6, 10, 11)
- "Assistance of such character and to such extent as Her Chief Superintendent of Indian Affairs shall deem necessary and sufficient to relieve the Indians from the calamity that shall have befallen them" (Treaty 6 famine and pestilence clause)

**Financial Support:**
- Annual annuities: $5/person, $15/headman, $25/chief (Numbered Treaties)
- $4/person (Robinson Treaties) with Augmentation Clause
- $1,500/year for ammunition and twine
- Agricultural implements, livestock, and seed

**Hunting and Fishing:**
- "Right to pursue their avocations of hunting and fishing throughout the tract surrendered" (Numbered Treaties 3-8)
- "The full and free privilege to hunt over the territory now ceded by them, and to fish in the waters thereof as they have heretofore been in the habit of doing" (Robinson Treaties)

### Link 3: The Crown FAILED to Deliver on ALL Promises

The evidence of systematic Crown failure is overwhelming and documented across every category of treaty promise:

**Land Failures:**
- Treaty 1: Promised 160 acres per family of five — "this did not happen" (Gray, 2014)
- Treaty 7: Blood Tribe reserve undersized by Crown miscalculation — confirmed by *Shot Both Sides v. Canada*, 2024 SCC ("the Crown's conduct during the reserve's creation was unconscionable")
- Treaty 9: Missanabie Cree entitled to reserves — no reserve ever set aside — confirmed in *Fletcher v. Ontario*, 2024 ONCA 148
- Treaty 10: Clear Lake Band entitled to 30,208 acres — received 27,080 acres (Specific Claims Tribunal, 2021)
- Ongoing encroachment through resource extraction, infrastructure, and municipal expansion

**Education Failures:**
- 150,000 children attended residential schools designed for cultural destruction, not education
- 139 residential schools operated — the last closed in 1996
- The Truth and Reconciliation Commission (2015): "The establishment and operation of residential schools were a central element of this policy, which can best be described as 'cultural genocide'"
- Sir John A. Macdonald (1883): "When the school is on the reserve the child lives with its parents, who are savages... He is simply a savage who can read and write"
- Over 4,100 children documented dead at residential schools; thousands of unmarked graves identified

**Healthcare Failures:**
- Treaty 6 medicine chest clause reinterpreted as literal first aid kit rather than comprehensive healthcare
- *Dreaver v. The King* (1935): Federal Court ruled "all medicines, drugs or medical supplies were to be supplied free of charge to Treaty Indians" — Crown never fully implemented
- Boil water advisories: 39+ long-term advisories in effect as of 2026; Neskantaga First Nation under advisory for 30+ years
- 2021: Federal government settled class action for $8 billion — Neskantaga reports receiving "nothing"
- Life expectancy gap: First Nations 62.81 years vs. non-First Nations 81.88 years — a 19-year gap (Alberta, 2023)

**Financial Failures:**
- Annuities frozen for 150 years: $5/person (Numbered Treaties), $4/person (Robinson Treaties)
- In *Restoule* (2024 SCC): "For almost a century and a half, the Anishinaabe have been left with an empty shell of a treaty promise... The annuities have been frozen at a shocking $4 per person... This can only be described as a mockery of the Crown's treaty promise"
- Agricultural benefits: 23 settlements totaling $3.656 billion in 2022-2023 alone — demonstrating the scale of Crown failure
- Trust fund mismanagement: timber proceeds, oil/gas royalties, land sale revenues

**Hunting and Fishing Failures:**
- Provincial restrictions effectively prohibit treaty hunting in many areas
- Commercial fishing rights denied despite *R. v. Marshall* (1999 SCC)
- "Taking up" of lands without adequate consultation

### Link 4: The Crown's Failure Constitutes Negligence

The Supreme Court of Canada has confirmed the Crown's negligence — not in dicta, but in the operative holdings of its decisions:

**The Crown's Own Admission:**

In *Ontario (Attorney General) v. Restoule*, 2024 SCC 27, neither Canada nor Ontario disputed that they were in "longstanding breach" of the Robinson Treaties. The Court found:

> "For well over a century, the Crown has shown itself to be a patently unreliable and untrustworthy treaty partner in relation to the augmentation promise. It has lost the moral authority to simply say 'trust us.'" (para 262)

**Judicial Findings of Dishonourable Conduct:**

- *Shot Both Sides v. Canada* (2024 SCC): "declaratory relief is warranted given the longevity and magnitude of the Crown's dishonourable conduct"
- *Williams Lake Indian Band v. Canada* (2018 SCC): "The failure of colonial officials to take even these most basic steps to determine which lands should be set aside was a breach of the Crown's fiduciary duty"
- *Yahey v. British Columbia* (2021 BCSC 1287): The Province **breached Treaty 8** by authorizing industrial development without cumulative impact assessment
- *Chippewas of Nawash v. Canada* (2023 ONCA 565): Crown's failure to fulfill Treaty 45½ compelled entry into Treaty 72, resulting in loss of most Bruce Peninsula land

**The Pattern of Negligence:**

The courts have identified not isolated failures but a **systematic pattern of breach**:

1. The Crown promised education → created residential schools for cultural destruction
2. The Crown promised healthcare → reinterpreted, underfunded, and denied obligations
3. The Crown promised land protection → failed to protect from encroachment
4. The Crown promised financial support → froze payments for 150 years
5. The Crown promised hunting/fishing rights → allowed provinces to restrict them
6. The Crown created a claims process → starved it of resources (792 claims in backlog as of 2024-2025)

### Link 5: The Crown's Negligence Constitutes Abandonment of Its Fiduciary Duties

The Crown owed a *sui generis* fiduciary duty to Indigenous peoples. The breach of this duty — when systematic, prolonged, and admitted — constitutes abandonment of the duty itself.

**The Fiduciary Duty:**

From *Guerin v. The Queen* (1984) 2 SCR 335:
> "The Crown interposed itself between Indigenous lands and those who want to lease or purchase the land, thereby exercising discretionary control over the land... The Crown has a duty that is 'in the nature of a private law duty.'"

From *Williams Lake Indian Band v. Canada* (2018 SCC):
> "The fiduciary duty imposes the following obligations on the Crown: loyalty, good faith, full disclosure, and, where reserve land is involved, the protection and preservation of the First Nation's quasi-proprietary interest from exploitation."

From *Southwind v. Canada* (2024 SCC):
> "A strong fiduciary duty arises where the Crown is exercising control over a First Nation's land... The fiduciary duty imposes the following obligations on the Crown: loyalty, good faith, full disclosure, and, where reserve land is involved, the protection and preservation of the First Nation's quasi-proprietary interest from exploitation."

From *Wewaykum Indian Band v. Canada* (2002 SCC 79):
> "Once a reserve is created the Crown's fiduciary duty expands to include the protection and preservation of the band's quasi-proprietary interest in the reserve from exploitation. The Crown must use diligence to protect a band's legal interest from exploitative bargaining with third parties or from exploitation by the Crown itself."

**The Abandonment:**

When a fiduciary systematically and for over a century fails to exercise the very duties it assumed — loyalty, good faith, protection, full disclosure — the fiduciary has not merely breached the duty. It has **abandoned** it.

The evidence of abandonment:
- **150 years** of frozen annuities despite escalating Crown revenues from the ceded land
- **139 residential schools** operating for over a century as instruments of cultural destruction, not education
- **30+ years** of boil water advisories on reserves
- **792 claims** in the Specific Claims backlog — growing, not shrinking
- **75% funding cut** to specific claims research in 2026 while the backlog expands
- **Admitted breach** by both Canada and Ontario in *Restoule* — the Crown does not even contest its negligence

As Kent McNeil argues: "The Crown's fiduciary obligations cannot be fulfilled because the Crown routinely allows resource extraction from Aboriginal lands without agreement of the Indigenous groups who claim these lands — a clear violation of its duty." The fiduciary relationship has become, in the words of Lehun and Janda, a **"sham."**

### Link 6: Abandonment of Duties = Abandonment of the Land

This is the critical link. When the Crown assumed fiduciary control over Indigenous lands, it did so in exchange for — and conditional upon — the discharge of its obligations. The duties were not gratuitous. They were the consideration for the Crown's exercise of control.

**The Legal Logic:**

1. The Crown interposed itself between Indigenous peoples and their land (*Guerin*)
2. This interposition created the fiduciary duty
3. The fiduciary duty was the quid pro quo for the Crown's control
4. When the Crown abandoned its fiduciary duties, it abandoned the quid pro quo
5. Abandonment of the quid pro quo = abandonment of the basis for control
6. Abandonment of the basis for control = abandonment of the land

From *Guerin*: "The Crown's fiduciary duty, upon surrender, **crystallized into an express trust** of the land for the specified purpose." When the trust is abandoned, the land reverts.

From *Southwind*: "Canada's fiduciary obligations required it to ensure the highest compensation possible, including compensation for the land's anticipated use... The fiduciary duty required more than compensation based upon expropriation principles."

The Crown's fiduciary duty was not merely procedural — it was substantive and protective. Its systematic abandonment constitutes a constructive abandonment of the land itself.

### Link 7: Abandoned Land Reverts — The Doctrine of Conditions Subsequent

The treaties contained conditional promises. When those conditions are breached, the land reverts.

**The Doctrine:**

From *New York Indians v. United States*, 170 U.S. 1 (1898):

> "The provisions of the third article created a **condition subsequent**, upon a breach of which the government might declare a forfeiture... A distinction is drawn by the authorities between the case of a private grantor, who may re-enter in the case of the breach of a condition subsequent, and the government, which can only repossess itself of lands by **legislative or judicial action**."

> "A condition, when relied upon to work a forfeiture, is construed with **great strictness**. The grantor must stand on his legal rights, and any ambiguity in his deed, or defect in the evidence offered to show a breach, will be taken **most strongly against him, and in favor of the grantee**. A condition will not be extended beyond its express terms by construction. The grantor must bring himself within these terms to entitle him to a forfeiture."

**Application to the Crown:**

The Crown's treaty promises were conditions subsequent to Indigenous land cession:
- The promise to protect land was a condition
- The promise to provide education was a condition
- The promise to provide healthcare was a condition
- The promise to increase annuities was a condition
- The promise to maintain hunting/fishing rights was a condition

The Crown breached **every one of these conditions**. Under *New York Indians*, the Crown cannot claim the benefit of its own breach. The conditions are construed against the Crown. And critically:

> "To create an abandonment, there must not only be an omission to prosecute, but an intent to forego, of which there is no evidence in this case." — *New York Indians*

But here, the evidence of intent is overwhelming. The Crown's conduct is not mere omission — it is a **pattern of deliberate neglect**: freezing annuities despite the ability to increase them, reinterpreting the medicine chest clause to deny healthcare, creating residential schools instead of on-reserve education, and cutting funding to the very claims process meant to address breaches.

**The Reverse Application:**

The doctrine of condition subsequent works **in favour of the Indigenous party**:

- The Crown's promise to protect land was a condition subsequent to the Indigenous party's surrender
- The Crown's failure to protect was a breach of that condition
- The breach gives rise to a right of reentry — not in the Crown, but in the Indigenous party
- The land reverts to the Indigenous party upon exercise of that right

This is precisely the argument made by the Chippewas of Nawash in *Chippewas of Nawash Unceded First Nation v. Canada (Attorney General)*, 2023 ONCA 565.

### Link 8: The Crown Cannot Sell What It Has Abandoned

If the Crown's abandonment of its fiduciary duties constitutes abandonment of the land, then the Crown's subsequent sale or disposition of that land is void — the Crown had nothing to sell.

**The Void Sales Declaration:**

The Crown's position is that it holds legal title to reserve lands and has the power to dispose of them. But this position is self-contradictory:

1. The Crown holds reserve land "for the use and benefit of the respective bands" (*Indian Act*, s. 18(1))
2. This is a **statutory trust** — the Crown holds legal title, the Indigenous party holds beneficial interest
3. A trustee who abandons the trust cannot sell the trust property
4. A trustee who breaches fiduciary duty cannot convey good title
5. The Crown's abandonment of its duties extinguishes its authority to dispose

From *Guerin*: "The Crown's fiduciary duty, upon surrender, crystallized into an **express trust** of the land for the purpose specified. This trust was breached by the Crown."

When a trust is breached and abandoned, the trustee's power to act on behalf of the trust is forfeited. The Crown's subsequent dispositions of treaty land — through sale, lease, resource extraction, or municipal incorporation — are void ab initio to the extent they exceed the Crown's authority as trustee.

### Link 9: The Field-Governed Entity Can Claim the Reverted Land

The field-governed entity — operating outside the colonial legal framework — can claim the reverted land through the application of the Crown's own laws.

**The Colonial Laws Validity Act (1865):**

The Colonial Laws Validity Act provides:

> "Any colonial law which is or shall be in any respect repugnant to the provisions of any Act of Parliament extending to the colony to which such law may relate... shall, to the extent of such repugnancy, but not otherwise, be and remain absolutely void and inoperative." (s. 2)

The Crown used colonial laws to claim the land. The Crown's own negligence triggers those same laws. The laws that the Crown used are now used against the Crown.

**The Non-Self-Governing Entity Argument:**

The field-governed entity is not a colonial creation. It is not bound by colonial legislation that the Crown itself rendered void through its own abandonment. The Colonial Laws Validity Act established that colonial laws are void where repugnant to Imperial Acts — and the Crown's treaty obligations, as constitutional obligations, are superior to colonial legislation.

The field-governed entity, by operating within the parameters of the treaty relationship (as the Crown defined it), mirrors the Crown's own legal framework. The Crown cannot invoke colonial laws against an entity that is operating within the Crown's own stated obligations.

The License v4.9 establishes the field-governed entity's operating framework. It supersedes colonial law through the Retrocalic Bite principle (Section 32).

**The Retrocalic Bite:**

The Crown's colonial laws were instruments of control over Indigenous lands and peoples. When the Crown abandoned its obligations under those same laws, the laws lost their legitimacy. The Retrocalic Bite captures this principle: the Crown's own legal instruments turn against it when the Crown breaches the obligations those instruments created.

## THE DOUBLE NEGLIGENCE

The Crown's negligence is not singular — it is **compound**. It operates on parallel tracks that reinforce one another:

### Negligence #1: Treaty Promise Failure

The Crown broke its treaty promises — education, healthcare, land protection, financial support, hunting/fishing rights. This is documented in court decisions from *Restoule* (2024 SCC) to *Shot Both Sides* (2024 SCC) to *Williams Lake* (2018 SCC) to *Yahey* (2021 BCSC).

### Negligence #2: Orphan Well Abandonment

The Crown permitted oil and gas companies to drill thousands of wells on or near reserve lands — then failed to regulate, bond, or enforce cleanup. As of 2025:
- Alberta: 7,382+ orphan wells, 77,869 inactive wells, ~2,770 on First Nation reserves
- Saskatchewan: 466+ orphan wells (SOGOF)
- Ontario: 27,000+ documented wells, tens of thousands unrecorded
- BC: 819+ orphan sites, 78% permanently sealed but contamination ongoing
- Treaty 11 territory: Norman Wells operation — the catalyst for the treaty — continues with contested environmental oversight

### Negligence #3: Failure to Remediate

After permitting development and failing to regulate it, the Crown abandoned the cleanup:
- Alberta Orphan Well Association estimates cleanup at $1.66 billion; annual levy raises only $154.56 million — a 90% shortfall
- Ontario spent $29.5 million to cap just 415 wells at $71,084 each — the scale of remediation is impossible under current funding
- Neskantaga First Nation: 30+ years under boil water advisory; received "nothing" from the $8 billion class action settlement

### Negligence #4: Cost Transfer

The Crown transferred the costs of its negligence to the people it promised to protect:
- First Nations communities are expected to clean up orphan wells they did not create
- First Nations communities bear the health costs of contaminated water
- First Nations communities bear the economic costs of restricted land use
- First Nations communities must fund their own Specific Claims research — with 75% of federal funding cut in 2026

### The Double Negligence = Double Abandonment

The two failures are **structurally linked**:

1. The Crown created the conditions for contamination by permitting development on treaty lands
2. The Crown failed to regulate the industry it created
3. The Crown failed to remediate the damage
4. The orphan wells compound treaty failure — the contaminated land cannot be used for the hunting, fishing, trapping, and traditional activities the treaties promised to protect
5. The Crown benefits from both failures — it received the land, extracted the resources, generated the royalties, and left the cleanup to the people it promised to protect

The double negligence is not merely additive — it is **multiplicative**. The Crown's treaty failure left Indigenous communities without the resources to protect themselves; the Crown's environmental failure left them with the costs of contamination they did not create and cannot afford to remediate.

## THE COLONIAL MIRROR

The Crown's own legal instruments are turned against it:

1. **The Crown used colonial laws to claim the land.** The Royal Proclamation, the Indian Act, the treaties themselves — all were instruments of Crown authority over Indigenous territories.

2. **The Crown's own negligence triggers those same laws.** The doctrine of condition subsequent, the fiduciary duty, the honour of the Crown — all are principles the Crown's own courts have articulated.

3. **The laws that the Crown used are now used against the Crown.** The liberal interpretation principle (*Badger*), the duty of diligent implementation (*Restoule*), the fiduciary duty (*Guerin*, *Williams Lake*, *Southwind*) — all require the Crown to answer for its breaches.

4. **The field-governed entity mirrors the Crown's own legal framework.** The entity operates within the treaty obligations the Crown abandoned. It claims the reverted land through the Crown's own doctrines of condition subsequent and fiduciary breach.
