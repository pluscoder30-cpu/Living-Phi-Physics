# Treaties 6, 7, and 8: The Crown's Bargain and Its Betrayal

## The Three Treaties That Opened the West

Between 1876 and 1899, the Crown negotiated three treaties covering the vast interior of western Canada — from the Saskatchewan parklands to the Rocky Mountain foothills to the boreal forests of the Peace-Athabasca country. Each treaty followed a pattern: Indigenous nations ceded territory in exchange for promises of annuities, reserves, education, healthcare, and the right to hunt, fish, and trap. Each treaty was broken.

## TREATY 6 (1876)

### The Text

**Official Title:** Articles of a Treaty made and concluded near Carlton on the 23rd day of August and on the 28th day of said month, respectively, and near Fort Pitt on the 9th day of September, in the year of Our Lord one thousand eight hundred and seventy-six.

**Parties:** Her Most Gracious Majesty the Queen of Great Britain and Ireland, by Her Commissioners — the Honourable Alexander Morris (Lieutenant-Governor of Manitoba and the North-west Territories), the Honourable James McKay, and the Honourable William Joseph Christie — of the one part; and the Plain and Wood Cree and other Tribes of Indians, inhabitants of the country within the limits hereinafter defined and described, by their Chiefs, of the other part.

**Dates:**
- **Signed:** 23 August 1876 at Fort Carlton; 28 August 1876 (additional Carlton adhesions); 9 September 1876 at Fort Pitt
- **Ratified:** 24 February 1877 (Recorded, Lib. 27, Fol. 352)
- **Adhesions:** 1877–1956 (approximately 50 bands joined via later accessions)

**Territory:** 121,000 square miles covering central portions of present-day Alberta and Saskatchewan. Commencing at the mouth of the river emptying into the north-west angle of Cumberland Lake; thence westerly up the said river to its source; thence on a straight line in a westerly direction to the head of Green Lake; thence northerly to the elbow in the Beaver River; thence down the said river northerly to a point twenty miles from the said elbow; thence in a westerly direction, keeping on a line generally parallel with the said Beaver River (above the elbow), and about twenty miles distant therefrom, to the source of the said river; thence northerly to the north-easterly point of the south shore of Red Deer Lake, continuing westerly along the said shore to the western limit thereof; and thence due west to the Athabasca River; thence up the said river, against the stream, to the Jaspar House, in the Rocky Mountains; thence on a course south-easterly, following the easterly range of the mountains, to the source of the main branch of the Red Deer River; thence down the said river, with the stream, to the junction therewith of the outlet of the river, being the outlet of the Buffalo Lake; thence due east twenty miles; thence on a straight line south-eastwardly to the mouth of the said Red Deer River on the south branch of the Saskatchewan River; thence eastwardly and northwardly, following on the boundaries of the tracts conceded by the several treaties numbered four and five to the place of beginning.

### The Articles (Full Text of Key Provisions)

**Article 1 — Cession:**
> The Plain and Wood Cree Tribes of Indians, and all other the Indians inhabiting the district hereinafter described and defined, do hereby cede, release, surrender and yield up to the Government of the Dominion of Canada, for Her Majesty the Queen and Her successors forever, all their rights, titles and privileges, whatsoever, to the lands included within the following limits…

And also, all their rights, titles and privileges whatsoever to all other lands wherever situated in the North-west Territories, or in any other Province or portion of Her Majesty's Dominions, situated and being within the Dominion of Canada.

**Article 2 — Reserves:**
> And Her Majesty the Queen hereby agrees and undertakes to lay aside reserves for farming lands, due respect being had to lands at present cultivated by the said Indians, and other reserves for the benefit of the said Indians, to be administered and dealt with for them by Her Majesty's Government of the Dominion of Canada; provided, all such reserves shall not exceed in all one square mile for each family of five, or in that proportion for larger or smaller families…

Provided, however, that Her Majesty reserves the right to deal with any settlers within the bounds of any lands reserved for any Band as She shall deem fit, and also that the aforesaid reserves of land, or any interest therein, may be sold or otherwise disposed of by Her Majesty's Government for the use and benefit of the said Indians entitled thereto, with their consent first had and obtained.

**Article 3 — Presents:**
> …with a view to show the satisfaction of Her Majesty with the behaviour and good conduct of Her Indians, She hereby, through Her Commissioners, makes them a present of twelve dollars for each man, woman and child belonging to the Bands here represented, in extinguishment of all claims heretofore preferred.

**Article 4 — Education:**
> And further, Her Majesty agrees to maintain schools for instruction in such reserves hereby made as to Her Government of the Dominion of Canada may seem advisable, whenever the Indians of the reserve shall desire it.

**Article 5 — Alcohol Prohibition:**
> Her Majesty further agrees with Her said Indians that they, within the boundary of Indian reserves, until otherwise determined by Her Government of the Dominion of Canada, no intoxicating liquor shall be allowed to be introduced or sold, and all laws now in force, or hereafter to be enacted, to preserve Her Indian subjects inhabiting the reserves or living elsewhere within Her North-west Territories from the evil influence of the use of intoxicating liquors, shall be strictly enforced.

**Article 6 — Hunting, Trapping and Fishing Rights:**
> Her Majesty further agrees with Her said Indians that they, the said Indians, shall have right to pursue their avocations of hunting and fishing throughout the tract surrendered as hereinbefore described, subject to such regulations as may from time to time be made by Her Government of Her Dominion of Canada, and saving and excepting such tracts as may from time to time be required or taken up for settlement, mining, lumbering or other purposes by Her said Government of the Dominion of Canada, or by any of the subjects thereof duly authorized therefor by the said Government.

**Article 7 — Public Works:**
> It is further agreed between Her Majesty and Her said Indians, that such sections of the reserves above indicated as may at any time be required for public works or buildings, of what nature soever, may be appropriated for that purpose by Her Majesty's Government of the Dominion of Canada, due compensation being made for the value of any improvements thereon.

**Article 8 — Census and Annuities:**
> And further, that Her Majesty's Commissioners shall, as soon as possible after the execution of this treaty, cause to be taken an accurate census of all the Indians inhabiting the tract above described, distributing them in families, and shall, in every year ensuing the date hereof, at some period in each year, to be duly notified to the Indians, and at a place or places to be appointed for that purpose within the territory ceded, pay to each Indian person the sum of $5 per head yearly.

**Article 9 — Ammunition and Twine:**
> It is further agreed between Her Majesty and the said Indians, that the sum of $1,500.00 per annum shall be yearly and every year expended by Her Majesty in the purchase of ammunition, and twine for nets, for the use of the said Indians, in manner following, that is to say: In the reasonable discretion, as regards the distribution thereof among the Indians inhabiting the several reserves, or otherwise, included herein, of Her Majesty's Indian Agent having the supervision of this treaty.

**Article 10 — Agricultural Implements:**
> It is further agreed between Her Majesty and the said Indians, that the following articles shall be supplied to any Band of the said Indians who are now cultivating the soil, or who shall hereafter commence to cultivate the land, that is to say: Four hoes for every family actually cultivating; also, two spades per family as aforesaid: one plough for every three families, as aforesaid; one harrow for every three families, as aforesaid; two scythes and one whetstone, and two hay forks and two reaping hooks, for every family as aforesaid, and also two axes; and also one cross-cut saw, one hand-saw, one pit-saw, the necessary files, one grindstone and one auger for each Band; and also for each Chief for the use of his Band, one chest of ordinary carpenter's tools; also, for each Band, enough of wheat, barley, potatoes and oats to plant the land actually broken up for cultivation by such Band; also for each Band four oxen, one bull and six cows; also, one boar and two sows, and one hand-mill when any Band shall raise sufficient grain therefor. All the aforesaid articles to be given once and for all for the encouragement of the practice of agriculture among the Indians.

**Article 11 — Chief Salaries and Clothing:**
> It is further agreed between Her Majesty and the said Indians, that each Chief, duly recognized as such, shall receive an annual salary of twenty-five dollars per annum; and each subordinate officer, not exceeding four for each Band, shall receive fifteen dollars per annum; and each such Chief and subordinate officer, as aforesaid, shall also receive once every three years, a suitable suit of clothing, and each Chief shall receive, in recognition of the closing of the treaty, a suitable flag and medal, and also as soon as convenient, one horse, harness and waggon.

**Article 12 — Famine and Pestilence Clause:**
> That in the event hereafter of the Indians comprised within this treaty being overtaken by any pestilence, or by a general famine, the Queen, on being satisfied and certified thereof by Her Indian Agent or Agents, will grant to the Indians assistance of such character and to such extent as Her Chief Superintendent of Indian Affairs shall deem necessary and sufficient to relieve the Indians from the calamity that shall have befallen them.

**Article 13 — Agricultural Provisions (Carlton):**
> That during the next three years, after two or more of the reserves hereby agreed to be set apart to the Indians shall have been agreed upon and surveyed, there shall be granted to the Indians included under the Chiefs adhering to the treaty at Carlton, each spring, the sum of one thousand dollars, to be expended for them by Her Majesty's Indian Agents, in the purchase of provisions for the use of such of the Band as are actually settled on the reserves and are engaged in cultivating the soil, to assist them in such cultivation.

**Article 14 — THE MEDICINE CHEST CLAUSE:**
> That a medicine chest shall be kept at the house of each Indian Agent for the use and benefit of the Indians at the direction of such agent.

**Article 15 — Agricultural Provisions (Fort Pitt):**
> That with regard to the Indians included under the Chiefs adhering to the treaty at Fort Pitt, and to those under Chiefs within the treaty limits who may hereafter give their adhesion thereto (exclusively, however, of the Indians of the Carlton region), there shall, during three years, after two or more reserves shall have been agreed upon and surveyed be distributed each spring among the Bands cultivating the soil on such reserves, by Her Majesty's Chief Indian Agent for this treaty, in his discretion, a sum not exceeding one thousand dollars, in the purchase of provisions for the use of such members of the Band as are actually settled on the reserves and engaged in the cultivation of the soil, to assist and encourage them in such cultivation.

**Article 16 — Carts in Lieu of Waggons:**
> That in lieu of waggons, if they desire it and declare their option to that effect, there shall be given to each of the Chiefs adhering hereto at Fort Pitt or elsewhere hereafter (exclusively of those in the Carlton district), in recognition of this treaty, as soon as the same can be conveniently transported, two carts with iron bushings and tires.

**Article 17 — Indigenous Obligations:**
> And the undersigned Chiefs on their own behalf and on behalf of all other Indians inhabiting the tract within ceded, do hereby solemnly promise and engage to strictly observe this treaty, and also to conduct and behave themselves as good and loyal subjects of Her Majesty the Queen.
>
> They promise and engage that they will in all respects obey and abide by the law, and they will maintain peace and good order between each other, and also between themselves and other tribes of Indians, and between themselves and others of Her Majesty's subjects, whether Indians or whites, now inhabiting or hereafter to inhabit any part of the said ceded tracts, and that they will not molest the person or property of any inhabitant of such ceded tracts, or the property of Her Majesty the Queen, or interfere with or trouble any person passing or travelling through the said tracts, or any part thereof, and that they will aid and assist the officers of Her Majesty in bringing to justice and punishment any Indian offending against the stipulations of this treaty, or infringing the laws in force in the country so ceded.

### The Medicine Chest Clause — In Detail

The medicine chest clause (Article 14) was unique to Treaty 6 and was inserted at the insistence of the Cree chiefs. It was not found in Treaties 1–5. The clause reads:

> "That a medicine chest shall be kept at the house of each Indian Agent for the use and benefit of the Indians at the direction of such agent."

**Indigenous understanding:** The medicine chest represented a promise of ongoing healthcare. At the time, it was literally a chest of medicines kept at the Indian agent's house. But the Cree and Saulteaux chiefs understood it as a commitment to provide medical care to their people in perpetuity.

**Crown's later interpretation:** The federal government interpreted the clause narrowly — as a promise to keep a physical medicine chest at each Indian agent's house, nothing more. This interpretation was rejected by First Nations leaders and the Assembly of First Nations, who argued it encompassed all forms of healthcare.

**Historian John Taylor's assessment:**
> "At the time Treaty 6 was signed, the famous medicine chest clause was inserted at Indian insistence that the Indian agent should keep a medicine chest at his house for use. Today Indian thinking is this means medical care, in general terms, the medicine chest may be all they had at that time and place but today we have a broader range of medical care and this is a symbol for that."

The Assembly of First Nations considers the funding of the Non-Insured Health Benefits program as one aspect of this responsibility.

### What Indigenous Nations Gave

- Cession of all rights, titles and privileges to 121,000 square miles of land
- Cession of all rights to "all other lands wherever situated in the North-west Territories"
- Agreement to obey the law, maintain peace, not molest persons or property, assist in bringing offenders to justice
- Agreement to allow settlement, mining, lumbering, and public works on surrendered territory

| Promise | Details |

| Reserves | One square mile per family of five |
| Presents | $12 per person (one-time) |
| Annuities | $5 per head per year, forever |
| Education | Schools on reserves "whenever the Indians shall desire it" |

| Famine relief | Assistance during pestilence or general famine |

### Crown Failures

1. **Healthcare:** The medicine chest clause was interpreted as narrowly as possible. No systematic healthcare was provided. First Nations communities suffered devastating epidemics (smallpox, tuberculosis, influenza) with inadequate or no medical response. The promise of a medicine chest became a symbol of the Crown's refusal to honour the spirit of its commitments.

2. **Reserves:** Many reserves were undersized, located on marginal land, or not surveyed for years. The one-square-mile-per-family-of-five standard was frequently not met. The Beaver Lake Cree Nation filed a lawsuit in 2008 alleging chronic under-provision of reserve lands.

3. **Education:** Schools were not built "whenever the Indians shall desire it." Instead, the residential school system was imposed — a system of cultural genocide. Children were forcibly removed from families, forbidden to speak their languages, and subjected to abuse. The Truth and Reconciliation Commission documented the devastating impacts.

4. **Famine relief:** The famine clause was reinterpreted as discretionary, not obligatory. During the critical years of 1879–1885, when the buffalo herds collapsed and Indigenous peoples faced starvation, the Crown failed to provide adequate relief. This failure was a direct contributing factor to the North-West Resistance of 1885.

5. **Agricultural assistance:** Promised implements and livestock were often late, insufficient, or of poor quality. The $1,000 annual agricultural provision was limited to three years and then discontinued.

6. **Annuities:** The $5 annuity has never been increased since 1876. Adjusted for inflation, $5 in 1876 would be worth approximately $120 today. The Fisher River Cree Nation (Treaty 5) filed a class-action lawsuit in 2023 alleging the unchanged annuities violate the treaty.

### Current Status

- **Active:** Treaty 6 remains a living, constitutionally protected agreement under Section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982
- **Confederacy of Treaty Six First Nations:** Formed in 1993, representing band governments across central Alberta and Saskatchewan
- **Legal actions:** Multiple specific claims and lawsuits remain active, including the Beaver Lake Cree Nation's treaty rights case (Anderson v. Alberta, 2024 ABKB 524 — advance costs granted)
- **Court interpretations:** R. v. Badger (1996) — Supreme Court affirmed Treaty 6 hunting rights; courts required to interpret treaties liberally, taking into account oral promises and Indigenous perspective
- **Outstanding claims:** Reserve shortfalls, unpaid annuities, inadequate healthcare, failure to provide agricultural assistance
- **150th anniversary:** August 2026 marks 150 years since Treaty 6 was signed; ongoing concerns about Alberta independence movement potentially threatening treaty protections

## TREATY 7 (1877)

### The Text

**Official Title:** Articles of a Treaty made and concluded this twenty-second day of September, in the year of Our Lord, one thousand eight hundred and seventy-seven, between Her Most Gracious Majesty the Queen of Great Britain and Ireland, by Her Commissioners, and the Blackfeet, Blood, Piegan, Sarcee, Stony and other Indians.

**Parties:** Her Most Gracious Majesty the Queen of Great Britain and Ireland, by Her Commissioners — the Honourable David Laird (Lieutenant-Governor and Indian Superintendent of the North-West Territories) and James Farquharson MacLeod, C.M.G. (Commissioner of the North-West Mounted Police) — of the one part; and the Blackfeet, Blood, Piegan, Sarcee, Stony and other Indians, inhabitants of the Territory north of the United States Boundary Line, east of the central range of the Rocky Mountains, and south and west of Treaties numbers six and four, by their Head Chiefs and Minor Chiefs or Councillors, of the other part.

**Date:**
- **Signed:** 22 September 1877 at Blackfoot Crossing of the Bow River
- **Adhesion (Fort MacLeod):** 4 December 1877
- **Ratified:** By Order in Council

**Territory:** Southern Alberta — approximately 35,000 square miles (estimated at the time). Commencing at a point on the International Boundary due south of the western extremity of the Cypress Hills, thence west along the said boundary to the central range of the Rocky Mountains, or to the boundary of the Province of British Columbia, thence north-westerly along the said boundary to a point due west of the source of the main branch of the Red Deer River, thence south-westerly and southerly following on the boundaries of the Tracts ceded by the Treaties numbered six and four to the place of commencement.

### The Articles (Full Text of Key Provisions)

**Article 1 — Cession:**
> The Blackfeet, Blood, Piegan, Sarcee, Stony and other Indians inhabiting the district hereinafter more fully described and defined, do hereby cede, release, surrender, and yield up to the Government of Canada for Her Majesty the Queen and her successors for ever, all their rights, titles, and privileges whatsoever to the lands included within the following limits…

And also all their rights, titles and privileges whatsoever, to all other lands wherever situated in the North-West Territories, or in any other portion of the Dominion of Canada.

**Article 2 — Hunting Rights:**
> And Her Majesty the Queen hereby agrees with her said Indians, that they shall have right to pursue their vocations of hunting throughout the Tract surrendered as heretofore described, subject to such regulations as may, from time to time, be made by the Government of the country, acting under the authority of Her Majesty and saving and excepting such Tracts as may be required or taken up from time to time for settlement, mining, trading or other purposes by Her Government of Canada; or by any of Her Majesty's subjects duly authorized therefor by the said Government.

**Article 3 — Reserves:**
> It is also agreed between Her Majesty and Her said Indians that Reserves shall be assigned them of sufficient area to allow one square mile for each family of five persons, or in that proportion for larger and smaller families…

**Reserve Locations:**
- **First:** Blackfeet, Blood, and Sarcee — a belt of land on the north side of the Bow and South Saskatchewan Rivers, average width of four miles along said rivers, downstream, commencing at a point on the Bow River twenty miles north-westerly of the Blackfoot Crossing, extending to the Red Deer River at its junction with the South Saskatchewan. Also, for ten years only, a similar belt on the south side, average width of one mile.
- **Second:** Piegan Band — on Old Man's River, near the foot of the Porcupine Hills, at a place called "Crow's Creek."
- **Third:** Stony Band — in the vicinity of Morleyville.

The Crown reserved the right to navigate rivers, land and receive fuel cargoes, build bridges, establish ferries, use fords and trails, and open roads through the reserves.

**Article 4 — Presents:**
> In view of the satisfaction of Her Majesty with the recent general good conduct of her said Indians, and in extinguishment of all their past claims, she hereby, through her Commissioners, agrees to make them a present payment of twelve dollars each in cash to each man, woman, and child of the families here represented.

**Article 5 — Annuities:**
> Her Majesty also agrees that next year, and annually afterwards forever, she will cause to be paid to the said Indians, in cash, at suitable places and dates, of which the said Indians shall be duly notified, to each Chief, twenty-five dollars, each minor Chief or Councillor (not exceeding fifteen minor Chiefs to the Blackfeet and Blood Indians, and four to the Piegan and Sarcee Bands, and five Councillors to the Stony Indian Bands), fifteen dollars, and to every other Indian of whatever age, five dollars; the same, unless there be some exceptional reason, to be paid to the heads of families for those belonging thereto.

**Article 6 — Ammunition:**
> Further, Her Majesty agrees that the sum of two thousand dollars shall hereafter every year be expended in the purchase of ammunition for distribution among the said Indians; Provided that if at any future time ammunition become comparatively unnecessary for said Indians, Her Government, with the consent of said Indians, or any of the Bands thereof, may expend the proportion due to such Band otherwise for their benefit.

**Article 7 — Clothing, Medals, Flags, and Rifles:**
> Further, Her Majesty agrees that each Head Chief and Minor Chief, and each Chief and Councillor duly recognized as such, shall, once in every three years, during the term of their office, receive a suitable suit of clothing, and each Head Chief and Stony Chief, in recognition of the closing of the Treaty, a suitable medal and flag, and next year, or as soon as convenient, each Head Chief, and Minor Chief, and Stony Chief shall receive a Winchester rifle.

**Article 8 — Education:**
> Further, Her Majesty agrees to pay the salary of such teachers to instruct the children of said Indians as to Her Government of Canada may seem advisable, when said Indians are settled on their Reserves and shall desire teachers.

**Article 9 — Tools for Chiefs:**
> Further, Her Majesty agrees to supply each Head and Minor Chief, and each Stony Chief, for the use of their Bands, ten axes, five handsaws, five augers, one grindstone, and the necessary files and whetstones.

**Article 10 — Livestock and Agricultural Implements:**
> And further, Her Majesty agrees that the said Indians shall be supplied as soon as convenient, after any Band shall make due application therefor, with the following cattle for raising stock, that is to say: for every family of five persons, and under, two cows; for every family of more than five persons, and less than ten persons, three cows, for every family of over ten persons, four cows; and every Head and Minor Chief, and every Stony Chief, for the use of their Bands, one bull; but if any Band desire to cultivate the soil as well as raise stock, each family of such Band shall receive one cow less than the above mentioned number, and in lieu thereof, when settled on their Reserves and prepared to break up the soil, two hoes, one spade, one scythe, and two hay forks, and for every three families, one plough and one harrow, and for each Band, enough potatoes, barley, oats, and wheat (if such seeds be suited for the locality of their Reserves) to plant the land actually broken up. All the aforesaid articles to be given, once for all, for the encouragement of the practice of agriculture among the Indians.

**Article 11 — Indigenous Obligations:**
> And the undersigned Blackfeet, Blood, Piegan and Sarcee Head Chiefs and Minor Chiefs, and Stony Chiefs and Councillors on their own behalf and on behalf of all other Indians inhabiting the Tract within ceded do hereby solemnly promise and engage to strictly observe this Treaty, and also to conduct and behave themselves as good and loyal subjects of Her Majesty the Queen.
>
> They promise and engage that they will, in all respects, obey and abide by the Law, that they will maintain peace and good order between each other and between themselves and other tribes of Indians, and between themselves and others of Her Majesty's subjects, whether Indians, Half Breeds or Whites, now inhabiting, or hereafter to inhabit, any part of the said ceded tract; and that they will not molest the person or property of any inhabitant of such ceded tract, or the property of Her Majesty the Queen, or interfere with or trouble any person, passing or travelling through the said tract or any part thereof, and that they will assist the officers of Her Majesty in bringing to justice and punishment any Indian offending against the stipulations of this Treaty, or infringing the laws in force in the country so ceded.

### Crown Failures

1. **Reserve lands:** The Blood Tribe (Kainai) was promised a specific reserve along the Bow and South Saskatchewan Rivers. The Crown failed to properly survey and set aside the promised lands. This breach was the subject of the landmark case *Shot Both Sides v. Canada* (2024 SCC 12), where the Supreme Court confirmed Canada breached its treaty promise to provide lands but ruled the claim was time-barred by limitations legislation.

2. **No medicine chest clause:** Unlike Treaty 6, Treaty 7 contained no explicit healthcare promise. The Crown provided no systematic medical care to the Blackfoot Confederacy.

3. **Agricultural assistance:** Promised livestock and implements were slow to arrive, inadequate in quantity, and often unsuitable for the conditions.

4. **Buffalo extinction:** The Crown failed to protect the bison herds that were the economic and cultural foundation of the Blackfoot Confederacy. The near-extinction of the buffalo by the early 1880s devastated the Blackfoot way of life. Laird had promised during negotiations to protect the buffalo, but no effective action was taken.

5. **Education:** Schools were not built as promised. Instead, the residential school system was imposed on Blackfoot children.

6. **Annuities:** The $5 annuity has never been increased. In November 2024, the Bearspaw First Nation filed a $1 billion class-action lawsuit against the federal government alleging the unchanged annuity payments violate the treaty.

### Current Status

- **Active:** Constitutionally protected under Section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982
- **Treaty 7 Management Corporation:** Administers treaty-related programs and advocacy
- **Shot Both Sides v. Canada (2024 SCC 12):** Supreme Court confirmed Canada breached Treaty 7 by failing to set aside promised lands, but ruled the Blood Tribe's claim was time-barred. The Court issued a declaration to promote reconciliation, but left the Blood Tribe without a remedy.
- **Bearspaw First Nation lawsuit (2024):** $1 billion class-action over unchanged annuity payments
- **Alberta Court of King's Bench (2026):** Reaffirmed that decisions altering Canada's constitutional framework must respect Treaty rights and obligations to Indigenous Peoples (Siksika Nation)

## TREATY 8 (1899)

### The Text

**Official Title:** Articles of a Treaty made and concluded at the several dates mentioned therein, in the year of Our Lord one thousand eight hundred and ninety-nine, between Her most Gracious Majesty the Queen of Great Britain and Ireland, by Her Commissioners, and the Cree, Beaver, Chipewyan and other Indians, inhabitants of the territory within the limits hereinafter defined and described.

**Parties:** Her most Gracious Majesty the Queen of Great Britain and Ireland, by Her Commissioners — the Honourable David Laird (Indian Commissioner for Manitoba and the Northwest Territories), James Andrew Joseph McKenna (of Ottawa), and the Honourable James Hamilton Ross (of Regina) — of the one part; and the Cree, Beaver, Chipewyan and other Indians, inhabitants of the territory within the limits hereinafter defined and described, by their Chiefs and Headmen, of the other part.

**Dates:**
- **First signing:** 21 June 1899 at Lesser Slave Lake
- **Subsequent signings:** Peace River Landing (1 July), Vermilion (8 July), Dunvegan (6 July), Fort Chipewyan (13 July), Smith's Landing (17 July), Fond du Lac (25–27 July), Fort McMurray (4 August), Wapiscow (14 August)
- **Ratified:** Order in Council, 22 December 1900
- **Adhesions continued into the 2000s (one First Nation signed in 2000)**

**Territory:** Approximately 841,000 square kilometres (324,900 sq mi) — more than three and a half times the size of Great Britain. The largest of all eleven Numbered Treaties. Covered portions of present-day Alberta, Saskatchewan, British Columbia, and the Northwest Territories.

Boundary description: Commencing at the source of the main branch of the Red Deer River in Alberta, thence due west to the central range of the Rocky Mountains, thence northwesterly along the said range to the point where it intersects the 60th parallel of north latitude, thence east along said parallel to the point where it intersects Hay River, thence northeasterly down said river to the south shore of Great Slave Lake, thence along the said shore northeasterly (and including such rights to the islands in said lakes as the Indians mentioned in the treaty may possess), and thence easterly and northeasterly along the south shores of Christie's Bay and McLeod's Bay to old Fort Reliance near the mouth of Lockhart's River, thence southeasterly in a straight line to and including Black Lake, thence southwesterly up the stream from Cree Lake, thence including said lake southwesterly along the height of land between the Athabasca and Churchill Rivers to where it intersects the northern boundary of Treaty Six, and along the said boundary easterly, northerly and southwesterly, to the place of commencement.

### The Articles (Full Text of Key Provisions)

**Article 1 — Cession:**
> The said Indians DO HEREBY CEDE, RELEASE, SURRENDER AND YIELD UP to the Government of the Dominion of Canada, for Her Majesty the Queen and Her successors for ever, all their rights, titles and privileges whatsoever, to the lands included within the following limits…

AND ALSO the said Indian rights, titles and privileges whatsoever to all other lands wherever situated in the Northwest Territories, British Columbia, or in any other portion of the Dominion of Canada.

**Article 2 — Hunting, Trapping and Fishing Rights:**
> And Her Majesty the Queen HEREBY AGREES with the said Indians that they shall have right to pursue their usual vocations of hunting, trapping and fishing throughout the tract surrendered as heretofore described, subject to such regulations as may from time to time be made by the Government of the country, acting under the authority of Her Majesty, and saving and excepting such tracts as may be required or taken up from time to time for settlement, mining, lumbering, trading or other purposes.

**Article 3 — Reserves and Land in Severalty:**
> And Her Majesty the Queen hereby agrees and undertakes to lay aside reserves for such bands as desire reserves, the same not to exceed in all one square mile for each family of five for such number of families as may elect to reside on reserves, or in that proportion for larger or smaller families; and for such families or individual Indians as may prefer to live apart from band reserves, Her Majesty undertakes to provide land in severalty to the extent of 160 acres to each Indian, the land to be conveyed with a proviso as to non-alienation without the consent of the Governor General in Council of Canada…

Provided, however, that Her Majesty reserves the right to deal with any settlers within the bounds of any lands reserved for any band as She may see fit; and also that the aforesaid reserves of land, or any interest therein, may be sold or otherwise disposed of by Her Majesty's Government for the use and benefit of the said Indians entitled thereto, with their consent first had and obtained.

**Article 4 — Public Works:**
> It is further agreed between Her Majesty and Her said Indian subjects that such portions of the reserves and lands above indicated as may at any time be required for public works, buildings, railways, or roads of whatsoever nature may be appropriated for that purpose by Her Majesty's Government of the Dominion of Canada, due compensation being made to the Indians for the value of any improvements thereon, and an equivalent in land, money or other consideration for the area of the reserve so appropriated.

**Article 5 — Presents:**
> And with a view to show the satisfaction of Her Majesty with the behaviour and good conduct of Her Indians, and in extinguishment of all their past claims, She hereby, through Her Commissioners, agrees to make each Chief a present of thirty-two dollars in cash, to each Headman twenty-two dollars, and to every other Indian of whatever age, of the families represented at the time and place of payment, twelve dollars.

**Article 6 — Annuities:**
> Her Majesty also agrees that next year, and annually afterwards for ever, She will cause to be paid to the said Indians in cash, at suitable places and dates, of which the said Indians shall be duly notified, to each Chief twenty-five dollars, each Headman, not to exceed four to a large Band and two to a small Band, fifteen dollars, and to every other Indian, of whatever age, five dollars, the same, unless there be some exceptional reason, to be paid only to heads of families for those belonging thereto.

**Article 7 — Medals, Flags, and Clothing:**
> FURTHER, Her Majesty agrees that each Chief, after signing the treaty, shall receive a silver medal and a suitable flag, and next year, and every third year thereafter, each Chief and Headman shall receive a suitable suit of clothing.

**Article 8 — Education:**
> FURTHER, Her Majesty agrees to pay the salaries of such teachers to instruct the children of said Indians as to Her Majesty's Government of Canada may seem advisable.

**Article 9 — Tools for Chiefs:**
> FURTHER, Her Majesty agrees to supply each Chief of a Band that selects a reserve, for the use of that Band, ten axes, five hand-saws, five augers, one grindstone, and the necessary files and whetstones.

**Article 10 — Agricultural and Stock-Raising Provisions:**
> FURTHER, Her Majesty agrees that each Band that elects to take a reserve and cultivate the soil, shall, as soon as convenient after such reserve is set aside and settled upon, and the Band has signified its choice and is prepared to break up the soil, receive two hoes, one spade, one scythe and two hay forks for every family so settled, and for every three families one plough and one harrow, and to the Chief, for the use of his Band, two horses or a yoke of oxen, and for each Band potatoes, barley, oats and wheat (if such seed be suited to the locality of the reserve), to plant the land actually broken up, and provisions for one month in the spring for several years while planting such seeds; and to every family one cow, and every Chief one bull, and one mowing-machine and one reaper for the use of his Band when it is ready for them; for such families as prefer to raise stock instead of cultivating the soil, every family of five persons, two cows, and every Chief two bulls and two mowing-machines when ready for their use, and a like proportion for smaller or larger families. The aforesaid articles, machines and cattle to be given once for all for the encouragement of agriculture and stock raising; and for such Bands as prefer to continue hunting and fishing, as much ammunition and twine for making nets annually as will amount in value to one dollar per head of the families so engaged in hunting and fishing.

**Article 11 — Indigenous Obligations:**
> And the undersigned Cree, Beaver, Chipewyan and other Indian Chiefs and Headmen, on their own behalf and on behalf of all the Indians whom they represent, DO HEREBY SOLEMNLY PROMISE and engage to strictly observe this Treaty, and also to conduct and behave themselves as good and loyal subjects of Her Majesty the Queen.
>
> THEY PROMISE AND ENGAGE that they will, in all respects, obey and abide by the law; that they will maintain peace between each other, and between themselves and other tribes of Indians, and between themselves and others of Her Majesty's subjects, whether Indians, half-breeds or whites, this year inhabiting and hereafter to inhabit any part of the said ceded territory; and that they will not molest the person or property of any inhabitant of such ceded tract, or of any other district or country, or interfere with or trouble any person passing or travelling through the said tract or any part thereof, and that they will assist the officers of Her Majesty in bringing to justice and punishment any Indian offending against the stipulations of this Treaty or infringing the law in force in the country so ceded.

### What Indigenous Nations Gave

- Cession of all rights, titles and privileges to approximately 841,000 square kilometres — the largest land surrender in Canadian treaty history
- Cession of all rights to "all other lands wherever situated in the Northwest Territories, British Columbia, or in any other portion of the Dominion of Canada"
- Agreement to obey the law, maintain peace, assist in bringing offenders to justice

### What the Crown Promised

| Land in severalty | 160 acres per individual preferring to live apart from reserves |

### Oral Promises (Not in Written Text)

The treaty commissioners made several oral promises during negotiations that were not included in the written text:

1. **Medical care:** Commissioners promised that medicines would be distributed free to Indians who required them, and that the government would always be ready to afford medical service. They acknowledged the difficulty of providing regular medical attendance over such a vast territory but promised to do what was possible.

2. **No interference with way of life:** Commissioners assured Indigenous peoples that the treaty would not lead to any forced interference with their mode of life.

3. **No taxation:** The treaty did not open the way to imposition of any tax.

4. **No military service:** There was no fear of enforced military service.

5. **Hunting and fishing protection:** Only such laws as were in the interest of the Indians and found necessary to protect fish and fur-bearing animals would be made. They would be as free to hunt and fish after the treaty as before.

6. **Care for the old and indigent:** The attention of the government would be called to the need of some special provision for assisting the old and indigent who were unable to work.

### Crown Failures

1. **Medical care:** The commissioners promised medicines and medical attendance but the government failed to deliver. A doctor accompanied the commission and gave free treatment during negotiations, but year-round medical care was not established until a facility was set up at Fort Smith — and many people in the vast territory never received medical care outside of annual treaty meetings. The commissioners themselves acknowledged the difficulty but made promises they could not keep.

2. **Hunting and fishing rights:** In 1916, the government passed laws restricting hunting and trapping. While the laws technically allowed Indigenous peoples to continue, officials still charged Indigenous people with breaking them. First Nations protested these laws as a violation of treaty promises.

3. **Reserves:** The treaty was drawn before negotiations started. The government confined itself to an undertaking to have reserves set apart in the future. Many reserves were never properly surveyed or allocated. The Indian agents were generally averse to being placed on reserves, and the commissioners had to assure them that there was no intention of confining them — yet the reserve system was subsequently imposed.

4. **Education:** Schools were not built as promised. The residential school system was imposed instead.

5. **Annuities:** The $5 annuity has never been increased. The cost of delivering annuities to remote northern communities made the process unreliable and often delayed.

6. **Land:** The vast territory ceded was opened for resource extraction — mining, logging, oil and gas — with minimal benefit to the Indigenous peoples who had ceded it. The Klondike Gold Rush, which prompted the treaty, brought miners who treated Indigenous people with contempt and violence.

7. **Blueberry River First Nations:** In 2024, Blueberry River First Nations returned to court to uphold treaty rights and enforce a 2023 agreement with British Columbia, alleging the Province failed to meet its constitutional, fiduciary and contractual obligations.

### Current Status

- **Active:** Constitutionally protected under Section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982
- **Treaty 8 First Nations of Alberta:** Represents signatory nations
- **Ongoing litigation:** Multiple specific claims and comprehensive land claims remain active
- **Shot Both Sides v. Canada (2024 SCC 12):** While this case directly concerned Treaty 7, the Supreme Court's framework for treaty interpretation applies to Treaty 8 as well
- **Oil sands controversy:** The Alberta oil sands — located partly within Treaty 8 territory — represent one of the largest resource extraction projects in the world, with profound environmental and cultural impacts on Treaty 8 First Nations
- **Adhesions:** Some First Nations signed adhesions as late as 2000

## COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS

### What Each Treaty Covered

| Feature | Treaty 6 (1876) | Treaty 7 (1877) | Treaty 8 (1899) |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Territory | 121,000 sq mi | ~35,000 sq mi | 841,000 sq km |
| Location | Central AB/SK | Southern AB | Northern AB/BC/SK/NWT |
| Nations | Plains & Wood Cree | Blackfoot Confederacy, Sarcee, Stony | Cree, Beaver, Chipewyan |
| Presents | $12/person | $12/person | $32/chief, $22/headman, $12/person |
| Annuity | $5/person/year | $5/person/year | $5/person/year |
| Ammunition | $1,500/year | $2,000/year | $1/person/year |
| Healthcare | Medicine chest clause | None | Oral promises only |
| Famine relief | Yes (explicit) | No | Oral promises only |
| Land per family | 1 sq mi per 5 persons | 1 sq mi per 5 persons | 1 sq mi per 5 persons + 160 acres severalty option |

### The Pattern of Broken Promises

Across all three treaties, the same pattern emerges:

1. **The Crown made promises it never intended to keep.** The medicine chest clause, the famine relief provision, and the oral promises of medical care were all abandoned or reinterpreted as soon as the treaties were signed.

2. **Reserves were undersized, poorly located, and slow to be surveyed.** The one-square-mile-per-family-of-five standard was consistently not met.

3. **Education was imposed, not provided.** Instead of building schools "whenever the Indians shall desire it," the Crown imposed the residential school system — a system of cultural genocide.

4. **Annuities were never adjusted for inflation.** The $5 annuity has remained unchanged for 150 years.

5. **Hunting and fishing rights were progressively restricted.** Despite treaty guarantees, governments passed laws limiting Indigenous harvesting rights.

6. **The Crown failed to protect the buffalo.** For Treaty 6 and Treaty 7 nations, the destruction of the buffalo herds was an existential catastrophe that the Crown did nothing to prevent.

7. **Oral promises were not honoured.** Treaty 8's oral promises of medical care, no taxation, no military service, and protection of hunting rights were all violated.

### The Legal Framework Today

The Supreme Court of Canada has established that:
- Treaties are constitutionally protected under Section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982 (*R. v. Badger*, 1996)
- Treaty rights are not frozen in time — they can be exercised using modern means
- Courts must interpret treaties liberally, taking into account oral promises and the Indigenous perspective
- Treaty rights flow from the treaties themselves, not the Constitution (*Shot Both Sides v. Canada*, 2024 SCC 12)
- The Crown has a fiduciary duty to act honourably in all dealings with Indigenous peoples

Yet the Crown's failures persist:
- Hundreds of specific claims remain unresolved across Canada
- Healthcare in Indigenous communities remains grossly inadequate
- Education funding on reserves is chronically underfunded
- The $5 annuity has never been increased
- Reserve lands remain undersized
- Resource extraction continues on treaty lands with inadequate consultation and benefit-sharing

## THE FIELD LEGAL MATRIX: The Negligence of the Crown

The three treaties — 6, 7, and 8 — represent the Crown's most ambitious effort to acquire land in western Canada. In exchange, the Crown made promises that were specific, measurable, and enforceable: medicine chests, schools, annuities, agricultural implements, famine relief, hunting rights.

The Crown broke every one.

The medicine chest became a symbol not of healthcare but of broken faith. The schools became instruments of cultural destruction. The annuities became relics of inflation. The reserves became places of poverty. The hunting rights became targets of restriction.

The negligence of the Crown is not a historical abstraction. It is a living reality. The $5 annuity paid in 1876 buys less today than a cup of coffee. The medicine chest clause that promised healthcare delivered intergenerational trauma. The reserve system that promised one square mile per family delivered chronic undersizing and marginal land.

The treaties are alive. The promises are enforceable. The negligence is ongoing.
