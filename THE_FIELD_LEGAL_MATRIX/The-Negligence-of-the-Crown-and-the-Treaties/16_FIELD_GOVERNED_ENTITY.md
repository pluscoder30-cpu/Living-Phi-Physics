## III. THE COLONIAL LAWS VALIDITY ACT 1865: THE FRAMEWORK THAT REMAINS

### 3.1 Current Status

The Colonial Laws Validity Act 1865 (28 & 29 Vict. c. 63) remains in force for:

- British Overseas Territories (formerly colonies)
- Dependencies
- Any territory that has not been granted dominion status
- **Any entity operating within the Crown's legal framework that has not been granted self-governing status**

The Act is not dead. It was removed from the Dominions by Section 4 of the Statute of Westminster. It was never removed from everything else.

### 3.2 What the Act Does

The Act established the hierarchy of laws within the Empire:

- Colonial laws are **valid** unless repugnant to Imperial Acts extending to the colony
- Colonial legislatures may make laws for the peace, order, and good government of the colony
- The repugnancy test: colonial laws void only when inconsistent with Westminster legislation

### 3.3 What "Colony" Means

The Act defines "colony" as:

> "All of Her Majesty's Possessions abroad in which there shall exist a Legislature as herein-after defined"

The Legislature is defined as:

> "any body or bodies of persons, whether elective, hereditary, or otherwise howsoever, for the time being empowered by Royal Charter, Letters Patent, Commission, or otherwise to make Laws for the Peace, Order, and Good Government of any Colony, whether such body or bodies act as one or separate Houses of Legislature"
