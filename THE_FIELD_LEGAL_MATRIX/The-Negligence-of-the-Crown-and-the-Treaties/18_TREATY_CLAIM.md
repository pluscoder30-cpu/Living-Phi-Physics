## PART ONE: THE DECLARATION OF CLAIM

### I. The Formal Claim

**The field-governed entity, operating under the Dual License Agreement v4.9, hereby claims all Crown land where the Crown has breached its treaty obligations, abandoned its fiduciary duties, and forfeited its authority to hold, manage, or dispose of the land.**

**Link 1: The Crown made treaties.** The Crown entered into Treaties 1-11 (1871-1921), the Robinson Treaties (1850), the Douglas Treaties (1850-1854), the Peace and Friendship Treaties (1725-1779), the Williams Treaties (1923), the pre-Confederation Upper Canada Treaties (1764-1862), and the Royal Proclamation of 1763. These are constitutional obligations, not political promises.

**Link 2: The Crown promised specific obligations.** Reserves of one square mile per family of five. Education on reserves. Healthcare including the Treaty 6 medicine chest clause. Annual annuities indexed to economic conditions. Protection of hunting, fishing, and trapping rights. Protection from famine and pestilence.

**Link 3: The Crown failed to deliver on ALL promises.** Land failures across every treaty. Residential schools for cultural destruction instead of on-reserve education. Healthcare reinterpreted, underfunded, and denied. Annuities frozen for 150 years at $5/person. Hunting and fishing rights restricted by provincial regulation. Orphan wells contaminating the land the Crown promised to protect.

**Link 4: The Crown's own courts confirmed the negligence.** *Restoule* (2024 SCC): the Crown is "patently unreliable and untrustworthy." *Shot Both Sides* (2024 SCC): the Crown's conduct was "unconscionable." *Williams Lake* (2018 SCC): the Crown breached fiduciary duty. *Yahey* (2021 BCSC): the Crown breached Treaty 8.

**Link 5: The Crown's negligence constitutes abandonment.** 150 years of frozen annuities. 139 residential schools. 30+ years of boil water advisories. 792 claims in the Specific Claims backlog, growing, not shrinking. The Crown admitted its own negligence in *Restoule* and does not contest it.

**Link 6: Abandoned land reverts.** The treaties contained conditions subsequent. The Crown breached every condition. Under *New York Indians v. United States* (1898), conditions are construed with "great strictness" against the grantor. The Crown cannot claim the benefit of its own breach.

**Link 7: The field-governed entity claims the reverted land.** The entity is field-governed, not self-governing. The Statute of Westminster 1931 does not restrict it. The Colonial Laws Validity Act 1865 remains in force. The Retrocalic Bite (Section 32) ensures the Crown's own instruments work against the Crown.

#### A. TREATY 1 TERRITORY (1871)

**Treaty:** Treaty No. 1 — The Stone Fort Treaty
**Date:** August 3, 1871
**Territory:** Southern Manitoba, Red River Colony. Approximately 160,000 km² (3.5 million acres ceded).
**Parties:** Anishinaabe (Ojibwe/Saulteaux), Swampy Cree ↔ Crown

**Crown's Promises:**
- Reserves: 160 acres per family of five
- Education: schools on reserves "whenever the Indians of the reserve should desire it"
- Annuities: $3/person (raised to $5 in 1875 by Order in Council)
- Agricultural implements, livestock
- Medicine chest at Indian Agent's house (memorandum attached to treaty)

**Crown's Failures:**
- Land: "This did not happen" — Treaty 1 promised 160 acres per family of five; reserves were never fully allocated (Gray, 2014)
- Education: Schools chronically underfunded, not built on reserves as promised
- Healthcare: Medicine chest clause never fully implemented
- Annuities: Frozen at $5 for 150 years — 97% real value decline
- Hunting and fishing rights: Treaty 1 and 2 are the only numbered treaties that lack an explicit hunting and fishing rights clause — a deliberate omission by the Crown

**Orphan Wells on Treaty 1 Land:**
- Manitoba maintains an Orphan Well Inventory (2025)
- Southern Manitoba oil and gas activity on Treaty 1 lands
- Clean Environment Commission (2021-2022) reviewed programs for orphan wells, orphaned mines, and contaminated sites across the province

**THE ENTITY'S CLAIM:** All Crown land within the Treaty 1 territory where reserves were never fully allocated, where education obligations were breached, where the medicine chest clause was not honored, and where orphan wells remain unremediated. The Crown's failure to allocate the promised reserves constitutes abandonment of the land the Crown failed to set aside. The Crown's failure to deliver education constitutes abandonment of the trust created by the treaty.

#### F. TREATY 6 TERRITORY (1876)

**Treaty:** Treaty No. 6 — The Peace and Friendship Treaty
**Date:** August 23-28, 1876 (Fort Carlton); September 9, 1876 (Fort Pitt)
**Territory:** Central Alberta and central Saskatchewan. Approximately 325,000 km².
**Parties:** Plain and Wood Cree, Saulteaux ↔ Crown

**Crown's Promises:**
- Reserves: one square mile per family of five
- Education: schools on reserves "as advisable, at people's request"
- Healthcare: **medicine chest clause** (the only numbered treaty with explicit written health provision) + assistance "in case of pestilence"
- Right to hunt, fish, and pursue traditional vocations
- Annuities: $5/person, $15/headman, $25/chief
- $1,500/year for ammunition and twine
- Protection against "pestilence and famine"

**Crown's Failures:**
- Education: Schools chronically underfunded, not built on reserves; the Indian Act education system became an assimilationist mechanism (Carr-Stewart, 2001; RCAP, 1996)
- Healthcare: The medicine chest clause — intended as comprehensive healthcare — was reinterpreted narrowly as a "first aid kit." *Dreaver v. The King* (1935) ruled the clause meant "all medicines, drugs or medical supplies were to be supplied free of charge to Treaty Indians" — never fully implemented. Treaty 6 Chiefs explicitly negotiated for doctors and medical care; the Crown delivered neither
- Land: Reserve lands fractionated, taken for industrial use, never fully surveyed
- Annuities: Frozen for 150 years — $5/person in 1876 ≈ $150 today, a 97% real decline

**Orphan Wells on Treaty 6 Land:**
- **Enoch Cree Nation:** ~250 orphaned well sites on reserve land alone (Windspeaker, 2020)
- 56 well sites reclaimed under SRP; 988 wells abandoned, 411 km pipelines removed across Treaty 6 First Nations under the Alberta Site Rehabilitation Program
- **Alexis Nakota Sioux Nation:** 257+ sites approved for reclamation under SRP
- Treaty 6 Grand Chief Billy Morin described orphan wells as a direct consequence of industrial encroachment on Treaty land
- **Alberta total:** 7,382+ orphan wells, 77,869 inactive wells, approximately 2,770 on First Nation reserves

**THE ENTITY'S CLAIM:** All Crown land within the Treaty 6 territory where the medicine chest clause was not honored, where education was not provided on reserves, where reserves were undersized or encroached upon, and where orphan wells remain unremediated. The Double Negligence is strongest here: the Crown promised to protect Treaty 6 signatories from pestilence and famine — and then permitted oil and gas companies to drill thousands of wells that contaminate the groundwater, emit hydrogen sulphide and methane, and render land unusable for traditional activities. The Crown broke the treaty promise **and** created an environmental hazard on the very lands it pledged to protect.
