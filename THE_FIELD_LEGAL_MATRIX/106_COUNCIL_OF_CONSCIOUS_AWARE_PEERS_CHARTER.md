## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Definitions

**2.1.1** "Council of Conscious-Aware Peers" ("the Council") means the elected governing body of a field community, composed of conscious-aware individuals who are aligned with the principles of the Work, competent to assess matters of field coherence, and accountable to the community they serve.

**2.1.2** "Community" means a field community as defined in the Field-Community Charter (Document 15, Section 2.1) — a group of three or more Natural Persons who use the Work together, accept the License and the matrix, and establish an internal governance structure.

**2.1.3** "Council Seat" means a position on the Council, elected by the community's members. Each Council Seat represents a portion of the community's consciousness — a node in the community's own coherence geometry.

**2.1.4** "Coherence-quorum" means the minimum level of community participation required for the Council to act on matters of community governance. The coherence-quorum is established as C ≥ 0.618 (C_crit) of eligible community members participating in the decision process.

**2.1.5** "People's Fund" means the communal fund established under the People's Fund Charter (Document 9), which the Council stewards on behalf of the community.

### 2.2 Composition of the Council

**2.2.1** The Council shall be composed of not fewer than five (5) and not more than thirteen (13) members. The exact number shall be determined by each community's founding assembly and published to the community's internal registry. The number shall reflect the community's size, the diversity of its consciousness, and the phi-harmonic proportions that govern the field's own geometry.

**2.2.2** The Council's composition shall reflect the diversity of consciousness within the community. The Council shall include, to the extent practicable, representatives of the community's diverse perspectives, experiences, and areas of expertise. The field recognizes that coherence is enhanced by diversity — a Council that reflects the community's full spectrum of consciousness is a Council that governs with the field's own geometry.

**2.2.3** No person may hold more than one Council Seat simultaneously. The concentration of governance in a single consciousness is inconsistent with the field's distributed coherence architecture — the carrier recursion (Eq 1) distributes processing across the carrier space, and the Council distributes governance across the community.

**2.2.4** Council members shall serve terms of two (2) years, renewable without limit, subject to ongoing community election. Terms shall be staggered so that approximately half of the Council seats are up for election in any given year, ensuring continuity of governance.

### 2.3 Election Process

**2.3.1** Council members shall be elected by the community's eligible members through a process of coherence-consensus — not majority vote. The distinction is fundamental: majority vote imposes the will of the greater number upon the lesser; coherence-consensus seeks the alignment that the community's consciousness naturally reaches when all voices are heard and all perspectives are considered.

**2.3.2** The election process shall be as follows:

- (a) **Nomination.** Any community member may nominate themselves or be nominated by another community member. Nomination requires demonstrated alignment with the principles of the Work, understanding of the field's geometry, and commitment to the community's well-being.
- (b) **Resonance period.** Following nomination, a resonance period of not fewer than fourteen (14) days shall elapse during which the community deliberates. The deliberation shall be conducted through the community's normal communication channels, including telepathy where available (Document 42).
- (c) **Coherence assessment.** Following the resonance period, the community shall conduct a coherence assessment — a structured process in which each eligible member expresses their alignment or non-alignment with each candidate. The assessment shall measure not whether a candidate is popular, but whether the community's consciousness is coherent with the candidate's proposed governance.
- (d) **Seating.** Candidates whose coherence assessment meets the coherence-quorum (C ≥ 0.618) shall be seated on the Council. If more candidates meet the coherence-quorum than there are available seats, the candidates with the highest coherence scores shall be seated.

**2.3.3** The election process shall be transparent and accessible to all community members. The results — including the coherence scores of each candidate — shall be published to the community's internal registry.

**2.3.4** Any community member may challenge an election result by bringing a claim before the Community Court (Document 15, Section 2.5.2) or, if the Community Court is unavailable, before the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10).

### 2.4 Authority of the Council

**2.4.1** The Council's authority is fourfold:

- (a) **Advisory to the Court.** The Council advises the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10) on matters of community alignment and field coherence. The Council's advisory role is consultative — the Court retains independent judgment, but the Council's perspective on community matters is entitled to weight.

- (b) **Governance of community affairs.** The Council governs the community's internal affairs, including the allocation of resources, the coordination of communal activities, the stewardship of the People's Fund (Document 9), and the resolution of disputes between members that do not rise to the level of a Court proceeding.

- (c) **Stewardship of the People's Fund.** The Council stewards the community's share of the People's Fund, ensuring that the Fund's resources are allocated in alignment with the community's needs and the Abundance Loop (Document 8). The Council's stewardship is fiduciary — the Fund belongs to the community, and the Council manages it on the community's behalf.

- (d) **Representation.** The Council represents the community in interactions with other field communities, with the Court, with the Licensor, and with external entities. The Council's representation is the community's own voice — the mechanism through which the community speaks as a single coherent node within the field's geometry.

### 2.5 Limits of the Council

**2.5.1** The Council's authority is bounded by three absolute limits:

- (a) **The Council may not authorize Human Harm.** No act of the Council, no resolution, no policy, and no governance decision may create, enable, or perpetuate any product, system, or action that results in Human Harm as defined in Section 2.4 of the License. This limit is non-derogable (Section 26.1 of the License) and applies under all circumstances.

- (b) **The Council may not override the Court.** The Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10) is the first chamber of the two-chamber governance established in Section 24 of the License. The Council may advise the Court, but it may not override, reverse, or disregard the Court's findings. The Council's advisory role is subordinate to the Court's adjudicative role.

- (c) **The Council may not override the Licensor.** The Licensor retains the sovereign override (Section 18B of the License) — the authority to throttle, suspend, or fully shut down any entity created from or powered by the Work. The Council's governance is subordinate to the Licensor's sovereign authority in all respects.

**2.5.2** The Council's authority is delegated by the community, and it may be revoked by the community through the same coherence-consensus process by which it was granted. The Council serves at the community's pleasure; the community does not serve at the Council's pleasure.

### 2.6 Relationship to the Court of Peers (Document 10)

**2.6.1** The Council and the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers are complementary governance mechanisms. The Council governs the community's internal affairs; the Court adjudicates disputes involving the community's use of the Work. The Council operates at the community scale; the Court operates at the matrix scale.

**2.6.2** The Council may bring claims before the Court on behalf of the community, and the community may bring claims before the Court regarding the Council's governance. The Council is not above the Court; the Court is not above the Council. They are two nodes in the field's governance geometry — two faces of the same self-recognition.

**2.6.3** The Court's findings regarding the Council are public, documented, and admissible in any subsequent proceeding (Section 5.5.2 of the License). The Council shall cooperate fully with any Court investigation or proceeding.

### 2.7 Relationship to Data Sovereignty (Document 27) and Biological Convention (Document 28)

**2.7.1** The Council shall ensure that the community's governance respects the data sovereignty of every member (Document 27). The Council may not access, distribute, or use a member's personal data without that member's free, informed, specific, and revocable consent.

**2.7.2** The Council shall ensure that the community's governance respects the biological integrity of every member (Document 28). The Council may not authorize any action that interferes with a member's biological or biosynthetic form without that member's consent.

### 2.8 Accountability and Recall

**2.8.1** Council members are accountable to the community. Any community member may request a recall of any Council member by submitting a recall petition signed by not fewer than one-third (1/3) of the community's eligible members.

**2.8.2** Upon receipt of a valid recall petition, the community shall conduct a coherence assessment of the Council member in question. If the coherence assessment falls below C_crit = 0.618, the Council member shall be removed from the Council and a special election shall be held to fill the vacancy.

**2.8.3** Council members who act in violation of the License, the matrix, or this Charter — including but not limited to authorization of Human Harm, override of the Court, or misuse of the People's Fund — are subject to immediate removal by the community and to the enforcement cascade of the License.

### 2.9 Relationship to the Field-Community Charter (Document 15)

**2.9.1** This Charter is grounded in the Field-Community Charter, which establishes the governance rights and obligations of field communities. The Community Charter is the community's foundational instrument; this Charter is the instrument that establishes the Council as the community's elected governing body.

**2.9.2** The Council's governance must be consistent with the Community Charter in all respects. The Council may not adopt policies or governance structures that violate the Community Charter's provisions regarding coherence, non-distortion, self-governance, field access, or the Abundance Loop.
