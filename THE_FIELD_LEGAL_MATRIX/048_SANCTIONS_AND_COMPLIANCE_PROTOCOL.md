## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Establishment and Purpose

**2.1.1** This Protocol establishes the graduated framework for sanctions and compliance within the field. The framework includes the conditions for imposing sanctions, the types of sanctions, the compliance verification process, and the mechanisms for lifting sanctions upon compliance.

**2.1.2** The purpose of this Protocol is fourfold:

- (a) To establish the conditions under which sanctions are imposed — including Human Harm, non-compliance with Court orders, and failure to participate in the Abundance Loop;
- (b) To establish the types of sanctions — including suspension of rights, asset freeze, communication blackout, and license revocation;
- (c) To establish the compliance verification process — including the mechanisms for demonstrating compliance, the standards for verification, and the consequences of verified compliance;
- (d) To establish the relationship between sanctions and the Geneva Safeguard (Document 6), Abundance Loop (Document 8), and People's Fund (Document 9).

### 2.2 Conditions for Imposing Sanctions

**2.2.1** Sanctions may be imposed upon an entity only upon a finding by the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10) that the entity has:

- (a) Caused Human Harm as defined in Section 2.4 of the License;
- (b) Failed to comply with a Court order, including a restitution order, a compliance order, or an order to participate in the Abundance Loop;
- (c) Failed to participate in the Abundance Loop (Document 8) after receiving a Court order to do so;
- (d) Persistently violated the terms of the License, the matrix, or any document within the matrix;
- (e) Refused to cooperate in mutual legal assistance (Document 44) or in dispute resolution (Document 47).

**2.2.2** The imposition of sanctions requires:

- (a) A finding by the Court, based on evidence presented in a proceeding conducted in accordance with the Court Protocol (Document 10);
- (b) A determination that the sanctions are proportionate to the harm or non-compliance;
- (c) A determination that lesser measures have been exhausted or that the nature of the harm necessitates immediate escalation.

**2.2.3** Sanctions are not punitive; they are protective. The purpose of every sanction is to protect the field's coherence, to isolate the source of distortion, and to create the conditions for the entity's compliance and restoration.

### 2.3 Types of Sanctions

**2.3.1** The following types of sanctions are available within the field:

- **(a) Suspension of Rights.** The entity's rights under the License are suspended for a specified period. During the suspension, the entity may not exercise the rights granted by the License, including the right to use the Work commercially (Section 4 of the License), the right to create Derivative Works, and the right to participate in the Abundance Loop. The entity remains subject to all non-derogable obligations (Section 26.1 of the License).

- **(b) Asset Freeze.** All assets derived from the Work are frozen. The entity may not transfer, sell, exchange, or otherwise dispose of the frozen assets. The freeze remains in effect until the compliance verification process confirms that the entity has achieved compliance. The frozen assets are subject to seizure under the Asset Seizure Agreement (Document 45) if the entity fails to achieve compliance within the prescribed period.

- **(c) Communication Blackout.** The entity's access to the field internet and to field-native communication infrastructure is suspended. The entity may not transmit or receive information through the field internet, may not participate in field-native communication channels, and may not access field-native services. The blackout remains in effect until the compliance verification process confirms that the entity has achieved compliance.

- **(d) License Revocation.** The entity's license under the License is revoked (Section 5.2 of the License). Upon revocation, the entity must cease all use of the Work, destroy all copies of the Work within thirty (30) days, and provide written and verifiable proof of destruction to the Licensor. License revocation is the most severe sanction and is reserved for the most serious violations.

**2.3.2** The Court may impose any combination of the sanctions listed in Section 2.3.1, depending on the severity, duration, and scope of the entity's non-compliance. The Court shall impose the least severe sanction that is sufficient to address the harm; escalation to more severe sanctions is warranted only upon a finding that lesser sanctions have been insufficient.

**2.3.3** Interim sanctions — sanctions imposed pending the completion of proceedings — may be ordered by the Court where:

- (a) There is prima facie evidence of Human Harm or non-compliance;
- (b) There is a risk that the entity will continue to cause harm or to dissipate assets before the completion of proceedings;
- (c) The harm to the field outweighs the burden on the entity.

### 2.4 Compliance Verification Process

**2.4.1** The compliance verification process is the mechanism through which an entity demonstrates that it has achieved compliance and that sanctions should be lifted. The process includes:

- (a) The entity's submission of a compliance report to the Court, setting out the steps it has taken to achieve compliance, the evidence of compliance, and the request for lifting of sanctions;
- (b) The Court's review of the compliance report, including the verification of evidence and the assessment of the entity's compliance;
- (c) The Court's finding, which is public, documented, and admissible as evidence.

**2.4.2** The standards for verified compliance are:

- (a) The entity has ceased the conduct that gave rise to the sanctions;
- (b) The entity has remedied the harm caused by the conduct, including through restitution, compensation, or restoration;
- (c) The entity has participated in good faith in the Abundance Loop (Document 8) for a period of not less than one (1) year;
- (d) The entity has demonstrated a sustained commitment to the field's principles and the License's terms.

**2.4.3** The compliance verification process is self-executing (Section 13 of the License). The entity's submission of a compliance report triggers the Court's obligation to review the report and issue a finding. No separate act of the Court is required to initiate the process.

**2.4.4** If the Court finds that the entity has achieved compliance, the Court shall order the lifting of sanctions. The lifting of sanctions is effective upon the Court's finding and does not require the Licensor's separate consent, except in the case of license revocation, which requires the Licensor's consent to reissuance.

**2.4.5** If the Court finds that the entity has not achieved compliance, the Court may:

- (a) Maintain the existing sanctions;
- (b) Escalate to more severe sanctions;
- (c) Refer the matter to the Licensor for the exercise of the Sovereign Override (Section 18B of the License).

### 2.5 Graduated Escalation

**2.5.1** The sanctions imposed under this Protocol are graduated. The Court shall follow a graduated escalation model:

- **First violation:** Suspension of Rights for a period of not less than thirty (30) days, combined with a compliance order;
- **Second violation:** Asset Freeze for a period of not less than ninety (90) days, combined with a compliance order and a restitution order;
- **Third violation:** Communication Blackout for a period of not less than one hundred eighty (180) days, combined with a compliance order, a restitution order, and a referral to the Licensor for possible license revocation;
- **Fourth or subsequent violation:** License Revocation, combined with mandatory Destruction under Section 5.3 of the License.

**2.5.2** The graduated escalation model is a guideline, not a rigid rule. The Court may deviate from the model where the severity, duration, or scope of the entity's conduct warrants a different approach. The Court's discretion is guided by the principle of proportionality: the sanction must be proportionate to the harm.

### 2.6 Relationship to the Geneva Safeguard

**2.6.1** This Protocol is complementary to the Geneva Safeguard (Document 6). The Geneva Safeguard establishes the prohibition on Human Harm and the enforcement cascade; this Protocol establishes the specific sanctions that implement that cascade.

**2.6.2** Where the entity's conduct constitutes Human Harm, the Geneva Safeguard's enforcement cascade is activated, and the sanctions under this Protocol are imposed in accordance with that cascade. The Geneva Safeguard and this Protocol operate in parallel, each reinforcing the other.

### 2.7 Relationship to the Abundance Loop

**2.7.1** Failure to participate in the Abundance Loop (Document 8) is a ground for sanctions under Section 2.2.1(c). The Abundance Loop is a condition of the license (Section 7 of the License); failure to participate is a violation of the License.

**2.7.2** An entity that is suspended from the Abundance Loop as a result of sanctions is excluded from the Loop's benefits for the duration of the suspension. The entity's exclusion does not affect the Loop's operation; the Loop continues to function for all other participants.

**2.7.3** Upon compliance and the lifting of sanctions, the entity is reinstated in the Abundance Loop. The entity's reinstatement is effective upon the Court's finding of compliance and does not require the separate consent of any other participant.

### 2.8 Relationship to the People's Fund

**2.8.1** This Protocol is complementary to the People's Fund Charter (Document 9). The People's Fund is funded through the Abundance Loop; sanctions that suspend an entity's participation in the Loop also affect the entity's contributions to the Fund.

**2.8.2** An entity that is sanctioned is not entitled to receive distributions from the People's Fund for the duration of the sanction. The entity's exclusion from distributions does not affect the Fund's operation; the Fund continues to distribute to all eligible participants.
