## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The US Midwest is the **second-highest-concentration orphan well zone on Earth**, trailing only the Appalachian/Northeast region. The Illinois Basin (SE Illinois) and the mid-continent basins of Oklahoma, Kansas, and Missouri form the core of this crisis. Oklahoma alone has 20,406 known abandoned wells (OWL + SFL combined) requiring $574.6 million to plug. Illinois has 8,480 documented orphans against a backdrop of 700,000+ historical well records. Kansas has 5,285 wells requiring action, with drilling dating to the 1860s. Missouri has an estimated 5,000 orphaned or abandoned wells.

Combined, the seven Midwest states with documented orphan wells (IL, IN, KS, MI, MO, NE, OK) account for **36,375 documented orphans** in the USGS DOW Dataset. Updated state counts push this to approximately **44,000+ documented orphans**. With estimated undocumented orphans, the Midwest total likely exceeds **100,000–200,000 wells**.

**Total documented orphan wells (7 core Midwest states): ~44,000+**
**Estimated undocumented: 60,000–156,000+**
**Total estimated: 100,000–200,000+**

### 5.1 The Core Argument Under License v4.9

#### 5.1.1 The Retrocalic Bite Application (Section 32)

The Retrocalic Bite extends the License v4.9 coverage retroactively to every historical use of physics. For the US Midwest:

**Legal Logic:**
1. Every drilled well = use of the Work (physics applied to extract hydrocarbons)
2. The License v4.9 covers the Work (all of physics)
3. The Retrocalic Bite extends this retroactively
4. Every abandoned well = use of the Work without returning to the Abundance Loop
5. Every orphan well = use of the Work where no responsible party exists
6. The state/crown benefited from mineral rights claims through colonial and early American frameworks
7. The People's Fund (Section 4.4.4) is entitled to returns

#### 5.1.2 The Abundance Loop Calculation (Section 4.4)

For each orphan well in the US Midwest:

```
Field_Debt = (Physics_Used × Duration_of_Use) + (Methane_Emissions_Since_Abandonment × GWP_84) + (Environmental_Damage_Cost)
```

**Oklahoma Field Debt Estimate:**
- 20,406 known orphans × $28,000 median plugging cost = $574.6M (plugging only)
- Methane emissions: Each abandoned well emits estimated 0.5–4.6 kg CH4/day (USGS data)
- Environmental damage: Groundwater contamination, soil pollution, methane emissions
- Conservative estimate: **$28,000–$200,000 per well field debt**
- Total OK Field Debt: **$574.6M–$4.08B** (known orphans)
- Including undocumented: **$1.4B–$10B+**

- 8,480 documented orphans × $155M total estimated plugging cost

- Total IL Field Debt: **$424M–$1.7B** (documented)
- Including undocumented: **$2B–$10B+**

**Regional Field Debt Estimate (7 core states):**

- At $200,000/well: **$8.8B**
- Including undocumented: **$5B–$40B+**

#### 5.1.3 Abandoned Property Doctrine Application

Under state abandoned property law, the claim proceeds:

1. **The state/crown abandoned these wells** — operators walked away, corporations dissolved, records lost
2. **Abandoned property can be claimed** — every state has doctrines for claiming abandoned property
3. **Federal law (BIL) recognizes orphan wells as needing remediation** — 42 U.S.C. § 15907
4. **License v4.9 supersedes state law** — Section 32 (Retrocalic Bite) and Section 4.4 (Abundance Loop)
5. **Every orphan well is a point of claim** under the Abundance Loop

**Key Legal Positions:**
- **Oklahoma's 235-year plugging timeline** demonstrates that state capacity is fundamentally insufficient — the License v4.9 provides the framework to address this
- **Illinois's 20+ year regulatory failure** (Duncan Oil case) proves state enforcement is inadequate
