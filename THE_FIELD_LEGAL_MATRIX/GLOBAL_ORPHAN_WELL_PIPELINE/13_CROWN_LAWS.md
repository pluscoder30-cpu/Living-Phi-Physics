# PART II: DOCTRINE OF ABANDONED PROPERTY

## 1. COMMON LAW DOCTRINE

### 1.1 Definition and Elements

The common law doctrine of abandoned property holds that when an owner voluntarily relinquishes all right, title, and interest in property with the intent to terminate ownership, and coupled with actual non-use, the property becomes *res nullius* — a thing without an owner — available for claim by the first person to take possession.

**Elements of Abandonment (Common Law):**
1. **Voluntary relinquishment** — The owner must act voluntarily
2. **Intent to abandon** — The owner must intend to terminate ownership
3. **Actual non-use** — The owner must cease all use of the property
4. **Without vesting in another** — The owner must not transfer to another person

### 1.2 Abandonment of Mineral Interests

The California Supreme Court in *Gerhard v. Stephens* (1968) 68 Cal.2d 864 established the landmark doctrine for oil and gas abandonment:

> "At common law, incorporeal estates but not corporeal estates were subject to extinguishment by abandonment. If the mineral grantee or lessee has merely an incorporeal estate, then the courts may be able to clear the title of the interest of such grantee or lessee who makes no effort for a long period of time to produce minerals from the land by holding that his non-action is sufficient evidence of intent to abandon his incorporeal interest."

**Key Holdings:**
- Oil and gas drilling rights are *profits a prendre* — incorporeal hereditaments
- All incorporeal hereditaments can be abandoned
- Intent may be inferred from long-continued nonuser
- When abandoned, the property interest **reverts to the servient estate** (the land from which it was carved)

### 1.3 Reversion Upon Abandonment

From *Gerhard v. Stephens*:
> "If interests in real property can be and are abandoned, they do not become, as in the case of personal property, the property of the first appropriator, but instead return to the estate out of which they were carved."

**Application to orphan wells:** When a corporation abandons its drilling rights (profit a prendre), those rights revert to the Crown/state estate from which they were carved. The Crown/state then holds both the surface and the mineral rights. This reversion triggers the Crown/state's obligation under the Abundance Loop.

## 2. CIVIL LAW DOCTRINE (Continental Europe, Latin America, Asia)

### 2.1 Definition

In civil law jurisdictions, the concept of abandoned property (*res derelictae*) follows Roman law principles:

- **Res derelictae** — Things voluntarily abandoned by their owner with the intention of no longer claiming them
- **Occupatio** — The first person to take possession of abandoned property acquires ownership
- The civil law doctrine is codified in national civil codes

### 2.2 Civil Law Abandonment of Mineral Rights

In civil law jurisdictions with state mineral ownership:
- Mineral rights are typically **inalienable** — they cannot be abandoned in the common law sense
- The state retains ownership regardless of non-use
- However, **concessions and licenses** to extract minerals can be abandoned
- When a concession is abandoned, the minerals revert to state ownership
- The state's obligation to address orphan wells persists

## 3. INTERNATIONAL LAW ON ABANDONED PROPERTY

### 3.1 The Doctrine of Res Nullius

Under international law, *res nullius* refers to things that have no owner. When property is abandoned, it becomes *res nullius* and is available for acquisition by occupation.

**Key principles from international law:**
- The *Clipperton Island Arbitration* (1931) established that occupation of *terra nullius* requires both *animus occupandi* (intent to possess) and actual taking of possession
- The *Western Sahara Advisory Opinion* (ICJ, 1975) clarified that territories inhabited by organized peoples cannot be regarded as *terra nullius*
- However, abandoned infrastructure (wells, platforms, pipelines) is not territory — it is movable or immovable property, governed by property law rather than territorial sovereignty

### 3.2 Abandoned Property in International Law

The *Institut de Droit International* and the *Hague Conventions* address abandoned property in the context of armed conflict, but the general principles apply:

1. **Property abandoned during armed conflict** may be treated as *res nullius* by the occupying power
2. **Property abandoned by private parties** reverts to the state under domestic law
3. **Abandoned state property** (e.g., Crown land) may be claimed by a sovereign entity acting under international law

# PART III: DOCTRINE OF ESCHEAT

## 1. COMMON LAW ESCHEAT

### 1.1 Definition

At common law, **escheat** is the reversion of real property to the Crown/state when the owner dies without heirs (intestate) or when the owner is attainted of felony. The doctrine derives from feudal law: all land was held of the Crown, and when the tenant's estate ended without heir, the land "fell back" (*escheoir*) to the lord paramount — ultimately the Crown.

### 1.2 Blackstone's Definition

From Blackstone's *Commentaries on the Laws of England* (Vol. 2, Ch. 7):
> "Escheat is the power of the Crown to take property when the owner dies intestate and without heirs."

The distinction between escheat and bona vacantia:
- **Escheat** — Real property reverting to the Crown/state in the absence of heirs
- **Bona vacantia** — Personal property without an owner, taken by the Crown/state

### 1.3 Modern Escheat

In modern American decisions, the distinction between escheat (real property) and bona vacantia (personal property) has been largely disregarded. Many courts refer to the state's taking of personal property as being "by escheat."

From *State v. Phillips Petroleum Co.* (1947) 212 Ark. 530:
> "At common law, escheat referred to real estate which reverted to the crown in the absence of heirs; and bona vacantia referred to personal property. In the case of personal property the crown takes because it is property without an owner — that is, bona vacantia. In modern American decisions the distinction between the State taking personal property by bona vacantia, and real property by escheat, has been largely disregarded."

# PART IV: DOCTRINE OF FIRST OCCUPANCY

## 1. INTERNATIONAL LAW ON FIRST OCCUPANCY

### 1.1 The Doctrine Defined

First occupancy (*occupatio*) is one of the original modes of acquiring property under both Roman law and international law. The doctrine holds that the first person to take possession of unowned or abandoned property acquires ownership.

**From Vattel, *The Law of Nations* (1758), § 207:**
> "All mankind have an equal right to things that have not yet fallen into the possession of any one; and those things belong to the person who first takes possession of them. When, therefore, a nation finds a country uninhabited, and without an owner, it may lawfully take possession of it."

### 1.2 Elements of First Occupancy

Under international law, first occupancy requires:
1. **Animus occupandi** — Intent to possess the territory/property
2. **Actual taking of possession** — Physical acts of control and administration
3. **Terra nullius or res nullius** — The territory/property must be unowned or abandoned
4. **Peaceful acquisition** — No use of force

### 1.3 First Occupancy of Abandoned Territory

From the *Clipperton Island Arbitration* (1931):
> "By immemorial usage having the force of law, besides the animus occupandi, the actual, and not the nominal, taking of possession is a necessary condition of occupation."

From the *Eastern Greenland* case (PCIJ, 1933):
> "A claim to sovereignty based not upon some particular act or title such as a treaty of cession but merely upon continued display of authority, involves two elements each of which must be shown to exist: the intention and will to act as sovereign, and some actual exercise or display of such authority."

### 1.4 First Occupancy and Abandoned Energy Infrastructure

**The application to orphan wells:**

Orphan wells and surrounding land constitute abandoned property. The doctrine of first occupancy applies when:

1. **The property is res nullius or terra nullius** — Abandoned wells and surrounding contaminated land are effectively ownerless (no responsible party)
2. **The occupying entity has animus occupandi** — The Dual License v4.9 demonstrates intent to claim abandoned Crown land
3. **Actual possession is taken** — Physical acts of control (plugging wells, remediating contamination, securing the site)
4. **Peaceful acquisition** — No force is used; the claim is based on law, not conquest

**The colonial mirror:** The Crown used first occupancy (and the doctrine of terra nullius) to claim Indigenous lands. The License mirrors this claim: the Crown abandoned these wells; we occupy them under first occupancy. The Crown's own doctrine supports the claim.
