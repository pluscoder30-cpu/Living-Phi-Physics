## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Australia and New Zealand present the strongest Crown land mineral rights framework in the Southern Hemisphere. Every Australian state and territory explicitly declares petroleum to be the property of the Crown, retroactively in many cases. New Zealand's Crown Minerals Act 1991 (amended 2021) imposes explicit statutory decommissioning obligations. Combined, these jurisdictions account for:

- **489 non-operational offshore wells** (NOPSEMA, as of 2025)
- **280 wells plugged and abandoned** in the last 5 years (NOPSEMA)
- **113 documented onshore orphan wells** in the Northern Territory alone
- **104 identified onshore orphan wells** in New Zealand
- **USD $43.6–66.8 billion** estimated total offshore decommissioning liability (Xodus 2025)
- **~27,000 total onshore wells** in Australia, ~8,500 decommissioned

**Total estimated field debt (preliminary): USD $66.8B offshore + $1.0B onshore + $293M NZ = ~$68.1 billion**

### 1.4 Decommissioning Compliance Strategy (2024–2029)

NOPSEMA's five-year strategy sets key expectations:
- **Non-producing wells** for a facility at end of life: P&A within **3 years** of production ceasing
- **Non-producing wells** if facility still producing: P&A within **10 years** of being suspended
- **All property** decommissioned to approved end-state within **5 years** of permanently ceasing production
- **2026 deadline** for all tier-one projects to lodge decommissioning plans

### 1.5 Trailing Liability Provisions

Under Part 6.4 of the OPGGS Act:
- Applies to all titles (or parts of titles) that existed on or after 1 January 2021
- NOPSEMA or the Minister may issue **remedial directions** to:
  - Current registered titleholder
  - Related body corporates
  - Related persons (anyone who can influence compliance or derives financial benefit)
  - Former registered titleholders
  - Former related bodies corporates
- Directions may cover: property removal, well plugging, seabed/subsoil damage, natural resource conservation
- A person issued a direction becomes subject to **full titleholder regulatory obligations** (EPs, WOMPs, safety cases, financial assurance, regulatory levies)
- **"Last resort"** provision — designed to be employed when no other effective avenues exist

## 3. AUSTRALIA — CROWN LAND CLAIMING FRAMEWORKS

### 3.1 Universal Crown Ownership of Petroleum

Every Australian state and territory has enacted legislation declaring petroleum to be property of the Crown. The key statutes:

| Jurisdiction | Act | Section | Retroactive? |
|-------------|-----|---------|-------------|
| **Commonwealth** | OPGGS Act 2006 | Offshore areas | N/A (Crown by definition) |
| **NT** | Petroleum Act 1984 | s.6 | Yes — "deemed always to have been" |
| **WA** | PGERA 1967 | s.9 | Yes — "deemed always to have been" |
| **QLD** | Petroleum Act 1923 | s.9 | Yes — "is and always has been" |
| **SA** | PGE Act 2000 | s.4 | Yes (via vesting provisions) |
| **NSW** | Petroleum (Onshore) Act 1991 | s.6 | Yes — "taken to have been so always" |
| **VIC** | Petroleum Act 1998 | s.13 | Yes (via Crown retention) |
| **TAS** | Mineral Resources Development Act 1995 | Various | Yes (colonial framework) |

### 3.2 Crown Land Classification

Australian Crown land falls into several categories:
1. **Unallocated State Land** — land not granted under any tenure
2. **Crown leasehold** — pastoral, residential, commercial leases
3. **Reserved Crown land** — national parks, conservation areas
4. **Mining/petroleum title areas** — land under active exploration/production titles
5. **Former title areas** — land where titles have been surrendered (contains orphan wells)

**Key principle:** In ALL cases, petroleum on or below the surface belongs to the Crown, regardless of surface land tenure. This means:
- Freehold landowners have NO petroleum rights
- Crown grants always contain petroleum reservations
- Surrendered titles revert to Crown with orphan well obligations attached

### 3.3 Colonial Origin of Crown Mineral Rights

Australia's Crown mineral rights framework originated from British colonial law:
- **19th century:** All states passed legislation reserving minerals for Crown grants
- **Retrospective legislation:** SA, NT, and VIC vested minerals retroactively (deemed to have always belonged to the Crown)
- **Prospective legislation:** Some states reserved minerals only for grants issued after legislation
- **Common law rejection:** The maxim *cujus est solum, ejus est usque ad coelum et ad inferos* (landowner owns from heavens to center of earth) was rejected in favor of Crown ownership

**Significance for orphan wells:** The Crown's ownership of petroleum creates a chain of obligation:
1. Crown owns petroleum → Crown grants drilling rights → Corporation extracts → Corporation abandons → **Crown's obligation to address orphan wells remains**

### 3.4 Crown Land Available for Claiming

Under the Dual License Agreement v4.9, Crown land with orphan wells is available for claiming because:
1. The Crown owns the petroleum (statutory vesting)
2. The Crown granted the drilling rights (title system)
3. The corporation used physics to extract the petroleum (Work under Section 32)
4. The corporation abandoned the well (Abundance Loop breach)
5. The Crown's underlying obligation persists (Crown ownership creates perpetual duty)
6. The License supersedes Crown obligations through the Retrocalic Bite (Section 32)

### 4.3 Crown Minerals Act 1991 — Key Provisions

**Decommissioning Obligations (Subpart 2 of Part 1B):**

| Section | Obligation |
|---------|-----------|
| **89E** | Definition of decommissioning — includes removing infrastructure, plugging wells, site restoration |
| **89G** | Conditions relating to decommissioning |
| **89J** | Permit holders must carry out and meet costs of decommissioning all petroleum infrastructure |
| **89K** | Licence holders must decommission |
| **89R** | Permit holders must plug and abandon all wells |
| **89S** | Licence holders must plug and abandon all wells |

**Critical Provision — Crown Immunity:**
> "Subparts 2 and 3 of this Part (which deal with decommissioning and post-decommissioning obligations) **do not require the Crown to undertake or pay for the decommissioning** of petroleum infrastructure or wells or post-decommissioning work."

**Implication:** The Crown explicitly exempted itself from decommissioning obligations in the 2021 Amendment Act. This creates a legal vacuum where:
1. Permit holders must decommission
2. If permit holders fail, trailing liability applies to former holders
3. If all liable parties fail, **the Crown has no statutory obligation**
4. This is where the Dual License Agreement v4.9 fills the gap

### 4.4 2025 Amendments — Discretionary Trailing Liability

The Crown Minerals Amendment Bill 2024 (introduced September 2024) made significant changes:
- **Removed automatic trailing liability** for immediately former permit holders
- **Added ministerial discretion** to require outgoing guarantees at point of transaction approval
- **Ministers can require** outgoing interest holders to provide guarantees for decommissioning costs
- **If outgoing person declines**, Minister may withhold consent to the transaction

**Impact:** This creates uncertainty — trailing liability is no longer automatic, and the Crown's exemption from decommissioning means orphan wells could fall through regulatory cracks.

## 6. DATA GAPS

1. **WA onshore well inventory** — No comprehensive public database of all non-decommissioned wells; DMIRS-025 dataset covers exploration/development but orphan status unclear
2. **SA well data** — Confidentiality period (2 years) limits access to recent well data; Cooper Basin cross-jurisdictional data gaps between SA and QLD
3. **NSW orphan well identification** — Project exists but no published comprehensive list with GPS coordinates
4. **VIC onshore wells** — Gippsland Basin onshore wells assessed but full inventory not public
5. **TAS petroleum wells** — Minimal data; very few wells but no systematic survey
6. **NZ onshore orphans** — 104 identified but GPS coordinates and individual well records not publicly available
7. **Methane emissions** — No comprehensive methane measurement program for Australian orphan wells (NT has partial data from CSIRO/GISERA)
8. **Historical wells** — Pre-1960s wells in NT and QLD may lack GPS coordinates (location errors up to 2 km)
9. **Cross-jurisdictional wells** — Cooper Basin wells spanning SA/QLD border create data gaps
10. **Financial assurance adequacy** — No public assessment of whether existing bonds/securities cover actual decommissioning costs
