## 2. DOCTRINE OF ABANDONED PROPERTY

### 2.1 Common Law Doctrine

The common law doctrine of abandoned property holds that when an owner voluntarily relinquishes all right, title, and interest in property with the intent to terminate ownership, and coupled with actual non-use, the property becomes *res nullius* — a thing without an owner — available for claim by the first person to take possession.

**Elements of Abandonment:**
1. Voluntary relinquishment
2. Intent to abandon
3. Actual non-use
4. Without vesting in another

### 2.2 The Master Case: *Gerhard v. Stephens*, 68 Cal.2d 864 (1968)

**Holding:** Oil and gas drilling rights are *profits a prendre* — incorporeal hereditaments — subject to abandonment at common law. Upon abandonment, the property interest **reverts to the servient estate** (the land from which it was carved).

**Application to Orphan Wells:** Every orphan well represents a corporation that held an incorporeal estate (profit a prendre / mineral lease) and walked away. Under *Gerhard*, that interest reverts to the Crown/state land from which it was carved. The Crown/state now holds both surface and mineral rights, and the Abundance Loop obligation attaches.

### 2.3 *Texaco v. Short*, 454 U.S. 516 (1982)

**Holding:** The US Supreme Court upheld the constitutionality of Indiana's Mineral Lapse Act: "failure to exercise some right of ownership over property for a specified period of time amounts to abandonment as a matter of law."

### 2.4 *Robinson v. Jacobs* (Texas, 1923)

**Holding:** An oil/gas lease is a determinable fee. Cessation of use = forfeiture. Abandonment of the enterprise ends the estate.

### 2.5 *Calhoon v. Neely* (Pennsylvania, 1902)

**Holding:** An oil lease is inchoate until oil is found. If the search is abandoned, title ends. Unexplained cessation of operations = presumed abandonment.

### 2.6 *State v. Phillips Petroleum Co.*, 212 Ark. 530 (1947)

**Holding:** At common law, escheat referred to real estate reverting to the crown in the absence of heirs; bona vacantia referred to personal property. In modern American decisions the distinction has been largely disregarded. Unclaimed royalties from oil wells constitute bona vacantia.

### 2.7 *Ronal Anderson v. Tenneco Oil Co.*, 2001-CA-0295 (Louisiana Ct. App., 2002)

**Holding:** When a company plugged and abandoned an offshore well and failed to remove casing, ownership of the casing **reverted by operation of law** to the State of Louisiana.

## 3. DOCTRINE OF ESCHEAT

### 3.1 Definition

At common law, **escheat** is the reversion of real property to the Crown/state when the owner dies without heirs (intestate) or when the owner is attainted of felony. In modern law, escheat applies to corporate dissolution, abandonment, and statutory abandonment.

### 3.2 Application to Orphan Wells

Orphan wells trigger escheat through four paths:
1. **Corporate Insolvency:** Corporation dissolves → mineral interests escheat to state

## 4. DOCTRINE OF FIRST OCCUPANCY

### 4.1 The Doctrine

First occupancy (*occupatio*) is one of the original modes of acquiring property under both Roman law and international law. The first person to take possession of unowned or abandoned property acquires ownership.

**From Vattel, *The Law of Nations* (1758), § 207:**
> "All mankind have an equal right to things that have not yet fallen into the possession of any one; and those things belong to the person who first takes possession of them."

### 4.2 Elements

1. **Animus occupandi** — Intent to possess the territory/property
2. **Actual taking of possession** — Physical acts of control and administration
3. **Terra nullius or res nullius** — The territory/property must be unowned or abandoned
4. **Peaceful acquisition** — No use of force

### 4.3 Application to Orphan Wells

Orphan wells and surrounding land constitute *res nullius* — ownerless things. The Dual License v4.9 demonstrates *animus occupandi*. Physical acts of control (plugging wells, remediating contamination, securing the site) constitute actual possession. First occupancy is satisfied.
