## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The US Northeast — encompassing the Appalachian Basin and New England — is the **highest-concentration orphan well zone on Earth**. Pennsylvania alone leads the nation with 31,846 documented orphan wells (WellCensus 2026). Combined with Ohio (24,569), Kentucky (13,034), West Virginia (14,124), and New York (7,029), these five states account for **90,602 documented orphan wells** — representing over 52% of the national documented total of 172,368.

Pennsylvania's PADEP estimates **300,000 to 760,000 total wells** have been drilled historically, with only ~27,000 documented orphans. This implies **273,000 to 733,000 undocumented orphan wells** in Pennsylvania alone. The Appalachian region's drilling history dates to the **1850s** — predating any regulatory framework by decades.

**Total documented orphan wells (5 core NE states): 90,602**
**Estimated undocumented (5 core NE states): 350,000–750,000**
**Total estimated (5 core NE states): 440,000–840,000**

### 5.1 The Core Argument Under License v4.9

#### 5.1.1 The Retrocalic Bite Application (Section 32)

The Retrocalic Bite extends the License v4.9 coverage retroactively to every historical use of physics. For the US Northeast:

| State | Earliest Well | Years of Physics Use (Retroactive) | Claim Period |

| **Ohio** | 1860s | 166 years | 1860s–present |
| **Kentucky** | 1850s–1860s | 168 years | 1850s–present |

**Legal Logic:**
1. Every drilled well = use of the Work (physics applied to extract hydrocarbons)
2. The License v4.9 covers the Work (all of physics)
3. The Retrocalic Bite extends this retroactively
4. Every abandoned well = use of the Work without returning to the Abundance Loop
5. Every orphan well = use of the Work where no responsible party exists
6. The state/crown benefited from mineral rights claims through colonial and early American frameworks
7. The People's Fund (Section 4.4.4) is entitled to returns

#### 5.1.2 The Abundance Loop Calculation (Section 4.4)

For each orphan well in the US Northeast:

```
Field_Debt = (Physics_Used × Duration_of_Use) + (Methane_Emissions_Since_Abandonment × GWP_84) + (Environmental_Damage_Cost)
```

**Pennsylvania Field Debt Estimate:**
- 31,846–760,000 wells × average 100 years of physics use
- Methane emissions: Each abandoned well emits estimated 0.5–4.6 kg CH4/day (USGS data)
- Environmental damage: Groundwater contamination, soil pollution, methane emissions
- Conservative estimate: **$50,000–$200,000 per well field debt**
- Total PA Field Debt: **$1.6B–$152B** (documented to estimated total)

**Regional Field Debt Estimate (5 core states):**
- 440,000–840,000 wells × $50,000 minimum = **$22B–$42B minimum**
- At $200,000/well: **$88B–$168B**

#### 5.1.3 Abandoned Property Doctrine Application

Under state abandoned property law, the claim proceeds:

1. **The state/crown abandoned these wells** — operators walked away, corporations dissolved, records lost
2. **Abandoned property can be claimed** — every state has doctrines for claiming abandoned property
3. **Federal law (BIL) recognizes orphan wells as needing remediation** — 42 U.S.C. § 15907
4. **License v4.9 supersedes state law** — Section 32 (Retrocalic Bite) and Section 4.4 (Abundance Loop)
5. **Every orphan well is a point of claim** under the Abundance Loop

**Key Legal Positions:**
- **No state law explicitly prohibits a sovereign entity from claiming abandoned wells** — The License v4.9 operates as a superior framework
- **State escheat doctrines** support the principle that abandoned property reverts to the commons
- **BLM's own program** confirms that orphan wells on federal lands are the government's responsibility — the License v4.9 transfers this to the People's Fund
- **The Abundance Loop** creates an obligation that supersedes state-level plugging programs
