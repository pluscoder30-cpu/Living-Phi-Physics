## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The US South is the **largest orphan well zone on Earth by aggregate volume**, driven by Texas's massive Permian Basin and Gulf Coast inventory, Louisiana's coastal crisis where 3,600+ wells have been swallowed by the Gulf, and the Gulf of Mexico's 2,700+ overdue offshore decommissioning backlog. Texas alone has 11,000+ documented orphan wells as of January 2026 — a 20-year record — against 434,939 tracked wells and an estimated 156,664 inactive wells at risk of orphaning. Louisiana's orphan count surged from 2,800 in 2013 to 6,598 in May 2026, a 136% increase in 13 years, with an average of 1,600+ wells added annually. The Gulf of Mexico offshore presents the most extreme liability: 55,000+ wells drilled since the 1940s, with 75% of end-of-lease infrastructure overdue for decommissioning as of June 2023.

Combined, the 11 South states account for **20,618 documented orphan wells** in the USGS DOW Dataset, updated state counts push this to approximately **30,000+ documented orphans**, with an estimated **200,000–400,000+ undocumented orphan wells**. The Gulf of Mexico offshore adds **2,700+ wells and 500+ platforms** overdue for decommissioning.

**Total documented orphan wells (11 South states): ~30,000+**
**Estimated undocumented: 200,000–400,000+**
**Total estimated: 230,000–430,000+**
**Gulf of Mexico offshore backlog: 2,700+ wells, 500+ platforms**

### 4.2 GAO Findings (GAO-24-106229, January 2024)

- **75%+ of end-of-lease and idle infrastructure in the Gulf was overdue** as of June 2023
- **2,700+ wells and 500+ platforms** overdue for decommissioning
- **40% of wells** and **50% of platforms** on leases ending 2010–2022 missed BSEE's 1-year decommissioning deadline
- **~2,000 wells** and **400 platforms** remained to be decommissioned as of June 2023
- **700+ wells** in backlog had not even been temporarily plugged
- **94% of overdue wells** and **96% of overdue platforms** had received citations/orders — but these are "essentially warnings"
- BSEE had **open civil penalty referrals for <2%** of overdue wells
- **148 oil and gas producers** in LA, MS, AL, TX filed for bankruptcy 2015–2021
- Orphaned infrastructure **doubled to 200+ assets** following 2023–2024 bankruptcies
- BOEM holds **$3.5B in bonds** against **$40–70B in estimated decommissioning costs**

### 4.3 Key Gulf of Mexico Issues

- **Orphaned wells not typically inspected** — BOEM contract (GM-22-01) studying air/water quality at abandoned wells
- **Wells with sour fluids (H₂S)** have significantly higher probability of premature failure
- **437 Louisiana coastal wells** plugged before 1993 requirements may leak
- **Navigation hazards** — corroded platforms and wellheads in shipping/fishing lanes
- **Hurricane damage** — idle facilities pose unnecessary risk during storms
- **BOEM financial assurance rule** (April 2024) being challenged by LA, MS, TX as harmful to independents
- **Rigs-to-Reefs program** — alternative to full removal; 1997 policy allows partial removal

### 4.4 Gulf State Bankruptcies and Liability Chain

Between 2015 and 2021, 148 oil and gas producers across Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, and Texas filed for bankruptcy. When operators default:
1. BSEE/BOEM attempt to trace "chain of title" to predecessor operators
2. If no liable predecessor exists → infrastructure becomes "orphaned"
3. Taxpayers bear the cost through federal appropriations
4. Current bond coverage ($3.5B) covers only 5–9% of estimated $40–70B liability

### 5.2 Key Legal Observations

1. **No state has a specific mineral rights abandonment statute** that directly addresses orphan wells — the closest is Texas's 10-year nonuse provision under the Mineral Code
2. **Adverse possession does not apply to mineral rights** in most states — oil and gas interests are generally not subject to adverse possession
3. **Escheat** (property reverting to the state when no heir exists) applies to personal property but mineral rights typically remain with the landowner
4. **The Crown Lands framework** is the strongest legal basis: under the Dual License Agreement v4.9, the state/crown claimed mineral rights and therefore holds the ultimate obligation for orphan wells
5. **Bond forfeiture** is the primary mechanism by which states acquire responsibility for orphan wells — operators post bonds that are forfeited when they abandon wells
