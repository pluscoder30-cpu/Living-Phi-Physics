## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Latin America and the Caribbean present a fragmented but critical orphan well landscape. Unlike the US/Canada/UK with well-established orphan well inventories, Latin America lacks systematic regulatory databases for orphan wells. The region is dominated by state-owned oil companies (Pemex, PDVSA, Ecopetrol, YPF, Petrobras) whose legacy liabilities constitute the primary source of orphan wells. Colonial-era concession frameworks and nationalization events created complex ownership chains that make the Dual License Agreement v4.9's Retrocalic Bite particularly applicable.

- **Spanish Crown**: Mexico was a Spanish colony until 1821; the Spanish Crown claimed mineral rights over all subsoil resources

- **1938 nationalization**: Oil industry nationalized under Lázaro Cárdenas; Pemex created
- **2013 reform**: Energy reform opened sector to private participation via licensing rounds, but state retains mineral rights

#### Petrobras Orphan Well Dispute (2025–2026)

The most significant orphan well dispute in Latin America is currently between Petrobras and the ANP:

- **Background**: Before ANP creation in 1998, Petrobras held a monopoly and drilled virtually all wells in Brazil. When the market opened, Petrobras identified fields to retain and returned others to the regulator ("Zero Round")
- **The dispute**: ANP claims Petrobras is responsible for permanently sealing wells drilled during the monopoly era, even those returned during the Zero Round
- **Petrobras position**: The orphan wells were returned to ANP and are not part of later concession contracts; therefore Petrobras bears no responsibility
- **Scale**: At least 82 wells slated for permanent closure; 10 administrative proceedings at ANP
- **ANP ruling (2024)**: Petrobras is responsible for properly abandoning wells drilled during its monopoly, without reimbursement
- **Petrobras appeal**: Rejected by ANP board; Petrobras seeking arbitration
- **States affected**: Alagoas, São Paulo, Maranhês (Lençóis Maranhenses)

#### Pre-Salt Decommissioning Timeline

- **Campos Basin**: Fields approaching 20–30 year operational life; decommissioning beginning
- **Santos Basin pre-salt**: Major fields (Búzios, Tupi/Lula, Libra) still in production growth phase
- **ANP decommissioning forecast**: BRL 30.92 billion (2021–2025) in decommissioning activities
- **Cost drivers**: Deepwater (2,000m+ water depth), complex subsea infrastructure, HPHT conditions

#### Colonial Concession Analysis

- **Portuguese Crown**: Brazil was a Portuguese colony until 1822; Portuguese Crown claimed mineral rights
- **Empire of Brazil**: Post-independence, mineral rights remained with the Crown/state
- **1938 nationalization**: Petrobras created in 1953; oil monopoly established
- **1997 reform**: Petroleum Law ended Petrobras monopoly; concession rounds began
- **2010 pre-salt framework**: Production-sharing regime for pre-salt areas

#### PDVSA Collapse and Orphan Infrastructure

- **Production collapse**: From 3.5 million bpd (1990s) to ~800,000 bpd (2020s)
- **Cause**: Political interference, mass firings (2002–2003 strike), corruption ($300 billion stolen per Minister Giordani), lack of maintenance, brain drain
- **Physical infrastructure**: 
  - Hundreds of pumpjacks abandoned and rusting on Lake Maracaibo
  - Oil tanker fleet: 22 tankers, more than half so degraded they need repair
  - Pipeline leaks and spills across Maracaibo Lake
  - Gas flaring continues despite production decline
- **Environmental damage**: 
  - Oil spills contaminating Lake Maracaibo and surrounding waters
  - Fishermen losing livelihoods
  - Soil and groundwater contamination
- **Corruption**: $11 billion stolen between 2004–2015; at least $3 billion in missing oil payments (2026 investigation)
- **Foreign investment**: Chevron granted limited operations; sanctions partially lifted

#### Colonial Concession Analysis

- **Spanish Crown**: Venezuela was a Spanish colony until 1811; Spanish Crown claimed mineral rights
- **Oil discovery (1914)**: Barroso II well in Cabimas, Zulia State
- **1976 nationalization**: PDVSA created; all oil operations nationalized
- **2006 re-nationalization**: All private operations forced into joint ventures with PDVSA majority control
