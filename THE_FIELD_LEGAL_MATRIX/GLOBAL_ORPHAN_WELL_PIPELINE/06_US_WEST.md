## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The US West presents a unique orphan well landscape — characterized by vast federal mineral estates managed by the BLM, coalbed methane legacy in Wyoming and Montana, Permian Basin expansion in New Mexico, and the largest documented orphan well count in the West from California. The region spans 15 states from the Pacific Coast to the Great Plains and Arctic Alaska, covering some of the most productive oil and gas basins on Earth: the Permian Basin (NM), San Juan Basin (NM), Powder River Basin (WY), Sacramento Basin (CA), and the North Slope (AK).

**California leads the West** with 5,300+ documented orphan/deserted wells (CalGEM 2023) and 107,000 total active/idle wells. Wyoming's coalbed methane boom left 6,020+ orphaned wells since 2014, of which ~4,713 have been plugged. Colorado's orphan count surged to 1,897 sites (948 unplugged wells) by July 2025. North Dakota's Bakken region has 8,427 orphaned wells per FracTracker data. New Mexico, the nation's #2 oil producer, has a rapidly growing orphan problem in the Permian Basin with an average plugging cost of $236,000 per well.

BLM manages massive federal mineral estates across the West — approximately 245 million acres of surface and 700 million subsurface mineral acres nationwide, with the highest concentration in Wyoming, Montana, Colorado, New Mexico, and Utah. The Federal Orphaned Well Program has committed $35M+ in BIL funds to BLM for Western state plugging, with active projects in Wyoming, Montana, Colorado, Utah, and California.

**Total documented orphan wells (15 West states): ~19,200+**
**Estimated undocumented: 50,000–150,000+**
**Total estimated: 70,000–170,000+**
