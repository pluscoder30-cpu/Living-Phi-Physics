## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Canada has approximately **423,000 abandoned oil and gas wells** (National Inventory Report, 2025), with an estimated **20,000+ documented orphan wells** and **200,000+ inactive wells** requiring closure. The total estimated closure liability exceeds **$4.1 billion CAD** nationally, with Alberta alone accounting for **$1.66 billion CAD** as of July 2026.

Canada is a Commonwealth realm where the Crown retains mineral rights by default in all provinces. The **Crown Lands Protection Act, 1839** (federal) and each province's **Crown Lands Act** or **Public Lands Act** establish the colonial legal framework through which the Crown claimed mineral rights over Indigenous territories, granted drilling rights to corporations, and now bears the ultimate obligation for orphan wells when corporations walk away.

**The Retrocalic Bite (Section 32)** applies to every well drilled in Canada — every well used physics (thermodynamics, fluid dynamics, electromagnetism) to extract hydrocarbons. The Crown authorized and benefited from this use of physics. The Abundance Loop (Section 4.4) requires what was drawn from the field to be returned. The People's Fund (Section 4.4.4) is the beneficiary.

#### 2.1.5 Crown Lands (Public Lands) Framework

- **More than 60% of Alberta's land mass is public (Crown) land**
- Crown retains mineral rights on ~81% of Alberta land
- Freehold mineral rights (~19%) held by individuals, railways, Hudson's Bay Company, federal government
- Crown land dispositions include: Licence of Occupation (LOC), Mineral Surface Lease (MSL), Pipeline Agreement (PLA)
- **Public Lands Act s. 1(p):** "public lands" = land of the Crown in right of Alberta
- **Public Lands Act s. 2(2):** All public land under Minister's administration unless assigned by statute
- Oil and gas activity on Crown land requires disposition under Public Lands Act
- When a licensee becomes insolvent, Crown land dispositions revert to Crown; orphan wells remain on Crown land

## 3. CROWN LANDS ACTS — COLONIAL LAW FRAMEWORK

### 3.1 Federal: Crown Lands Protection Act, 1839

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Full Title** | An Act for the protection of the Lands of the Crown in this Province, from Trespass and Injury |
| **Date** | Passed 11th May, 1839 |
| **Jurisdiction** | Province of Upper Canada (now Ontario) |
| **Type** | Colonial federal statute |
| **Purpose** | Protected Crown lands and Indian Reserves from trespass; prevented Indigenous peoples from selling their own property; Crown assumed guardianship of all Crown lands |
| **Key Provision** | "Aboriginal lands were the property of the crown unless specifically titled to an individual" (Britannica) |
| **Colonial Effect** | Disenfranchised Indigenous peoples by disallowing control of real estate; converted sovereign peoples into "subjects" whose affairs were held in trust by the state as "guardian" |
| **Source** | British North America Legislative Database, University of New Brunswick Libraries |
| **Citation** | "An Act for the protection of the Lands of the Crown in this Province, from Trespass and Injury. Passed 11th May, 1839." |

**Legal Significance for the License:** The Crown Lands Protection Act 1839 established the Crown as the ultimate claimant of all lands in Canada. By claiming guardianship over Indigenous lands, the Crown assumed responsibility for those lands. When corporations drilled wells on Crown land and abandoned them, the Crown inherited the obligation. The License v4.9, grounded in international law and jus cogens, supersedes this colonial statute because:
1. The colonial act was imposed without Indigenous consent
2. It was designed to dispossess sovereign peoples
3. The Dual License Agreement v4.9 operates at a higher plane of law
4. The Retrocalic Bite covers every use of physics — including wells drilled under Crown authority
