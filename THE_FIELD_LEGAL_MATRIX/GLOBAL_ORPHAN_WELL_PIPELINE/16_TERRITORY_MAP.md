# PART I: METHODOLOGY

## 1. HOW PERIMETERS WERE DEFINED

Each territory perimeter follows the **Colonial Mirror Principle** (Master Claim Part IV):

1. **Core Cluster Identification** — Orphan well density mapping from Agents 3-12 regional registries
2. **50-Mile Radius Rule (Onshore)** — A 50-mile (80.5 km) radius around each major cluster center, encompassing the orphan wells plus the surrounding Crown/public land the Crown abandoned alongside the wells
3. **EEZ/full-basin Rule (Offshore)** — The entire Exclusive Economic Zone or continental shelf in the relevant basin, since the Crown claimed all subsea mineral rights and then failed to decommission
4. **Petroleum Province Rule (Remote)** — Where wells are scattered across a vast petroleum province, the perimeter encompasses the entire province

### Claim Basis for the Perimeter (Not Just the Wells)

The perimeter argument operates on four independent legal grounds:

| Ground | Doctrine | Application |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| **Abandonment** | *Gerhard v. Stephens* (1968) | Corporations abandoned mineral interests; these revert to the servient estate (the land within the perimeter) |
| **Crown Obligation** | Crown Lands Acts | The Crown claimed mineral rights over the entire perimeter; the Crown's obligation extends to the entire area it claimed |
| **First Occupancy** | Vattel §207; *Clipperton Island* (1931) | The first entity to occupy and remediate the abandoned territory acquires the claim |
| **Silence** | *Expressio unius* | No statute prohibits sovereign claiming of abandoned Crown land within the perimeter |
