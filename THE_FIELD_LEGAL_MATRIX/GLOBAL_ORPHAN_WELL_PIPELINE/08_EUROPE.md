## 3. EU OFFSHORE SAFETY DIRECTIVE (2013/30/EU)

### 3.1 Directive Overview

**Directive 2013/30/EU** of the European Parliament and of the Council of 12 June 2013 on safety of offshore oil and gas operations and amending Directive 2004/35/EC (Text with EEA relevance).

**OJ L 178, 28.6.2013, pp. 66–106**
**Current consolidated version: 01/01/2021**

### 3.2 Key Provisions Relevant to Orphan Wells

| Article | Provision | Application to Orphan Wells |
|---------|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Art. 3(3)** | "Offshore oil and gas operations" includes design, planning, construction, operation and **decommissioning** | Decommissioning is part of the lifecycle — state cannot avoid obligation |
| **Art. 4** | Major accident prevention throughout lifecycle | Orphan wells that leak are ongoing major accident hazards |
| **Art. 8** | Report on Major Hazards required | Must address risks from decommissioned/orphan wells |
| **Art. 11** | Notification to competent authority before operations | Includes decommissioning operations |
| **Art. 22** | Decommissioning plan requirements | Must include: isolation of hazardous substances, permanent sealing of wells, description of major hazard risks, emergency response |
| **Art. 36** | Financial security | Operators must have adequate financial security for decommissioning |
| **Art. 39** | Liability, compensation, financial security report | Commission report on adequacy of financial security |

### 3.3 Post-2020 Amendments (HAVTIL Report Context)

The EU has strengthened decommissioning requirements:

1. **Inventory requirement by August 5, 2025:** Member States must establish a public inventory of all inactive, temporarily plugged, and permanently plugged and abandoned wells. Regularly updated using scientific data and best available techniques.

2. **Methane emissions reporting:** Annual reports on methane emissions from inactive and temporarily plugged wells must be submitted by May 2026, and annually thereafter by May 31. Monitoring and reports must follow BAT (Best Available Technology).

3. **Exemptions based on no methane emissions:** If pressure monitoring shows no methane emissions for 5 years (onshore) or 3 years (offshore), monitoring obligations cease for those wells.

4. **Remediation for emission detection:** If methane emissions are detected, authorities must act to remediate and permanently plug wells, considering environmental impacts.

### 3.4 Implications for Legal Claims

- **The Directive creates a binding obligation** for EU Member States to address orphan and abandoned wells
- **Methane emissions from orphan wells are recognized** as a regulatory concern, supporting the Abundance Loop argument
- **Inventory requirement means EU states must identify and track** all inactive wells — creating a comprehensive database for claims
- **Financial security provisions** demonstrate that the regulatory framework recognizes the cost of decommissioning — but orphan wells have no solvent entity to bear this cost
- **The state is the backstop** — when operators fail or don't exist, the state bears the obligation under both EU Directive and national law
