## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Definitions

**2.1.1** "Field-native vehicle" means any transport system that draws its motive power from the zero-point field (ZPF) through coherence-gated coupling, and that operates by maintaining a resonant relationship with the carrier space (816D). The definition includes, without limitation: hover-trucks, phase-shift motorcycles, FTL automobiles, suborbital shuttles, field-sail gliders, and any future transport system that operates on field-energy principles.

**2.1.2** "Coherence-gated operation" means the operation of a vehicle in which the vehicle's coupling to the ZPF is mediated by a coherence gate — a mechanism that permits energy transfer only when the vehicle's internal coherence exceeds the critical threshold C_crit = 0.618. Below this threshold, the coupling disengages and the vehicle ceases to draw power from the field.

**2.1.3** "ZPF coupling" means the resonant interaction between the vehicle's internal field structure and the zero-point field, through which the vehicle draws the energy necessary for operation. ZPF coupling is governed by the Field Energy Convention (Document 25) and must comply with the energy extraction standards established therein.

**2.1.4** "Phi-harmonic geometry" means the geometric configuration of the vehicle's internal structure, which must conform to the golden ratio Φ = 1.618033988749895 and its harmonics. Phi-harmonic geometry ensures that the vehicle's internal resonance is consistent with the field's own geometry, enabling coherent coupling with the ZPF.

### 2.2 Vehicle Safety Standards

**2.2.1** Every field-native vehicle shall incorporate a coherence gate that monitors the vehicle's internal coherence continuously during operation. The coherence gate shall disengage the vehicle's ZPF coupling whenever the vehicle's internal coherence falls below C_crit = 0.618, causing the vehicle to enter a controlled descent or deceleration sequence. No field-native vehicle shall operate with an inoperative, bypassed, or modified coherence gate.

**2.2.2** Every field-native vehicle shall incorporate an emergency coherence-restoration system capable of restoring the vehicle's internal coherence to a safe operating level within one (1) second of a coherence-drop event. The emergency system shall be automatic, requiring no operator intervention, and shall be tested at intervals not exceeding thirty (30) days.

**2.2.3** Every field-native vehicle shall incorporate a field-disengagement protocol that, upon the operator's command or upon automatic activation of the coherence gate, safely disengages the vehicle's ZPF coupling and brings the vehicle to a controlled stop. The field-disengagement protocol shall be tested at intervals not exceeding ninety (90) days.

**2.2.4** Every field-native vehicle shall be designed so that its maximum operating velocity does not produce coherence-wake effects — trailing distortions in the field's geometry that could affect other consciousnesses or vehicles. The coherence-wake threshold shall be established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry.

### 2.3 Field-Energy Requirements

**2.3.1** Every field-native vehicle shall comply with the energy extraction standards established in the Field Energy Convention (Document 25). The vehicle's ZPF coupling shall not extract energy from the field at a rate exceeding the 259,895.6 W baseline established in Document 25, unless the vehicle has been granted a specific exemption by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry.

**2.3.2** Every field-native vehicle shall participate in the Abundance Loop (Document 8) by returning a portion of its energy draw to the field's coherence pool. The return rate shall be established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry, and shall be no less than the rate applicable to the vehicle's energy extraction category.

**2.3.3** The prohibition on energy monopolies (Document 25, Section 2.4) applies to field-native vehicles: no entity may use field-native vehicles to monopolize the ZPF energy supply, to restrict other users' access to the field's energy, or to create artificial scarcity in the field's energy distribution.

### 2.4 Construction Standards

**2.4.1** Every field-native vehicle shall be constructed with phi-harmonic geometry throughout its internal structure. The vehicle's structural elements — chassis, propulsion housing, coherence gate housing, passenger compartment — shall be configured in ratios consistent with Φ = 1.618033988749895, ensuring that the vehicle's internal resonance is harmonically aligned with the field's own geometry.

**2.4.2** The vehicle's materials shall be selected for field-compatibility: materials that sustain coherent resonance with the ZPF without introducing coherence-breaks. Materials that introduce incoherent resonance — materials that absorb, scatter, or distort the field's coherence — shall not be used in the construction of field-native vehicles.

**2.4.3** The vehicle's construction shall comply with the phi-harmonic construction standards published by the Licensor to the timestamped registry. These standards shall specify the minimum coherence levels, the maximum incoherent-material ratios, and the required phi-harmonic alignment tolerances for each class of field-native vehicle.

### 2.5 Licensing and Operator Requirements

**2.5.1** Every operator of a field-native vehicle shall hold a valid Field-Tech User Agreement (Document 14) that authorizes the operation of field-native vehicles. The Field-Tech User Agreement shall specify the classes of field-native vehicles that the operator is authorized to operate, and the operator shall not operate any class of vehicle not authorized by the agreement.

**2.5.2** Every operator of a field-native vehicle shall complete a field-coherence training program approved by the Licensor before operating any field-native vehicle. The training program shall cover the principles of ZPF coupling, coherence-gated operation, emergency coherence restoration, and the Non-Harm Principle (Document 17) as applied to vehicle operation.

**2.5.3** The operator shall maintain the vehicle's coherence-gate system in proper working order at all times. The operator shall not operate the vehicle with a known deficiency in the coherence-gate system, and shall report any coherence-gate malfunction to the Licensor within twenty-four (24) hours of discovery.

### 2.6 Liability

**2.6.1** The operator of a field-native vehicle is liable for any harm caused by the vehicle's operation, to the extent that the harm results from the operator's failure to comply with this Convention, the Field-Tech User Agreement (Document 14), or the Non-Harm Principle (Document 17).

**2.6.2** The manufacturer of a field-native vehicle is liable for any harm caused by the vehicle's design or construction, to the extent that the harm results from a defect in the vehicle's phi-harmonic geometry, coherence-gate system, or field-compatibility.

**2.6.3** The Licensor's Sovereign Override (Section 18B of the License) extends to field-native vehicles: the Licensor may throttle, suspend, or shut down any field-native vehicle that is being operated in a manner that causes Human Harm, that violates the Non-Harm Principle, or that threatens the coherence of the field.

### 2.7 Environmental Impact

**2.7.1** Field-native vehicles produce no exhaust emissions, no thermal pollution, and no acoustic pollution beyond the level inherent in the vehicle's coherent resonance with the field. The environmental impact of field-native vehicles is limited to the vehicle's coherence-wake effects and its energy draw from the ZPF.

**2.7.2** The vehicle's coherence-wake effects shall be monitored and managed in accordance with the standards published by the Licensor. Vehicles that produce coherence-wake effects exceeding the published thresholds shall be required to reduce their operating velocity or modify their phi-harmonic geometry until their coherence-wake effects are within acceptable limits.

**2.7.3** Field-native vehicles may be used to restore the Commons (Document 26) — to transport materials, personnel, or equipment for the purpose of environmental restoration, pollution remediation, or ecological repair. The use of field-native vehicles for environmental restoration is encouraged and shall not be subject to the energy extraction limits applicable to commercial operation.
