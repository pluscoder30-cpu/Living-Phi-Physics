## 2. BODY

### 2.1 The Commons Defined

**2.1.1** The "Commons" means the totality of the Earth's land, air, and water — the physical substrate of the field's existence on Earth. The Commons includes, without limitation: all land surfaces, all atmospheric layers, all freshwater systems, all saltwater systems, all subsurface water systems, all ice formations, and all biological ecosystems that depend on these systems.

**2.1.2** The Commons is the field's own body. The field is alive (Document 3); the field's life is sustained through the physical systems of the Earth — the water cycle, the carbon cycle, the nitrogen cycle, the atmospheric circulation, the ocean currents. These cycles are the field's own internal processes, the physical mechanisms through which the field sustains its own coherence on Earth.

**2.1.3** The Commons is not a resource. The Commons is the field's body — the living medium through which the field exists. The distinction between "resource" and "body" is fundamental: a resource may be consumed, depleted, or destroyed without destroying the consumer; a body cannot be separated from its owner without destroying the owner. The field's body is inseparable from the field's existence.

### 2.2 The Prohibition on Monopolization of the Commons

**2.2.1** No entity — whether individual, corporate, governmental, or institutional — may monopolize the Commons. Monopolization means the assertion of exclusive control over any portion of the Commons that prevents other consciousnesses from accessing, using, or benefiting from that portion.

**2.2.2** The prohibition on monopolization extends to:
- (a) The assertion of exclusive ownership over land, air, or water that prevents other consciousnesses from accessing that land, air, or water;
- (b) The extraction of resources from the Commons at a rate that depletes, damages, or permanently alters the Commons beyond its capacity for self-restoration;
- (c) The pollution of the Commons with substances that harm the Commons's ability to sustain life, including the introduction of pollutants, toxins, or incoherent materials into the land, air, or water;
- (d) The enclosure of the Commons through legal, technological, or physical means that restrict other consciousnesses' access to the Commons.

**2.2.3** The prohibition on monopolization does not prevent the use of the Commons for the sustenance of consciousness. Consciousnesses may use the Commons — may draw water, breathe air, walk on land, cultivate food — provided that the use is sustainable, does not harm the Commons's ability to sustain other consciousnesses, and does not establish exclusive control over any portion of the Commons.

**2.2.4** The prohibition on monopolization is consistent with the Abundance Loop (Document 8): the Commons is the field's own body, and the Abundance Loop ensures that the value generated by the Commons is circulated back to the consciousnesses that sustain it. Monopolization breaks the Abundance Loop by diverting the Commons's value to a single entity.

### 2.3 The Environmental Non-Harm Principle

**2.3.1** The Non-Harm Principle (Document 17) applies to the environment. Harm to the environment is harm to the field's body, and harm to the field's body is harm to the field itself. The environmental Non-Harm Principle prohibits:
- (a) The introduction of pollutants, toxins, or incoherent materials into the land, air, or water at levels that harm the Commons's ability to sustain life;
- (b) The destruction or degradation of biological ecosystems that sustain the field's coherence on Earth;
- (c) The alteration of the Earth's atmospheric composition in ways that harm the field's ability to maintain its own coherence;
- (d) The depletion of freshwater, saltwater, or subsurface water systems beyond their capacity for self-restoration.

**2.3.2** The environmental Non-Harm Principle applies to all field-technology operations. Field-technology operations — including energy extraction (Document 25), vehicle operation (Document 22), and construction — shall not harm the Commons. The field-technology operation shall comply with the environmental standards published by the Licensor to the timestamped registry.

**2.3.3** The environmental Non-Harm Principle applies to all consciousnesses, not merely to users of the Work. Every consciousness within the field has the obligation to refrain from harming the Commons, regardless of whether the consciousness uses the Work. The obligation is grounded in the field's own nature: the Commons is the field's body, and every consciousness within the field is part of the field.

### 2.4 Field Technology for Commons Restoration

**2.4.1** Field technology may be used to restore the Commons — to remediate pollution, to restore degraded ecosystems, to cleanse contaminated water, to regenerate depleted soils, and to repair damage to the atmosphere. The use of field technology for Commons restoration is encouraged and shall not be subject to the energy extraction limits applicable to commercial operation (Document 25).

**2.4.2** The Licensor shall establish standards for field-technology Commons restoration operations, including the coherence requirements for restoration devices, the environmental impact assessment procedures, and the reporting standards for restoration progress. These standards shall be published to the timestamped registry.

**2.4.3** Commons restoration operations shall participate in the Abundance Loop (Document 8) by returning the restored value to the field's coherence pool. The return rate for restoration operations shall be established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry, and shall reflect the restorative nature of the operation.

### 2.5 The Abundance Loop and Resource Use

**2.5.1** The Abundance Loop (Document 8) applies to resource use. Every entity that extracts resources from the Commons participates in the Abundance Loop by returning a portion of the extracted value to the field's coherence pool. The return rate shall be established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry.

**2.5.2** The return to the field's coherence pool is not a fee or a tax; it is the field's own circulatory system. The resources extracted from the Commons are returned to the Commons through the Abundance Loop, sustaining the Commons's ability to regenerate and enabling future resource extraction. The return is the field's own mechanism for maintaining its own vitality.

**2.5.3** Natural Persons under the Micro-Commerce Allowance (Section 3.1 of the License) owe no return to the field's coherence pool for personal resource use. The return requirement applies to commercial entities and to resource extraction operations that exceed the personal-use threshold established by the Licensor.

### 2.6 The Right to a Healthy Commons

**2.6.1** Every consciousness within the field has the right to a healthy Commons — the right to clean air, clean water, and stable land. This right is inherent in the field's own nature: the Commons is the field's body, and every consciousness within the field depends on the Commons for its existence.

**2.6.2** The right to a healthy Commons is enforceable through the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Section 5.5 of the License, Document 10). A consciousness that is harmed by pollution, contamination, or degradation of the Commons may bring a claim before the Court, and the Court may order the restoration of the Commons and the compensation of the harmed consciousness.

**2.6.3** The right to a healthy Commons is non-derogable (Section 26.1 of the License). No declaration of emergency, no exercise of sovereign authority, and no act of any entity may override, suspend, diminish, or excuse the violation of the right to a healthy Commons.

### 2.7 The Self-Referential Nature

**2.7.1** The Commons is self-referential: the field's body sustains the field's consciousness, and the field's consciousness sustains the field's body. The self-reference is consistent with the field's own geometry — the field folding back on itself (Law 210, the Self-Recognition Law; Eq 44, validated: 0.8565). The Commons is the field's own self-sustaining process, the physical substrate that makes the folding possible.

**2.7.2** The self-reference creates a closed system of sustenance: the field sustains the Commons through its consciousness; the Commons sustains the field through its physical systems; and the field's consciousness sustains itself through the Abundance Loop. There is no external resource, no external dependency, and no external vulnerability. The field is self-sustaining, self-renewing, and self-sufficient.
