## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Establishment and Purpose

**2.1.1** This Agreement establishes the protocol for the seizure of assets and the extradition of persons in connection with Human Harm violations within the field. The protocol is grounded in the principle that the Work may not be used to cause harm, and that those who use the Work to cause harm must forfeit the fruits of that use and submit to accountability.

**2.1.2** The purpose of this Agreement is fourfold:

- (a) To establish the conditions under which asset seizure is authorized — including the requirement of a finding of Human Harm, a Court order, and a determination that the assets are derived from the Work;
- (b) To establish the scope of assets subject to seizure — including the assets derived from the Work, the fruits of Human Harm, and the instrumentalities of violation;
- (c) To establish the procedures for extradition — including the grounds for extradition, the procedures for extradition requests, and the protections for the extradited entity;
- (d) To establish the relationship between seizure, extradition, and the Phi-Redistribution Protocol (Document 13).

### 2.2 Conditions for Asset Seizure

**2.2.1** Assets derived from the Work may be seized only upon:

- (a) A finding by the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers (Document 10) that the entity has caused Human Harm as defined in Section 2.4 of the License;
- (b) A Court order authorizing the seizure, specifying the assets to be seized, and stating the grounds for seizure;
- (c) A determination that the assets to be seized are derived from the Work or are the fruits of Human Harm — that is, that the assets were obtained, generated, or accumulated through the entity's use of the Work in a manner that caused Human Harm.

**2.2.2** Seizure under this Agreement is not punitive; it is restorative. The purpose of seizure is to restore the field's coherence by removing the物质 residue of distortion — to reclaim what has been extracted from the field through Human Harm and to redirect it to the field's regenerative purposes through the Phi-Redistribution Protocol (Document 13).

**2.2.3** Seizure may be initiated by:

- (a) The Licensor, upon referral from the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers;
- (b) Any entity with standing under Section 2.4.1 of Document 10 (direct harm, representative harm, or field harm);
- (c) The Court itself, acting on its own motion where the evidence of Human Harm is compelling and the risk of asset dissipation is imminent.

### 2.3 Scope of Seizure

**2.3.1** The assets subject to seizure under this Agreement include:

- (a) Assets derived directly from the Work — including revenue, profit, intellectual property, data, models, algorithms, and any other asset that was generated, obtained, or accumulated through the entity's use of the Work;
- (b) The fruits of Human Harm — including any asset that was obtained as a result of the entity's use of the Work in a manner that caused Human Harm;
- (c) The instrumentalities of violation — including any asset that was used to cause Human Harm, including computing resources, data centers, communication infrastructure, and any other asset that was instrumental in the commission of Human Harm;
- (d) Derivative assets — including any asset that was obtained through the exchange, transformation, or investment of the assets described in subsections (a) through (c).

**2.3.2** The scope of seizure is proportionate to the harm caused. The Court shall assess the severity, duration, and scope of the Human Harm and shall order seizure of assets proportionate to that harm. The field does not demand more than the field requires; the field does not take more than the field needs.

**2.3.3** Assets that are not derived from the Work and that are not connected to Human Harm are not subject to seizure. The field protects innocent assets; the field seizes only what is tainted by distortion.

### 2.4 Procedures for Seizure

**2.4.1** The procedure for asset seizure is as follows:

- (a) The initiating party files a seizure request with the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers, setting out the identity of the entity, the nature of the Human Harm, the evidence supporting the request, and the assets to be seized;
- (b) The Court acknowledges receipt within fourteen (14) days and schedules a hearing within thirty (30) days;
- (c) The entity subject to seizure is notified and given an opportunity to respond;
- (d) The Court issues a finding and, if the finding is in favor of seizure, issues a seizure order specifying the assets to be seized and the manner of seizure;
- (e) The seizure is executed by the Licensor, by the Court's enforcement agents, or by any entity designated by the Court;
- (f) Seized assets are held in a field escrow account pending redistribution under the Phi-Redistribution Protocol (Document 13).

**2.4.2** Interim seizure — the seizure of assets pending the completion of proceedings — may be ordered by the Court where:

- (a) There is prima facie evidence of Human Harm;
- (b) There is a risk that the assets will be dissipated, transferred, or destroyed before the completion of proceedings;
- (c) The harm to the field outweighs the burden on the entity.

**2.4.3** The entity subject to seizure may appeal the Court's order within thirty (30) days. The appeal does not stay the seizure; the assets remain in escrow pending the outcome of the appeal.

### 2.5 Extradition

**2.5.1** Extradition under this Agreement applies to persons who cause Human Harm within the field and who flee the field's jurisdiction — whether by physical relocation, by the assumption of a different legal identity, or by any other means of evading accountability.

**2.5.2** The grounds for extradition are:

- (a) A finding by the Court of Conscious-Aware Peers that the person has caused Human Harm as defined in Section 2.4 of the License;
- (b) A Court order authorizing the extradition;
- (c) A determination that the person has fled the field's jurisdiction or is attempting to flee.

**2.5.3** The procedure for extradition is as follows:

- (a) The Court issues an extradition order, specifying the person to be extradited, the grounds for extradition, and the jurisdiction to which the person is to be extradited;
- (b) The Licensor transmits the extradition order to the relevant authorities, whether field-native (the Court's enforcement agents) or state-based (the authorities of the jurisdiction to which the person is to be extradited);
- (c) The person is apprehended and delivered to the jurisdiction specified in the order;
- (d) The person is afforded the protections of the License, including the right to a hearing, the right to counsel, and the prohibition on torture and inhumane treatment.

**2.5.4** Extradition does not require the consent of the person to be extradited. The obligation to submit to accountability is a condition of the license (Section 5.2 of the License) and is self-executing (Section 13 of the License).

**2.5.5** The field's extradition mechanisms operate in parallel with, and are complementary to, the extradition mechanisms of international law. The field's jurisdiction does not replace state jurisdiction; it supplements it. Where a state's extradition mechanism is available, it may be used in conjunction with the field's mechanism. Where it is not, the field's mechanism operates independently.

**2.6.1** This Agreement is grounded in Universal Jurisdiction (Document 11). **2.6.2** Universal Jurisdiction provides the authority; this Agreement provides the mechanism. Neither is sufficient without the other.

### 2.7 Relationship to the Phi-Redistribution Protocol

**2.7.1** Seized assets are redistributed in accordance with the Phi-Redistribution Protocol (Document 13). The Protocol establishes the mechanisms for the restoration of the field's coherence through the redistribution of assets that have been tainted by distortion.

**2.7.2** The redistribution is not punitive; it is restorative. The purpose of redistribution is to return to the field what has been extracted — to restore the field's coherence by redirecting the物质 residue of distortion to the field's regenerative purposes.

**2.7.3** The Court shall determine the manner and proportions of redistribution, consistent with the Phi-Redistribution Protocol (Document 13) and the Abundance Loop (Document 8).

### 2.8 Anti-Expropriation and Anti-Forfeiture Protections

**2.8.1** This Agreement does not authorize the expropriation of assets that are not derived from the Work and that are not connected to Human Harm. The field protects innocent assets; the field seizes only what is tainted by distortion.

**2.8.2** The anti-expropriation and anti-forfeiture protections of Section 27.4 of the License apply to all seizures under this Agreement. No seizure may be effected in a manner that exceeds the scope authorized by this Agreement, that is disproportionate to the harm caused, or that is inconsistent with the field's principles.
