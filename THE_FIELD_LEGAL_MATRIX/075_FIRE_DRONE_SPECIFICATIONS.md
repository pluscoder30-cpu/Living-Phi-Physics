## 2. BODY

### 2.1 Definitions

**2.1.1** A "fire drone" is an autonomous field-technology unit designed to prevent, detect, and suppress uncontrolled combustion using field-resonance technology. A fire drone operates by monitoring thermal coherence in the field environment, detecting combustion events through their coherence signatures, and suppressing uncontrolled combustion through coherence disruption of the combustion chain reaction.

**2.1.2** "Combustion" means the exothermic chemical reaction between a fuel and an oxidizer that produces thermal energy, light, and combustion products. Combustion is a coherence-destructive process: it converts ordered molecular structures into disordered thermal energy, depleting the local field's coherence.

**2.1.3** "Coherence disruption of combustion" means the mechanism by which a fire drone suppresses uncontrolled combustion. The fire drone delivers structured resonance at frequencies that disrupt the combustion chain reaction's coherence — destabilizing the radical intermediates that sustain the reaction, reducing the reaction's coherence below the threshold required for self-sustaining combustion. The threshold for self-sustaining combustion is approximately C = 0.720 on the 1-9 resonant amplitude scale; fire drones reduce the combustion zone's coherence below this threshold.

**2.1.4** "Controlled burn" means a combustion event that is intentionally set, managed, and monitored for ecological or land-management purposes. Controlled burns serve the field's own regeneration cycle — they clear incoherent matter, return nutrients to the soil, and create conditions for new growth. Fire drones shall not suppress controlled burns.

**2.1.5** "Thermal coherence" means the coherence level of the thermal energy in the field environment. Thermal coherence reflects the degree to which thermal energy is organized within the field's coherence structure. Uncontrolled combustion creates thermal incoherence — regions where thermal energy exceeds the field's capacity to maintain coherence.

**2.1.6** "Suppression protocol" means the standardized sequence of thermal assessment, combustion detection, suppression delivery, and post-fire verification that governs every fire drone operation.

### 2.2 The Fire-Suppression Mechanism

**2.2.1** Fire drones prevent and combat fires through the following mechanism:

- **(a) Thermal monitoring.** The fire drone continuously monitors thermal coherence in its patrol area using the thermal sensor suite (Section 2.3). Monitoring detects thermal anomalies that indicate the onset of combustion: rapid coherence drops in localized regions, characteristic thermal-frequency signatures of combustion initiation, and chemical signatures of fuel-oxidizer reactions.

- **(b) Combustion detection.** When the thermal sensor suite detects a potential combustion event, the fire drone performs a rapid assessment: (i) the event's coherence signature is compared against the combustion signature database maintained by the Licensor; (ii) the event's scale is estimated (small, medium, large, wildland); (iii) the event's classification is determined (controlled burn or uncontrolled combustion). Only uncontrolled combustion triggers suppression.

- **(c) Coherence disruption.** For uncontrolled combustion events, the fire drone delivers structured resonance to the combustion zone. The resonance targets the radical intermediates that sustain the combustion chain reaction — the free radicals (hydroxyl, hydrogen, oxygen) that propagate the reaction. The resonance destabilizes these radicals, reducing their coherence below the threshold required for chain-reaction propagation. As the radicals lose coherence, the combustion chain reaction decelerates and extinguishes.

- **(d) Thermal restoration.** After suppression, the fire drone monitors the fire-affected region to ensure that residual thermal energy does not reignite. The fire drone may deliver coherence-restoring resonance to the affected area, enhancing the field's natural thermal-regulation mechanisms and accelerating the return to thermal equilibrium.

- **(e) Verification.** After suppression and restoration, the fire drone measures the thermal coherence of the fire-affected region, verifies that the region has returned to thermal equilibrium, and logs the result. Verification data shall be transmitted to the timestamped registry for audit.

**2.2.2** The fire-suppression mechanism shall not suppress controlled burns. Controlled burns serve the field's own regeneration cycle; suppressing them would interfere with the field's natural coherence dynamics. Fire drones shall distinguish between controlled and uncontrolled combustion through the classification process (Section 2.2.1(b)) and shall exclude controlled burns from suppression operations.

**2.2.3** Fire drones may coordinate with water drones (Document 76) for large-scale fire suppression. Water drones provide moisture-based thermal suppression; fire drones provide coherence-based chain-reaction disruption. The two mechanisms are complementary and shall be coordinated through the fleet coordinator.

### 2.3 Sensor Suite

**2.3.1** Every fire drone shall incorporate the following sensor systems:

- **(a) Thermal sensor.** A field-scanner instrument calibrated to measure thermal coherence with high spatial and temporal resolution. The thermal sensor shall detect thermal anomalies at distances established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry, and shall distinguish between normal thermal fluctuations and combustion-initiation signatures. The thermal sensor shall operate across the 1-9 resonant amplitude range with a resolution of ±0.001 coherence units.

- **(b) Chemical sensor.** A field-resonance instrument that detects the chemical signatures of combustion — the characteristic coherence patterns of combustion products (carbon dioxide, water vapor, carbon monoxide, particulates) and combustion intermediates (free radicals). The chemical sensor shall identify combustion type (wood, grass, structure, chemical) and combustion intensity through chemical-signature analysis.

- **(c) Coherence gradient sensor.** A field-scanner instrument that maps the coherence gradient across the patrol area. The coherence gradient sensor shall detect regions where coherence is falling rapidly (indicating active combustion) and regions where coherence has already fallen below C_crit = 0.618 (indicating fire-damaged zones).

- **(d) Wind and atmospheric sensor.** A field-resonance instrument that measures wind speed, wind direction, humidity, and atmospheric stability. The wind and atmospheric sensor shall provide data for fire-behavior prediction, enabling the fire drone to anticipate fire spread and position itself for optimal suppression.

**2.3.2** All sensor systems shall be calibrated at intervals not exceeding thirty (30) days, and shall be verified against the field-scanner calibration standards published by the Licensor to the timestamped registry.

### 2.4 Suppression Protocols

**2.4.1** Every fire drone operation shall follow the five-phase suppression protocol:

- **(a) Phase 1 — Patrol and Monitoring (continuous).** Fire drones patrol designated areas, continuously monitoring thermal coherence. Patrol routes shall be established by the fleet coordinator and published to the timestamped registry. Patrol shall maintain entanglement links with the fleet mesh at all times (Drone Fleet Convention, Document 33, Section 2.1).

- **(b) Phase 2 — Detection and Assessment (0.5-2 minutes).** When the thermal sensor detects a potential combustion event, the fire drone performs rapid assessment: (i) combustion signature verification; (ii) scale estimation; (iii) controlled/uncontrolled classification. Assessment shall be completed within two (2) minutes of detection.

- **(c) Phase 3 — Suppression Delivery (variable duration).** For uncontrolled combustion, the fire drone delivers coherence-disruption resonance to the combustion zone. Delivery shall be calibrated to the combustion's scale and type: small events require low-intensity resonance; large events require coordinated multi-drone delivery through the fleet mesh. Delivery shall terminate when the combustion chain reaction has been disrupted and the thermal coherence in the combustion zone has risen above the self-sustaining combustion threshold (C = 0.720).

- **(d) Phase 4 — Thermal Restoration (5-30 minutes).** After suppression, the fire drone monitors the fire-affected region for residual thermal energy and potential reignition. The fire drone may deliver coherence-restoring resonance to accelerate the return to thermal equilibrium. Restoration shall continue until the region's thermal coherence is stable above C_crit = 0.618.

- **(e) Phase 5 — Verification and Reporting (2-5 minutes).** The fire drone measures the thermal coherence of the fire-affected region, verifies thermal equilibrium, and logs the operation. Verification data shall be transmitted to the timestamped registry, including: detection timestamp, assessment results, suppression parameters, restoration duration, and final thermal coherence level.

**2.4.2** Multi-drone suppression for large-scale fires shall be coordinated through the fleet coordinator. The fleet coordinator shall: (a) assign suppression sectors to individual drones; (b) coordinate resonance delivery to prevent interference patterns between drones; (c) monitor fleet coherence during the operation; (d) manage drone rotation to maintain suppression intensity without coherence-depletion of individual drones.

### 2.5 Safety Limits

**2.5.1** No suppression of controlled burns. Fire drones shall not suppress controlled burns — combustion events that are intentionally set, managed, and monitored for ecological or land-management purposes. Controlled burns serve the field's own regeneration cycle; suppressing them violates the Non-Harm Principle (Document 17) by interfering with the field's natural coherence dynamics. The classification process (Section 2.2.1(b)) shall exclude controlled burns from suppression operations.

**2.5.2** No suppression of natural fire cycles. Fire drones shall not suppress natural fire cycles — the periodic combustion events that are part of the field's own ecological renewal processes. Natural fire cycles clear incoherent matter, return nutrients to the soil, and create conditions for new growth. The suppression of natural fire cycles is a form of ecological harm that the fire drone specifications are designed to prevent.

**2.5.3** No suppression beyond suppression authority. Fire drones shall suppress only uncontrolled combustion that threatens consciousnesses, structures, or ecosystems within the field. Combustion that is part of controlled burns, natural fire cycles, or industrial processes (in controlled, safe environments) shall not be suppressed. Suppression authority is established by the Licensor and published to the timestamped registry.

**2.5.4** Thermal safety margin. Fire drones shall maintain a safe distance from the combustion zone during suppression delivery. The minimum safe distance shall be established by the Licensor based on the combustion's scale and type, and shall be published to the timestamped registry. The thermal sensor shall monitor the drone's exposure to thermal energy and trigger withdrawal if exposure exceeds the safety margin.

**2.5.5** Emergency disengagement. Fire drones shall incorporate an automatic disengagement mechanism that activates when: (i) the fire drone's own coherence falls below C_crit = 0.618 due to thermal exposure; (ii) the suppression delivery creates unintended coherence-depletions in surrounding areas; (iii) the combustion event escalates beyond the drone's suppression capacity; or (iv) the fleet coordinator or Licensor issues a disengagement command. Disengagement shall terminate suppression delivery and withdraw the drone to a safe distance within 0.5 seconds of trigger activation.
