### Finding 4: ITAR Violations â€” Litton Industries Exported Air Force One Source Code to Russia

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

Following Northrop Grumman's 2001 acquisition of Litton Industries, the company discovered and self-disclosed massive export control violations:

**State Department (ITAR) Penalties:**
- **110 violations** of the Arms Export Control Act and ITAR
- Violations occurred between September 1998 and November 1998 (pre-acquisition)
- Litton Industries provided **portions of source code** for inertial navigation software systems **unique to Air Force One** (Boeing VC-25) to a company in **Russia**
- The State Department stated the violations **"resulted in harm to U.S. national security"** because the source code identified "certain capabilities, limitations, and vulnerabilities of Air Force One to Russia"
- Additionally, between 1994 and 2003, the firms failed to notify the State Department about navigation systems being transferred to **Angola, Indonesia, Israel, China, Ukraine, and Yemen**
- **Penalty: $15 million fine** + **$5 million in remedial compliance measures**

**Commerce Department (EAR) Penalties:**
- **131 violations** of the Export Administration Regulations
- 73 violations by Litton (1994-2001) + 58 violations by Northrop Grumman as successor (2001-2002)
- Exports of navigation equipment components and manufacturing data to Italy, Malaysia, Philippines, UK, Singapore, Germany without required licenses
- **Penalty: $400,000 civil penalty**

**Total Combined Penalties: $20.4 million**

**Source:** ABC News (Source #15); BIS Settlement Agreement (Source #16); BIS Civil Penalty (Source #17); Covington & Burling (Source #18); Military Aerospace (Source #19); Wikipedia (Source #14)
