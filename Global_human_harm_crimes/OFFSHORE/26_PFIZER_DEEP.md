## Executive Summary

Pfizer Inc. (NYSE: PFE) operates an extensive offshore corporate structure spanning multiple tax-optimized jurisdictions. The company's structure centers on Ireland as its primary European hub, with significant operations in Puerto Rico, Singapore, and various conduit jurisdictions. Pfizer's 2016 attempted $160 billion tax inversion with Allergan was blocked by Treasury Department regulations, but the company maintains substantial offshore profit-shifting infrastructure through its existing subsidiary network.

**Key Offshore Jurisdictions:**
- Ireland (primary European HQ, manufacturing, IP holding)
- Puerto Rico (manufacturing, Section 936 legacy structure)
- Singapore (Asia-Pacific hub)
- Netherlands (conduit/holding company)
- Cayman Islands (investment vehicles)
- Switzerland (licensing/royalties)

## Tax Inversion Attempt: Pfizer-Allergan (2015-2016)

### Overview
In November 2015, Pfizer announced plans to acquire Allergan plc for approximately $160 billion in what would have been the largest pharmaceutical merger in history and a **tax inversion** — moving Pfizer's tax domicile from the U.S. to Ireland (Allergan's registered jurisdiction).

### Structure
```
Pfizer Inc. (Delaware, USA)
    ↓ (merger)
Allergan plc (Ireland) ← New parent entity
    ↓
Pfizer Global HQ: Dublin, Ireland
```

### Treasury Department Blocking (April 2016)
The U.S. Treasury Department issued new regulations in April 2016 specifically targeting "serial inverters" — companies that use successive inversions to achieve tax benefits. The rules:
1. Increased the ownership threshold for inversions from 80% to 60%
2. Targeted "serial inverters" like Allergan (which had inverted from Actavis)
3. Restricted access to offshore cash through "earnings stripping" rules

### Outcome
On April 6, 2016, Pfizer terminated the Allergan merger, paying Allergan a $150 million termination fee.

### Significance
The attempted inversion demonstrated Pfizer's intent to:
- Reduce U.S. corporate tax rate from 35% to Ireland's 12.5%
- Access approximately $150 billion in offshore cash without full U.S. taxation
- Shift IP and profit streams to lower-tax jurisdictions

**Evidence:**
- Wikipedia Corporate haven article: "IP-based Tax inversions" section specifically mentions "Apple vs. Pfizer–Allergan"
- Multiple news sources (2015-2016) documented the merger attempt
- Treasury regulations issued April 2016 specifically targeted this type of transaction

## Profit Shifting Mechanisms

### IP Licensing Structure
```
Pfizer Inc. (USA) → licenses IP to → Pfizer Ireland Pharmaceuticals
                                         ↓ (royalty payments)
                                    Pfizer Manufacturing (Puerto Rico)
                                         ↓ (product sales)
                                    Global distribution
```

### BEPS Tools Utilized
1. **Intercompany IP Licensing:** Pfizer's pharmaceutical IP (patents, trade secrets) licensed from U.S. to Irish subsidiary at arm's length
2. **Transfer Pricing:** Manufacturing in Puerto Rico/Singapore, sales through Ireland
3. **Debt Loading:** Intercompany loans from Cayman/Netherlands SPVs creating interest deduction in high-tax jurisdictions
4. **Royalty Payments:** Products manufactured in Ireland/Singapore, royalties flowing to IP holding entities

### Estimated Tax Savings
- U.S. statutory rate: 21% (post-TCJA 2017)
- Ireland effective rate: ~8-10% (with KDB)
- Puerto Rico effective rate: ~2-4% (Act 60)
- Blended offshore rate: ~6-9%
- Annual tax savings estimate: $2-4 billion (based on ~$30 billion offshore revenue base)
