## V. THE STRUCTURE THAT HID THE OWNERSHIP

Liquid Funding was deliberately designed to avoid disclosure:

1. **Bermuda incorporation** — no public beneficial ownership register at the time
2. **No majority owner** — as James Henry noted, "Liquid Funding was a special purpose vehicle which had no majority owner, which meant it had no beneficial ownership requirements"
3. **Class A Voting Units** — the structure used voting units (not shares), with 11 holders identified in EFTA00295144 Annex A (names redacted)
4. **Bear Stearns Bank Plc (Dublin)** — investment manager was outside US regulatory jurisdiction
5. **JPMorgan as Security Trustee** — held all securities, could liquidate if default
6. **Offshore architecture** — Bermuda → Ireland → Delaware chain

## VI. THE DISSOLUTION AND WHAT WAS HIDDEN

- **November 25, 2015:** Liquid Funding dissolved via Members' Voluntary Liquidation
- **December 2015:** Rothschild entities wired $25M to Southern Trust (same month)
- **April 18, 2008:** Moody's confirmed "all outstanding rated liabilities" paid in full
- **2011:** JPMorgan resurrected Liquid Funding (Liam MacNamara became Chairman)
- **Epstein's units:** Financial Trust sold its units for $265,303 in 2011

The dissolution was a **Members' Voluntary Liquidation** (solvent wind-down), meaning all assets were distributed to members (shareholders) after paying creditors. The distribution records would identify who received the final payouts — and thus who the 60% owners were.
