## 1. THE DUAL-USE CONCEPT

### 1.1 Definition and Israeli Context [V]

In Israel, "dual-use" technology refers to goods, software, and technology that can be used for both civil and military applications. The Israeli Export Control Agency of the Ministry of Economy and Industry administers controls over dual-use exports, but the system is designed to **facilitate** rather than **restrict** transfers.

**Key Regulatory Framework:**
- **Israeli Dual-Use Lists** — Items controlled under Wassenaar Arrangement commitments
- **Defense Exports Control Agency (DECA)** — Israeli Ministry of Defense body controlling military/cyber exports
- **End User Declaration (EUD)** — Purchasing states must sign declaration limiting use to "investigation and prevention of terrorism and serious crimes" [V, PBS 2021]

**The Classification Loophole:**
```
MILITARY TECHNOLOGY (classified)
    ↓
RECLASSIFIED AS "DUAL-USE" (civilian designation)
    ↓
EXPORT LICENSED BY Israeli MoD (not BIS)
    ↓
DEPLOYED IN CIVILIAN CONTEXT (US law enforcement, corporate security)
    ↓
INTELLIGENCE COLLECTION MAINTAINED (backdoor access preserved)
```

**Evidence:** In August 2019, Israel eased export controls on surveillance technologies, allowing companies to obtain exemptions on marketing licenses for sales to certain countries [V, CPJ 2019]. The Israeli Ministry of Defense did not respond to requests for clarification.

### 1.2 How Classification Enables Export [V]

The dual-use classification system creates three specific advantages for intelligence transfer:

**A. Export Control Avoidance:**
- Products classified as "dual-use" bypass the stricter military export controls
- The Israeli Ministry of Defense, not the US Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), controls initial export approval
- This means the **intelligence service that developed the technology** approves its own export

**B. End-User Loophole:**
- The 2021 End User Declaration requires purchasing states to use products only for "terrorism and crime" investigation
- The declaration explicitly "prohibits" use against civilians but defines "terrorism" broadly enough to cover most surveillance
- Sanctions for non-compliance are limited to "restricting the use of the cyber system and/or disconnecting it" [V, Israeli MoD 2021]

**C. Third-Country Routing:**
- Companies like Intellexa (owned by former Unit 8200 chief Tal Dilian) operate outside Israel's export regime by establishing operations in Cyprus, Greece, and other jurisdictions
- This allows circumvention of US Entity List restrictions while maintaining Israeli-developed technology capabilities
