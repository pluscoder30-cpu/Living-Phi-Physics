## 1. B. Stanley Pons

**Full Name:** B. Stanley Pons  
**Institution:** University of Utah, Department of Chemistry  
**Research:** Cold fusion / Low Energy Nuclear Reactions (LENR) — excess heat from palladium-deuterium electrochemical cells  
**Suppression Mechanism:** Coordinated public smear campaign, fraud allegations, academic ostracism  
**Evidence:**
- March 23, 1989: Pons and Fleischmann announced discovery of cold fusion at University of Utah press conference
- MIT Plasma Fusion Center Director Ronald R. Parker told Boston Herald on April 28, 1989 that Pons and Fleischmann's work was "misrepresentation and maybe fraud" and "scientific schlock" (source: Boston Herald, Nick Tate reporting)
- Parker later denied making these statements in a media advisory from MIT News Office, but Boston Herald reporter Nick Tate had recorded the phone call, confirming Parker made the statements
- MIT held a "funeral party" or "Wake for Cold Fusion" before their replication experiment was even complete
- Parker distributed mugs reading "The Utah University: Department of Fusion Confusion" with mocking instructions on the back
- Dr. Steven Koonin of Caltech called the Utah report "the incompetence and delusion of Pons and Fleischmann" at the American Physical Society meeting May 1, 1989 — audience burst into applause
- Pons was forced to leave academia; relocated to France to continue research in obscurity
**Potential Impact:** If validated, cold fusion would have provided nearly unlimited clean energy from seawater, eliminating fossil fuel dependency
