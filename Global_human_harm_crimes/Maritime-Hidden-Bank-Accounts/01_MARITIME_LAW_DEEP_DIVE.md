## 1. CANADA SHIPPING ACT 2001

### 1.1 The Canadian Register of Vessels

The Canada Shipping Act 2001 (CSA 2001) establishes the **Canadian Register of Vessels** under Section 43:

> **Section 43(1):** "The Chief Registrar is responsible for establishing and maintaining a register to be known as the Canadian Register of Vessels. The Chief Registrar is to divide the Register into parts, including a small vessel register, for the classes of vessels that the Chief Registrar specifies."

> **Section 43(2):** "The Register is to contain records of the information and documents specified by the Chief Registrar in respect of a Canadian vessel or a fleet that is registered under this Part, including its description, its official number, the name and address of its owner and, in the case of a vessel that is not registered in the small vessel register, details of all mortgages registered in respect of it."

**Key observations for the enterprise investigation:**

## 3. IN RE DE MESTRE [1830] â€” BIRTH ON VESSEL = BIRTH IN TERRITORY

### 3.1 The Case

**In re de Mestre [1830] NSWSupC 31 (23 April 1830)**

This is the foundational case for the doctrine that **birth on a vessel constitutes birth within the territory** of the vessel's flag state.

**Facts:** Prosper de Mestre claimed to be a British subject because he was allegedly born on a British ship on the high seas while his French Royalist parents were fleeing the French Revolution.

**The Court's reasoning (Chief Justice Forbes):**

The court cited **Vattel** (a French jurist of acknowledged authority in Admiralty Courts):

> "As to Children born at Sea, if they are born in those parts of it, that are possessed by their Nation, they are born in the Country; and, if the Children are born in a Vessel belonging to the Nation, they may be reputed born in its Territories; for **it is natural to consider the Vessels of a Nation as parts of its Territory**, especially when they sail upon a Free Sea, since the State retains its Jurisdiction over these Vessels" â€” Vattel I, 19, Sec. 216

The court also cited 1 Wood, 386:

> "The issues of an Alien, born within the Realm, are accounted Natural Subjects, in which respect, there is not any difference between our Laws and those of France. In each Country, **Birth confers the rights of Naturalization**."

And 28 Henry 8th, c. 15; 39 Geo. 3, c. 37.

**Ruling:** The court held that being born on board a British Ship, at Sea, of Parents at the time within the protection and subject to the operation of the Laws of Great Britain, **confers the same Rights as being born within the Realm**.

### 3.2 The Irony

Historical research later revealed that Prosper de Mestre was **actually born on dry land in France**, not at sea. He fabricated the story to establish British subject status for commercial purposes (registering a British ship). The lie was not exposed until 2008 â€” 178 years later â€” when his great-great-great granddaughter Maree Amor found his baptismal record.

**However, the legal precedent stands**: the case established that birth on a vessel = birth in territory, regardless of the factual falsity of the specific claim.

### 3.3 Significance for the Enterprise Investigation

The court held:
