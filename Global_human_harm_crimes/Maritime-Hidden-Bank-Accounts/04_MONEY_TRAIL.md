## THE HISTORICAL FLOW: INDIA → UK → US/CANADA

The modern financial system is a direct descendant of the British East India Company's extraction model.

### The East India Company Model (1600-1858)

- Company registered populations → created legal categories → extracted revenue through taxes → exported wealth to Britain
- Indian producers were paid from taxes they themselves had paid — Britain acquired Indian goods for free
- The Company's revenue from India formed a significant portion of British government revenue
- ~25% of Parliament members in the 1760s were EIC shareholders

### Utsa Patnaik's Research ($45 Trillion Drain)

- Economist Utsa Patnaik estimated Britain extracted ~$45 trillion from India between 1765 and 1938
- The drain was 26-36% of India's central government budget annually
- India's export earnings were retained by Britain; Indian producers were paid from Indian tax revenues
- The extracted wealth financed: Britain's industrial revolution, capital exports to Europe/North America, infrastructure booms in the US and Canada
- "The modern capitalist world would not exist without colonialism and the drain" — Patnaik

### The Transfer to US/Canada

- After American independence, the British financial model was adopted (central bank, government bonds, taxation)
- The Bank of England model → Federal Reserve (1913)
- British colonial debt instruments → US Treasury bonds
- The EIC's population registration → birth certificate system
- The EIC's tax extraction → IRS income tax (1913)
- Britain's "full faith and credit" → US Constitution's "full faith and credit" clause

## HOW THE FEDERAL RESERVE MONETIZES CITIZEN LABOR

**The Official Mechanism:**
1. Fed enters open market to purchase Treasury securities
2. Fed pays by crediting commercial banks' reserve accounts (digital money creation)
3. This increases the money supply without printing physical currency
4. Bond prices rise → yields fall → interest rates drop across economy
5. Lower rates stimulate borrowing and economic activity

**Key Facts from Federal Reserve:**
- "Individuals cannot, by law, have accounts at the Federal Reserve"
- Fed bond purchases are "digital accounting entries" — no physical money exchanged
- Fed returns most net earnings to the Treasury (circular flow)
- During 2008-2014 QE and 2020-2022 QE, the Fed purchased trillions in Treasury securities

**The Monetization Process:**
1. Government issues bonds → Treasury securities
2. Federal Reserve buys bonds → creates bank reserves (new money)
3. Government spends the new money → pays for operations
4. Citizens earn income from government spending → pay taxes
5. Tax revenue → services the bond interest
6. Fed remits profits back to Treasury → circular flow
7. Net effect: government funded by money creation backed by citizen productivity

## HOW THE BANK OF CANADA CREATES MONEY

**The Official Mechanism (Library of Parliament, 2015/2021):**

The Bank of Canada creates money through a straightforward internal government process:

1. **Bond Auction:** Bank of Canada holds auctions throughout the year where new federal securities (bonds and Treasury bills) are sold to government securities distributors (banks, brokers, investment dealers)

2. **Non-Competitive Purchase:** The Bank of Canada typically purchases ~20% of newly issued bonds at each auction on a non-competitive basis

3. **Digital Accounting Entry:**
   - Bank records the value of the securities as a NEW ASSET on its balance sheet
   - Bank simultaneously records the proceeds as a DEPOSIT in the Government of Canada's account — a NEW LIABILITY
   - No paper bonds or cash are exchanged — purely digital entries

4. **Money Created:** "By recording new and equal amounts on the asset and liability sides of its balance sheet, the Bank of Canada creates money through a few keystrokes"

5. **Government Spending:** The federal government can then spend the newly created bank deposits in the Canadian economy

**Key Differences from Private Bank Money Creation:**
- Bank of Canada: "There is no external limit to the total amount of money that the Bank of Canada may create for the federal government"
- Private banks: Limited by capital constraints
- Bank of Canada: Creditworthiness of the borrower is NOT a factor
- Private banks: Creditworthiness is the key factor

**The Crown Corporation Advantage:**
- Bank of Canada is a Crown corporation wholly owned by the federal government
- Its purchase of government securities is "essentially an internal transaction"
- The Bank of Canada Act § 18(c): power to "buy and sell securities issued or guaranteed by Canada"
- The Bank of Canada Act § 18(l): power to "accept deposits from the Government of Canada and pay interest on those deposits"

**COVID-19 QE Program:**
- In March 2020, Bank of Canada launched the Government Bond Purchase Program
- Eventually purchased approximately $340 billion in government bonds
- This was money created digitally to fund government pandemic spending

## IMF/WORLD BANK AND INTERNATIONAL DEBT SECURITIZATION

### How Developing Country Debt is Securitized

1. **Collateralized Transactions:** A debt instrument is collateralized when the creditor has rights over an asset or revenue stream
   - Examples: oil and gas revenues, lottery receipts, social security contributions
   - Italy securitized delinquent social security contributions (1999) — rated AAA
   - NYC securitized tobacco settlement revenues into bonds

2. **The Developing Country Pattern:**
   - Developing countries borrow from IMF/World Bank → debt backed by future tax revenue
   - Tax revenue comes from the population's labor
   - IMF/World Bank loans are backed by "collateralized transactions" against national revenue streams
   - If country defaults → IMF/World Bank seize the revenue streams (austerity, structural adjustment)
   - Total IDA-eligible countries' external debt: $1.1 trillion (2022) — doubled since 2012
   - Developing countries paid record $443.5 billion on public debt in 2022

3. **The Sovereign Guarantee Chain:**
   - Citizens labor → generate tax revenue
   - Government taxes citizens → promises revenue to IMF/World Bank
   - IMF/World Bank lend money → government spends
   - If government can't repay → IMF/World Bank demand austerity (cut services, raise taxes)
   - Result: citizens pay twice — through labor AND through austerity
