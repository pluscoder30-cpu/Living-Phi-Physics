**Original Agent 10 report (Account Access Protocol) has been removed.**

The original report provided step-by-step instructions for accessing a purported "birth certificate trust account" at RBC (account 9035163), including instructions to tell the bank "I believe this account was created through the birth registration process." This is sovereign citizen theory applied to a specific bank account. The claim that birth certificates create trust accounts has been rejected by every court that has considered it (see 06_LEGAL_PATH.md).

**Sovereign citizen claims removed from this file:**
- The claim that RBC account 9035163 was "created through the birth registration process" — **unverified and legally baseless**
- The instruction to tell RBC "I am the beneficial owner" of a birth certificate trust — **sovereign citizen theory with 100% court rejection rate**
- The PIPEDA access request framed as a tool to access a "trust account" — **misuse of legitimate privacy law**
- The "Notice of Live Birth" filing as a method to claim trust beneficiary status — **sovereign citizen remedy, no court acceptance**
- The entire "Cestui Que Vie Trust" framework as applied to birth certificates — **rejected by courts in every jurisdiction**

**Factual data from this report retained elsewhere:**
- RBC account 9035163 (transit 02482, institution 003) exists and is linked to Affirm — **verified by PAD confirmation** (cross-ref: evidence files)
- Payments Canada Rule H1 requires commercially reasonable verification — **verified regulatory requirement**
- PIPEDA section 4.9 provides access to personal information — **legitimate statutory right** (not a trust access tool)
- BC Vital Statistics Act provisions — **verified statutory framework** (not a trust access tool)

**Admissible legal tools for accessing financial records:**
- PIPEDA s. 4.9 — Access to personal information held by organizations (legitimate privacy request, not trust claim)
- Bank Act (Canada) s. 271 — Right to account statement
- Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act (BC) — Government records access
- Civil Resolution Tribunal (BC) — Dispute resolution for financial accounts

**Statutory references:**

- 18 U.S.C. §514 — False financial instruments (for A4V / trust account claims)
