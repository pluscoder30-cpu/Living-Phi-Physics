### 1. UNITED KINGDOM

**CPI 2025 Score:** 70/100 (ranked 20th of 182)
**Trend:** Declining - lowest ever score (was 82 in 2017)

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

#### Finding 1.1: Systemic Political Corruption Driving Decline

The UK score fell from 82 (2017, ranked 8th) to 70 (2025, ranked 20th) - a drop of **12 points and 12 places** in 8 years.

**Key drivers identified by Transparency International:**
- Political donors receiving government favours (cash for access)
- MPs acting as paid lobbyists
- Epstein-Mandelson controversy
- Record political campaign spending relying on wealthy benefactors
- Conservatives accepting 15 million pounds from a single donor (Frank Hester) in under 12 months
- Elon Musk considering 100 million dollar donation to Reform UK
- Labour donor Waheed Alli receiving privileged PM access

**Money trail:** Political donations -> access to government -> policy influence
**Verification:** [VERIFIED] via Sources 1, 2, 3

#### Finding 1.2: UK as Enabler of Global Corruption

Transparency International explicitly notes that countries with high CPI scores (including the UK) "enable corruption in other countries by facilitating the transfer of proceeds of corruption across borders and into money laundering schemes."

**Harm produced:** UK financial centres serve as conduits for dirty money from low-scoring countries.

**Money trail:** Corrupt foreign officials -> UK financial centres (London) -> money laundering -> clean assets
**Verification:** [PV] - Source 2 states this explicitly but does not quantify amounts

#### Finding 1.3: Defense Procurement Concerns

The NAO issued a clean audit opinion for MoD accounts 2025-26 (Source 7), but the MoD has a history of procurement cost overruns and waste.

**Money trail:** UK taxpayer -> MoD budget -> defense contractors -> cost overruns
**Verification:** [PV] - clean audit opinion confirms regularity, not value-for-money

### 2. RUSSIA

**CPI 2025 Score:** 22/100 (ranked 157th of 182)
**Trend:** Declining - worst score since 2012

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

#### Finding 2.1: Kleptocratic State System

Russia is characterized as a **kleptocracy**, **oligarchy**, and **plutocracy** operating under **crony capitalism**. Transparency International stated in 2022: "Corruption is endemic in Russia."

**Scale:** Independent experts estimate corruption consumes **25% of Russia GDP**. Official Rosstat estimates (3.5-7% of GDP) are widely considered understated.

**Money trail:** State treasury -> oligarchs -> offshore accounts -> London/other financial centres
**Verification:** [PV] - Source 4 cites multiple estimates but exact figure is disputed

#### Finding 2.2: Massive Soviet-Era Theft

CIA station chief Richard Palmer described the dissolution of the Soviet Union as enabling "illegal dispersal of the equivalent of **billions of dollars** from the Soviet state treasury into private accounts across Europe and the US."

**Money trail:** Soviet state treasury -> private accounts in Europe/US -> legitimized wealth
**Verification:** [PV] - Source 4 cites Palmer testimony; specific amounts not quantified

#### Finding 2.3: Medvedev Embezzlement Allegations

The 2017 documentary "He Is Not Dimon to You" by Alexei Navalny estimated **1.2 billion dollars** was embezzled by then-Prime Minister Dmitry Medvedev. The film received 30 million views.

**Money trail:** State funds -> Medvedev-linked entities -> 1.2 billion dollars estimated
**Date(s):** 2017 (film release); corruption period unclear
**Verification:** [UNVERIFIED] - single source (Navalny/Anti-Corruption Foundation)

#### Finding 2.4: Military Corruption Crisis (2024)

In 2024, a wave of arrests exposed systematic military corruption:

**Verification:** [VERIFIED] - Source 4 cites multiple arrest reports

#### Finding 2.5: Bribery Escalation

Average bribe size increased dramatically:
- 2008: 9,000 rubles
- 2009: 23,000 rubles
- 2010: 61,000 rubles
- 2011: **236,000 rubles** (26x the 2008 average)

**Verification:** [PV] - Source 4 cites Russian Interior Ministry data

#### Finding 2.6: Anti-Corruption Efforts Undermined

Russia withdrew from the Criminal Law Convention on Corruption in 2022. The Anti-Corruption Foundation (founded by Navalny) has been effectively shut down.

**Harm:** Russian citizens pay for corruption through inflated housing, water, gas, and electricity tariffs that significantly outpace inflation.

## Verdict

This investigation reveals that **government fraud is a global systemic problem**, not isolated to any single country or region. The key findings establish:

1. **Global average corruption (CPI 42/100)** is at its worst level in over a decade
2. **Established democracies are declining** - the UK, US, Canada, France, Sweden, and New Zealand all show falling scores
3. **Russia operates as a kleptocracy** with corruption potentially consuming 25% of GDP
4. **High-scoring countries enable corruption** in low-scoring countries through financial centre money laundering
5. **150 journalists** have been murdered investigating corruption since 2012
6. **International organizations** (UN, World Bank, IMF, NATO) have limited transparency in their own procurement and spending

The most actionable finding is that **financial centres in "clean" countries (UK, Switzerland, Singapore) serve as the infrastructure for global corruption**, enabling the laundering of proceeds from kleptocratic states. This creates a structural incentive where the "cleanest" countries profit from the corruption they claim to oppose.
