## Findings

### Finding 1: Grain Trading — The ABCD Oligopoly Controls Global Food Flow

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

The ABCD group (ADM, Bunge, Cargill, Louis Dreyfus) plus COFCO and Viterra collectively control 70-80% of globally traded commercial grains and oilseeds per EU Parliament (2024) and ETC Group (2026). Bunge's July 2025 merger with Viterra created the world's third-largest commodity trader with 242 million tonnes annual capacity.

In 2023, the world's 8 largest ag commodity traders generated $730 billion in combined revenues — close to the value of all Canadian exports that year. The ABCDs operate 3,310 subsidiary companies collectively.

A 2025 academic study (Wiley, Anderson et al.) using vessel nomination data found the CR4 for all grains/oilseeds globally is 32% FOB and 27% CNF — significantly lower than the commonly cited 70-90%. The study concluded competition is "fierce." However, the EU Parliament's 2024 study estimated the ABCD share at 50-60% of COPs trade, rising to 70-80% with COFCO and Viterra included.

State-owned actors are acquiring equity stakes: UAE acquired 45% of Louis Dreyfus in 2020; Saudi Arabia announced 80% controlling stake in Olam Agri in early 2025. These entangle commercial concentration with geopolitical leverage.

**Evidence:**
- EU Parliament Study IPOL_STU(2024)747276: "ABCDs handle around 50-60% of worldwide trade in essential COPs"
- ETC Group 2026: "70-80% of globally traded commercial grains and oilseeds"
- Food & Water Watch 2026: ADM/Bunge real gross profits rose 37%/57% in five years after 2020

**Connections:**
- Links to USDA procurement (Finding 8)
- Links to fertilizer oligopoly (Finding 3)
- Links to political spending (Finding 9)

### Finding 2: Meatpacking — The Big Four Process 80-85% of US Beef

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

Four companies — JBS, Tyson Foods, Cargill, and National Beef — handle approximately 80-85% of US steer and heifer slaughter capacity. The four-firm concentration ratio reached this level by 1993 and has remained mostly constant since. The four-firm share of hog purchases is 67%.

The USDA Packers and Stockyards Division and GAO both confirm the 80-85% figure. In 2025, total cattle slaughter was 29.25 million head (lowest since 2015). Of 937 federally inspected plants, 11 plants with capacity over 1 million head/year accounted for 47.3% of total slaughter.

Tyson Foods produces approximately 20% of US beef, pork, and chicken combined, with $54 billion in FY2025 sales. JBS (Brazilian-owned) is the world's largest meatpacker.

**Evidence:**
- USDA ERS (Jan 2024): "Four largest firms handling 85 percent of all steer and heifer purchases"
- Drovers (May 2026): "11 plants with capacity in excess of 1 million head/year accounted for 47.3% of total cattle slaughter"
- Tyson Facts (ir.tyson.com): "producing approximately 20% of the beef, pork and chicken in the United States"

**Connections:**

### Finding 4: Rare Earths — China Controls 91% of Separation, 94% of Magnet Production

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

China dominates every stage of the rare earth supply chain:
- **Mining**: 60% of global production (270,000 of ~390,000 tonnes REO in 2025)
- **Separation and processing**: ~91% of global rare earth separation and metal production
- **Permanent magnet manufacturing**: ~94% of sintered NdFeB magnet production

China's dominance extends beyond mining into midstream and downstream. In April 2025, Beijing imposed export controls on seven medium and heavy rare earth elements (samarium, gadolinium, terbium, dysprosium, lutetium, scandium, yttrium). In October 2025, five more were added (europium, holmium, erbium, thulium, ytterbium).

The Chinese rare earth industry is consolidated into two state-owned enterprises with strict production quotas: China Northern Rare Earth and China Rare Earth Group. In August 2025, new regulations extended quota controls to imported rare earths for the first time.

The US was 67% net import reliant for rare earths in 2025 (up from 53% in 2024). The USGS reports US domestic production of 51,000 tonnes REO from Mountain Pass, CA and a Georgia mine.

**Evidence:**
- RUSI (June 2026): "China accounts for approximately 91% of global rare earth separation and metal production, and around 94% of sintered NdFeB permanent magnet production"
- USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026: "Net import reliance as a percentage of apparent consumption: Compounds and metals 67%"
- CFR (Feb 2026): "China's near-total control over many critical minerals — and willingness to weaponize that dominance"

**Connections:**
- Links to defense supply chain (Report 02)
- Links to semiconductor supply chain (Finding 5)
- Links to EV/wind energy transition

### Finding 5: Semiconductors — TSMC Holds 73% Foundry Market, ~95% Leading-Edge

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

TSMC captured 73% of the global pure-play foundry market in Q1 2026 (Counterpoint Research). At the leading edge (3nm class), TSMC holds an estimated ~95% market share (Yole Group/EE Times). Samsung Foundry holds 7%, SMIC 5%, UMC 4%, GlobalFoundries 3%.

The global "Foundry 2.0" market reached $320 billion in revenue in 2025, growing 16% YoY. TSMC alone grew 36% YoY.

Five economies — China, Taiwan, Korea, Japan, and the United States — account for nearly 90% of global wafer fabrication capacity (OECD 2025). The ten largest companies account for ~50% of total global production capacity.

Geopolitical bifurcation is accelerating: Chinese foundries outperformed Tier-2 peers due to domestic localization policies. SMIC achieved 7nm capability (equivalent to TSMC circa 2019).

**Evidence:**
- Counterpoint Research (Jun 2026): "TSMC captured 73% of the pure-foundry market"
- EE Times (Apr 2026): "TSMC holds an estimated ~95% market share" at leading edge
- OECD (2025): "five economies account for nearly 90% of global capacity"

**Connections:**
- Links to rare earth supply chain (Finding 4)
- Links to defense/aerospace applications
- Links to AI compute demand

### Finding 6: Pharmaceutical Supply Chain — China Controls 41% of KSM Sole-Sourcing

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

US Pharmacopeia (USP) analysis found:
- **58%** of Key Starting Materials (KSMs) for US-approved APIs are sole sourced from a single country
- **41%** of KSMs are sole sourced from China
- **16%** of KSMs are sole sourced from India
- **17%** of US-approved APIs rely on a single country for ALL their KSMs
- **12%** of APIs rely entirely on China for all KSMs
- **37%** of all APIs have at least one KSM solely sourced from China

Amoxicillin, the second most prescribed oral antibiotic in the US, depends on four KSMs each produced almost entirely in China, with 6-aminopenicillanic acid (6-APA) being the most critical. India's pharma supply chain is 65% dependent on China for critical inputs (NITI Aayog 2026).

Brookings Institution estimates Chinese API is included in approximately one-quarter of drug volume sold in the US. China is also dominant in auxiliary chemicals (reagents, solvents) used in API synthesis — a factor omitted from most vulnerability analyses.

**Evidence:**
- USP (2025): "41% of KSMs used for US-approved APIs are sole sourced from China"
- Brookings (Jul 2025): "Chinese API is included in perhaps a quarter of drug volume sold in the US"
- NITI Aayog (Jun 2026): "65 per cent of APIs and intermediates sourced from China"

**Connections:**
- Links to India as intermediary (India sources 65% of API inputs from China)
- Links to US national security concerns
- Links to tariffs/reshoring policy debate

### Finding 7: Digital Control of Agriculture — AI Platforms Consolidate Data Power

**Confidence:** [PV]

Major agricultural input companies are deploying proprietary digital farming platforms that collect farm data and drive product sales:
- **Bayer's Climate FieldView**
- **Syngenta's Cropwise**
- **Corteva's Granular**

These platforms partner with Big Tech (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta). The Indian government's Agri Stack database, built with Microsoft, gives detailed information on 80 million Indian farmers and is being used as a model for World Bank programs in other countries.

Grain traders also command proprietary datasets tracking crops, land use, and logistics. Using AI-driven analytics and blockchain platforms like Covantis, they monitor supply chains in real time and anticipate market shifts before competitors or regulators.

John Deere's See & Spray technology (partnership with Syngenta and InnerPlant) uses cameras to detect weeds and collect on-farm data. The company positions future profits from digital platforms rather than equipment sales.

**Evidence:**
- ETC Group (2026): "Indian government's new digital database, Agri Stack, gives Microsoft detailed information on 80 million Indian farmers"
- ETC Group (2026): "Bayer's Climate FieldView, Syngenta's Cropwise and Corteva's Granular"
- ETC Group (2026): "Traders command proprietary datasets that track crops, land use and logistics with granular precision"

**Connections:**

The food and agriculture sector spent a record $178 million on lobbying in 2023, with more than 1,300 lobbyists representing the sector. ## Verdict

**What is established (factual):**

1. **Food supply chains are highly concentrated at every stage**: grain trading (70-80%), meatpacking (80-85%), seeds (50%), pesticides (56%), fertilizer (25-65% by type). The same companies appear across multiple stages — Cargill is in grain, meat, and fertilizer.

2. **China controls critical minerals supply chains**: 91% of rare earth separation, 94% of permanent magnet production, and sole-sourcing for 41% of US pharmaceutical KSMs. These are structural chokepoints.

3. **TSMC dominates advanced semiconductor manufacturing**: 73% of foundry market, ~95% of leading-edge. Five economies hold 90% of global capacity.

4. **Taxpayer dollars flow directly to concentrated corporations**: USDA's $4.8B in annual food procurement is dominated by the same companies that control the supply chains (Tyson, ADM, JBS, Cargill). Federal crop insurance and disaster programs provide billions to large operations.

5. **Political spending reinforces the structure**: $340M+ in lobbying, 1,300+ lobbyists, $124M in campaign spending, and $500M+ in state/local tax subsidies create a self-reinforcing cycle.

6. **Corporate consolidation is increasing, not decreasing**: The Bunge-Viterra merger (2025), seed/pesticide mega-mergers (6→4 companies), and continued meatpacking concentration all point in one direction.

**What requires interpretation:**

- Whether this concentration constitutes "control" or is simply a feature of modern globalized markets
- Whether passive index fund ownership creates anticompetitive effects beyond concentration metrics
- Whether food supply chain vulnerability is primarily a national security concern or a market failure
- The appropriate policy response — antitrust enforcement, supply chain diversification, or industrial policy
