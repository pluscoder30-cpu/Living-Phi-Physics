# Investigation Agent Report #13: Following the Taxpayer Dollar to Physics

**Agent:** Investigation Agent 15  
**Mission:** Trace every dollar from the taxpayer to its destination in physics research  
**Date:** August 21, 2026  
**Classification:** PUBLIC — All data sourced from government budget documents, CRS reports, and appropriations legislation

---

## Executive Summary

Federal taxpayer dollars flowing to physics research reach their destination through a labyrinth of appropriations bills, subcommittee markups, agency budget requests, and congressional add-ons. The five primary funding pipelines:

| Pipeline | FY2026 Enacted | Who Decides | Physics Share |
|----------|---------------|-------------|---------------|
| DOE Office of Science | $8.40B | House/Senate E&W Subcommittee | ~100% physics-focused |
| NSF (total) | $8.75B | House/Senate CJS Subcommittee | ~$1.5B direct (MPS) |
| DoD RDT&E | $148.6B | House/Senate Defense Subcommittee | ~$2.4B (basic+applied) |
| NIH | $47.2B | House/Senate Labor-HHS Subcommittee | Indirect (biophysics) |
| NIST | $1.18B | House/Senate CJS Subcommittee | ~$844M (STRS) |

**Total federal physics-relevant R&D: approximately $15+ billion annually.**

## 1. THE CHAIN OF CUSTODY

### Step 1: Taxation
- Individual income taxes: ~$2.2 trillion/year
- Total federal revenue: ~$5 trillion/year
- Physics-relevant R&D: ~0.2-0.3% of total federal outlays (~$7 trillion FY2025)

### Step 2: Presidential Budget Request
The President submits a budget request (recommendation, not law). OMB coordinates agency requests.

### Step 3: Congressional Appropriations
12 appropriations bills fund government. Physics flows through:

| Appropriations Bill | Physics-Relevant Agencies |
|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Commerce, Justice, Science (CJS) | NSF, NIST, NASA |
| Energy and Water Development (E&W) | DOE Office of Science |
| Defense | DoD RDT&E, DARPA |
| Labor, HHS, Education | NIH |

### Step 4: Subcommittee Markups
Each bill: subcommittee draft → full committee markup → floor debate → House/Senate conference.

### Step 5: Agency Discretion
- Competitive peer review (NSF, DOE)
- Formula distribution (some NIH)
- Congressional add-ons/earmarks
- Program officer discretion

NIH supports physics indirectly through:

- **National Cancer Institute (NCI):** physics of imaging, radiation therapy

## 9. TRANSPARENCY TOOLS: Where Taxpayers Can Track Their Dollars

### USAspending.gov
- Official open data source for federal spending
- Created by Federal Funding Accountability and Transparency Act (FFATA, 2006)
- Enhanced by Digital Accountability and Transparency Act (DATA Act, 2014)
- Tracks contracts, grants, loans above $25,000
- GAO found only 2-7% of awards fully consistent with agency records (GAO-14-476)

### Other Tracking Tools
- **FedSpends.com:** USAspending API wrapper, easier to search
- **ScienceSpending.org:** Tracks obligations at five major science agencies
- **Grants.gov:** Federal grant applications portal
- **SAM.gov:** System for Award Management
- **CRS Reports:** Congressional Research Service analyses (everycrsreport.com)
- **AIP FYI Budget Tracker:** Detailed science policy tracking

### Transparency Gaps
- GAO-26-109034 (March 2026): "challenges remain" in federal spending data quality
- Subaward data incomplete on USAspending
- Other transaction agreements not always reported
- Some agencies lag in reporting

Source: GAO-26-109034; CRS R44027; USAspending.gov

### The Decision Process

1. **White House/OMB** sets priorities (e.g., proposed 55.8% NSF cut)
2. **Agency heads** defend budgets before Congress
3. **Subcommittee chairs** draft bills with staff
4. **Committee markups** adjust numbers
5. **Floor votes** pass bills (House: simple majority; Senate: 60-vote cloture threshold)
6. **Conference committees** reconcile differences
7. **President** signs or vetoes

**Critical insight:** The subcommittee chairs are the real power. A single committee chair can add or remove hundreds of millions through markup decisions. Congressional add-ons (earmarks) further redirect funds.

## 11. THE STATED REASON vs. THE ACTUAL EFFECT

### Stated Reasons for Physics Funding

| Agency | Stated Purpose | Statutory Authority |
|--------|---------------|---------------------|
| DOE Office of Science | "Deliver scientific discoveries and major scientific tools to transform our understanding of nature" | DOE Organization Act (P.L. 95-91) |
| NSF | "Promote the progress of science; to advance the national health, prosperity, and welfare" | National Science Foundation Act of 1950 |
| DoD S&T | "Maintain technological superiority on the battlefield" | 10 U.S.C. |
| NIH | "Enhance health, lengthen life, reduce illness and disability" | Public Health Service Act |

### Actual Effects

**What the money actually funds:**
- University research grants (individual investigators: $100K-$500K/year)
- National laboratory operations (17 DOE labs, 3 NSF-funded centers)
- Major facility construction ($100M-$1B+ per facility)
- Graduate student stipends ($30K-$40K/year)
- Postdoctoral researcher salaries ($50K-$70K/year)
- Equipment and instrumentation

**Where the money flows geographically:**
- Concentrated in states with major research universities and national labs
- California, Massachusetts, Illinois, New York, Texas receive disproportionate shares
- EPSCoR program ($250M/year directed by Congress) targets underrepresented states

**Who benefits:**
- ~2,500 research institutions receive NIH funding alone
- ~2,000 NSF grantees at universities nationwide
- 17 DOE national laboratories (managed by contractors: universities, nonprofits, corporations)
- Private sector through technology transfer and workforce development

## 12. KEY FINDINGS AND RED FLAGS

### Finding 1: Congress Overwhelmingly Rejected Proposed Cuts
The administration proposed cutting NSF by 55.8%, DOE Office of Science by 14.9%, and NIH by 40.5%. Congress rejected every one of these cuts, providing near-FY2025 levels for most agencies. **The appropriations subcommittees, not the White House, set the actual funding levels.**

### Finding 2: The Subcommittees Hold the Real Power
Six subcommittee chairs (3 House, 3 Senate) effectively determine where $260+ billion in science-related funding goes. Their markup decisions — often made with minimal public input — are the primary allocation mechanism.

### Finding 3: Earmarks Have Returned
Congressional directed spending returned in FY2022 and now directs hundreds of millions to specific projects. The $663M in NIST earmarks alone represents 56% of NIST's total budget.

### Finding 4: Transparency Is Improving But Incomplete
USAspending.gov tracks federal awards, but GAO has found significant data quality issues. Only 2-7% of awards were fully consistent with agency records. Subaward data is particularly incomplete.

### Finding 5: The Gap Between Request and Enacted Is Enormous
The FY2026 cycle saw the largest gap between presidential request and congressional enactment in modern science funding history. Congress provided $4.85 billion MORE for NSF than requested — a 124% increase over the request.

### Finding 6: Physics Funding Is Concentrated
DOE Office of Science funds physics primarily through 17 national laboratories. NSF funds physics primarily through university grants. DoD funds physics through service laboratories and university research. The flow is not evenly distributed.
