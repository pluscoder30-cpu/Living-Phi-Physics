## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The fossil fuel industry controls one of the largest concentrations of capital in human history, yet devotes a vanishingly small fraction to R&D — roughly 0.3% of revenue, compared to 15–20% in pharmaceuticals. More significantly, the industry has spent billions on lobbying ($2.95 billion from 1998–2024) and climate denial ($145.5 million from Koch foundations alone, $37+ million from ExxonMobil) to maintain the fossil-fuel-dependent energy paradigm. This financial structure creates a dual mechanism for the alleged enterprise: (1) alternative energy and physics research that threatens fossil fuel business models receives no industry funding, and (2) active suppression through lobbying and denial campaigns prevents policy support for alternatives. The R&D that IS conducted focuses exclusively on extraction efficiency and carbon capture — technologies that extend fossil fuel dominance rather than replace it.

## 5. CONNECTIONS TO THE ALLEGED ENTERPRISE

### 5.1 The Dual Suppression Mechanism

The fossil fuel industry maintains the alleged enterprise through two parallel mechanisms:

**Mechanism 1: Starvation of Alternative Research**
- Big Oil R&D is focused exclusively on extraction efficiency and carbon capture
- No industry funding goes to paradigm-shifting physics that could disrupt fossil fuels
- Alternative energy research receives <1% of oil company R&D budgets
- The ~$3.94B/yr industry R&D budget is spent on maintaining, not replacing, the fossil fuel paradigm

**Mechanism 2: Active Suppression Through Lobbying and Denial**
- $153M+/yr in federal lobbying (OpenSecrets)
- $219M per election cycle on political influence (Yale Climate Connections)
- $145.6M+ in climate denial foundation funding (Greenpeace)
- $558M+ in broader denial network funding (Brulle study)
- $34.8B/yr in government subsidies creating artificial price advantages

### 5.2 What Would Threaten This Structure

| Threat | How the Industry Responds |
|--------|--------------------------|
| Renewable energy | Lobbying against subsidies/tax credits |
| Climate science | Funding denial organizations |
| Carbon regulation | Lobbying against cap-and-trade, carbon tax |
| Alternative physics (perpetual energy, zero-point) | Not funded; no response needed if paradigms stay contained |
| Consciousness-field research | No industry interest; no threat perceived within current paradigm |

### 5.3 The R&D Funnel — Where the Money Actually Goes

The fossil fuel industry's R&D spending follows a strict hierarchy:

1. **Extraction technology** (fracking, deepwater drilling, enhanced oil recovery) — ~60% of R&D
2. **Refining and processing** — ~20% of R&D
3. **Carbon capture and storage** — ~10% of R&D (enables continued fossil fuel use)
4. **Biofuels** — ~5% of R&D (marginal alternative)
5. **Renewables/alternative energy** — ~5% of R&D (minimal)

**Source:** ExxonMobil R&D Brochure, 2019; IEA World Energy Investment 2020

**Key Finding:** Even the "clean" portion of fossil fuel R&D (CCS, biofuels) is designed to extend fossil fuel viability, not replace it. CCS is the clearest example: it addresses the environmental cost of burning fossil fuels while keeping the fuel source intact.
