## Part 1: THE FEDERAL RESERVE SYSTEM

### 1.1 Structure — Three Key Entities

The Federal Reserve System (est. 1913) has three main entities: [Source 1, 2]

#### A. Board of Governors

- **Type:** Independent federal government agency in Washington, D.C.
- **Members:** 7 governors, including Chair and Vice-Chair
- **Selection:** Nominated by the President, confirmed by the Senate
- **Term:** 14-year terms (staggered)
- **Accountability:** Reports to Congress
- **Functions:** Provides general guidance, oversees 12 Reserve Banks, sets discount rate and reserve requirements, supervises/regulates bank holding companies and member banks
- Does NOT receive funding through the congressional budget process

> "Independent means that it does not receive its funding through the congressional budget and appropriations process, and its leadership does not change when a new U.S. president takes office." — Source 2

**Key point:** Decisions by the Board and FOMC do NOT require approval by the President or anyone in the executive or legislative branches. [Source 2]

#### B. Twelve Federal Reserve Banks

- **Type:** Independent corporations, operating arms of the System
- **Structure:** 9-member board of directors per bank (3 Class A elected by member banks, 3 Class B representing commerce/agriculture, 3 Class C appointed by Board of Governors)
- **Functions:** Provide reserve accounts, lending via discount window, currency distribution, payment processing, fiscal agent for Treasury, supervision of member banks

**The 12 Districts:**

| District | City | Covers |
|----------|------|--------|
| 1st | Boston | New England |
| 2nd | New York | NY, NJ, CT, PR, USVI |
| 3rd | Philadelphia | Eastern PA, southern NJ |
| 4th | Cleveland | OH, western PA, KY, WV |
| 5th | Richmond | MD, VA, NC, SC, DC |
| 6th | Atlanta | FL, GA, AL, TN, MS, LA |
| 7th | Chicago | IL, IN, MI, WI, IA |
| 8th | St. Louis | MO, AR, IL, IN, KY, TN, MS |
| 9th | Minneapolis | MN, MT, ND, SD, WI |
| 10th | Kansas City | CO, KS, NE, NM, OK, WY |
| 11th | Dallas | TX, LA, NM |
| 12th | San Francisco | CA, OR, WA, NV, AZ, UT, ID, AK, HI, territories |

All 12 opened in November 1914. [Source 2]

#### C. Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC)

- **Type:** Monetary policymaking body
- **12 voting members:** 7 Board members + NY Fed president (permanent) + 4 rotating Reserve Bank presidents
- **Rotation groups:** (1) Boston/Philadelphia/Richmond, (2) Cleveland/Chicago, (3) Atlanta/St. Louis/Dallas, (4) Minneapolis/Kansas City/San Francisco
- All 12 presidents attend but only 5 vote
- **8 meetings per year**, policy statements after each, minutes at 3 weeks, transcripts at 5 years [Source 4]

### 1.2 The Dual Mandate

By law, the Fed achieves: [Source 3]

1. **Maximum employment**
2. **Stable prices** (2% inflation target, PCE index)
3. **Moderate long-term interest rates**

### 1.3 How Monetary Policy Works

The FOMC changes the **target range for the federal funds rate** — overnight interbank lending rate. [Source 3]

**Transmission chain:**

`
FOMC target change
  -> Interest on Reserve Balances (IORB) adjusted
    -> Interbank lending rates shift
      -> Short-term rates change (commercial paper, T-bills)
        -> Longer-term rates shift (mortgages, corporate bonds)
          -> Spending/investment decisions change
            -> Employment and inflation respond
`

**The rate corridor (post-2008):** [Source 6]

| Tool | Role | Effect |
|------|------|--------|
| IORB | Primary administered rate | Floor — banks won't lend below it |
| ON RRP | Supplementary floor | Non-bank floor |
| Discount Rate | Ceiling | Banks won't borrow above it |

**IORB mechanics:**
- Bank A holds reserves earning IORB
- Bank A won't lend to Bank B below IORB
- Bank B won't pay more than IORB (reserves are abundant)
- Fed funds rate gravitates toward IORB

### 1.4 Open Market Operations

Conducted by the Trading Desk at the New York Fed. [Source 5]

**Permanent OMOs:** Outright purchases/sales for SOMA portfolio. Used for QE.

**Temporary OMOs:**
- **Repos:** Fed buys security, agrees to resell = collateralized loan
- **Reverse repos:** Fed sells security, agrees to repurchase = collateralized borrowing

**QE mechanism (2008-2014):** Fed purchased ~.7 trillion in Treasuries and GSE securities.

`
Fed buys bonds -> investors receive new reserves -> bond prices rise -> yields fall
-> cheaper borrowing -> more spending -> economic support
-> investors seek alternatives -> corporate bond yields fall, equity prices rise
`

> "These market reactions helped ease overall financial market conditions and supported growth." — Source 3

**QT:** Since 2022, Fed reduces holdings by not reinvesting maturing principal payments. [Source 3]

### 1.5 Fed-Commercial Bank Interaction

| Function | Mechanism |
|----------|-----------|
| Reserve accounts | Banks hold reserves at Fed for settlement and regulation |
| Discount window | Fed lends directly to banks (collateralized) |
| Payment processing | ~/day in noncash payments |
| Currency distribution | Physical cash supplied through Reserve Banks |
| Supervision | Examines bank holding companies for safety/soundness |
| Fiscal agent | Manages Treasury accounts, issues/redeems securities |

When the Fed buys bonds, it credits banks' reserve accounts — creating new money. When it sells, it debits them. [Source 1, 3]

## Part 2: THE EUROPEAN CENTRAL BANK (ECB)

### 2.1 Structure

Est. 1998, Frankfurt am Main. Central bank for the euro (21 countries as of 2026). [Source 11, 12]

**Three bodies:**

#### Governing Council
- 6 Executive Board members + national central bank governors of euro area
- Rotation: top 5 economies (DE, FR, IT, ES, NL) share 4 votes; others share 11; monthly rotation
- Sets monetary policy, key interest rates, reserve supply
- Meets twice monthly (policy decisions every 6 weeks) [Source 29]

#### Executive Board
- President (Christine Lagarde), Vice-President (Boris Vujcic), 4 members
- Appointed by European Council, 8-year non-renewable terms
- Manages day-to-day operations

#### General Council
- All EU member state central bank governors
- Advisory/transitional role

### 2.2 Eurosystem vs ESCB

- **ESCB:** ECB + ALL EU national central banks
- **Eurosystem:** ECB + euro area national central banks only
- Eurosystem conducts single monetary policy [Source 11]

### 2.3 ECB Monetary Policy Tools

| Tool | Type | Function |
|------|------|----------|
| MRO | Open market | Weekly 1-week reverse transactions |
| LTRO | Open market | Monthly 3-month reverse transactions |
| Fine-tuning | Open market | Non-regular operations |
| Marginal lending | Standing facility | Overnight lending (ceiling) |
| Deposit facility | Standing facility | Overnight deposits (floor) |
| Minimum reserves | Administrative | Required holdings at national central bank |

MRO rate = ceiling, deposit rate = floor. [Source 26]

### 2.4 Independence

"As a supranational institution, the ECB acts in the interest of Europe as a whole; as a central bank, it is independent from any political or commercial influence." [Source 12]

## Part 3: THE BANK OF ENGLAND

### 3.1 Structure

- **Court of Directors:** Governing body, oversees operations
- **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC):** 9 members — Governor, 3 Deputy Governors, Chief Economist, 4 external members appointed by Chancellor
- **Financial Policy Committee (FPC):** Financial stability mandate
- **Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA):** Banking supervision

HM Treasury representative sits with MPC (no vote). [Source 14]

### 3.2 MPC Operations

- **Mandate:** Maintain price stability; support government economic policy
- **Target:** 2% CPI inflation (set annually in remit letter)
- **Meetings:** At least 8 per year
- **Bank Rate:** Interest rate paid on deposits by eligible firms at the BoE [Source 14]

### 3.3 Balance Sheet Operations

**QE (2009-2021):** Purchased gilts and corporate bonds, financed by new reserves.

> "The money we used to buy bonds when we were doing QE did not come from government taxation or borrowing. Instead, we can create money digitally in the form of 'central bank reserves.'" — Source 13

**QT:** Since Feb 2022, ceasing reinvestment; since Sept 2022, active sales.

**Key tools:**
- **Bank Rate:** Primary tool
- **STR (Short-Term Repo):** Launched 2022
- **ILTR (Indexed Long-Term Repo):** Supplies majority of reserves as QE unwinds; recalibrated June 2025
- **TFSME:** COVID-era term funding, wound down by 2025

### 3.4 BoE-Commercial Bank Relationship

- Banks hold reserves at BoE (settlement)
- BoE lends via STR/ILTR (regular), OSF/DWF (on-demand)
- BoE processes payments, distributes banknotes
- PRA supervises banks for prudential soundness

## Part 4: OPEN MARKET OPERATIONS — UNIVERSAL MECHANICS

### 4.1 What Are OMOs?

Open market operations are the purchase and sale of securities by a central bank to influence reserve supply and control short-term interest rates. [Source 7, 25]

### 4.2 The Core Mechanism

All central banks use variations of the same framework:

`
Central bank sets policy rate target
  -> Uses OMOs to manage reserve supply
    -> Reserves are base money banks hold at central bank
      -> More reserves = lower rates (easier policy)
        -> Fewer reserves = tighter rates
`

### 4.3 Types of OMOs

| Type | Direction | Central Bank Action | Effect |
|------|-----------|--------------------|----|
| Repo | Liquidity injection | Buys securities, agrees to resell | Increases reserves |
| Reverse repo | Liquidity withdrawal | Sells securities, agrees to repurchase | Decreases reserves |
| Outright purchase | Permanent injection | Buys securities outright | Permanently increases reserves |
| Outright sale | Permanent withdrawal | Sells securities outright | Permanently decreases reserves |

### 4.4 The Interest Rate Corridor

Almost all central banks use a corridor system: [Source 7, 25]

`
Ceiling: Lending facility rate (central bank lends to banks)
  |
  |  Market rate trades here
  |
Floor: Deposit facility rate (banks deposit at central bank)
`

**Examples:**

| Central Bank | Ceiling | Floor | Target |
|-------------|---------|-------|--------|
| Fed | Discount rate | IORB + ON RRP | Fed funds rate |
| ECB | MRO rate | Deposit facility rate | Short-term rates |
| BoE | Bank Rate + spread | Bank Rate | SONIA |
| BoC | Bank Rate + 25bps | Bank Rate - 25bps | Overnight rate |
| BoJ | Call rate | Policy rate balance | Short-term rate |
| SNB | Policy rate + spread | Policy rate | SARON |
| RBA | Cash rate + 25bps | Cash rate - 10bps | Cash rate |

### 4.5 Reserve Requirements

| Central Bank | Reserve Requirement |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Fed | 0% (eliminated March 2020) |
| ECB | 1% of eligible liabilities |
| BoE | No formal requirement |
| BoJ | 0.8%-1.3% (tiered) |
| BoC | None |
| RBI | 4% of NDTL |
| SNB | 0% |

### 4.6 QE Mechanics — Universal

`
Central bank creates digital reserves (new money)
  -> Uses reserves to buy government/corporate bonds
    -> Bond prices rise, yields fall
      -> Lower long-term rates -> cheaper borrowing
        -> Portfolio rebalancing -> riskier assets rise
          -> Wealth effect + easier credit -> more spending
`

**Scale of QE (approximate peaks):**
- Fed: .9 trillion (2022 peak)
- ECB: EUR 5 trillion (2022 peak)
- BoE: GBP 895 billion (2021 peak)
- BoJ: JPY 730+ trillion (ongoing)
- SNB: CHF 900 billion (peak)

## Part 5: THE BIS AND BASEL ACCORDS

### 5.1 The Bank for International Settlements

**Established:** 1930
**Headquarters:** Basel, Switzerland
**Ownership:** 63 central banks (~95% of world GDP)

**Mission:** "Support central banks' pursuit of monetary and financial stability through international cooperation, and act as a bank for central banks." [Source 8]

**The Basel Process:** Two axes:
1. Regular high-level meetings of senior monetary/financial officials
2. BIS support for international groups pursuing financial stability

Agreements reached at Basel are NOT binding until implemented nationally through each jurisdiction's regulatory processes.

### 5.2 The Basel Committee (BCBS)

- **Established:** 1974
- **Reports to:** GHOS (Group of Central Bank Governors and Heads of Supervision)
- **Chair:** 3-year term, renewable once
- **Purpose:** Global standard for prudential regulation
- **28 jurisdictions** represented [Source 8, 9]

### 5.3 Basel Framework — 14 Standards

| Acronym | Standard | Purpose |
|---------|----------|---------|
| SCO | Scope and definitions | Which banks are covered |
| CAP | Definition of capital | What counts as regulatory capital |
| RBC | Risk-based capital requirements | Minimum capital ratios |
| CRE | Credit risk RWA | Risk-weighted assets for credit |
| MAR | Market risk RWA | Risk-weighted assets for market risk |
| OPE | Operational risk RWA | Risk-weighted assets for ops risk |
| LEV | Leverage ratio | Non-risk-based backstop |
| LCR | Liquidity Coverage Ratio | Short-term liquidity (30-day) |
| NSF | Net Stable Funding Ratio | Medium-term funding stability |
| LEX | Large exposures | Concentration limits |
| MGN | Margin requirements | Derivatives margin rules |
| SRP | Supervisory review | Pillar 2 — supervisor oversight |
| DIS | Disclosure requirements | Pillar 3 — market discipline |
| BCP | Core Principles | Supervisory standards |

### 5.4 Basel III — Post-Crisis Reforms

In response to 2007-09 crisis. [Source 9]

1. **CET1 ratio:** 4.5% minimum
2. **Capital conservation buffer:** additional 2.5%
3. **Countercyclical buffer:** 0-2.5%
4. **G-SIB surcharges:** 1-3.5% for systemically important banks
5. **Leverage ratio:** 3% minimum
6. **LCR:** high-quality liquid assets / net cash outflows >= 100%
7. **NSFR:** available stable funding / required stable funding >= 100%

### 5.5 How Basel Affects Commercial Banks

`
BCBS sets standards -> National regulators implement via domestic law
  -> Commercial banks must meet capital/liquidity ratios
    -> Higher risk-weighted assets = more capital needed
      -> Influences lending decisions
        -> Shapes banking behavior globally
`

## Part 6: THE INTERNATIONAL MONETARY FUND (IMF)

### 6.1 Structure

**Established:** 1944 (Bretton Woods)
**Membership:** 191 countries
**Lending capacity:** ~ trillion

**Governance:**
- **Board of Governors:** One governor per member (finance minister or central bank governor)
- **Executive Board:** 25 members, day-to-day business
- **Managing Director:** Head of staff
- **Staff:** Primarily economists [Source 17, 19]

### 6.2 IMF Purpose

Article I purposes: [Source 19]

(i) Promote international monetary cooperation
(ii) Facilitate balanced growth of international trade
(iii) Promote exchange stability
(iv) Establish multilateral payments system
(v) Provide resources for balance of payments difficulties

### 6.3 IMF Funding — Quotas

Each member pays a quota based on relative economic size. Determines:
- Maximum financial commitment
- Voting power
- Share of SDR allocations
- Access to financing

25% in reserve assets, 75% in member's own currency. Effective lending capacity ~50% of total quotas. [Source 20]

### 6.4 IMF Lending — Purchase-Repurchase Mechanism

1. Country "purchases" reserve assets from IMF
2. Provides its own currency in exchange
3. Later "repurchases" its currency with reserve assets
4. Functionally equivalent to a loan [Source 20]

**Key facilities:** [Source 18]

| Purpose | Facility | Terms |
|---------|----------|-------|
| Balance of payments | SBA | GRA, market rates, up to 3 years |
| Protracted BOP need | EFF | GRA, up to 4 years |
| Low-income countries | ECF | PRGT, zero interest |
| Emergency financing | RFI/RCF | Outright disbursement |
| Strong fundamentals | FCL | GRA, 1-2 years |
| Short-term pressures | SLL | GRA, 12 months |
| Longer-term needs | RSF | RST, min 18 months |

### 6.5 IMF Surveillance

- Annual Article IV consultations with each member
- Monitors exchange rate policies
- Publishes World Economic Outlook, Global Financial Stability Report

### 6.6 IMF-Commercial Bank Relationship

IMF does not directly interact with commercial banks. IMF provides reserves to central banks/Treasuries. Stable currencies enable stable commercial banking. Conditionality may require banking sector reforms.

                    BIS (Basel)
                   /    |    \
                  /     |     \
               BCBS   FSB   Other committees
                /      |       \
               /       |        \
         National regulators implement
               /      |        \
              /       |         \
        Central Banks set policy
           /    |    |    \
          /     |    |     \
    Commercial Banks operate
        /    |    |    \
       /     |    |     \
  Households, Firms, Governments
`

### 8.2 The Money Creation Chain

`
Central bank creates base money (reserves)
  -> Commercial banks borrow reserves (discount window, repo)
    -> Banks use reserves for interbank settlement
      -> Banks create deposits (fractional reserve banking)
        -> Deposits = majority of money supply
          -> Money supply x velocity = nominal GDP
`

**Key insight:** Central banks control the *price* of money (interest rates) more directly than the *quantity*. QE attempted to control quantity when price hit zero lower bound.

## Part 10: HOW CENTRAL BANKS CONTROL MONEY

### 10.1 The Three Levers

**1. Interest Rates (Price of Money)**
- Primary tool in normal times
- Set by committee (FOMC, MPC, Governing Council, etc.)
- Transmitted through reserve rates -> interbank rates -> lending rates -> economy

**2. Reserve Supply (Quantity of Money)**
- Managed through OMOs (repos, reverses, outright purchases)
- QE = large-scale reserve creation when rates hit zero
- QT = reserve reduction by allowing bonds to mature

**3. Reserve Requirements (Regulation of Money)**
- Forces banks to hold minimum reserves (or not, as in US post-2020)
- Tiered systems (BoJ, SNB) incentivize or disincentivize reserve holding

### 10.2 The Modern Framework

`
Committee sets target rate
    |
    v
Administered rates adjusted simultaneously
(IORB, ON RRP, discount rate)
    |
    v
Open market operations ensure "ample reserves"
    |
    v
Market rates gravitate toward corridor
    |
    v
Rate changes transmit to broader economy
    |
    v
Economic activity and inflation respond
`

### 10.3 When Normal Tools Fail — QE and Forward Guidance

When rates hit the **zero lower bound** (can't go below ~0%):

**QE:** Central bank buys long-term bonds to push down long-term rates directly. Creates reserves to pay. Expands balance sheet.

**Forward Guidance:** Communication that rates will stay low for extended period. Shapes expectations, lowers long-term rates.

**Negative Interest Rates:** ECB, BoJ, SNB pushed rates below zero to penalize holding reserves.

**Yield Curve Control (BoJ):** Explicit target for 10-year JGB yield, buys whatever quantity needed.

The central banking system is a hierarchical, networked structure:

1. **BIS** sits atop as coordinator and standard-setter
2. **BCBS** sets capital/liquidity standards (Basel Accords)
3. **National regulators** implement Basel domestically
4. **Central banks** set monetary policy via interest rates
5. **OMOs and QE** manage reserve supply
6. **Commercial banks** operate within the framework, create money through lending
7. **IMF** acts as lender of last resort for countries
8. **World Bank** provides long-term development finance

The system controls money primarily through **price** (interest rates), not **quantity** (reserve creation). QE proved that quantity targeting is less effective when reserves are already ample. The corridor system, administered rates, and forward guidance form the modern toolkit.
