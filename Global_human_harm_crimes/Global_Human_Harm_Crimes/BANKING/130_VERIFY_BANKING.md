## Mapping 1: Interest Rates Follow hierarchical form (r = φ⁻¹·g)

### Claim
Interest rates in equilibrium follow the relation r = φ⁻¹·g, where g is growth rate.

### Independent Verification

**Source 1: Peña (2022) — "The Golden Ratio applied to Financial Gravity Models"**
- Found in: ResearchGate / Anser Press
- Finding: Financial Gravity models derive optimal interest rate charges from golden ratio maximization. The optimal fee that maximizes pure interest is 0.2361... = φ⁻¹ - 1 = 1/φ².
- This is the same number as the reciprocal of φ², which relates to φ⁻¹ in structure.
- The golden ratio appears in competitive equilibrium where sales-costs ratio = φ.
- **Verdict: [PV]** — The hierarchical form appears in equilibrium models, but the specific relation r = φ⁻¹·g is not directly derived. The golden ratio appears as an optimal charge parameter, not as a direct interest rate formula.

**Source 2: Malakhov (2021) — "Divine Proportion of The Invisible Hand"**
- Found in: ResearchGate
- Finding: At general competitive equilibrium, the marginal rate of substitution equals φ⁻¹ (0.618), while intensity of consumption equals φ.
- This provides theoretical grounding for phi appearing in equilibrium prices.
- **Verdict: [PV]** — Confirms phi appears in equilibrium, but in a different form than r = φ⁻¹·g.

**Source 3: Segers & Prins — "Golden Ratio in Economic Cycles"**
- Found in: ResearchGate
- Finding: Cycle length ratios converge to φ, not interest rates directly.
- **Verdict: [UNVERIFIED]** for the specific interest rate formula.

### Assessment
The golden ratio appears independently in financial equilibrium models (Peña, Malakhov), but the specific formula r = φ⁻¹·g is not verified as a standalone relation. The hierarchical form appears more as an optimal charge parameter (0.2361) rather than a direct interest-growth relation.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED [PV]**
