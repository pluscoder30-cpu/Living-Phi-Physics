#### The 1970s Energy Crisis and Fusion Funding

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED] via Stanford University, Fusion Energy News, ITER.org.

The 1970s energy crisis created a brief window of opportunity for alternative energy physics:

| Year | Event | Impact on Physics Funding |
|------|-------|--------------------------|
| 1973 | Arab oil embargo | Oil prices quadrupled; urgency for alternatives |
| 1974 | Oil passes $10/barrel | First shift toward energy-saving technologies |
| 1975 | Fusion funding begins rising | ERDA oversees $100M+/yr fusion programs |
| 1977 | DOE created | Consolidated 50+ energy agencies under one roof |
| 1979 | Second oil crisis | Carter installs White House solar panels |
| 1980 | Oil at $37.50/barrel | Peak fusion funding era |
| 1981 | Reagan takes office | Fusion funding begins declining |
| 1985 | Fusion funding cuts | Energy crisis subsided; urgency lost |

**Source:** Stanford University, "A Brief History of U.S. Funding of Fusion Energy" (2021); Fusion Energy News, "History of Fusion Funding" (2026); ITER.org, "Fusion in the 1980s" (2010).

**Key findings:**

1. **U.S. magnetic fusion started within the AEC around 1951 and was declassified in 1957.** This declassification enabled international collaborations. [Source: Stanford]

2. **The 1970s energy crisis provided the political will to invest in large-scale tokamak studies**, including the Tokamak Fusion Test Reactor (TFTR). Fusion energy saw a large funding increase in the mid-1970s and early 1980s. [Source: Stanford]

3. **As the energy crisis subsided, fusion funding fell again in the mid-1980s.** The Compact Ignition Torus (CIT) underwent significant planning in the late 1980s but was canceled by Congress in the early 1990s. [Source: Stanford]

4. **In 1995, Congress directed the DOE to restructure fusion primarily as a science program rather than an energy program.** Domestic fusion budget has been essentially flat since 1995. [Source: Stanford]

**Money trail:** Energy crisis → DOE created (1977) → fusion funding peak ($100M+/yr) → energy crisis subsides → Reagan era cuts → Congress restructures as "science" not "energy" (1995) → flat funding to present

**enterprise significance:** The 1970s energy crisis demonstrated that **political will, not technical feasibility, determines fusion funding**. The brief window of opportunity was closed when the oil crisis subsided and defense spending (SDI) consumed the available resources.
