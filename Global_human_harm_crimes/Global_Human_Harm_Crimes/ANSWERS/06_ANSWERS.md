## ANSWER 6: How does the heat dome > wildfire > debris flow > cascade appear in federal cost accounting?

**Sources:** FEMA Event Summary guidance, CRS IN12384, FAS (2024), UChicago Legal Forum (2024), CRS IN11696, FEMA PA Wildfire Memo (2022), Gov. Newsom 2022 request

**The cascade is tracked as SEPARATE events, not one compound event.** This is a critical structural gap:

1. **FEMA explicitly states:** It evaluates "discrete events and impacts, not seasonal or general atmospheric conditions."

2. **California 2022 denial:** Gov. Newsom requested a major disaster for the heat dome AND resulting wildfires together. FEMA denied it, saying damage was "actually attributable to the wildfires" not the heat dome.

3. **Incident periods defined separately:** FEMA assigns distinct incident periods for each declared event. Storms within 72 hours of each other can be grouped; cascading hazards across different timeframes cannot.

4. **Stafford Act gap:** "Slow-onset disasters" (drought, heat, compound cascades) are not defined in the Stafford Act. CRS notes these events "may not have clearly defined start or end dates, and cumulative damage may not be immediately apparent."

5. **Proposed reforms (not enacted):**
   - FAS recommends adding heat, smoke, and compound events to Stafford Act Section 102(2)
   - Extreme Heat Emergency Act of 2023 would explicitly include heat
   - CRS recommends standardizing procedures for determining when disparate events are a single compound event
   - FEMA PA Wildfire Memo (2022) acknowledged wildfire incidents have "significantly longer incident periods" but did not create compound event tracking

6. **Practical consequence:** The heat dome causes wildfire which causes debris flow which damages infrastructure. Each step gets a separate disaster declaration (if any), separate cost tracking, separate funding streams. The cascading causal chain is invisible in federal accounting.
