**Sources:** Duke Fuqua Feb 2026; Columbia CLS Blue Sky Blog Jan 2026; ScienceDirect Aug 2024; Phil McKinney Substack June 2026.

### Q6: Does the "lead agency" bilateral model actively prevent paradigm-challenging research?

**ANSWER:** The lead agency model (NSF-DFG, NSF-ANR, etc.) means each country pays its own researchers and reviews against national standards. Since all national peer review panels enforce the same Standard Model criteria, paradigm-challenging research is passively excluded rather than actively prevented. No cross-border accountability exists because no money crosses borders. The model is structurally redundant: even without conspiracy, homogeneity of national peer review ensures paradigm lock-in globally.

**Sources:** NSF DCL-NSF-DFG; NSF DCL-NSF-ANR; CASRAI guide to co-funding; 10_INTERNATIONAL_CONNECTIONS.md Section 10.
**Verdict:** [VERIFIED] Passive exclusion through structural homogeneity. No active suppression needed.

### Q8: How do DOE national labs' LDRD budgets fund exotic physics?

**ANSWER:** FY2024 total LDRD: $986.33 million across 2,649 projects at 17 labs (4.22% of total cost base). Statutory maximum is 6%. LDRD is "seed funding for high-risk, high-reward research ideas." However, projects must align with lab missions. Princeton Plasma Physics Lab LDRD: $5.95M (5.99% rate). SLAC: $9.67M (1.85%). Sandia: $262.15M (5.42%). No evidence of exotic physics funding through LDRD; mission alignment requirements limit paradigm-challenging work. LDRD funds "cutting-edge, creative work that benefits laboratory programs" — not fundamental paradigm challenges.

**Sources:** DOE FY2024 LDRD Report to Congress (Dec 2024); DOE FY2025 LDRD Report (March 2026); IFP "Lab-Directed R&D at the National Labs" Nov 2025.
**Verdict:** [VERIFIED] LDRD budget documented. No exotic physics funding evident; mission alignment limits paradigm-challenging work.
