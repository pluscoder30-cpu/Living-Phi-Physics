### Q1: What is the total individual personal injury cost of PFAS exposure?

**Answer:** No court-approved global personal-injury settlement matrix exists as of August 2026. The $12.5B+ in settlements (3M: $10.3B; DuPont/Chemours/Corteva: $1.185B; Tyco: $750M) cover public water systems only.

Individual claims proceed separately in MDL 2873. As of July 2026: **15,244 pending lawsuits**. Legal estimates place individual PFAS cancer claims at **$150,000 to $500,000** per case. First personal injury bellwether trial scheduled **September 2026** (thyroid cancer claim).

Projected total: If 15,000+ plaintiffs average $200K-$300K, individual claims could reach **$3-4.5 billion** beyond utility settlements.

**Sources:** King Law (Aug 2026); KnowYourExposure (May 2026); Drugwatch (Jun 2026); TortAdvisor (Jul 2026)
**Confidence:** [PV]

### Q2: How many additional military bases remain untested for PFAS?

**Answer:** DoD identified **723 active installations** requiring PFAS assessment (Sep 2025), up from 712 in 2024. Critical gaps remain:
- Army lagged Navy/Air Force in testing by years; systematic sampling only common from mid-2010s
- Private wells near bases largely untested
- GAO (Mar 2026): full cleanup of known sites won't complete until **2047**
- Pre-2000 contamination data essentially missing (Navy flagged concerns in 1980s)
- MilitaryPoisons.org: "10-15 years of foam use erased from the record... a systematic undercount"

**Sources:** VA PFAS (Apr 2026); GAO (Mar 2026); MilitaryPoisons.org (Jan 2026)
**Confidence:** [PV]

### Q5: Total cost of nitrate-related health effects?

**Answer:** No aggregate national cost exists. Key data: California study found statistically significant correlation between nitrate and thyroid cancer; disadvantaged communities had **2x thyroid cancer rate**. 5.6M Americans exposed via community water. Associated with colorectal, bladder, stomach cancers; adverse pregnancy outcomes. Aggregate likely **hundreds of millions annually**.

**Sources:** ATSDR (2023); PMC8165459 (2021); ScienceDirect (2025)

### Q6: Will EPA PFAS MCLs survive rollback?

**Answer:** Under active rollback but not fully rescinded. PFOA/PFOS MCLs at 4 ppt remain legally in effect but compliance extended to 2031. Four other PFAS standards (PFHxS, PFNA, GenX, PFBS) formally proposed for rescission (May 20, 2026). D.C. Circuit denied EPA's Jan 2026 vacatur request. States (IL, NJ) adopted independent stricter standards.

**Sources:** Harvard ELP (May 2026); Jones Day (May 2026); Illinois EPA (May 2026)
**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

### R1: Talent pipeline from fossil fuel R&D labs

**Answer:** Over **25% of US oil/gas employees** near retirement (McKinsey, 2024). Industry scores below average for career opportunities and culture. Broader US research brain drain: **75% of researchers** considering leaving US (Nature, Mar 2025); **32% more applications to foreign positions**; **17% decline in international student enrollment**. Fossil fuel R&D talent moves to consulting, renewables, national labs, academia, or other energy companies. IEA notes companies rebalancing "away from riskier and longer-term research investments."

**Sources:** McKinsey (Feb 2024); Nature (Mar 2025); AAU (Jan 2026); IEA (Feb 2026)
**Confidence:** [PV]

### R2: Patent portfolios blocking alternative energy

**Answer:** Michigan v. BP et al. (Jan 2026) alleged a "capture-and-kill" strategy - buying solar/EV patents then allowing them to lapse. Exxon obtained EV charging station patents but never used them. After developing first hybrid prototype, Exxon abandoned EV/solar research in early 1980s. Companies used patent litigation to deter market entrants. Callaway Climate Insights (2020): many top-50 green patent producers are oil/gas companies raising "patent thicket" concerns. IEA: US fossil fuel patenting surged 2010-2014 with unconventional oil boom.

**Sources:** Michigan v. BP et al. (Jan 2026); Virginia Tech (2002); Callaway Climate Insights (Nov 2020); IEA Patent Explorer (Sep 2025)
**Confidence:** [PV] - Allegations unproven in court

### R3: CCS funding allocation - does it extend fossil fuel viability?

**Answer:** Yes, substantially. DOE has funded CCS since 1997; **.2B in FECM appropriations** since FY2010, with **.7B directed to CCS**. IIJA added **.5B for CCS** plus **.6B for DAC**. However: Over **80% of CCS projects use captured CO2 for Enhanced Oil Recovery (EOR)** - extracting more oil. CIEL reports **85% of US CCS subsidies go to EOR**. Fossil fuel industry involved with ~**85% of known CCS projects**. A power plant with CCS burns **up to 30% more fossil fuels** for same energy output. Despite decades of funding, CCS remains "in its development and testing phase" (Global CCS Institute). GAO (2024): DOE FECM "failed to follow its own guidance on risk reduction" for CCS project selection.

**Sources:** CRS Reports R44902, IF11861; Geoengineering Monitor (Nov 2021); EESI; GAO (2024); Power Magazine (Oct 2018)
**Confidence:** [VERIFIED] - CCS primarily extends fossil fuel viability via EOR
