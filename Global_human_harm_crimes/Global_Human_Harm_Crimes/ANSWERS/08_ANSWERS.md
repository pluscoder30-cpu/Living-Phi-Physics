## Interpretive Questions from File 14 (BlackRock/Vanguard)

These are labeled What Requires Interpretation (lines 327-330), not formal open questions. Brief answers:

### IQ1: Whether this structure constitutes control or is simply a feature of modern capital markets?

**Answer:** [INFERENCE] Both are true simultaneously. The Big Four manage 26.5% of global AUM ($39T of $147T) through passive index strategies. This gives them proxy voting power over corporate governance at virtually every major public company. Whether this constitutes control depends on definition: they vote proxies but do not direct operations. However, common ownership of competing firms (e.g., Vanguard owns ~9% of Lockheed Martin AND ~9% of RTX AND ~9% of Northrop Grumman) creates structural incentives that may suppress competition. The SEC and FTC have examined but not resolved this question.

### IQ2: Whether passive index investing creates anticompetitive effects?

**Answer:** [INFERENCE] Academic literature is split. Posner-Scott-Weyl anticompetitive thesis argues common ownership by index funds reduces product-market competition. Empirical evidence (Azar, Schmalz, Tecu 2018) found airline ticket prices increased due to common ownership. However, dissenting research (Cavaco-Skreta, 2022) finds limited effects. The structural issue remains: 3 firms own 20-25% of every S&P 500 company, creating board-level alignment that may not serve consumers.

### IQ3: Whether pension fund investments create conflicts of interest?

**Answer:** [PV] Texas AG Ken Paxton (Aug 2022) accused BlackRock of politicizing pension fund management through ESG criteria. 19 states joined a letter to Larry Fink challenging ESG. The structural conflict: pension funds are clients of BlackRock/Vanguard, but these firms also manage investments that may conflict with pension beneficiaries interests (e.g., defense contractors, energy companies). BlackRock explicitly markets public pension solutions.

### IQ4: The appropriate role of ESG criteria in institutional investing?

**Answer:** [INFERENCE] This is a political and regulatory question, not a factual one. BlackRock scaled back ESG support in 2023 under political pressure. Vanguard launched new ESG funds for institutional investors (Sept 2025) while drastically reducing ESG project funding. The SEC eliminated wider societal interest requirement for shareholder proposals. The tension: ESG criteria may serve long-term risk management (verified: ESG-labeled funds underperformed in 2022-2023) but also function as a mechanism for institutional control over corporate behavior.
