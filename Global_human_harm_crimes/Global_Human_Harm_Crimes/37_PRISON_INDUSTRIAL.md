### Finding 5: Mandatory Minimum Sentencing and Three Strikes Laws

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

**War on drugs escalation:**
- June 18, 1971: President Nixon declared drug abuse "public enemy number one"
- 1984: Reagan signed Comprehensive Crime Control Act — established mandatory minimum sentences, expanded marijuana penalties
- 1986: Reagan signed Anti-Drug Abuse Act — galvanized by death of Len Bias
- Drug offenders in state prisons: increased twelvefold since 1980
- 2000: 22% of federal/state prisoners convicted on drug charges
- Drug offenses = 1 in 5 people in U.S. prisons

**Mandatory minimums impact:**
- 2013: At least 3,278 Americans serving life without parole for nonviolent crimes (including selling $10 worth of drugs)
- 80%+ of these life sentences resulted from mandatory sentencing laws
- Three strikes laws: life sentences increased 83% between 1992-2003, while violent crimes fell in the same period

**War on drugs cost:**
- Federal and state spending on incarceration: ~$81 billion annually (prison, parole, probation operations)
- Court costs, bail bond fees, prison phone fees: ~$38 billion annually
- Total carceral system cost: ~$119 billion annually

### Finding 8: Convict Leasing — Historical Continuity

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

The prison-industrial complex has direct historical roots in the slavery economy.

**Timeline:**
- 1817: Auburn Prison (NY) — first for-profit prison, forced labor
- 1865: 13th Amendment — slavery abolished EXCEPT as punishment for crime
- 1866-1869: Southern states begin leasing convicts (AL, TX, LA, AR, GA, MS, FL)
- 1860s: Black Codes criminalized vagrancy, unemployment — created prison labor force
- 1928: Alabama last state to abolish convict leasing
- 1979: PIECP/Prison-Industries Act — private sector prison labor returns
- 1995: Businesses win exemptions from minimum wage laws for prison labor

**Modern parallels to convict leasing:**
- Louisiana Angola prison: 18,000 acres (former slave plantation), 65%+ Black inmates, $0.02/hour
- Arkansas: prison farms supply Walmart, Tyson, Cargill — unpaid labor
- Texas: 140,000+ inmates, no pay, $88.9M in Texas Correctional Industries (2014)
- Arizona: $0.50-$1.50/hour after deductions — rural economies "dependent" on prison labor

**13th Amendment exception text:** "Neither slavery nor involuntary servitude, except as a punishment for crime whereof the party shall have been duly convicted, shall exist within the United States."
