## Executive Summary

The Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP) was a $22M DIA contract (2008–2012) administered by Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS). Under this program, 38 Defense Intelligence Reference Documents (DIRDs) were commissioned from external physicists, engineers, and academics — surveying frontier aerospace science from warp drives to metamaterials to antigravity. **37 of 38 were released via FOIA on March 25, 2022. DIRD #38 remains withheld as SECRET//NOFORN.** The title of the withheld DIRD is known: *"State of the Art and Evolution of High Energy Laser Weapons"* by J. Albertine — but only the unclassified version was released. The classified version's content remains secret and is the subject of an ongoing FOIA appeal by The Black Vault.

### Finding 2: DIRD #38 — The Classified SECRET//NOFORN Report

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

**Title:** *State of the Art and Evolution of High Energy Laser Weapons*
**Author:** J. Albertine, Directed Technologies, 109 Kingswood Rd., Annapolis, MD 21401
**Document ID:** DIA-08-1003-019
**Classification:** SECRET//NOFORN (the classified version; the UNCLAS//FOUO version is public)
**Status:** Withheld from FOIA release; subject of ongoing appeal by The Black Vault

**What the unclassified version covers (31 pages):**
- Overview of megawatt-class high-energy laser (HEL) weapons
- Evolution from chemical lasers to electrically powered solid-state and free-electron lasers
- Military applications from kilowatt to megawatt power levels
- Space-based laser weapon concepts for spacecraft in or beyond Earth orbit
- Atmospheric propagation challenges (absorption, scattering, turbulence, blooming)
- Beam control, tracking, and targeting systems
- Historical systems: Airborne Laser Laboratory, Mid-Infrared Advanced Chemical Laser, MDA Airborne Laser, Advanced Tactical Laser
- Discussion of policy concerns about weaponization of space
- Future projections: spacecraft laser weapons conceivable within 20 years

**Why it was classified:**
The UNCLASSIFIED version itself discusses megawatt-class laser weapons, space-based weapon concepts, and military capabilities extending decades into the future. The SECRET version likely contains:
- Specific classified laser weapon programs or capabilities
- Threat assessments of adversary HEL programs
- Technical specifications beyond what the unclassified version reveals
- Intelligence on foreign (e.g., Chinese, Russian) HEL development
- [INFERENCE] Potentially connections to UAP-related directed energy observations

### Finding 6: Puthoff's Account — Why the DIRDs Were Written

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

From Dr. Hal Puthoff's speech at the SSE/IRVA Conference, June 15, 2018:

> "The Defense Intelligence Agency put out a Broad Area Announcement, in fact it was unclassified. They wanted to evaluate twelve potential threat areas with regard to these Advanced Aerospace Vehicles… There's a list of them. Exactly what you think you'd want to know: lift, propulsion, signature reduction, human effects and so on."
>
> "There's so much high-level security and compartmentalization in this subject area, and there's a lot of it, it's difficult for contractors to obtain expert opinions on advanced technologies because they would expose why they are interested. So, I acted as a surrogate. I was contracted to commission mostly unclassified whitepapers from experts around the globe about where their particular subject areas would be in (the year) 2050 as like a general survey of aerospace futures."
>
> "I let out 38 contracts over a two-year period… positron aerospace propulsion, IEC fusion as a compact energy source, warp drive, dark energy, extra dimensions, metallic glasses for aerospace use… negative mass propulsion, antigravity for aerospace applications, programmable matter, invisibility cloaking."
>
> "The Defense Intelligence Agency bundled up these 38 papers and put them out as Defense Intelligence Reference Documents posted on a JWICS server that anyone in the government or contractors could get access to."

**Key implication:** The DIRDs were designed as a "plausibility cover" — real science papers that allowed the DIA to study exotic topics without revealing why they were interested. The papers themselves were unclassified, but the program context (UAP threat assessment) was classified.

### Finding 7: The SAP Request and Its Denial

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

On June 24, 2009, Senator Harry Reid wrote to Deputy Secretary of Defense William Lynn requesting that AAWSAP/AATIP be placed under Special Access Program (SAP) protection with a "bigoted list" (restricted access list). The intent was to protect methodologies, personnel, and potential exotic technology applications.

**Outcome:** James R. Clapper (Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence) recommended against SAP status. DIA completed a quality review in October–November 2009 and formally advised no SAP protection was warranted, citing the "unclassified/basic-science nature of deliverables to date."

**Source documents released via FOIA:**
- `DR_Reid_Brief_May09.pdf` — Reid meeting talking points
- `20090624_Reid_to_DEPSECDEF_ref_AAITP_in_SAP.pdf` — Reid's SAP request letter
- `U-09-2660CE-IM-Review_of_Special_AccessProgram_Request.pdf` — DIA review
- `OUSDI_IM_on_AATIP_Final.pdf` — Under SecDef for Intelligence memo

**Significance:** The DIA explicitly classified the DIRD deliverables as "unclassified/basic-science" — yet the program context was classified, and DIRD #37 was withheld as SECRET//NOFORN. This suggests the classification applied not to the individual papers but to the program's purpose and threat-assessment framework.

### Finding 8: The DIRD Corpus as Exotic Physics Blueprint

**Confidence:** [INFERENCE]

The 38 DIRDs, taken as a corpus, map precisely onto the 10 threat areas from the AAWSAP solicitation. But the solicitation's framing — "foreign advanced aerospace weapon threats from the present out to the next 40 years" — combined with the specific topics chosen, suggests the DIA was not merely surveying future tech but investigating observed phenomena.

**The exotic physics topics form a coherent cluster:**
1. **Spacetime engineering** (vacuum propulsion, warp drive, wormholes, antigravity)
2. **Negative energy/mass** (negative mass propulsion, negative energy states, quantum vacuum energy)
3. **Signature management** (invisibility cloaking, metamaterials)
4. **Directed energy** (high-energy lasers, pulsed microwaves)
5. **Human effects** (field effects on biological tissues — the only DIRD directly addressing UAP-related health effects)

**The timewar.org analysis states:**

> "Metamaterials and cloaking bear on signature management; metric engineering bears on inertial and acceleration questions; advanced energy concepts bear on power density. This correspondence is strong enough to make AAWSAP more than an arbitrary science-fiction reading list. It is not strong enough to identify a recovered object, a program of replication, or the origin of the phenomenon."

## Verdict

The 38 AAWSAP DIRDs represent the most extensive confirmed U.S. government survey of exotic physics ever made partially public. The program was real, funded at $22M, and produced classified and unclassified technical reports on topics from warp drives to antigravity to quantum vacuum energy. The authors were real physicists — not fringe figures — from Lockheed Martin, major universities, and specialized consultancies. The classification of DIRD #37 as SECRET//NOFORN, despite the unclassified version being publicly available, indicates the classified version contains information beyond the published technical content — likely threat assessments, classified program connections, or specific capability data. The DIRDs' correspondence to the 10 solicitation threat areas, combined with the program's UAP context, suggests the DIA was systematically investigating observed phenomena, not merely speculating about future technology.

**Overall confidence:** HIGH — backed by primary FOIA documents, multiple independent sources, and direct participant statements.
