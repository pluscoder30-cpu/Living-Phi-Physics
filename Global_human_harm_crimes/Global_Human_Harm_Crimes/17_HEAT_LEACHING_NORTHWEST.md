## Findings

### Finding 1: The 2021 Pacific Northwest Heat Dome — A Catastrophic Preview

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

The June 25–July 7, 2021 heat dome over the Pacific Northwest was one of the most anomalous extreme heat events ever recorded on Earth. Temperature anomalies reached 16–20°C above normal. Lytton, British Columbia recorded 49.6°C (121.3°F) — the highest temperature ever recorded north of 45° latitude — and was destroyed by wildfire the following day.

**Mortality:**
- **British Columbia:** 619 confirmed heat-related deaths (June 25–July 1); 95% increase in population mortality over 8 days
- **Oregon:** 83 confirmed deaths (ages 37–97); most lived alone without working A/C
- **Washington:** 100–171 confirmed heat-related deaths; 1,232 excess deaths during heat dome period
- **Alberta:** 66+ deaths
- **Total PNW estimate:** ~1,400+ excess deaths across the region

**Economic Damage:**
- **NOAA estimate:** ≥$8.9 billion (2021 USD) in US damages
- **BC cumulative (heat dome + wildfires + atmospheric river):** $10.6–$17.1 billion CAD (3–5% of provincial GDP)
- **Insured damages:** Only $854M — 5–8% of total costs; the rest borne by households and businesses
- **Non-insured damages:** $1.7–$5.1 billion

**Key Vulnerability Factor:** 98% of BC heat deaths occurred indoors due to lack of adequate cooling and poor ventilation. Two-thirds of fatalities were people over age 70. 28% lived in the most materially deprived neighborhoods.

**Sources:** [1, 2, 9, 21]

### Finding 3: Infrastructure Damage — The Hidden Multiplier

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

#### Roads and Bridges
- **2021 PNW:** Sections of Interstate 5 and State Route 162 buckled in Washington and Oregon; multiple road closures
- **Portland:** Heat exceeded design limits of TriMet MAX system, melting train power lines and shutting down trains for a day
- **Edmonton, BC:** 57 sidewalks buckled from thermal expansion of concrete
- **2023 nationally:** Federal Railroad Administration recorded **600+ buckling-related events** — steel tracks expanding and warping — leading to nearly as many train derailments
- **Heat-related buckling:** 16 fatalities, ~$320 million in damages (2023, FRA data)
- **Future projection:** Road maintenance/replacement costs from rising temperatures could reach **$19–26.3 billion nationwide by 2040** (JEC)
- **EU projection (4°C scenario):** Transport O&M costs rise €4.8 billion (+6.9%) annually

#### Power Grid
- **BC Hydro:** Set all-time summer peak demand record at 8,568 MW on June 28, 2021 — 600 MW above previous record
- **Washington State:** Rolling power outages in Spokane area; Idaho State Capitol voluntarily turned off lights
- **California 2022:** Single heat event caused $230M in power outage costs
- **Across 7 CA events:** $580M combined power outage damages
- **National (2024):** Extreme heat + energy strain cost the US over **$162 billion** — nearly 1% of GDP (FAS)
- **Power line sagging:** Full-capacity lines sag in high heat → fire risk if touching trees → utilities reduce flow → supply drops on peak demand days
- **Nuclear/coal plants:** Cooling water starts warmer → less heat absorption → forced production slowdowns

#### Aircraft
- **Heat degrades aircraft performance:** Reduces lift capacity, requires payload reductions, longer takeoff distances
- **2023:** Flight delays/cancellations in Seattle, Portland, London, Phoenix

### Finding 5: Heat Island Effect — Structural Mortality Amplifier

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

Urban heat islands (UHIs) amplify heat-related mortality through elevated nighttime temperatures that prevent physiological recovery.

| City/Region | UHI-Attributed Deaths | % of Heat Deaths | Source |
|-------------|----------------------|------------------|--------|
| London (2022 heatwave) | 141 | 38% of 370 heat deaths | IOPscience 2024 |
| London (2018 summer) | 403 | 50% of 780 heat deaths | SSRN 2024 |
| Tokyo Metro (2010-2019) | 2,207 | 30% of urban heat deaths | IOPscience 2024 |
| Sydney (2006-2018) | 108/yr | 92% reduction if no UHI | MDPI 2022 |
| 93 European cities (2015) | 6,700 | 4.33% of all summer deaths | Lancet Planetary Health 2023 |

**Key Finding:** In Sydney, >90% of heatwave days would NOT breach heatwave thresholds without the UHI effect. Tree canopy reduces UHI by ~50% on average. Increasing tree coverage to 30% would prevent ~2,644 deaths across 93 European cities.

**Economic Cost of UHI (Europe):** If UHI effect were neutralized, death toll could drop >50% in cities like Paris, Istanbul, Cologne. Paris Agreement compliance + UHI mitigation could prevent 2 million+ lives in Europe this century and save €150 billion annually.

### Finding 6: The Disaster Industrial Complex — Who Profits

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

#### Disaster Cost Passthrough to Ratepayers

- Duke Energy Florida: Storm protection charge up **3,000%** since 2021
- Puget Sound Energy (WA): Added ~$14/year wildfire mitigation surcharge
- PacifiCorp and Portland General Electric (OR): Passing fire-related mitigation costs to customers
- PG&E: Wildfire-related charges = ~18% of system costs in 5 years preceding 2023

## Verdict

**Extreme heat in Northwest/Northern America represents a structural leaching mechanism with three primary extraction channels:**

1. **Mortality Leaching:** 21,518+ officially recorded US heat deaths (1999-2023), with true excess deaths likely 2x higher. The 2021 PNW heat dome killed 1,400+ people in a region whose infrastructure was not designed for such temperatures. Deaths concentrated among elderly, low-income, Indigenous, and minority populations.

2. **Infrastructure Cost Leaching:** Heat damages roads, rails, bridges, and power grids designed for cooler climates. These costs are systematically passed to taxpayers through utility disaster surcharges (58+ charges in 18+ states), federal disaster spending ($16B+/yr FEMA), and rising insurance premiums (30% since 2020). The infrastructure was not built for the temperatures it now faces — and the upgrade costs are socialized while utility profits are privatized.

3. **Disaster Industrial Complex Extraction:** Private equity firms have acquired 72+ disaster restoration companies since 2020, many of the same firms also own fossil fuel assets contributing to climate change. The disaster economy has grown from ~$80B/yr (1990s) to ~$1T/yr (2025). An estimated 10% (~$18B/yr) is lost to fraud. Utility companies extract 15% profit margins on $200B+ in annual revenue, with disaster costs passed to captive ratepayers who cannot choose alternative providers.

**The Northwest is ground zero for this leaching:** infrastructure designed for 60-70°F summers now faces 110-120°F heat domes. Every component — asphalt, rail steel, power lines, building codes, A/C penetration — was calibrated for a climate that no longer exists. The gap between designed-for and actual conditions is the leaching surface area, and it expands with every degree of warming.
