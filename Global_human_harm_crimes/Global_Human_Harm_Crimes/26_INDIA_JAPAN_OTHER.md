#### Finding 5: India Private Sector R&D Gap

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

In countries where R&D investment is 2%+ of GDP, over 50% comes from private sector. In India:
- Private sector: 36% of national R&D expenditure
- Government: 64%
- Total R&D as % of GDP: ~0.65% (vs 2%+ in US, China, Germany, Japan)
- India's share in global scientific publications: 5.1% (2020)

**Harm produced:** Fundamental physics research in India is starved of resources. The private sector contribution gap means government cannot fund both applied and fundamental research adequately.

## Verdict

This investigation documents the structural fiscal suppression of physics research across India, Japan, South Korea, Brazil, Australia, Canada, and Israel. Key findings:

1. **India** has significant physics ambitions (LHC, quantum, space) but is severely constrained by low R&D spending (0.65% GDP vs 2%+ target) and a private sector gap.

2. **Japan** leads in physics infrastructure (RIKEN, JT-60SA, quantum computing) but researchers report budget increases eroded by rising costs and supplementary budget dependence.

3. **South Korea** is actively restructuring away from basic physics research toward applied R&D, with direct cuts to IBS and international collaborations like Hyper-Kamiokande.

4. **Brazil** has suffered chronic underfunding (41% cut 2015-2021) with persistent regional concentration of resources.

5. **Israel** faces war-related disruption to scientific infrastructure, with recovery underway.

**Overall assessment:** The Alleged Enterprise operates globally. Even in wealthy nations like Japan and South Korea, basic physics research faces structural constraints. In developing nations like India and Brazil, the gap is existential.
