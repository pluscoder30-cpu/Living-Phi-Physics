### Finding 1: The Jekyll Island Meeting - Federal Reserve Architects

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

In November 1910, six men met secretly at the Jekyll Island Club off the coast of Georgia to draft what would become the Federal Reserve Act. The meeting was organized by Senator Nelson Aldrich, chair of the Senate Finance Committee, following the Panic of 1907.

**Participants (the First Name Club):**
1. **Nelson Aldrich** - U.S. Senator, Republican, Rhode Island. Father-in-law of John D. Rockefeller Jr. [VERIFIED via Source 1]
2. **A. Piatt Andrew** - Assistant Secretary of the Treasury. Harvard economics professor. [VERIFIED via Source 1]
3. **Henry P. Davison** - Partner at J.P. Morgan & Co. Founded Bankers Trust Company in 1903. [VERIFIED via Source 1]
4. **Arthur Shelton** - Aldrich's private secretary. [VERIFIED via Source 1]
5. **Frank Vanderlip** - President of National City Bank (forerunner of Citibank). [VERIFIED via Source 1]
6. **Paul Warburg** - Partner at Kuhn, Loeb & Co. German-born banker with European central banking expertise. [VERIFIED via Source 1]

**Possible additional participant:**
7. **Benjamin Strong** - Vice President of Bankers Trust Company. Became first Governor of the NY Fed in 1914. [PV - Source 1]

**The Cover Story:** The group traveled by private rail car from New Jersey, using only first names. They told staff they were on a duck hunting trip. Warburg brought duck hunting equipment despite never having shot a duck. [VERIFIED via Source 1, 3]

**What They Drafted:** A plan for the Reserve Association of America - a single central bank with 15 branches governed by boards elected by member banks, with larger banks getting more votes. Presented to Congress as the Aldrich Plan. [VERIFIED via Source 1]

**The Connection to the Fed Act:** After the 1912 election, Congressman Carter Glass and Senator Robert Owen drafted new legislation. Paul Warburg directly consulted with Glass, Owen, and Wilson's chief political adviser Col. E.M. House. The final Federal Reserve Act, signed December 23, 1913, closely resembled the Aldrich Plan with different political governance structures. [VERIFIED via Source 1, 2]

> It was absolutely an epoch-making event... We worked morning, noon, and night. At the end of our week, we had whipped into shape a bill that included what we felt, pridefully, should be presented to Congress.
> - Frank Vanderlip, Saturday Evening Post (1935)

**Enterprise Connection:** The same banking families who created the Federal Reserve (Morgan, Rockefeller through Aldrich, Kuhn Loeb through Warburg) would later profit from WWI financing through the very institution they designed. The Fed provided the monetary infrastructure for war lending that enriched its architects.
