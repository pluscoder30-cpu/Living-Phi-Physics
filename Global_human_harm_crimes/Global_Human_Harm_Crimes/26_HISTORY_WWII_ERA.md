### Finding 1: Manhattan Project - $2 Billion Hidden from Congress

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

The Manhattan Project cost approximately $1.845-2 billion (equivalent to ~$25-36 billion today). Only seven members of Congress were briefed on the total program before the war ended. Funding was concealed inside War Department appropriations bills. Senator Harry Truman was persuaded to drop his investigation by Secretary of War Henry Stimson.

**Key facts:**
- Total cost: $1.845 billion by end of war (~9 days of wartime spending)
- Peak workforce: 129,000 (June 1944); 84,500 construction, 40,500 plant operators
- Over 500,000 people worked on the project total due to high turnover
- Only 7 of 435 Congress members briefed before war end
- Funding buried in War Department appropriations as classified line items

**Money trail:** U.S. Treasury ($2B) -> Manhattan Engineer District -> contractors -> Oak Ridge + Hanford + Los Alamos

### Finding 5: OSS to CIA - The Birth of Americas Permanent Secret Government

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

The CIA was created on September 18, 1947, when President Truman signed the National Security Act. Its predecessor was the Office of Strategic Services (OSS), which employed ~13,000 people during WWII.

**The lineage:**
1. 1941: Coordinator of Information (COI) created under William J. Donovan
2. 1942: OSS replaces COI under Joint Chiefs of Staff
3. October 1945: Truman dissolves OSS
4. 1946: Central Intelligence Group (CIG) established (interim, no budget/mandate)
5. July 26, 1947: National Security Act signed, creating CIA
6. September 18, 1947: CIA officially begins operations

**Key detail:** A third of CIA's initial personnel were OSS veterans. Four future CIA directors served in OSS: Allen Dulles, Richard Helms, William Colby, and William Casey.

**Key figures in the transition:**
- William J. Donovan: OSS director, "Wild Bill," built the wartime intelligence apparatus
- Allen Dulles: OSS station chief in Switzerland, became longest-serving CIA Director (1953-1961)
- Frank Wisner: Created Office of Policy Coordination (covert action branch)
- James Jesus Angleton: Counterintelligence chief, paranoid mole hunts
- Walter Pforzheimer: Helped draft the National Security Act

### Finding 7: Operation Paperclip - Nazi Scientists Build NASA

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

**Note:** President Truman officially banned Nazi Party members from the program, but the JIOA systematically whitewashed scientists' records. SS memberships and forced labor connections were concealed.

**The forced labor connection:** V-2 rockets were built at the Mittelbau-Dora concentration camp where an estimated 20,000 prisoners died. The scientists who designed and tested these rockets were aware of the slave labor production system.
