## Findings

### Finding 1: Legal Aid Funding — Structural Starvation

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

The Legal Services Corporation (LSC), established by Congress in 1974, is the primary federal funder of civil legal aid. Its funding has been systematically starved relative to need.

**Key Data:**
- LSC received **$560 million** in FY 2024 (flat funding)
- The House proposed a **$71 million cut** for FY 2025
- The White House proposed **eliminating LSC entirely** in the FY 2026 budget
- LSC's own analysis determined it would need **$1.749 billion** just for Basic Field programs to meet demand
- The federal government provides only **~19% of total legal aid funding**
- LSC funds **130 legal aid programs** across every state and territory

**The Gap:**
- LSC actual funding: $560M
- LSC calculated need: $1.749B (Basic Field alone)
- Full FY 2025 request: $1.797B
- **Funding gap: $1.189 billion** (66% underfunded)

**State-level deterioration:**
- New York swept **$55 million** from the IOLA (Interest on Lawyer Account) Fund — money meant for legal services — to fund a homeowner protection program
- DC Mayor proposed a **67% cut** to the Access to Justice Initiative (from $31.5M to $10.5M)
- California proposed cutting **$260 million** from homeless housing assistance

**Verification:** Sources 1, 2, 3, 4

### Finding 2: Public Defender Crisis — Constitutional Right in Name Only

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

The public defender system in the United States is collapsing under underfunding and overwhelming caseloads, creating a two-tier justice system where the quality of defense depends on financial means.

**Key Data:**
- **75% of public defenders** manage caseloads far exceeding professional standards
- Professional standards recommend max **150 felony / 400 misdemeanor cases** per attorney annually
- ABA estimates roughly **13.9 hours per felony case** and **5.2 hours per misdemeanor** under current caseloads
- RAND Corporation recommends **286 hours for serious felony cases** (life without parole) and **248 hours** for murder charges
- Harris County, TX: **6,540 active criminal cases** with **82 public defenders** — capacity shortfall of **7,558 hours** just for misdemeanor cases

**Federal crisis:**
- The federal panel attorney program **ran out of money** in July 2025, causing 3-month payment delays for court-appointed attorneys
- The judiciary warned many private lawyers could **decline new cases**

**Research impact on outcomes:**
- Clients of public defenders in the **25th percentile of caseloads** receive sentences **nearly twice as long** as those in the 75th percentile (Caspi, SSRN)
- Higher public defender caseloads **increase pre-trial detention** and lead to **longer custodial sentences** (2020 study, Journal of Political Economy)

**Verification:** Sources 5, 6, 7, 26

### Finding 4: Mandatory Arbitration — The Hidden Class Action Ban

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

Mandatory arbitration clauses, buried in fine print of consumer and employment contracts, have effectively stripped tens of millions of Americans of their right to sue in court.

**Scale of the problem:**
- **Over 60 million American workers** have signed contracts with mandatory arbitration clauses and class action waivers
- Mandatory arbitration clauses appear in contracts for: credit cards, cell phones, nursing homes, employment, car loans, insurance, retirement accounts, franchise agreements
- Most consumers **do not read** these clauses
- Most employees **cannot negotiate** them — signing is a condition of employment or purchase

**Structural advantages for corporations:**
- **Substantial up-front costs:** Filing fees of hundreds to thousands of dollars — often exceeding the amount in dispute
- **Prohibition of class actions:** Prevents plaintiffs with small claims from pursuing cases collectively, providing legal immunity for widespread small injuries
- **Favorable venue selection:** Corporations choose arbitration locations convenient to themselves (e.g., eBay required customers to travel to San Jose, CA)
- **One-way agreements:** Only consumers/employees are bound to arbitration; corporations retain right to sue in court
- **Arbitrator selection bias:** Companies select arbitrators or arbitration organizations, creating repeat-business incentives to favor the company
- **No public record:** Decisions are confidential — no precedent, no public accountability
- **Limited discovery:** Restrictions on information gathering prevent plaintiffs from proving patterns of abuse
- **Limited judicial review:** Under the Federal Arbitration Act, virtually no review of substantive merits

**Supreme Court complicity:**
- In *AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion* (2011), the Court effectively overturned state protections against class action waivers
- In *Epic Systems Corp. v. Lewis* (2018), the Court upheld mandatory arbitration in employment contracts
- The 2010 decision left challenges to arbitration fairness "entirely in the hands of arbitrators themselves"

**SEC reversal (2025):**
- On September 17, 2025, the SEC reversed its longstanding policy prohibiting mandatory arbitration clauses in investor claims — opening the door to further expansion

**FAIR Act:**
- Passed the House in September 2019, would ban mandatory arbitration for workers and consumers
- Has not passed the Senate

**Verification:** Sources 10, 11, 12, 13

### Finding 6: Class Action Trends — Defensive Strategies and Declining Access

**Confidence:** [PV]

While class action filings remain active, corporations have deployed multiple strategies to reduce their exposure.

**2025 litigation trends:**
- **24% of respondents** reported class action litigation in the past 12 months (Norton Rose Fulbright 2025 Survey)
- Most common areas: **employment and labor, consumer protection, cybersecurity/data privacy**
- Consumer protection was cited by **41%** of respondents who experienced class actions in 2024
- **49%** of respondents were concerned about class actions in 2025

**Securities class actions:**
- **105 federal securities class actions** resolved in H1 2026
- At current pace, **210 resolutions** in 2026 — down 9% from 232 in 2025
- Average settlements reached a **decade high**
- AI-related filings reached a **five-year high** in H1 2026

**Mass arbitration as class action substitute:**
- Some law firms have turned to **mass arbitration** — filing thousands of individual arbitrations against companies that use class action waivers
- Under AAA fee schedule, a business facing **5,000 consumer arbitrations** must pay **$13 million in fees immediately** plus **$9.5 million** for arbitrator appointments
- Sunk costs cannot be recovered even if the business wins every case
- Some arbitration institutions have implemented measures to address "abusive mass arbitrations"

**TCPA as dominant class action vehicle:**
- Class actions accounted for **78.6% of all TCPA filings** (June 2025)
- TCPA litigation has **steadily increased** throughout 2025

**Verification:** Sources 24, 25

### Finding 8: The Civil Justice Gap — Unrepresented Litigants

**Confidence:** [VERIFIED]

The civil justice system is failing to protect the rights of low-income Americans, with a massive gap between legal needs and available resources.

**Key statistics:**
- **74% of low-income households** face at least one civil legal problem annually
- Low-income Americans received **no or insufficient legal help for 92%** of their civil legal problems
- In landlord-tenant matters, typically **90% of tenants appear unrepresented** while **90% of landlords appear with counsel**
- In New York State courts, an estimated **1.8 million people** appeared without a lawyer in 2015
- **98% of tenants** in eviction cases and **95% of parents** in child support cases were unrepresented
- In Georgia alone, **30,000–45,000+ people** annually appear unrepresented in domestic civil cases
- More than **75% of civil court cases** have at least one self-represented litigant
- Federal courts: more than **25% of nonprisoner civil cases** are filed pro se

**The structural problem:**
- There is **no constitutional right to counsel** in civil matters (only *Gideon v. Wainwright* for criminal)
- Legal aid organizations **turn away almost half of eligible applicants** due to insufficient resources
- The civil justice system lacks basic data infrastructure — courts still use paper files, no machine-readable pleadings
- We cannot even measure how unrepresented individuals fare against represented businesses

**Verification:** Sources 21, 22, 23

## Verdict

The legal system is structurally designed to favor the wealthy through multiple reinforcing mechanisms:

1. **Funding asymmetry:** BigLaw generates $178.95B in revenue while the entire federal legal aid system receives $560M (319:1 ratio). The legal aid system is funded at 32% of calculated need.

2. **Constitutional gap:** There is no right to counsel in civil matters, where the most consequential life outcomes (housing, custody, benefits) are decided. 92% of low-income civil legal problems go unaddressed.

3. **Forced arbitration:** 60M+ workers and untold consumers are stripped of court access through hidden clauses in adhesion contracts, with structural advantages built in for corporations.

4. **Lobbying capture:** $5.3B in annual federal lobbying spending (including $490M+ from the Chamber's tort reform arm) systematically produces legislation limiting corporate liability.

5. **Consolidation:** Law firm mergers are creating an oligopoly of mega-firms charging premium rates, while mid-market and legal aid firms are squeezed.

6. **Public defender collapse:** Overburdened defenders produce measurably worse outcomes — clients receive sentences up to 2x longer based on caseload alone.

**The result:** A legal system where justice is a function of wealth, not rights.
