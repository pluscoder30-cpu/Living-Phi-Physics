## PART III: THE BANK OF ENGLAND

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Founded | July 27, 1694 |
| Founder | William Paterson (Scottish merchant) |
| Initial Loan | £1.2 million to government (~£200M today) |
| Purpose | Fund war against France (Nine Years' War) |
| First Governor | Sir John Houblon |
| Staff at Opening | 17 clerks, 2 gatekeepers |
| Source | 3 |

### Founding Context

**1. The Problem**
- William III needed funds for wars against France
- Charles II had not repaid debts to goldsmiths
- Goldsmith businesses collapsed - no one would lend to monarchs
- **[VERIFIED]** Source 3

**2. The Solution (Paterson's Plan)**
- Public lends £1.2 million to government
- Subscribers become shareholders in joint-stock company
- "The Governor and the Company of the Bank of England"
- Banking privileges including note issuance
- Royal Charter by King William and Queen Mary
- **[VERIFIED]** Source 3

### Slavery Connection [CRITICAL - Source 9]

**1. Bank's Direct Involvement**
- Services to EIC, Royal African Company, South Sea Company (all had accounts)
- Bank owned two plantations and enslaved workforce in Grenada (1700s, security on defaulted loan)

**2. Governors and Directors**
- Governor required £4,000 in Bank stock (hundreds of thousands today)
- Some Governors/Directors were slave traders and plantation owners
- Wealth invested in Bank stock included slavery-generated wealth

**3. Guinea Coin**
- First minted 1663, named from Guinea coast (center of English slave trade)
- African gold transported by Royal African Company
- Minted into coins for English circulation
- Elephant and castle symbol = Royal African Company

**4. William Morice ("Prince of Slavers")**
- From 1704: invested in 103+ voyages to Africa
- ~30,000 Africans forced across Atlantic
- Business papers now in Bank of England Archive

**5. Regional Banks and Slavery**
- Liverpool, Bristol, Manchester built on slave trade wealth
- Cotton from American South (enslaved labor) sustained British Industrial Revolution
- Up to 80% of cotton in Manchester mills from American South
- 1826: cotton price crash - 5 London banks collapsed, Bank of England rescued with emergency loans

**6. Hibbert Family**
- Samuel Hibbert: Bank Director 1819-37
- Family fortune from slavery economy
- Manufactured cotton in Manchester, shipped to West Africa (triangular trade)
