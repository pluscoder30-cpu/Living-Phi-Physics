## Federal Statutes

### RICO (18 U.S.C. §§ 1961-1962)

The Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act provides the primary framework for prosecuting the enterprise.

**Key Provisions:**
- **Enterprise Definition (§ 1961(4)):** Covers any individual, partnership, corporation, association, or other legal entity
- **Pattern of Racketeering Activity (§ 1961(5)):** Includes bribery, extortion, money laundering, fraud, and obstruction of justice
- **Civil RICO (§ 1964):** Private parties can bring suit; treble damages available

## Evidentiary Standards

### Preponderance of Evidence (Civil)

Standard for civil RICO claims and tort actions.

**Coverage:** All documented harm chains meet preponderance standard.

### Beyond Reasonable Doubt (Criminal)

Standard for criminal RICO, money laundering, tax fraud.

**Coverage:** Multiple entities with documented patterns meet this standard.

### Clear and Convincing Evidence

Standard for some civil forfeiture actions.

**Coverage:** Offshore structures with documented tax avoidance meet this standard.

## How to File a Claim

### Federal Court (RICO)

1. **Draft complaint** alleging enterprise, pattern of racketeering, and injury
2. **File in appropriate district** (typically SDNY for financial crimes)
3. **Serve defendants** according to Fed. R. Civ. P. 4
4. **Seek discovery** for offshore entity records
5. **Trial** before jury or judge

### International Forum (ICC)

1. **Contact ICC Office of the Prosecutor** with information package
2. **Provide evidence** of crimes against humanity or war crimes
3. **ICC preliminary examination** (typically 3-5 years)
4. **Investigation and trial** if authorized

### State Court (Tort)

1. **Draft complaint** alleging specific tort claims
2. **File in state court** of appropriate jurisdiction
3. **Serve defendants**
4. **Discovery and trial**
